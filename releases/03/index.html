The incident the FBI can only show as a digital recreation· 2023

In 2023, a federal law enforcement special agent reported a two-day incident near a sensitive national security site in the western United States. No actual footage of the event exists: what the FBI published, at the Department of War's request in 2026, are two 'digital recreations' —animations based on the agent's first-hand account— catalogued as FBI-UAP-PR005 and PR006, internally titled 'Narrative Statement 3-1' and '3-2.' It is the only case in the corpus where the government itself explicitly acknowledges that the public visual material is not a recording of the event, but a reconstruction made years later from oral testimony.

Four orb sightings within the same 25-mile radius, 2021-2025· 2021–25

●

Between November 2021 and July 2025, civilian witnesses filmed on their phones four sightings of spherical lights within a 25-mile radius of a rural area in the northeastern United States. The FBI assessed the witnesses as 'highly credible' (the first three) or 'credible' (the fourth, with two independent witnesses), and the U.S. government authenticated at least one of the videos. The four clips —'Triangle-Orbs' (2021), 'Red Orb Rotation' (2022), 'Orbs Over the Pond' (2024) and an untitled fourth (2025)— were released in PURSUE's third drop (June 12, 2026) under a distinct series ('FBI-UAP-PR', not 'DOW-UAP-PR'), with supporting documents (FBI-UAP-D004 through D010) not publicly available outside DVIDS. It is, within the corpus, one of the few multi-year geographic recurrence patterns with an explicit FBI credibility assessment.

Four administrative documents declassified in PURSUE Release 03 (June 12, 2026) showing the 'long tail' of how the U.S. government handled UFO interest over half a century: an FBI correspondence referral (1949), the file of the FBI's Newark field office (1952-1967), the FBI's 'UFO' investigation in Washington State (1952-1960), and a file of correspondence among Congress, the White House and citizens about UFOs (1998). Eighteen CIA documents declassified in PURSUE Release 03 (June 12, 2026) portraying the Agency's dual role toward UFOs: steering U.S. policy and collecting foreign intelligence. It includes the Scientific Advisory Panel (Robertson Panel, 1952-53), Project Blue Book Special Report No. 14 (Battelle's statistical analysis), the report on the U-2 and OXCART programs (1954-1974) —which acknowledges that many UFO reports of the era were the CIA's own spy flights—, the 'current status of the UFO project', and the Leon Davidson case; plus a broad collection of intelligence on sightings in the Soviet bloc and abroad (Budapest, Soviet scientists, Sary Shagan, Ladakh/Himalaya, British and Australian activity) and a modern Harare case (2008). Its value is provenance: it documents how the CIA managed and monitored the phenomenon as a counterintelligence matter, not that the objects were anomalous.

NASA · Gemini and Mercury-era crew debriefings (1962-1966, PURSUE)· 1962–66

Eight NASA documents declassified in PURSUE Release 03 (June 12, 2026): the technical and scientific crew debriefings of the Gemini program (missions 4, 5, 7 and 9, 1965-66) plus the Mercury-era Astronaut Scientific Debriefings (1962-63). They are the primary transcripts behind the most-cited anomalous observations of early spaceflight: the cylindrical object James McDivitt photographed on Gemini 4 and the 'bogey' and 'fireflies' Borman and Lovell reported on Gemini 7. The corpus already analyzes those sightings separately (gemini4-mcdivitt-1965, gemini7-borman-1965); here the full primary sources are incorporated. Their value is provenance: most is routine technical debrief, and the anomalous content —mostly explained as ice/debris/booster stage— is a minor fraction, with the McDivitt object as the notable residue.

Seven FBI documents declassified in PURSUE Release 03 (June 12, 2026) make up a single file: repeated 'orb' observations in the Northeastern US over years (~2021-2024), near a residence whose location is redacted. Five FD-1057s (2024) and two FD-302s (2026) capture the witness account —'plasma orbs', a 'red sphere' below the tree line, objects moving laterally, caught on a home camera— and, unusually, corroboration by FBI special agents who observed the activity on-site in 2024 with full line of sight. Colorado Springs 2022 · object over Cheyenne Mountain (FBI/IC, PURSUE)· 2022

On February 15, 2022, at 09:35 (Mountain Time), five U.S. Army service members at Fort Carson observed an airborne object over Cheyenne Mountain (Colorado Springs), home to NORAD's hardened command complex. PURSUE declassified the file in its third tranche (Release 03, June 12, 2026): an FBI FD-302 (interview), an FD-1057 (internal UAP report), a digital rendering of the object, and an intelligence-community analysis (ICA-UAP-D001). The assessment concluded the object was 'possibly backscattering of sunlight', but with 'low confidence' — a combination that leaves the case formally unresolved. Its value is institutional provenance and analytical honesty, not a proven anomaly.

●

Around late June 2026, in connection with the Washington D.C. Disclosure Forum, Representative Anna Paulina Luna (R-FL), chair of the House Oversight Committee's UAP Task Force, announced that lawmakers are working with the White House to grant immunity to a list of whistleblowers who claim to have information about alleged recovered non-human craft and 'biologics'. Harare Airport · object over the runway in a CIA document (Zimbabwe, 2008)· 2008

On July 2, 2008, an unidentified object was observed over Harare International Airport in Zimbabwe, possibly by both radar and optical means. A CIA cable dated the next day —classified 'Secret' and declassified only in 2026 through the third PURSUE release— describes a 'disc-like' object with a hollow center, a series of rotating lights on the underside and 'beams' emanating from it, hovering at an undetermined altitude. The report escalated to the White House Situation Room and the intelligence community, putting CIA assets in Zimbabwe on high alert; internally there was debate over whether it was a foreign government's surveillance platform or an object of non-human origin. There is no independently verifiable evidence beyond the intelligence document itself.

PURSUE · Presidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP Encounters· 2026

●

PURSUE (Presidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP Encounters) is the executive directive signed by the Trump administration in May 2026 ordering the newly renamed Department of War (formerly DoD) to execute an 'expeditious' interagency release of unresolved UAP records. The war.gov/ufo/ portal opened on May 8, 2026 and published in successive tranches: Release 1 (May 8), Release 2 (May 22), Release 3 (June 12), Release 4 (July 10) and Release 5 (August 7). By mid-June the portal had surpassed 1.7 billion accesses. The AARO assessment accompanying the third release (signed by its director Jon Kosloski, June 5) emphasizes 'plasma-like' orbs —including an orange 'mother orb' releasing smaller red orbs— and concedes that ~40% of cases remain unexplained. The fourth release (July 10, 2026) contributed 40 additional files (14 documents, 19 videos, 4 audio recordings, 3 images), including infrared video of a six-pointed object over the Yellow Sea captured by U.S. Indo-Pacific Command in 2025, testimony from a military aviator with 28 years of service about a rectangular object with 'flight characteristics unlike anything I had seen' (Eastern US, 2019), documentation of an intrusion over the Pantex nuclear plant (Texas, September 2015), and a transcript of a Los Alamos conference (1949) where physicists attempted to explain the 'green fireballs' over the nuclear laboratory. It is the first U.S. federal UAP-release infrastructure with a direct presidential mandate and a stable public URL. The content remains more procedural than probative —orb videos, historical audio and declassified documents, without the conclusive piece that would settle the material question— but it is no longer an empty channel.
