Ademar José Gevaerd

Brazilian ufologist · founder and editor of Revista UFO · founder of CBPDV · 700+ field investigations

Biography

Ademar José Gevaerd (1962–2022), known as A.J. Gevaerd, was Brazil's most influential ufologist and one of the reference figures for UAP research in Latin America. A chemist by training, he left teaching in the mid-1980s to devote himself fully to the subject and, in 1985, founded «Revista UFO», the country's longest-running specialized magazine, which he directed for more than three decades. From that platform he organized much of the Brazilian research community and built a case archive that is, by volume and continuity, one of the richest in the southern hemisphere.

Gevaerd founded and directed the Centro Brasileiro de Pesquisas de Discos Voadores (CBPDV) and served as Brazil's director for the international MUFON network. Over his career he conducted or coordinated more than seven hundred field investigations within Brazil and lectured in nearly thirty countries, making him the main bridge between Brazilian ufology and the international conversation. His work stood out for insisting on documentation —photographs, military reports, corroborated testimony— over speculation, in a field especially prone to sensationalism.

His most significant institutional contribution was the campaign «UFOs: Liberdade de Informação Já» («UFOs: Freedom of Information Now»), which through the 2000s pressured the Brazilian state to declassify and transfer its military files on sightings to the National Archives. The campaign produced concrete results: Brazil became one of the countries with a relatively open policy of archiving and reporting UAP cases, and episodes such as the so-called «Operation Saucer» in the Amazon (1977) or the «Varginha case» (1996) became better documented thanks to that pressure. For the corpus, that declassified file in the Brazilian National Archives is a primary piece directly tied to his work.

For the UAP Codex map, Gevaerd represents the Latin American, archival dimension of the phenomenon: not a theorist of exotic hypotheses but an organizer who professionalized regional research and forced the opening of state archives. His critical flank is the usual one in media ufology: the closeness between the investigative and the editorial role —being at once archivist, director of a commercial magazine and spokesman— can create dissemination incentives that strain rigor. Even so, his documentary legacy and his push for institutional transparency place him among the indispensable actors of the South American record. He died in December 2022.

Varginha· 1996

Brazilian Army operation in January 1996, one or more non-human creatures reported by civilians, soldier Marco Eli Cherese dead six weeks later from atypical bacterial infection. Thirty years later, the case remains politically alive: Brazilian witnesses testified behind closed doors in the U.S. Congress (Longworth, Jan 2026), a Brazilian neurosurgeon described a lilac-eyed entity, James Fox and Leslie Kean hosted the 30th-anniversary press conference at the National Press Club (Jan 2026), the Brazilian Superior Tribunal Militar dismissed the case as 'error and rumor' (Jan 2026), and the bipartisan Capitol press conference of Jun 9, 2026 specifically called to declassify Varginha records.

Colares (Operação Prato)· 1977–78

Between 1977 and 1978, residents of Colares island (Pará) endured hundreds of nightly encounters with luminous objects projecting vertical beams, producing medically documented geometric burns and anemia. The Brazilian Air Force ran Operação Prato for fourteen months and officially acknowledged the case in 2010.

The Official UFO Night (Brazil)· 1986

On the night of 19 May 1986, the radars of CINDACTA I (Brasília) detected some 21 unidentified objects over the airspace of São Paulo, Rio and Goiás. The FAB scrambled five supersonic fighters (F-5 and Mirage) from Santa Cruz (Rio) and Anápolis (Goiás); the pilots had radar and visual contacts with objects that evaded and accelerated to extreme speeds. Days later, the Minister of Aeronautics, Brigadier Octávio Moreira Lima, confirmed the episode in a press conference alongside the pilots, admitting there was no conclusive technical explanation. It is one of the best-documented military UAP cases in the world.

National Archives · FAB UFO collection (Brazil)· 2008

Brazil's National Archives keeps public —via the SIAN platform— the 'Óvnis Fund': a collection of UFO records produced by the Air Force Command (FAB) spanning 1952 to 2023 (reports, official communications, images, audio and drawings). The institutional flow begins in 2008, when the FAB started routing declassified material to the archive. The National Archives' official figure is around 743 records; the 2026 press reported it as close to 900. In 2026, amid the global disclosure push, it was highlighted by the press as one of the world's most open state UAP archives — a contrast with U.S. strategic ambiguity.
