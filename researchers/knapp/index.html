George Knapp

Investigative journalist, KLAS-TV Las Vegas (40+ years) + 7-time Peabody Award winner + historic Bob Lazar report (1989)

Biography

George Knapp is an American investigative journalist based in Las Vegas, lead anchor of the CBS affiliate KLAS-TV and, since 2006, a regular co-host of the late-night radio program Coast to Coast AM. Over four decades he has made the UAP phenomenon a sustained, documented beat —not an occasional one— which makes him one of the few mainstream-media reporters with a long, verifiable track record on the subject. His work has earned numerous regional Emmy Awards and press-association honors, a relevant detail because it anchors his credibility in conventional journalism rather than the ufology circuit.

His first major media milestone came in 1989, when he interviewed Robert «Bob» Lazar on KLAS, who claimed to have worked on reverse-engineering craft of non-human origin at a facility called S-4, near Area 51 in the Nevada desert. The interview introduced Area 51 and the name «Bob Lazar» into popular culture and remains contested: neither the academic credentials Lazar claims nor his technical assertions have been independently verified, and over the years Knapp has held a reporter's stance —presenting the testimony and its inconsistencies— rather than a believer's.

Since 1996 Knapp has led the journalistic coverage of Skinwalker Ranch in Utah's Uintah Basin, where a family reported a sequence of anomalous phenomena. It was he who publicly connected the ranch to businessman Robert Bigelow, and later to the federal AAWSAP/BAASS program the Department of Defense funded there between 2008 and 2010. In 2021 he co-wrote, with the program's DIA director James Lacatski and biochemist Colm Kelleher, the book «Skinwalkers at the Pentagon», one of the few sources to narrate from the inside the chain running from the ranch to the Pentagon.

For the UAP Codex corpus, Knapp is a documentary node: not a theorist or a witness, but the journalist who for decades archived, cross-checked and published the materials —letters, contracts, testimony under declaration— on which the 2017–2025 disclosure cycle was later built. His methodological importance is twofold: he contributed to the public record pieces that would otherwise have stayed classified, and he modeled a way of handling the topic —skeptical, documented, free of sensationalism— that later journalism (Blumenthal, Kean, Cooper at the New York Times) would take up. His critical flank is closeness to interested sources such as Bigelow and Lazar, which some colleagues read as a lack of distance; his defense is that covering a subject for forty years requires access, and that he always published the inconsistencies alongside the claims.

Skinwalker Ranch · AAWSAP and field experiments· 2008–10

U.S. DoD federal program (~USD 22M, 2008–2010), requested by Senator Harry Reid, contracted to the DIA and executed by Robert Bigelow's BAASS from the Skinwalker Ranch in Utah. It produced 38 Defense Intelligence Reference Documents (DIRDs) on exotic physics and biological effects. The first formally documented U.S. federal program after Project Blue Book — with contracts, paid receipts and testimony under declaration — and the direct precursor to AATIP (Elizondo) and the disclosure wave that culminates in AARO and the 2023–2025 congressional hearings. The case also folds in the three instrumented-measurement campaigns at the property —NIDS (1996–2004), BAASS and Brandon Fugal's televised team— and their results, of far lower evidential quality than the program's paperwork.

USS Omaha — transmedium sphere· 2019

On 15 July 2019, in a restricted warning area off San Diego, the Combat Information Center of the USS Omaha recorded with FLIR/night vision a sphere that flew over the sea, stopped, and descended into the water with no visible wake ('transmedium' behavior). Radar showed at one point up to 14 spheres surrounding the ship. Jeremy Corbell and George Knapp released the material in 2021; the Pentagon confirmed it was captured by Navy personnel and was under UAPTF analysis —authenticity confirmed, not explanation.
