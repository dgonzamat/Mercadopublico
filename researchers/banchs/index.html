Section A · Analytical ufology (Argentina)

Roberto Banchs

Architect and social psychologist · pioneer of the biopsychosocial approach to UFOs in Argentina

Biography

Argentine architect and social psychologist, the dean of the country's analytical ufology and a reference for reflexive skepticism: he studies cases by interviewing witnesses and cross-checking newspaper archives and bibliography, falling into neither credulity nor automatic refutation. In the 1960s he collaborated with the UFO commission of the Argentine Navy Secretariat and observed phenomena on radar from an outpost. He is the author of 'Ovnis: peregrinos del silencio' (1993), 'Fenómenos aéreos inusuales: un enfoque biopsicosocial' (1994 —the first book on the subject published by an Argentine university—) and the 'Guía biográfica de la ufología argentina' (2000).

The Trancas Case· 1963

On the night of 21 October 1963, the Moreno family reported several luminous objects at their farm in Trancas (Tucumán): one settled near the house, by the railway tracks, for about 45 minutes, while others lined up in the distance. A beam of light was directed at the house, and a residue was collected from the site. It is one of the most famous Argentine cases for the number of witnesses and for having a laboratory analysis of the residue (mostly calcium carbonate). It retains dramatically toned reported elements and never had a formal resolution.

Argentine cattle mutilations· 2002

In April 2002, a wave of dead animals with precise 'mutilations' (clean cuts, absence of blood, missing soft organs) shook public opinion in central Argentine provinces, linked by many to UFOs. The animal-health agency SENASA concluded it was natural scavenging, attributing the pattern to wildlife —including the 'red mouse opossum'—, an explanation that was heavily debated. It is the Argentine skeptical case par excellence: apparent anomaly vs. natural explanation.
