George Adamski

Contactee · lecturer on philosophy and religion at Palomar Gardens, California · author of "Flying Saucers Have Landed" (1953, with Desmond Leslie)

Los Angeles Times, 1938 · Los Angeles Times Photographic Archive, UCLA Library (Wikimedia Commons) · CC-BY 4.0

Biography

He was born in 1891 in Bromberg, then in the German Empire and today Bydgoszcz, Poland, and came to the United States as a child. In the 1930s he taught a blend of Christianity and Eastern religions in California, and from 1944 he ran a café at the foot of Palomar Mountain, where he gave talks and showed photos he said were of spacecraft. He is the best known of the 1950s "contactees": he said that on November 20, 1952, near Desert Center, he communicated by gestures and telepathy with a man from Venus, and in later books he added trips aboard spacecraft to the Moon and other planets. His story spread widely; the Air Force gave it no credence, the FBI warned him for claiming it had approved his lectures, and most UFO researchers considered him a fraud. According to researcher Peter Rogerson, the description of the woman in the Vilas-Boas case suggests his influence.
