The turning point came in October 2017, when the Pan-STARRS telescopes detected 1I/ʻOumuamua, the first confirmed interstellar object passing through the Solar System. Loeb's value to the debate is not that he «proved» anything, but that he forced a methodological discussion: what evidence would be required to treat the technological hypothesis as science rather than taboo.

In 2021 Loeb founded the Galileo Project, a research program with dedicated telescopes and sensors that systematically searches, with open data, for three things: interstellar objects like ʻOumuamua, possible artifacts of non-human origin, and UAP in Earth's atmosphere. In 2023 he led an expedition to the Pacific Ocean off Papua New Guinea to recover fragments of the interstellar meteor IM1 (CNEOS 2014-01-08); the metallic spherules collected and their composition triggered a fresh round of dispute over whether they were interstellar or terrestrial/industrial contamination.
