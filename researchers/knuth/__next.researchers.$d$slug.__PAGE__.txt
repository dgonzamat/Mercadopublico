Su especialidad técnica —métodos bayesianos y de máxima entropía aplicados al aprendizaje automático y a la inferencia en física— es justamente lo que da peso a su participación en el debate UAP: no aborda el fenómeno desde la creencia, sino desde la estadística de la evidencia y el diseño de instrumentos de medición.

Knuth es uno de los pocos físicos académicos en activo que ha llevado el estudio de los UAP a publicaciones revisadas por pares. Ha coescrito análisis cuantitativos de casos militares —estimando, a partir de los datos de sensores disponibles, parámetros como aceleración, velocidad y potencia implicada— para argumentar que ciertos incidentes (como los asociados a los videos de la Marina de EE.UU.) son difíciles de explicar con tecnología convencional si los datos se toman al pie de la letra. Su postura característica es metodológica y cautelosa: insiste en que la cuestión no es «creer o no creer», sino que el campo carece de datos calibrados y multisensor, y que sin ese rigor instrumental cualquier conclusión —escéptica o entusiasta— es prematura.

Knuth is a professor of Physics at the University at Albany (SUNY), where he earned his PhD in Physics in 1995, and editor-in-chief of the scientific journal «Entropy» (MDPI). Between 2001 and 2005 he worked as a researcher at NASA's Ames Research Center, in the Intelligent Systems Division, designing artificial-intelligence algorithms for astrophysical data analysis. His technical specialty —Bayesian and maximum-entropy methods applied to machine learning and inference in physics— is precisely what gives weight to his role in the UAP debate: he approaches the phenomenon not from belief but from the statistics of evidence and the design of measurement instruments.

Knuth is one of the few active academic physicists to have taken the study of UAP into peer-reviewed publications. He has co-authored quantitative analyses of military cases —estimating, from available sensor data, parameters such as acceleration, velocity and implied power— to argue that certain incidents (like those tied to the U.S. Navy videos) are hard to explain with conventional technology if the data are taken at face value. His characteristic stance is methodological and cautious: he insists the question is not «believe or not believe», but that the field lacks calibrated, multi-sensor data, and that without such instrumental rigor any conclusion —skeptical or enthusiastic— is premature.

In 2022 he traveled to the U.S. west coast to study, on site, the phenomena behind leaked Navy sightings, and since then he has been at the forefront of the effort to equip UAP study with scientific protocols: sensor stations, open data, peer review. He is tied to the topic's emerging academic ecosystem —among others, the Sol Foundation— and is a frequent voice in forums that seek to separate the scientific problem (what are these objects when the data are good?) from the cultural noise around it.

For the UAP Codex map, Knuth represents —alongside Avi Loeb— the «open-scientific» narrative: the bet on treating the phenomenon as a problem of physics and instrumentation, not of faith or leaks. His most valuable contribution to the corpus is not a claim about what UAP are, but a demand about how they should be studied.
