Travis Walton

Logger on an Arizona forestry crew · the disappearance case with the most convergent witnesses and polygraphs in the corpus (1975)

Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA

Biography

His case is the one that best withstands the standard objection to abductions —'a single witness, no corroboration'—: six crewmates reported the event to the sheriff while Walton was still missing, that is, before knowing the outcome and at a moment when the report exposed them to a homicide investigation. Five of them passed polygraph tests. The objection that persists is not a lack of witnesses but the context: the crew had a behind-schedule contract whose penalty was suspended by force majeure, a motive skeptics consider sufficient and which the convergence of accounts does not entirely rule out.

Travis Walton (Snowflake)· 1975

On 5 November 1975, Travis Walton and six fellow members of a forestry crew in Arizona saw a glowing disc-shaped object; by the convergent testimony of all six, Walton approached it and vanished for five days. The seven reported the event to the sheriff before the outcome was known, five passed polygraphs, and an institutional search by the Forest Service and USAF is documented.
