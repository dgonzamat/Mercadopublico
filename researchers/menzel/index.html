Donald H. Menzel

Astrophysicist · director of the Harvard College Observatory (acting from 1952, director from 1954 to 1966)

Babette Whipple; retrato cedido por Elizabeth Menzel Davis (Wikimedia Commons) · CC-BY-SA 3.0

Biography

A theoretical astrophysicist at Harvard University, he studied the Sun's chromosphere and gaseous nebulae and directed the university's observatory. He was one of the first well-known scientists to speak publicly about flying saucers, and in three books (1953, 1963 and 1977) he argued that sightings are explained by stars, planets, aircraft or atmospheric effects such as reflections, mirages and temperature inversions. His proposals appear in several cases: refracted city lights at Lubbock, a searchlight seen through layers of haze on the Nash and Fortenberry flight, ball lightning at Levelland, and a hoax in the Barra da Tijuca photos and at Falcon Lake. Other scientists rejected some of them: J. Allen Hynek dismissed his balloon version of Socorro, and physicist James E. McDonald the weather explanation Menzel backed for the Washington radar sightings.
