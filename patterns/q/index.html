PURSUE · Presidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP Encounters· 2026

●

PURSUE (Presidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP Encounters) is the executive directive signed by the Trump administration in May 2026 ordering the newly renamed Department of War (formerly DoD) to execute an 'expeditious' interagency release of unresolved UAP records. The war.gov/ufo/ portal opened on May 8, 2026 and published in successive tranches: Release 1 (May 8), Release 2 (May 22), Release 3 (June 12), Release 4 (July 10) and Release 5 (August 7). By mid-June the portal had surpassed 1.7 billion accesses. The AARO assessment accompanying the third release (signed by its director Jon Kosloski, June 5) emphasizes 'plasma-like' orbs —including an orange 'mother orb' releasing smaller red orbs— and concedes that ~40% of cases remain unexplained. The fourth release (July 10, 2026) contributed 40 additional files (14 documents, 19 videos, 4 audio recordings, 3 images), including infrared video of a six-pointed object over the Yellow Sea captured by U.S. Indo-Pacific Command in 2025, testimony from a military aviator with 28 years of service about a rectangular object with 'flight characteristics unlike anything I had seen' (Eastern US, 2019), documentation of an intrusion over the Pantex nuclear plant (Texas, September 2015), and a transcript of a Los Alamos conference (1949) where physicists attempted to explain the 'green fireballs' over the nuclear laboratory. It is the first U.S. federal UAP-release infrastructure with a direct presidential mandate and a stable public URL. The content remains more procedural than probative —orb videos, historical audio and declassified documents, without the conclusive piece that would settle the material question— but it is no longer an empty channel.

2026A95%143🇷🇺Soviet Union.

SETKA Program · Soviet state UAP investigation (1978–1991)· 1978–91

Official Soviet program for investigating anomalous aerial phenomena, established in October 1978 following the 1977–78 sighting wave (particularly Petrozavodsk). It operated in two parallel branches: SETKA-AN within the Academy of Sciences and SETKA-MO within the Ministry of Defense, ordered by the Military-Industrial Commission. The IZMIRAN Institute for terrestrial magnetism and radio-wave propagation led the scientific coordination. For thirteen years (1978–1990/91) a standing order required every Soviet serviceman to report any anomalous phenomenon in writing — approximately three thousand reports were collected. It is the near-exact institutional parallel of Project Blue Book, executed outside the West and chronologically symmetric with the AAWSAP cycle that would come three decades later in the U.S.

Sky Canada Project · federal scientific review of UAP reporting· 2025

●

The Sky Canada Project is the review the Office of the Chief Science Advisor of Canada (OCSA), under Mona Nemer, devoted to the state of public reporting of unidentified aerial phenomena. Launched in 2022, its report — 'Management of Public Reporting of Unidentified Aerial Phenomena in Canada' — had a preview in January 2025 and full publication in mid-2025. It finds that Canada receives between 600 and 1,000 UAP reports a year but that since 1995 no federal body has held the mandate to collect them: they are scattered across the RCMP, Transport Canada and the Canadian Space Agency. It recommends creating a single national lead to standardize, collect and publish the data. It is the first Canadian federal-scientific acknowledgment that the reporting system is broken; it does not investigate sightings nor pronounce on their nature.

MUFON (Mutual UFO Network) is the largest and longest-running civilian UAP reporting and investigation network. It enters the corpus as a large-scale reporting institution with contested methodological credibility.

2026B15%380🇳🇿New Zealand.

Twelve volumes, right before Christmas: New Zealand's 2010 UFO declassification· 1952–10

On December 22, 2010 —two days before Christmas Eve— the New Zealand Defence Force (NZDF) released twelve volumes, roughly 2,500 pages, of Royal New Zealand Air Force (RNZAF) UFO files dating back to 1952: nearly sixty years of reports from civilian witnesses, military personnel and pilots, including the material documenting the famous 1978 'Kaikōura Lights' (see [[kaikoura-1978]]). The release came after two years of lobbying by researcher Suzanne Hansen, director of UFOCUS NZ, via an Official Information Act request. It was, at once, the largest UFO declassification by an Oceania country and a textbook case of the limits of a 'complete' release: it only covered the Air Force —the Navy and Army kept their own records classified—, with privacy embargoes running until 2050 and entire pages illegible due to redaction.

Aeronautica Militare: Italy's 2014 UFO declassification· 1978–14

Following a wave of sightings in 1978, Prime Minister Giulio Andreotti designated the Aeronautica Militare (Italian Air Force) as the institutional body responsible for receiving and investigating UFO reports in Italy. Decades of files accumulated under 'Secret'/'Reserved' stamps stayed closed until journalists Lao Petrilli and Vincenzo Sinapi gained access to them for the book 'UFO: I dossier italiani' (March 2014), revealing previously unpublished cases —including a radar contact confirmed by a C-130 at Grazzanise (2005)—. Today the Air Force keeps a public year-by-year archive dating to 1972 on its official site, though the institution itself has never framed that routine transparency as a formal act of declassification.

1978–14B25%383🇮🇪Ireland.

Ireland's 'X-Files': UFO records via FOI (2023)· 1962–23

In July 2023, a Freedom of Information request uncovered six decades of Irish government correspondence on UFOs, scattered across the Office of the Taoiseach, Defence, Transport and the Irish Aviation Authority (IAA), with no agency ever formally taking ownership of the subject. The documented starting point is Transport Minister Albert Reynolds's 1981 reply confirming eight reports since 1962; the archive ranges from a 2021 email warning the Taoiseach of 'quite possibly the biggest news in the history of the planet' to TDs relaying constituents' sightings. The picture that emerges is not deliberate concealment but sustained bureaucratic confusion: no one in the Irish state was ever designated the single point of responsibility for the topic.
