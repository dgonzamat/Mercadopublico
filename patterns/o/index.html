2026B80%203🇬🇧United Kingdom.

Berwyn Mountain · Llandrillo· 1974

On the night of 23 January 1974, the Berwyn Mountains at Llandrillo (Wales) shook with a roar and a glow that many took for a crashed UFO. The official evidence — MoD files declassified in 2010 and geological-survey data — points to the coincidence of a magnitude 3.5 earthquake (20:38) with a bright meteor seen over north Wales and England. Nicknamed the 'Welsh Roswell', it is a model case of how two simultaneous natural events spawn a cover-up retrieval legend.

1974B12%206🇬🇧United Kingdom.

Bonnybridge · Falkirk Triangle· 1992–97

Since 1992, the Scottish town of Bonnybridge, in the centre of the so-called 'Falkirk Triangle', concentrated hundreds of reports of lights and objects. Local councillor Billy Buchanan took the matter to government: he wrote to the Prime Minister and the Ministry of Defence and pushed a council meeting in 1993. It is less an incident than a sustained 'hotspot' phenomenon — high volume, low individual evidence quality — and a case study in how a community and its local government process a wave.

1992–97B14%209🇺🇸United States.

Maury Island· 1947

In June 1947, days before Kenneth Arnold's sighting, Harold Dahl and Fred Crisman claimed to have seen six doughnut-shaped objects over Maury Island (Washington); one supposedly dropped metallic slag that injured his son and killed his dog. Dahl said he was visited by a 'man in black' who warned him to stay quiet. Two intelligence officers died when the B-25 carrying the 'samples' crashed. The FBI rated it a hoax — and Dahl himself admitted he would call it a fraud. It is the foundational case of the 'men in black' myth.

1947B10%212🇿🇦South Africa.

The Kalahari Incident· 1989

It was claimed that on 7 May 1989 South African Air Force Mirage fighters shot down an unidentified object that fell in the Kalahari Desert, with the craft and two occupants recovered. The whole case rests on documents supplied by James van Greunen, examined and found fraudulent; he himself admitted faking details. Researcher Cynthia Hind dismantled it in 'Anatomy of a hoax'. It is one of the most solidly debunked 'crash retrieval' cases.

1989B7%279🇲🇽Mexico.

State Department cable · Mexican Congress 2003 (PURSUE)· 2003

A U.S. State Department diplomatic cable (document #059UAP00013, filed September 16, 2003) records that, on September 12, 2003, the Mexican Congress heard testimony on unidentified aerial phenomena as part of debate over a proposed 'Aerial Space Protection Law'. According to the cable, presenters showed 'two alleged alien corpses' and videos of Mexican pilots' encounters. The document itself notes the disagreement over the validity of the purported corpses, takes no analytical position, and the law did not pass. The cable resurfaced declassified in the first release of the PURSUE system (May 8, 2026): it is a diplomatic record of the event, not a verification of the claims, and it precedes Jaime Maussan's 2023 hearing by twenty years.

Mexico eclipse wave · the object filmed during totality on 11 July 1991· 1991

On 11 July 1991, during the totality of the last total solar eclipse of the 20th century over Mexico City, dozens of people camcordered a bright, seemingly metallic object hovering in the darkened daytime sky. Broadcast on television by Jaime Maussan, it triggered the '1991-1993 wave' and founded the Latin American UFO-media ecosystem. Astronomical analysis identifies the object as the planet Venus.
