Pattern 8g

Sustained local persistence

UAPs returning to the same site over years (Hessdalen, Popocatépetl, Kumburgaz).

Cases exhibiting this pattern (21)

24🇳🇴Norway.

Hessdalen lights· 1981

Norway's Hessdalen valley has produced recurring anomalous lights since 1981 and is the only UAP phenomenon under continuous instrumented scientific monitoring for over four decades, with an automated measurement station and peer-reviewed publications.

Popocatépetl· 1992

Since 1992, the thermal and visible cameras with which CENAPRED monitors the Popocatépetl volcano have recorded between 50 and 200 events of objects emerging from or overflying the crater, automatically archived by a system not designed to detect UAP. AARO acknowledged at least one as anomalous (PR053): continuous, selection-bias-free instrumentation over active geological infrastructure.

Kumburgaz (Yalman)· 2007–09

Night guard Yalçın Yalman filmed disc-shaped objects over the Sea of Marmara for two years, including in daylight — over 35 hours of video; Turkey's state scientific council (TÜBİTAK) analyzed the tapes and concluded there was no manipulation.

2007–09A62%39🇺🇸United States.

Skinwalker Ranch · AAWSAP and field experiments· 2008–10

U.S. DoD federal program (~USD 22M, 2008–2010), requested by Senator Harry Reid, contracted to the DIA and executed by Robert Bigelow's BAASS from the Skinwalker Ranch in Utah. It produced 38 Defense Intelligence Reference Documents (DIRDs) on exotic physics and biological effects. The first formally documented U.S. federal program after Project Blue Book — with contracts, paid receipts and testimony under declaration — and the direct precursor to AATIP (Elizondo) and the disclosure wave that culminates in AARO and the 2023–2025 congressional hearings. The case also folds in the three instrumented-measurement campaigns at the property —NIDS (1996–2004), BAASS and Brandon Fugal's televised team— and their results, of far lower evidential quality than the program's paperwork.

2008–10A60%59🇳🇿New Zealand.

Kaikōura Lights· 1978

Between 21 and 31 December 1978, commercial pilots, Wellington controllers and an Australian TV crew recorded lights over New Zealand's South Island; the colour 16 mm footage was correlated simultaneously with civilian radar returns. The CIA called the case, in writing, "unique among civilian UFO reports".

1978S80%60🇺🇸United States.

Hudson Valley Boomerang· 1983–86

Between 1983 and 1986, some 5,000 people across the Hudson Valley (New York and Connecticut) reported a large, silent triangular object with structured lights —the "Westchester Boomerang"— among them IBM engineers, state police and FAA controllers. It is the most-witnessed sustained UAP event in the U.S., cited without dispute by NICAP, CUFOS and MUFON.

1983–86A65%73🇮🇳India.

Ladakh · ITBP/Army UAP· 2012

Between August and October 2012, the Indo-Tibetan Border Police unit at Pangong Tso lake (Ladakh) logged over a hundred sightings of luminous objects crossing from the Chinese side. The Army deployed radar and three technical agencies —ISRO, DRDO and NTRO— ruled out drones, satellites and UAVs before filing the case unresolved.

2012S48%136🇸🇪Sweden.

POSS-I Transients ↔ nuclear tests + UAP (Bruehl & Villarroel)· 2025

Peer-reviewed paper in Nature Scientific Reports (Oct 2025) by Bruehl and Villarroel. Dataset of 2,718 days (Nov 19, 1949 to Apr 28, 1957, fully pre-Sputnik) with transients identified on Palomar Observatory Sky Survey plates. Two statistically significant associations: transients are 45% more likely within ±1 day of a nuclear test (p=0.008) and for each additional same-day UAP report there are 8.5% more transients identified (p=0.015). It is the strongest peer-reviewed correlation in the corpus between nuclear activity, UAP records of the era and independent astronomical observations.

2025S60%139🇦🇺Australia.

Tully Saucer Nests · RAAF investigation· 1966

On January 19, 1966, rural worker George Pedley (28) reported observing a metallic disc roughly 7.5m wide rising from Horseshoe Lagoon near Tully, Queensland. At the site he found a circular depression of about 9m of flattened reeds he called a 'nest'. Five additional nests were later found. The Royal Australian Air Force (RAAF) conducted an official investigation — the first sample-analysis investigation in Australia. The RAAF conclusion was that the reeds died 'from natural submersion'; the University of Queensland attributed the patterns to 'severe turbulence' compatible with regional line squalls. The case is the first 'crop-circle' paradigm with documented official military investigation, a chronological parallel of Westall (same year, different modus).

1966A35%174🇨🇱Chile.

Collahuasi (CEFAA)· 2013

In April 2013, technicians at the Doña Inés de Collahuasi mine —over 4,300 m in the Atacama altiplano— photographed a disc-shaped object that lingered nearby for about an hour before moving off. CEFAA, the official committee of Chile's DGAC under General (ret.) Ricardo Bermúdez, investigated the images with optical and thermal analysis and one analyst rated them an object or phenomenon of great interest, unidentified. Bermúdez himself urged caution: it was a single analyst's conclusion and the witnesses did not cooperate.

2013A52%176🇺🇸United States.

Kelly-Hopkinsville· 1955

On the night of 21 August 1955, the Sutton and Taylor families —eleven people— said they were besieged for hours at a farmhouse near Hopkinsville, Kentucky, by several small creatures with large heads and bright eyes that approached the house. They fired at them with no apparent effect and fled to the police station in terror. Police and state troopers investigated: they found no traces or landing marks, but did find bullet holes and shell casings. It is the most cited multi-witness encounter of its era and the origin of the phrase 'little green men'.

1955B20%206🇬🇧United Kingdom.

Bonnybridge · Falkirk Triangle· 1992–97

Since 1992, the Scottish town of Bonnybridge, in the centre of the so-called 'Falkirk Triangle', concentrated hundreds of reports of lights and objects. Local councillor Billy Buchanan took the matter to government: he wrote to the Prime Minister and the Ministry of Defence and pushed a council meeting in 1993. It is less an incident than a sustained 'hotspot' phenomenon — high volume, low individual evidence quality — and a case study in how a community and its local government process a wave.

1992–97B14%207🇲🇬Madagascar.

Tananarive · Madagascar· 1954

On 16 August 1954, over the capital of Madagascar — then Tananarive, a French colony — tens of thousands of people watched a large green metallic-looking sphere pass at low altitude, followed by an identical second object. Witnesses described an electrical blackout as it passed and the stampede of zebu herds. The base commander, General Fleurquin, ordered an investigation led by the observatory director, who questioned more than 5,000 witnesses. It is one of the best-attested mass sightings of the period.

1954B30%266🇷🇺Russia.

Molebka Zone · M-Triangle· 1983

The 'Molebka anomalous zone' or 'Perm Triangle', in the Russian Urals, became famous after 1983, when geologist Emil Bachurin said he saw a luminous sphere and found a ~62 m circular print in the snow. In the 1980s-90s it drew expeditions reporting lights, equipment failures and 'mirages'. It is the most famous anomalous zone of the former USSR, on a site already sacred to the Mansi.

1983B22%295🇺🇸United States.

Northeastern US orbs · FBI file corroborated by agents (PURSUE)· 2021–26

Seven FBI documents declassified in PURSUE Release 03 (June 12, 2026) make up a single file: repeated 'orb' observations in the Northeastern US over years (~2021-2024), near a residence whose location is redacted. Five FD-1057s (2024) and two FD-302s (2026) capture the witness account —'plasma orbs', a 'red sphere' below the tree line, objects moving laterally, caught on a home camera— and, unusually, corroboration by FBI special agents who observed the activity on-site in 2024 with full line of sight. Heavily redacted material, no published radar or telemetry; the morphology matches the Northeastern drone/UAS wave of that period. Value: qualified testimony and FBI chain of custody, not a proven anomalous origin.

2021–26B38%302🇺🇸United States.

Marfa Lights · Texas· 1883

Since the late 19th century, travelers and residents of West Texas have described diffuse lights that appear at night over Mitchell Flat, east of the town of Marfa, toward the Chinati Mountains: orbs that seem to float, split, merge, and move at a distance. The phenomenon became an icon of Texan folklore, with an official viewing platform on US Route 90. Systematic investigations —notably a study by physics students at the University of Texas at Dallas (2004)— correlated most of the lights with vehicle headlights on US Route 67 between Marfa and Presidio, refracted and distorted by thermal inversions of the desert air. It is, in essence, a case of misidentification of real light sources amplified by atmospheric optics, with a minor tail of reports without closed explanation.

1883B20%303🇦🇺Australia.

Min Min Lights · Australian outback· 1838

On the remote plains of the Channel Country, in western Queensland, travelers of the Australian outback have recounted since the 19th century a light —the 'Min Min'— that appears at night, seems to follow whoever observes it, keeps its distance, and fades when approached. The name comes from a now-vanished hotel on the route between Boulia and Winton; the tradition is even older, present in Aboriginal accounts. In 2003, neuroscientist Jack Pettigrew (University of Queensland) demonstrated experimentally that the Min Min light is a Fata Morgana-type mirage: light from real and very distant sources —campfires, headlights, even stars below the horizon— ducted over tens of kilometers by a thermal inversion of cold air over the ground. It is a real optical phenomenon, not an object, and he reproduced the effect under controlled conditions.

1838B15%304🇺🇸United States.

Brown Mountain Lights · North Carolina· 1913

In the foothills of the Appalachian Mountains of North Carolina, over the low ridge of Brown Mountain, lights that appear at night have been reported for more than a century: orbs that rise, flicker, and go out. The phenomenon achieved national fame in the early 20th century and prompted two investigations by the United States Geological Survey (USGS). The 1922 report, by George Rogers Mansfield, attributed most of the lights to locomotive and automobile headlights, brush fires, and fixed lights refracted by the valley atmosphere. Later research (Appalachian State University, 2016) reproduced some episodes as plasma-type discharges under specific conditions, but the dominant explanation remains the misidentification of conventional light sources under atmospheric optics.

1913B22%325🇲🇾Malasia.

Malaysia's tiny-humanoid wave· 1970–79

A wave of tiny-humanoid sightings —only a few centimetres tall— reported mostly by schoolchildren across peninsular Malaysia between 1970 and 1979. Over three days in August 1970 a press count logged no fewer than 39 tiny UFOs and 31 reports of three-inch occupants. Catalogued by Ahmad Jamaludin (Flying Saucer Review; CUFOS, 1981).

1970–79B20%326🇻🇳Vietnam.

Vietnam DMZ · the June 1968 lights and friendly-fire incident· 1968

Through the spring of 1968 U.S. Marines reported hovering night-time lights over the Vietnamese Demilitarized Zone and the sea off Tiger Island; on the night of 15-16 June some 30 slow lights triggered an incursion alarm. The Allied response degenerated into friendly fire: F-4 Phantom fighters sank the swift boat PCF-19 and struck the cruisers USS Boston and HMAS Hobart, with fatalities. The naval inquiry blamed radar confusion and misidentification.

1968B22%381🇺🇸United States.

Chris Bledsoe (Cape Fear River)· 2007

On January 8, 2007, contractor Chris Bledsoe and four witnesses —his 17-year-old son and three subcontractors— reported three orange objects appearing to land near the Cape Fear River in North Carolina, followed that same night by an alleged entity encounter at his home. MUFON investigated with regressive hypnosis, a psychiatric evaluation, and a polygraph that flagged signs of deception on part of Bledsoe's answers; he has since built a public career of books and documentaries that includes the claim —never corroborated by any document or named NASA official— of having been investigated by that agency for eight years.
