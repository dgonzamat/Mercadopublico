Monitoring catastrophic risk sources

UAP concentration over nuclear sites + active volcanoes + submarines.

Roswell· 1947

The 509th Bombardment Group's base issued history's only official military press release claiming recovery of a 'flying disc', retracted within hours as a weather balloon; the official explanation later changed twice more (Mogul 1994, crash-test dummies 1997).

Sandia 'green orbs'· 1948–50

Between 1948 and 1950, 209 sightings of green spheres were logged over the Albuquerque–Los Alamos nuclear corridor, investigated under military sponsorship and acknowledged by AARO in the PURSUE R02 release: the first documented wave over US nuclear infrastructure.

Trindade Island· 1958

About a hundred officers and scientists aboard the ship Almirante Saldanha watched a Saturn-shaped object off Trindade Island; Almiro Baraúna's photos were developed on board under supervision and Brazil's Navy released them with presidential authorization.

Malmstrom ICBM shutdown· 1967

All ten Minuteman ICBMs at Malmstrom AFB's Oscar-Flight Launch Control Center simultaneously went off-alert while security reported a luminous object over the bunker; the USAF logged it as 'cause undetermined' and Captain Salas testified publicly.

Popocatépetl· 1992

Since 1992, the thermal and visible cameras with which CENAPRED monitors the Popocatépetl volcano have recorded between 50 and 200 events of objects emerging from or overflying the crater, automatically archived by a system not designed to detect UAP. AARO acknowledged at least one as anomalous (PR053): continuous, selection-bias-free instrumentation over active geological infrastructure.

Minot AFB B-52· 1968

Before dawn on 24 October 1968, an object interacted simultaneously with an airborne B-52 —whose UHF comms failed and which was captured on radarscope film— and with security personnel at Minuteman missile silos at Minot AFB. It joins two corpus patterns: airborne electromagnetic interference and nuclear-infrastructure surveillance.

Byelokoroviche · UFO over Soviet missile base· 1982

On October 4, 1982, over a Strategic Rocket Forces base near Byelokoroviche (Ukrainian SSR), military personnel reported a disc-shaped object stationary for about an hour, with radio interference. During the event, per the Soviet investigation, the missiles' launch-control panels began a pre-launch sequence on their own for about 15 seconds, with no command, then reset. It is the Soviet parallel to Malmstrom 1967.

1982A50%224🇺🇸United States.

Loring AFB overflights · SAC nuclear bases· 1975

In October–November 1975, unidentified objects repeatedly penetrated the airspace of several Strategic Air Command (SAC) nuclear bases. At Loring AFB (Maine), a red object the size of a car overflew the nuclear weapons storage area on two consecutive nights; F-106 fighters scrambled without catching it. The pattern repeated at Wurtsmith, Malmstrom and Minot. The chain of OSI messages to the National Military Command Center speaks of an 'unknown entity'; NORAD confirmed radar returns. The USAF closed the case as 'unidentified helicopters'. Tier A: official documentation, radar, multiple bases.

Kirtland AFB · controllers and Blue Book· 1957

On November 4, 1957, amid the national wave following Levelland, two Civil Aeronautics Administration controllers in the Kirtland Air Force Base tower (Albuquerque, New Mexico) reported a luminous egg-shaped object that descended to about sixty meters over the field's restricted area, stopped, and then climbed, with maneuvers improper for an aircraft. Project Blue Book opened a file; the official conclusion was 'aircraft', but the controllers rejected it and the case was later discussed in the Condon Report. Proximity to the Manzano nuclear storage loads the case with institutional ambiguity.

1957B34%278🇷🇺Unión Soviética (RSS de Kazajistán).

Sary Shagan · green object over Soviet weapons range (CIA-UAP-D001)· 1973

In late summer 1973, at Site 7 of the Sary Shagan weapons testing range —the Soviet anti-ballistic-missile and directed-energy proving ground near Lake Balkhash—, a worker at the facility observed to the west, at about 70° elevation, a sharp green circular object. Within 10-15 seconds the circle widened and several concentric green circles formed around the mass; the color faded within minutes, with no sound or explosion, under a clear sky. The account reached the West as a CIA HUMINT report (CONFIDENTIAL) and resurfaced declassified in the second release of the PURSUE system (May 22, 2026). The signature —an expanding concentric green cloud— is consistent with a Soviet high-altitude test (chemical release or rocket plume) as much as with an optical-atmospheric phenomenon.

DOE · UAP records from the nuclear complex (Pantex, Los Alamos — PURSUE R02)· 1976–24

Three Department of Energy documents declassified in PURSUE Release 02 (May 22, 2026) share a thread: sustained interest in UAP within the U.S. nuclear-weapons complex. DOE-UAP-D001 is a 'Pantex Unidentified Object Incident Report' (marked UCNI) about the country's sole nuclear-weapons assembly/disassembly plant, with an image captured from a ground surveillance radar tower and enhanced by Sandia National Labs. DOE-UAP-D002 is 1970s correspondence of Los Alamos physicist James L. Tuck (Manhattan Project; later controlled fusion) about anomalous phenomena, ball lightning and the Condon Report. DOE-UAP-D003 is a 1986 invitation from the Pajarito Astronomers of Los Alamos to a talk by physicist Dr. John Warren (LANL) titled 'Why Should a Scientist be Concerned about UFO's?'. Their value is institutional context —they document decades of attention to the phenomenon inside the nuclear apparatus, not that the objects were anomalous.

Colorado Springs 2022 · object over Cheyenne Mountain (FBI/IC, PURSUE)· 2022

On February 15, 2022, at 09:35 (Mountain Time), five U.S. Army service members at Fort Carson observed an airborne object over Cheyenne Mountain (Colorado Springs), home to NORAD's hardened command complex. PURSUE declassified the file in its third tranche (Release 03, June 12, 2026): an FBI FD-302 (interview), an FD-1057 (internal UAP report), a digital rendering of the object, and an intelligence-community analysis (ICA-UAP-D001). The assessment concluded the object was 'possibly backscattering of sunlight', but with 'low confidence' — a combination that leaves the case formally unresolved. Its value is institutional provenance and analytical honesty, not a proven anomaly.

Big Sur · the UFO and the test warhead (Vandenberg, 1964)· 1964

In September 1964, according to the testimony of then Air Force lieutenant Robert Jacobs, an optical tracking station at Big Sur (California) filmed the test flight of an Atlas missile launched from Vandenberg base with a dummy warhead. On reviewing the film days later, Jacobs says a disc-shaped object was seen approaching the warhead in suborbital flight and firing four beams of light at it from different angles, after which the warhead became destabilized. His superior, Major Florenze Mansmann, reportedly ordered him to keep silent and confiscated the material. Jacobs went public in 1982. It is an emblematic case of the UAP-nuclear-weapons narrative, but it rests on a single witness, with no surviving film, and with disputed technical details.

1964B30%315🇨🇦Canada.

Falconbridge · NORAD radar and the Northern Tier wave· 1975

On November 11, 1975, the Canadian Forces radar station at Falconbridge, Ontario, tracked objects 40-50 km to the south that climbed from 8,000 to 14,000 and then to 22,000 meters, stopped, and climbed again; the commanding officer, Major Robert Oliver, and five other military personnel saw them through binoculars, and seven OPP officers observed them. NORAD scrambled F-106 fighters that found nothing, and issued a statement framing the event within a wave of reports since October 28 over the 'Northern Tier' nuclear bases: Loring, Wurtsmith, Malmstrom, Minot and Falconbridge.

1975S75%322🇺🇸United States.

Pantex · unidentified object over the nuclear plant (2015)· 2015

On 1 September 2015, the Ground Surveillance Radar (GSR) at the Pantex Plant —the primary U.S. nuclear-weapons assembly facility, in Amarillo, Texas— detected an unidentified object west of the facilities, flying north at 10–15 mph. The Protective Force activated the drone (UAS) response plan, secured all gates and pursued the object by vehicle. Witnesses with binoculars found no propulsion and heard no sound; the object —'diamond'-shaped, ~4 ft, which sped up and changed course when followed— was also tracked by the CROWS camera on a Bearcat and filmed from the GSR tower. The video was sent to Sandia National Labs and the evidence handed to the FBI. UCNI report declassified in 2026 (AARO, DOEUAPD005).
