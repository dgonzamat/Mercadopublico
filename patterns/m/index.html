Before dawn on September 24, 1959, police officer Robert Dickerson and FAA specialist Laverne Wertz watched a very bright light over Redmond, Oregon, for more than an hour; both described it to the Air Force as low in the northeast, hovering around one point though it seemed to move rapidly toward and away from them. At the same time the Keno radar held a return in the area, and six F-102s in four waves, an F-89, a helicopter and a light plane carrying a Geiger counter were sent out, finding nothing. The operators attributed that return, which never moved, to an antenna on a mountain. In June 1960 the Air Force concluded that the light was the refraction of Venus and Regulus, both very low in the east.

Seoul · air defense fires over the Blue House· 1976

At dusk on October 14, 1976, thousands of Seoul residents saw more than a dozen luminous objects advancing in a semicircular formation over the city; when the group entered the restricted airspace over the Blue House (Cheong Wa Dae), the presidential residence, the air-defense units surrounding downtown opened fire. According to the Ministry of Defense, there were two bursts of warning anti-aircraft fire around 18:19; one person died and 31 were injured by falling ordnance. The official explanation — a U.S. freighter that strayed into the zone — was a Northwest cargo plane flying from Gimpo to Osaka; SBS's 2020 special judged it hard to identify that plane with the lights the witnesses described.

Twelve volumes, right before Christmas: New Zealand's 2010 UFO declassification· 1952–10

On December 22, 2010 —two days before Christmas Eve— the New Zealand Defence Force (NZDF) released twelve volumes, roughly 2,500 pages, of Royal New Zealand Air Force (RNZAF) UFO files dating back to 1952: nearly sixty years of reports from civilian witnesses, military personnel and pilots, including the material documenting the famous 1978 'Kaikōura Lights' (see [[kaikoura-1978]]). The release came after two years of lobbying by researcher Suzanne Hansen, director of UFOCUS NZ, via an Official Information Act request. It was, at once, the largest UFO declassification by an Oceania country and a textbook case of the limits of a 'complete' release: it only covered the Air Force —the Navy and Army kept their own records classified—, with privacy embargoes running until 2050 and entire pages illegible due to redaction.

Aeronautica Militare: Italy's 2014 UFO declassification· 1978–14

Following a wave of sightings in 1978, Prime Minister Giulio Andreotti designated the Aeronautica Militare (Italian Air Force) as the institutional body responsible for receiving and investigating UFO reports in Italy. Decades of files accumulated under 'Secret'/'Reserved' stamps stayed closed until journalists Lao Petrilli and Vincenzo Sinapi gained access to them for the book 'UFO: I dossier italiani' (March 2014), revealing previously unpublished cases —including a radar contact confirmed by a C-130 at Grazzanise (2005)—. Today the Air Force keeps a public year-by-year archive dating to 1972 on its official site, though the institution itself has never framed that routine transparency as a formal act of declassification.

Nuccetelli · the Vandenberg «Red Square»· 2003–05

Between 2003 and 2005, Air Force military police officer Jeffrey Nuccetelli documented five UAP incidents at Vandenberg Air Force Base, California, home of the National Missile Defense Project, during what, per his testimony, the National Reconnaissance Office called the 'most important' launches 'in twenty-five years'. The most cited, on October 14, 2003, is known as the 'Vandenberg Red Square': Boeing contractors reported a massive, glowing red square silently hovering over two missile defense sites. That same night, Nuccetelli responded to a second incident involving a rectangular craft seen by five witnesses, and months later he had his own off-duty encounter with a blue-white sphere that appeared and disappeared in the sky. He testified under oath on September 9, 2025, before the House Task Force on the Declassification of Federal Secrets.
