On May 5, 1981, around 18:00 Moscow time, cosmonaut Vladimir Kovalyonok —Major General, twice Hero of the Soviet Union— observed from a porthole of the Salyut-6 station an object flying alongside the spacecraft over southern Africa. It is the corpus case with the highest possible witness cohort (two cosmonauts on duty) within the Soviet space program.

1975A50%170🇺🇸United States.

Kenneth Arnold · the sighting that coined 'flying saucer' (USA, 1947)· 1947

On June 24, 1947, private pilot Kenneth Arnold was flying near Mount Rainier (Washington) when he reported nine bright objects in formation moving at great speed between Rainier and Mount Adams. He timed their passage against the peaks and estimated a speed of about 1,700 km/h, far above any known aircraft of the era. His description of the motion —'like a saucer skipped across water'— led the press to coin the term 'flying saucer.' It is the foundational sighting of the modern era of the phenomenon: the one that triggered the 1947 wave and the vocabulary we still use. Later prosaic explanations (mirages, meteors, birds) never closed it conclusively.

Bonilla observation · Zacatecas Observatory· 1883

On August 12 and 13, 1883, astronomer José Arbol y Bonilla, director of the Zacatecas Observatory, photographed with wet-plate more than 440 dark objects crossing the Sun's disk in formation. It is the first instrumented astronomical observation of unidentified objects published in a peer-reviewed scientific journal ('L'Astronomie', 1885–86). In 2011, a UNAM team reinterpreted the data as fragments of a comet that would have passed very close to Earth — a prosaic but extraordinary hypothesis still debated. The case is a test of method: impeccable period documentation, an object still not closed.

On August 15, 1950, Nick Mariana, manager of a Great Falls (Montana) baseball team, filmed in 16 mm two silvery objects moving over the stadium — one of the earliest films of an unidentified aerial phenomenon. Maracaibo phenomenon · consular report· 1886

On the stormy night of October 24, 1886, a family of nine in a hut near Maracaibo reportedly woke to a loud hum and a dazzling light that filled the house; afterward they suffered vomiting, swellings and sores, and the trees around the house withered on the ninth day — a picture reminiscent of radiation sickness. The U.S. consul Warner Cowgill reported it in 'Scientific American' (Dec 18, 1886). It is one of the earliest descriptions of a luminous phenomenon with biological effects in the scientific press, but it rests on a single second-hand report. Tier B.

On March 5, 2004, the crew of a Mexican Air Force Merlin C26A (501st squadron), on an anti-drug patrol over the Bay of Campeche, recorded with its StarSAFIRE II infrared sensor as many as eleven objects that seemed to surround the aircraft; only some appeared on radar. On September 1, 1974, farmer Edwin Fuhr was harvesting rapeseed near Langenburg (Saskatchewan, Canada) when he reported five metallic, inverted-bowl-shaped objects spinning low over the field. The VM6 plate from the Apollo 17 photographic archive (December 1972) shows, when the lower-right quadrant of the lunar sky is magnified, three luminous dots in a regular triangular formation. The skeptical explanations (ice, paint flaking, optical artifact) remain the most likely.

1972B38%278🇷🇺Unión Soviética (RSS de Kazajistán).

Sary Shagan · green object over Soviet weapons range (CIA-UAP-D001)· 1973

In late summer 1973, at Site 7 of the Sary Shagan weapons testing range —the Soviet anti-ballistic-missile and directed-energy proving ground near Lake Balkhash—, a worker at the facility observed to the west, at about 70° elevation, a sharp green circular object. Within 10-15 seconds the circle widened and several concentric green circles formed around the mass; the color faded within minutes, with no sound or explosion, under a clear sky. The account reached the West as a CIA HUMINT report (CONFIDENTIAL) and resurfaced declassified in the second release of the PURSUE system (May 22, 2026). The signature —an expanding concentric green cloud— is consistent with a Soviet high-altitude test (chemical release or rocket plume) as much as with an optical-atmospheric phenomenon.

Skylab · light flashes and reddish object (NASA, PURSUE)· 1973–74

Document NASA-UAP-D7, declassified in the first PURSUE release, gathers excerpts from the technical debriefings of the three crews that occupied Skylab —the first U.S. space station— between 1973 and 1974. It records three kinds of observation: light flashes the astronauts saw with their eyes closed (the well-known cosmic-ray phosphene phenomenon), a bright reddish object the Skylab 3 crew tracked for 5-10 minutes and themselves described as 'obviously a satellite in a very similar orbit to our own', and flashing lights outside with relative motion that the Skylab 4 crew attributed to pieces of the station itself or other satellites. It is a documentary-transparency case: all three observations have a prosaic explanation acknowledged by the witnesses themselves.

Document 341_110677 (PURSUE Release 01) contains Air Intelligence Information Report IR 193-55, signed on October 14, 1955 by U.S. air attaché Lt. Col. Thomas S. Ryan. 1955A40%283🇺🇸United States.

File 62-HQ-83894 · the FBI's flying-disc archive (PURSUE)· 1947–73

File 62-HQ-83894 is the FBI's central 'flying discs' archive, opened in 1947 and fed through the 1970s with investigative reports, witness testimonies, press clippings and correspondence between the FBI and the Air Forces. PURSUE declassified its serials in the first release; this file embeds in its viewer a sample of Volume 1 (Sections 1-10, subfile SUB A and Serials 130, 153, 164, 220, 403, 438 and 449 (the heaviest sections were split into parts for the viewer)), investigative correspondence from the 1947 wave. The archive contains famous pieces —such as the 1950 Hottel memo on 'three saucers recovered', which the corpus treats separately— but its bulk is fieldwork that resolved most reports into balloons, hoaxes and misidentifications. Its value is institutional: it documents how the U.S. security apparatus processed the flying-disc panic, not that the discs were anomalous.

On the morning of 1 December 1987, a former police officer the literature calls 'Philip Spencer' (a pseudonym) said he crossed Ilkley Moor, in West Yorkshire, and photographed a ~1.2 m green humanoid with a large head and long arms that gestured at him and fled. Investigated by Peter Hough and Jenny Randles, it remains unresolved: skeptics read it as a hoax (cut-out/person); its value is as an iconic testimonial case, not as evidence.

NASA · Gemini and Mercury-era crew debriefings (1962-1966, PURSUE)· 1962–66

Eight NASA documents declassified in PURSUE Release 03 (June 12, 2026): the technical and scientific crew debriefings of the Gemini program (missions 4, 5, 7 and 9, 1965-66) plus the Mercury-era Astronaut Scientific Debriefings (1962-63). They are the primary transcripts behind the most-cited anomalous observations of early spaceflight: the cylindrical object James McDivitt photographed on Gemini 4 and the 'bogey' and 'fireflies' Borman and Lovell reported on Gemini 7. The corpus already analyzes those sightings separately (gemini4-mcdivitt-1965, gemini7-borman-1965); here the full primary sources are incorporated. Their value is provenance: most is routine technical debrief, and the anomalous content —mostly explained as ice/debris/booster stage— is a minor fraction, with the McDivitt object as the notable residue.

Barnea managed to take four photographs as the object advanced, brightened, and then accelerated out of sight. Technicians at the University of Cluj studied the plates and built scale models; ufologist Ion Hobana investigated the witnesses. It is one of the most analyzed photographic cases of the Eastern Bloc, taken three days before the invasion of Czechoslovakia.

1968A64%324🇪🇸Spain.

Talavera la Real · the 'Green Man' (1976)· 1976

In the early hours of 12 November 1976, two conscript sentries in the fuel area of Talavera la Real Air Base (Badajoz, Spain) reported interference, a high-pitched whistle, a partial blackout and an agitated military dog. One of them, José María Trejo, described a humanoid figure some three meters tall formed by points of green light, with long arms and no hands or feet, before losing consciousness; his companions opened fire on the apparition, which vanished. The Spanish Air Force opened a file that the Ministry of Defense declassified in the 1990s. The documentation confirms a real alarm occurred, but does not conclusively support the presence of an object or a being; the strongest reading points to perceptual disturbance and contagious hysteria during a night watch.

1976A20%325🇲🇾Malasia.

Malaysia's tiny-humanoid wave· 1970–79

A wave of tiny-humanoid sightings —only a few centimetres tall— reported mostly by schoolchildren across peninsular Malaysia between 1970 and 1979. Over three days in August 1970 a press count logged no fewer than 39 tiny UFOs and 31 reports of three-inch occupants. Catalogued by Ahmad Jamaludin (Flying Saucer Review; CUFOS, 1981).

1970–79B20%327🇹🇿Tanzania.

Mount Kilimanjaro · metallic object over Tanganyika seen by an East African Airways crew (1951)· 1951

On 19 February 1951, the crew and seven passengers of an East African Airways flight between Nairobi and Mombasa watched a metallic, bullet-shaped object over 60 metres long hover for 17 minutes above Mount Kilimanjaro before rising and vanishing at about 12,000 m. Nine witnesses signed a sworn affidavit; attempts to photograph it yielded nothing conclusive. A classic aviation case of the 1950-51 wave.

1951A34%385🇺🇸United States.

Herbert Schirmer · the Ashland patrolman· 1967

In the early hours of December 3, 1967, 22-year-old police officer Herbert Schirmer reported an oval object hovering over Highway 6 near Ashland, Nebraska, and immediately noticed a red mark under his ear and unaccounted-for minutes on his shift. The case reached the University of Colorado's Condon Committee, which in 1968 had Schirmer hypnotized by psychologist Leo Sprinkle (University of Wyoming) and polygraphed — he passed. Under hypnosis he described pale grey-skinned occupants with slanted eyes —the grey archetype, not reptilian despite a winged-serpent emblem on their uniforms— and a warning of two future visits. The Condon Committee itself labeled the case 'psychological'.

1967B28%387🇨🇱Chile.

Pampa Soledad · the artists chased by lights· 1974

In the early hours of 16 August 1974, five Chilean popular artists —among them folk singer Tito Fernández, «El Temucano»— were driving back from a performance at the Oficina Victoria toward Antofagasta when, on the desert stretch of Pampa Soledad, they were followed by an orange light that split into two and then merged into a single object, beneath which they said they saw a tall humanoid figure. They fled at over 130 km/h; a truck driver who crossed their path independently corroborated having been chased by the same lights. The phenomenon stopped once they reached a Carabineros post in Quillagua, where they gave statements. Tito Fernández later wrote «El mensaje de Sirio», describing an alleged subsequent telepathic communication with the entity.

1974B20%388🇺🇸United States.

Nuccetelli · the Vandenberg «Red Square»· 2003–05

Between 2003 and 2005, Air Force military police officer Jeffrey Nuccetelli documented five UAP incidents at Vandenberg Air Force Base, California, home of the National Missile Defense Project, during what the National Reconnaissance Office called the 'most important' launches 'in twenty-five years'. The most cited, on October 14, 2003, is known as the 'Vandenberg Red Square': Boeing contractors reported a massive, glowing red square silently hovering over two missile defense sites. That same night, Nuccetelli responded to a second incident involving a rectangular craft seen by five witnesses, and months later he had his own off-duty encounter with a blue-white sphere that appeared and disappeared in the sky. He testified under oath on September 9, 2025, before the House Task Force on the Declassification of Federal Secrets.
