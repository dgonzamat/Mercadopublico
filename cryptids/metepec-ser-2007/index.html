🇲🇽2007–2012·Metepec municipality, State of Mexico, Mexico

Alleged extraterrestrial beings·Metepec creature

Mexico.Metepec being · a small creature caught in a mouse trap

According to a 2011 story in El Universal Estado de México, on May 26, 2007, workers at a poultry ranch in Metepec, near Toluca, found a small being with humanoid features in a mouse trap and drowned it. Journalist Jaime Maussan said DNA tests by scientists he did not name showed it was an unknown being; the worker who found the body said it was a titi monkey. In 2012, Channel 4's documentary "Alien Investigations" examined the body, and according to British critic Tom Sutcliffe's review it turned out to be a marmoset, in a confessed hoax.

What happened

The oldest account of the find that can still be read is a story by journalist Emmanuel Suberza in El Universal Estado de México, the State of Mexico edition of the Mexico City daily, published in January 2011 and preserved as a copy on a blog. According to that story, on May 26, 2007, a group of workers at the Las Margaritas ranch, a poultry farm in the municipality of Metepec, next to Toluca, found a small being with humanoid features caught in a mouse trap, and the employees drowned it. While cleaning the trap, worker Ángel Núñez discovered the body and told the owner, whom the story calls Mario López.

Months later, the story goes on, journalist Jaime Maussan, Mexico's best-known UFO promoter and host of the TV program Tercer Milenio, began investigating where the creature came from; it became known as "the Metepec being." In the interview with the paper, Maussan said DNA tests by scientists in Spain, Canada and Mexico had found the creature had "a 90 percent similarity with primates and 99 percent with human beings." The story does not name those scientists or their labs. Maussan concluded that "we are dealing with a being of unknown origin" and said his team was seeking permits to take the body to the United States for a final DNA test.

The same story carries the opposite view, and it comes from the very worker who found the body: Ángel Núñez maintained that the Metepec being was nothing more than a titi monkey, a Spanish name for several small South American monkeys. Maussan called those statements "absurd," said more than 40 scientists from several countries had validated his research and invited anyone with proof that the creature was not an unknown species to bring it to his team.

A 2024 article by Viceversa Noticias, an online outlet in Toluca, gives other names and another date. It calls the owner Mario Moreno López, a businessman who ran the Excelencia radio-taxi company in Toluca, and says he was the one who contacted Maussan on May 11, 2007, to offer him the creature, fifteen days before the date of the find given by El Universal. According to the article, some men found it alive in a steel trap, photographed it and then held it underwater for hours until it died. The two dates cannot both be right, and no 2007 report of the find is known that would settle it.

On September 14, 2007, the Toluca news agency MVT reported that the owner of the Excelencia radio taxis, Mario Moreno López, had been shot dead by his business partner, along with an employee of another ranch, a racehorse ranch they kept near Ixtlahuaca, and that the attacker then set fire to the pickup truck they were traveling in. The MVT story does not mention the creature. The Viceversa article dates the crime to August 3, 2007, and adds that police in Jalisco arrested the accused in September 2009, but it spells the names of the dead employee and of the survivor differently. Because the MVT story says nothing of the creature and El Universal gives only "Mario López," the link between the ranch owner and the murdered businessman rests on later accounts such as that article.

The same article tells where the body came from, according to the man who says he made it. A young taxidermist, Urso Moreno Ruiz, who went by the name Ángel Palacios Núñez, wrote on taxidermy forums that he had built the creature from the tanned corpse of a titi monkey, joined to fur and fluids from other animals. That, he wrote, is why "every DNA sample will come out different." According to the article, he added: "It was a joke that got out of hand." The taxidermist also said that after Moreno's death Maussan paid the widow some 300,000 pesos for the body; the article places the handover in late 2008 and says Maussan showed photos of it at a UFO convention in Laughlin, Nevada, in February 2009. The taxidermist's false name resembles that of the worker Ángel Núñez, who according to El Universal said it was a titi, but neither source says whether they are the same person.

In 2012 the case reached British television. According to Channel 4's press release of November 15, 2012, its documentary "Alien Investigations," made by Wild Dream Films and directed by Stuart Clarke, gained access to the body and examined it with scans and DNA tests. Paleontologist Darren Naish, who appeared in the program, wrote that it aired on Sunday, December 2, at 8 p.m. He added that he believed it had already aired in North America on the Science Channel as "Alien Mummies." On December 2, the day of the broadcast, critic Tom Sutcliffe wrote in The Independent that "the Alien Mummy of Metepec," allegedly caught on a Mexican ranch, "turned out to be a marmoset," a small South American monkey that Spanish calls tití, and faulted the program for illustrating that ending with an animated alien instead of a reconstruction of "the confessed hoax involved."

Why it matters

The Metepec being shows how an alien-creature case is built from a small animal and a ranch story. The hoax explanation does not come from a single voice: the worker who found the body gave it, according to the 2011 story, the taxidermist who said he made it set it down in writing, and in 2012 it was reported by the critic who reviewed the British documentary that tested the body. All three point to a titi monkey or marmoset; Mexico News Daily, an English-language paper in Mexico, described it in 2026 as an altered squirrel monkey, another small South American monkey, and the exact species depends on a test whose data have not been published.

The record is weak on both sides. No report from 2007 about the find is known: the oldest account is the 2011 interview, the dates of the event differ by fifteen days and the owner's name changes from one source to another. On the other side, the 2011 story does not name who ran the DNA tests Maussan cited, and there is no known signed report of the Channel 4 test: it is known from the broadcast and from reviews. What is known about the body rests on the confession and on what the program showed.

The case foreshadows the Nazca mummies, with the same central figure. In 2023 Maussan presented two small bodies to Mexico's Congress and again cited DNA tests that, according to anthropologist Andrew Nelson, he had not submitted for peer review ↗ Mexican Congress, and in 2024 Peru's forensic service concluded that two figures similar to Maussan's, seized at Lima airport were dolls made of animal bones and glue ↗ Nazca mummies. In Lima, unlike Metepec, the state kept custody of the figures and its forensic service presented the results in public, with the experts present.
