🇲🇽2021·Bella Unión, Arteaga municipality, Coahuila, Mexico

Winged humanoids·Birdman

Mexico.Bella Unión · a resident reports a birdman to 911

In mid-July 2021, a man from Bella Unión, a small community in the municipality of Arteaga, Coahuila, called 911 to report that he had seen a "bird man." Arteaga's Civil Protection went to the scene and, according to the newspaper Vanguardia, so did the state prosecutor's office and public security, but they found only the man. Civil Protection director Leonel Martínez Mata said the man was drunk when he called, and the prosecutor's office replied that it had no record of the visit.

What happened

In mid-July 2021, a man called the 911 emergency number to report that he had seen a "bird man" in Bella Unión, a small community on the edge of the mountains in the municipality of Arteaga, which borders the city of Saltillo to the east, in the Mexican state of Coahuila. Regional outlets reported it. La Prensa de Coahuila ran the story on the night of July 20 without giving the date of the call, and the radio station Radio Medios carried the same text. The Saltillo newspaper Vanguardia published it on July 22 and placed the call "about eight days ago," which puts it around July 14. Neither outlet names the man: La Prensa writes that his details are withheld "out of respect."

According to La Prensa de Coahuila, the man described a creature whose upper half was a bird and whose other half was human. The encounter, again by his account, took place in front of the lienzo charro, the arena where Mexican rodeos are held. The man followed the creature to the first hill visible from Highway 57 and said that when he shot at it, it flew away. He also said it lives in a cave in the mountains, where he did not dare follow it, and that it feeds on livestock it steals from the neighbors.

Arteaga's Civil Protection unit went to the scene. According to Vanguardia, the call also brought out the state prosecutor's office (Fiscalía General del Estado) and public security; the report does not say whether that meant municipal or state police. The two reports agree on what was found: only the man who had made the call. Neither mentions a search of the hill or the cave. Arteaga's Civil Protection director, Leonel Martínez Mata, quoted by Vanguardia, explained that the call did not come straight to his unit but through 911, that several agencies responded and that his was the first to arrive.

Martínez Mata described the man as someone who, "at times, I feel he's not in his right mind." According to the director, the man "was drunk" when he called, and he believes the man uses other substances besides alcohol; according to Martínez Mata, the conclusion reached was that the vision was part of "some hallucinations in the middle of a trance." The director also summed up what the man told them: that he saw a bird man "that comes down to drink water and shelters in a cave."

Both reports say it was not the first time. According to La Prensa, Civil Protection said the same man had made the report "on several occasions," but that "nothing has ever been found." Vanguardia attributes to the region's security authorities the statement that the complaint "has been made repeatedly by this same person," without saying how many times.

Vanguardia also asked the state prosecutor's office. Its press office replied that the crews that handle reports and complaints "had no record of the visit to the site," and that the call was not logged because, in its view, it lacked "the seriousness of a security matter." That answer does not square with the Vanguardia report itself, which lists the prosecutor's office among the agencies that responded: either the prosecutor's office did not go, or its visit was not recorded, and Vanguardia does not say which. La Prensa de Coahuila closed its story by saying the report "remains part of a town legend," because "there are no reliable accounts," and that the authorities do their duty by answering every call.

Why it matters

Everything known about the event comes from two press reports published within days, with the place, an approximate date and the name of the Civil Protection director, whose unit responded. The only witness is unnamed, and the reports record no other. There is no known official statement from Civil Protection, the prosecutor's office or the municipality; what those authorities said comes through the reporters, and the prosecutor's office replied that it had no record of the visit.

The two versions of the story differ. In La Prensa de Coahuila, the creature is half bird and half human, the man chased it and shot at it, and it lives in a cave from which it steals livestock. In Martínez Mata's account, as quoted by Vanguardia, it only comes down to drink water and hides in a cave, and neither the shot nor the livestock appears. Both agree on the cave, on the man having reported it before, and on nothing being found.

The only explanation anyone offered is the Civil Protection director's: hallucinations by a man who had been drinking and may use other substances. It is his view, not a finding, and the reports mention no medical examination. Neither suggests a misidentified animal or a hoax.

The name "bird man" was already in circulation in northeastern Mexico. On the same day, July 22, Vanguardia ran a second story, by editor Carlos Martínez, that equates it with the Mothman of Point Pleasant, West Virginia (↗ Mothman), and reviews tales of winged beings in Nuevo León and Tamaulipas going back to 1877. Bella Unión shows how such a legend reaches the authorities today: through a 911 call, which they must answer even when the report keeps coming back.
