🇲🇽2026·Anapra and other parts of Ciudad Juárez, Chihuahua, Mexico

Living pterosaurs·Ciudad Juárez pterodactyl

Mexico.Ciudad Juárez pterodactyl · winged shape in a viral video

In early November 2026, the journalist Jaime Maussan showed on his programs videos of a winged shape over Ciudad Juárez, Chihuahua; one, a second and a half long, came, he said, from a security camera in the Anapra neighborhood. He presented it first as a pterodactyl and then as a Pteranodon. Sergio Rodríguez, director of Municipal Civil Protection, called the recordings "digital entertainment content," and on November 18 EFE Verifica ruled out that Maussan's video showed a pterodactyl. Snopes concluded that another viral clip was generated or edited with artificial intelligence.

Record of the event

What happened

In early November 2026, the journalist Jaime Maussan, known in Mexico for his UFO programs, began showing videos of a winged creature in the sky over Ciudad Juárez, the Chihuahua city across the border from El Paso, Texas. According to the U.S. fact-checking site Snopes, he posted them to his YouTube channel on or around November 5, 6 and 10. The November 5 video featured Miguel Rojas, a man who, according to that account, had filmed the animal in 2024 in the San Antonio neighborhood. A researcher on Maussan's team also interviewed a boy who said he filmed it on November 7 and said the creature tried to carry off a dog and dropped it before flying away; that attack does not appear in the footage Maussan released.

On November 9, the Ciudad Juárez newspaper Norte Digital reported that Maussan had shown, on his program No Humano, footage of a winged shape filmed in Anapra, a neighborhood in the city's northwest on the edge of El Paso. According to Maussan, the recording lasted a second and a half and existed only because some cats had moved a security camera. The paper gave no date for it; at least two later outlets, including the British International Business Times (IBTimes), placed it on October 29 without saying where the date came from.

Maussan offered two hypotheses: a prehistoric animal that had survived to the present, or a being of interdimensional origin. He also recalled a similar sighting that, he said, was reported in 1996 in the city's Parque Central. Online, according to the same paper, some users pointed to a bird, such as a pelican or a shoebill, and others to a hoax.

At first Maussan spoke of a pterodactyl, the popular name for pterosaurs, the flying reptiles of the age of dinosaurs. On his program Interestelar that week he corrected himself, according to Norte Digital on November 12: "this being that we called pterodactyls, which is actually the pteranodon, a being just like the pterodactyl but bigger." He called for "a thorough investigation" and showed another video that, according to the paper, a boy filmed in the Felipe Ángeles neighborhood, with El Paso's Franklin Mountains in the background.

Several different videos circulated on X. On November 11, Maussan posted a 32-second excerpt from his channel, Maussan Televisión, with the question: "Is the creature recently filmed in the Juárez Valley evidence that a dimensional portal is located there?" In it, a dark silhouette with pointed wings and a crested head crosses, at dusk, in front of a hillside of lit houses. On November 13 he posted a 70-second montage from his Canal 3.3 that strings together several recordings: a shape perched on an antenna tower, another at night over a vacant lot, the hillside clip, a hairless animal on a trail camera, and images labeled as made with "inteligencia artificial" (artificial intelligence). He posted it again on the 18th.

On November 16 at 12:00 UTC, the Russian network RT posted another clip, seven seconds long and in broad daylight: a dark-winged animal over green hills with palm trees, which RT said had gone viral on TikTok. RT wrote that local officials put it down to a large bird, although Ciudad Juárez Civil Protection did not mention a bird: it called the recordings digital entertainment content. The clip was reposted the same day; one account presented it as the second-and-a-half recording from Anapra. That day, the Publimetro journalist Martín Fuentes also shared a 30-second video of a supposed sighting in downtown Monterrey, filmed at night from a moving car by a group of young people who, according to his article, thought it was a heron or some other bird.

There is no known official response other than the city's. Sergio Rodríguez, director general of Municipal Civil Protection in Ciudad Juárez, called the recordings "digital entertainment content," as SDPNoticias and the Guatemalan channel Chapín TV reported on November 16; neither says where he made the statement. He asked people not to share false information and said that if a prehistoric animal had been found, institutions such as the National Institute of Anthropology and History (INAH) would already have stepped in. In the same article, SDPNoticias wrote that unnamed specialists considered a local bird likely, such as a heron ("garza azul o morena"). According to the Ecuadorian newspaper El Telégrafo, Maussan replied that the authorities were dismissing the videos without investigating on the ground.

On November 17, Snopes published a fact-check by the journalist Laerke Christensen on a clip circulating on Instagram, Facebook and Threads, in versions that claimed it was filmed on November 14 or 15. It concluded that the clip was generated or edited with artificial intelligence: letters on a building that spell "Jalpan de" start out as illegible shapes and become readable within a second, which printed text does not do in authentic video. Snopes said the conclusion applied only to that clip. Judging by the hills, the palm trees and a sign whose letters warp at the start, RT's clip appears to be the same one. Snopes contacted Rodríguez and INAH to confirm the quote and ask for their assessment, and was still awaiting replies when it published.

On November 18, EFE Verifica, the fact-checking service of the EFE news agency, rated as false the claim that the video Maussan shared on X on November 11 showed a pterodactyl. It relied on Civil Protection and on the fact that these reptiles died out about 66 million years ago, but left the origin of the footage open: the poor quality made it impossible to confirm what the silhouette was, it found no evidence the video had been generated digitally, and the SynthID tool detected no digital watermark, which, EFE noted, does not rule out the use of artificial intelligence.

The same day, according to Norte Digital, Marianggy del Carmen Gómez Ávila, coordinator of the Geosciences Engineering program at the Autonomous University of Ciudad Juárez (UACJ), explained that the rocks of the Sierra de Juárez hold ammonites and shells from an ancient shallow sea, but no remains of vertebrates such as pterosaurs. She warned that artificial intelligence makes it easy to fabricate sightings that look real. In the popular science outlet IFLScience, the paleontologist Evan Jevnikar pointed out, in comments he had made on Instagram, that a Pteranodon would not flap its wings like that and that it lived by an ancient inland sea in the U.S. Midwest, and that Chihuahua is far from where its fossils are found.

Why it matters

The case shows how a cryptid is born today. In two weeks, a few seconds of video went from a television program to news outlets in several countries, with new versions every few days, reposts that changed the place and the date, and a clip made with artificial intelligence mixed in among the rest. The fact-checks pulled apart what the posts had lumped together: Snopes showed that one specific clip was fake and limited its conclusion to that clip, and EFE could neither identify the silhouette in Maussan's video nor prove it was synthetic.

It also fits Maussan's track record: in 2023 he brought to the Mexican Congress two bodies he presented as non-human (↗ Mexican Congress). In Ciudad Juárez, within a few weeks, he added the Pteranodon, a "Mothman," flying jellyfish and a hairless creature in the Juárez Valley blamed for attacks on farm animals, according to Norte Digital. The stir led Civil Protection to dismiss the videos and a university to explain in public that pterosaurs died out about 66 million years ago.

For the idea of a living pterosaur, what decides the matter is biology, not pixels. An animal with a wingspan of several meters would have to live, feed and breed in an urban area while leaving no trace beyond a few seconds of video; in the Sierra de Juárez, according to UACJ, there are not even vertebrate fossils. What remains are two proposed explanations: a large bird seen at night in poor resolution, such as a heron, which unnamed specialists suggested for Maussan's videos, and the AI fabrication Snopes documented for another clip.
