16:T8b1,On January 27, 1994, during a Tajik Air flight on a Boeing 747SP, the crew —chief pilot Edward (Ed) Rhodes, a U.S. citizen, and two colleagues also U.S. pilots— observed at 41,000 feet over Kazakhstan an intensely luminous object approaching from the east at high speed and at an altitude far above the aircraft. According to the report, the position was given around 45° north latitude and 55° east longitude. The object was tracked visually by the crew for about 40 minutes, describing circular, corkscrew and sharp 90-degree trajectories 'under very high G's', until it took a high-speed horizontal course and disappeared over the horizon. As it was dark, they could not discern its shape: they described its light as having a 'bow wave', like a high-speed photo of a bullet, in which a very small object gives off a much larger trailing wave of heat and light. Some 45 minutes after the initial sighting, as the sun was rising, the aircraft, making over 500 knots, flew under the contrails the object had left; Rhodes estimated them at about 100,000 feet, noted that at that altitude there is too little air and moisture for the engines of an ordinary aircraft able to reach it to form contrails, and said their paths reflected the maneuvers. The virtue: that three American professional pilots flying for a Tajik airline went to their embassy and that the State Department drafted, dated and kept the report for three decades gives the episode an institutional traceability unusual for a sighting from the region. The limit: the cable faithfully conveys what the pilots said, not what an instrument measured, and the photographs that might have elevated the case never appeared in the file. A prolonged visual observation at high altitude also admits conventional explanations —a bright planet or star whose apparent motion induces an illusion of maneuvering (autokinesis), reflections, other air traffic, re-entries or a rocket launch, whose high-altitude trails light up at sunrise— that the available material cannot rule out. That is why the case is testimonially strong and documentarily solid yet limited: exemplary chain of custody, physical corroboration absent.
