🇰🇿1994·Kazakhstan airspace (Tajik Air flight)

Kazakhstan.Tajik Air · UFO over Kazakhstan (PURSUE)

On January 27, 1994, Tajik Air's chief pilot, the American Ed Rhodes, and two American pilot colleagues on a Boeing 747SP reported, at 41,000 feet over Kazakhstan, a light of enormous intensity that came from the east at great speed and much higher than them and maneuvered for some 40 minutes in circles, corkscrews and 90° turns. The episode was documented in a U.S. State Department cable and appears among the declassified PURSUE files (2026).

Primary documents

The night in question

On January 27, 1994, during a Tajik Air flight on a Boeing 747SP, the crew, made up of chief pilot Edward (Ed) Rhodes, a U.S. citizen, and two colleagues also U.S. pilots, observed at 41,000 feet over Kazakhstan an intensely luminous object approaching from the east at high speed and at an altitude far above the aircraft. According to the report, the position was given around 45° north latitude and 55° east longitude. The object was tracked visually by the crew for about 40 minutes, describing circular, corkscrew and sharp 90-degree trajectories 'under very high G's', until it took a high-speed horizontal course and disappeared over the horizon. As it was dark, they could not discern its shape: they described its light as having a 'bow wave', like a high-speed photo of a bullet, in which a very small object gives off a much larger trailing wave of heat and light. Some 45 minutes after the initial sighting, as the sun was rising, the aircraft, making over 500 knots, flew under the contrails the object had left; Rhodes estimated them at about 100,000 feet, noted that at that altitude there is too little air and moisture for the engines of an ordinary aircraft able to reach it to form contrails, and said their paths reflected the maneuvers. To the embassy's suggestion that it was a meteor skipping off the atmosphere, the crew replied that in their years with Pan Am they had seen thousands of falling stars and space junk and that this was nothing like a meteor, and Rhodes expressed the opinion, which his crew seemed to support, that the object was extraterrestrial and under intelligent control. The cable closes with 'we have no opinion and report the above for what it may be worth'. Jonathan McDowell's GCAT launch catalogue lists no launch on 27 January, but it does list Progress M-21 from Baikonur, some 650 km east of the given position, on 28 January at 02:12 UTC, an hour and a half before ground sunrise at that position (about 03:44 UTC); the object catalogue also logs, for the 27th, the re-entry of an Ariane upper stage, with no time or place. Tim Printy, in the sceptical newsletter SUNlite (January-February 2022), concluded that what was seen was 'probably the Progress M-21 launch': a light arriving from the east and high-altitude trails lit at sunrise are typical of a launch during nautical twilight, and he argues the real date was the 28th because Tajik Air's only 747SP, the 'Snow Leopard', did not leave London on Wednesdays: Tajik Air's published timetable, reproduced on the James P. Baldwin page he cites, gives Heathrow departures on Monday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday and Sunday nights, arriving in Dushanbe the next morning. Against it, the cable sets the date as 27 January in a report made two days later and describes some 40 minutes of manoeuvres, more than a rocket's powered flight lasts, though not more than a launch's sunlit trails can persist; and Printy himself grants that his figures give about sixty minutes to sunrise, not forty-five, though he puts this down to the cable's times being estimates, and notes the pilots did not report seeing a launch as well as the object.

To the testimony of three professional crew members is added their documentary trail. The pilots reported it to the embassy on January 29, and the sighting was recorded in a cable from the U.S. embassy in Dushanbe (Tajikistan), also distributed to the CIA and the DIA, dated January 31, 1994, and titled 'Tajik Air Pilots Report Unidentified Flying Object'; the document carries a State Department 'Released in Full' stamp dated February 25, 2026, and resurfaced publicly in the PURSUE declassification wave (war.gov/ufo, 2026); it was not new, since Printy took it from CUFON's compilation of State Department documents 'reviewed and released 7 February 2000', and Richard Hall had already summarised it in 'The UFO Evidence, Volume 2'. The cable itself notes that Rhodes took several photographs with a pocket Olympus compact camera and said he would send copies to the embassy and to the State Department's Tajikistan desk if they came out; no photographs, however, accompany the released cable, so the visual part of the report remains without material corroboration.

In short: it is a strong case both testimonially (three professional pilots, long duration, anomalous maneuvers) and documentarily (a dated, titled embassy cable + PURSUE declassification), but the cable mentions no radar or other sensor, and the mentioned photographs are not in the available material. What is preserved is the diplomatic transcription of a pilots' report, not an instrumental measurement.

Why it matters

It combines two strengths: multiple professional crew with ~40 minutes of observation, and a verifiable official documentary chain (U.S. embassy Dushanbe cable, Jan 31, 1994 → PURSUE 2026 declassification). It connects Central Asian airspace with the U.S. disclosure thread, showing that material released by the U.S. includes encounters that occurred far from its territory.

The case illustrates a virtue and a limit of the diplomatic record at once. The virtue: that three American professional pilots flying for a Tajik airline went to their embassy and that the State Department drafted, dated and kept the report for three decades gives the episode an institutional traceability unusual for a sighting from the region. The limit: the cable faithfully conveys what the pilots said, not what an instrument measured, and the photographs that might have elevated the case never appeared in the file. A prolonged visual observation at high altitude also admits conventional explanations that the available material cannot rule out: a bright planet or star whose apparent motion induces an illusion of maneuvering (autokinesis), reflections, other air traffic, re-entries or a rocket launch, whose high-altitude trails light up at sunrise; the Progress M-21 launch is the only concrete cause anyone has proposed in writing, and it clashes with the date the cable gives. That is why the case is testimonially strong and documentarily solid yet limited: exemplary chain of custody, physical corroboration absent. The detail of the promised photographs that never reached the file is, in itself, a calibrating datum: it marks the exact point where a good testimony could have become material evidence and did not, reminding us that the documentary solidity of a report does not equal the physical solidity of the phenomenon reported.

  2. 02~40 minutes of observation at 41,000 ft over Kazakhstan (≈45°N 55°E), in darkness and with no discernible shape, with circular, corkscrew and 90° maneuvers; at sunrise the aircraft flew under the trails it left, estimated at ~100,000 ft.
  3. 03Documented in a U.S. embassy Dushanbe cable, Jan 31, 1994, 'Released in Full' (Feb 25, 2026).
