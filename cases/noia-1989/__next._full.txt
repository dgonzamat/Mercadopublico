2e:T618,At 19:00 UTC, 'once the area had been carefully examined', the operators saw on two displays, the RAPPI and the UPA-35, a 'blitz', an echo, 'in primary and secondary', at position 060/32 NM, that is, some 59 kilometres east-north-east of the station, with a height of 57,000 feet on the RHI, the height-finder display. They measured it continuously: the height varied up to 75,000 feet, about 23 kilometres, with 'few positional variations with respect to the first detection'. Santiago tower coordinated through Nécora, the squadron's call sign, the 'indication of civil aircraft near the U.F.O. for visual confirmation', 'without positive results being obtained'. At 20:00 UTC, according to the tower, its alarms went off on the emergency frequencies, 121.5 and 243.0, with 'interference and radio confusion' between Santiago, the Madrid control centre and Nécora. At 20:40 UTC a direction finder placed the source of that alarm between 160 and 167 degrees from Santiago, to the south-south-east, and at that moment the UPA-35 showed 'several objects', three of them at flight levels 290, 320 and 420.
