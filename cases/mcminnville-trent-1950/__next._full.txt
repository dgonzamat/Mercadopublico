28:T49d,The Trents did not immediately seek publicity; the photos first circulated among acquaintances and appeared in the local McMinnville newspaper, the Telephone-Register, and shortly after in the nationally circulated LIFE magazine, becoming one of the visual icons of the phenomenon. Their analytic notoriety, however, came in 1968-1969, when the University of Colorado included them in the government-funded study known as the Condon Report. Astronomer and planetary physicist William K. Hartmann, an investigator for the committee, conducted a detailed analysis of the negatives —using photometry to measure the brightness of the various areas and elements of photogrammetry to estimate distance— and wrote the sentence that would make the case famous: that this was one of the few reports in which all factors investigated, geometric, psychological and physical, appeared consistent with the assertion that an extraordinary, silvery, metallic, disc-shaped object, tens of meters across and evidently artificial, had flown within sight of the two witnesses. It was one of the very few positive conclusions of a report designed, in its general orientation, to close the subject.29:T491,That assessment was not the last word. The skeptic Robert Sheaffer advanced in the 1970s the most influential counterargument: from the direction of the shadows beneath the telephone wires, he held that the photos could have been taken in the morning rather than at dusk as the Trents claimed, which opened the door to a staging and weakened the testimony; his analysis even led the original Condon investigator to reconsider his conclusion. Decades later, in 2013, the French group IPACO applied photogrammetric analysis and claimed to have detected a suspension thread above the object, concluding that it was a small hollow-bottomed model hung from a wire, possibly tied to the power line. In the opposite direction, defenders such as physicist Bruce Maccabee reanalyzed the negatives and disputed the morning-shadows thesis, holding that the light was compatible with an overcast late afternoon as reported.
