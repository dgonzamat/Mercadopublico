14:T897,The Trents did not immediately seek publicity; the photos first circulated among acquaintances and appeared on June 8, 1950 in the local McMinnville newspaper, the Telephone-Register, and the following week in the nationally circulated LIFE magazine, becoming one of the visual icons of the phenomenon. Their analytic notoriety, however, came with the government-funded University of Colorado study known as the Condon Report, published in 1969. Astronomer and planetary physicist William K. Hartmann, an investigator for the committee, interviewed the Trents in 1967, located the original negatives, conducted a detailed analysis —using photometry to measure the brightness of the various areas and elements of photogrammetry to estimate distance— and wrote the sentence that would make the case famous: that this was one of the few reports in which all factors investigated, geometric, psychological and physical, appeared consistent with the assertion that an extraordinary, silvery, metallic, disc-shaped object, tens of meters across and evidently artificial, had flown within sight of the two witnesses. But the same conclusion added that 'it cannot be said that the evidence positively rules out a fabrication', and Hartmann himself had raised the two objections the skeptics would later adopt. He noted that the object, the pole and the distant house are lit from the east, so that one could argue the photos were taken on a dull day around 10 a.m., although densitometry showed the sky brighter toward the west, as expected at dusk. And he observed that the object appears beneath the same point of the wire in both photos, despite their being taken from different positions: his tests did not rule out a small model hanging from that wire by an unresolved thread, and in his table of interpretations that is the only fabrication hypothesis not marked as rejected. What tipped the balance was his photometry, which placed the object at about 1.3 km; in the first volume's summary he classified the case as 'clearly either a fabrication or an extraordinary object'.
