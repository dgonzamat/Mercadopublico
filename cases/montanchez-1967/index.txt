2c:T449,On the afternoon of 3 June 1967, a T-33 jet trainer of the Talavera la Real Jet School, callsign Duende 20, with two captains aboard, was flying from Torrejón to its base. It had taken off at 15:55 universal time. On reaching the overhead of Montánchez, at flight level 220, the pilots saw at eleven o'clock a very bright object well above them. They reported it to the Matador alert and control squadron, which asked the operations centre for permission to attempt an interception and got it. The pilots climbed towards the object but could not reach it: they had the impression it was rising as they rose. That is how it was recorded on the magnetic tape of the conversation, which the file transcribes: 'it is stationary… it looks like a white, bright balloon', and later, 'the higher I climb, the higher it gets'. On the same tape, however, the pilot insists: 'it is not an aircraft we are seeing, it is something strange', and later, 'it is a strange object, not an aircraft or anything, but I do not know what it is'. Twice they noticed strong radio interference when passing beneath it.2d:T4fc,The pilots described a shape that changed with the angle: first rounded, at 16:37 triangular, with the side facing away from the Sun brighter. At 16:26 a second alert and control squadron, Bolero, picked up on its radar an object that seemed to be orbiting over the Talavera area; since the T-33 was in contact with Matador, it only listened. At 16:45 Matador's radar also had it, with a double echo on the height display, although only the T-33 showed on the search display; the controller read the second echo about 2,000 feet below the aircraft, and the pilot corrected him: the object was 'much, much' higher, 'close to 50,000 feet'. He added that it could be seen 'from 200 miles around' and that it seemed to be drifting very slowly towards the south-southwest. At 16:59, with the T-33 short of fuel, a pair of F-86F fighters from Torrejón, callsign EL-01, carrying cameras, was ordered up. They made visual contact at 17:24; one climbed to 30,000 feet and the other to 42,000, and they tried to intercept for about twenty minutes without being able to close on an object that, according to their reports, kept gaining height. They took photographs; the file keeps only one very poor-quality print showing a point of light. All three aircraft returned for lack of fuel.2e:T568,On 8 June the Air Defence Command sent the Air Staff a report with the statements of the Matador and Bolero commanders, the track records from both radars, the tape transcript, the F-86 pilots' reports and the photograph; on 14 June it added the report of the two T-33 captains and a clipping from Badajoz's 'Hoja del Lunes', which had published the sighting on the 5th. Its conclusions were cautious: the real existence of a strange object in Badajoz airspace, which none of the pilots could identify; that all three aircraft always flew below it without reaching its height; differing views on its shape, which seemed to change with the observer's position; and very slow or no horizontal movement over a long period.
