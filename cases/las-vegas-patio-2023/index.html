🇺🇸2023·Northwest Las Vegas valley, Nevada, U.S.

United States.Las Vegas · a family reports two aliens in its backyard

At about 11:50 p.m. on April 30, 2023, a green fireball crossed the sky over the southwestern U.S.: the American Meteor Society received 23 reports from four states, and a Las Vegas police officer's camera recorded it. At 12:29 a.m. on May 1, according to police, a young man called 911 from the northwest Las Vegas valley: his family had seen something fall into the backyard, and two beings 8 to 10 feet tall were there. Two officers came and, according to police, found no UFO; the call was closed as unfounded. NASA attributed the light to a meteor that passed high up and far from the city.

What happened

On the night of Sunday, April 30, 2023, shortly before midnight, a fireball crossed the sky over the southwestern United States. The American Meteor Society (AMS), an association that collects reports from people who see meteors, lists it as its event 2408 of 2023, with 23 reports from Nevada, California, Utah and Arizona and an average time of 11:48 p.m. Las Vegas time. Most describe it as green or blue and lasting a few seconds. The trajectory the AMS computes automatically from those reports, which the AMS itself rates as low confidence, places it over central Nevada: by a calculation from the AMS's own coordinates, more than 220 km (135 miles) north of Las Vegas. At about 11:50 p.m., according to the station KLAS 8 News Now, the camera a Las Vegas Metropolitan Police Department (LVMPD) officer wore on his uniform recorded the light as it streaked low across the sky.

About 40 minutes later a 911 call came in. According to the statement the LVMPD emailed to the press, dispatch received a call about "a suspicious situation" at 12:29 a.m. on Monday, May 1. The caller was a young man at a ranch-style home in the northwest Las Vegas valley; KLAS called him Angel, and Fox News, in 2024, Angel Kenmore. In the recording KLAS released, he says his family saw something fall from the sky and that in the backyard there is "like an 8-foot person beside it, and another one's inside." The dispatcher asks whether there are two people, and he answers: "Correct, and they're very large. They're like 8 foot, 9 feet, 10 foot." He adds that they have big, shiny eyes and a big mouth, and that they are "100% not human." According to KLAS, the dispatcher at first wondered whether to send a crisis intervention team.

The date of the call is not the same in every source. PolitiFact, in June 2023, and NewsNation, later, place it on April 30; the time the police give puts it on May 1, about 40 minutes after the fireball, which is also the gap KLAS reports.

What the family later told KLAS adds details the call does not contain. According to the report by journalist George Knapp, two brothers and their father were working on a vehicle in the yard when they saw a sparkling object; as it came down, they were hit by what they described as a shock wave, and the spot where it landed looked blurry, as if camouflaged. According to KLAS, they said one of the beings climbed behind the controls of a large front loader stored in the yard; according to Fox News, that it was hiding behind a forklift. They also said they heard footsteps in the yard and then on the roof, and that a circular mark was left in the dirt, which KLAS showed in drone video. Knapp adds that at nearly the same time as the light, a doorbell camera at a house in the area recorded a strange noise and what sounded like a crash.

Two officers went to the house. KLAS gives two arrival times: 38 minutes after the call in Knapp's report and "about a half-hour" in that of his colleague David Charns. In June 2023 the LVMPD posted two police camera videos tagged with incident number LLV230500000097 on the YouTube channel of its public records unit, and Motherboard (Vice) and the Los Angeles Times transcribed what can be heard. On the way, one officer says he is very nervous and that someone saw a shooting star and now these people say there are aliens in their backyard. Four people from the house are waiting on the lawn; the sources do not say who each of them was. "It was like a big creature," one says, "around 10 feet tall." The officer replies: "I'm not going to BS you guys. One of my partners said they saw something fall out of the sky, too."

The officers searched the backyard. Police cut 16 minutes of that stretch because, they told Motherboard, it was filmed on private property, and they said no UFO was found. When the video resumes, according to Motherboard, the officer explains that while he was on another call at a nearby Target store, a colleague saw what she took for a shooting star at the same moment the family saw the object fall. Another officer flagged down a passing car to ask whether its occupants had seen anything fall, and summed up for them what the family had described: something "with eight to nine feet, no clothes, green-colored men, not human," with glowing eyes. The people in the car said they had seen nothing. As the officers left, one of the witnesses said he was going to spend the night in a hotel. The LVMPD closed the call as unfounded after a preliminary investigation.

Days later, according to the family, two LVMPD sergeants came back with follow-up questions, and the family told KLAS they saw men in suits and sunglasses drive by in a car with government plates. Nellis and Creech Air Force bases, near Las Vegas, told KLAS they had no interest or involvement in the incident. On June 10, 2023, someone at the house called police again, believing a camera had been placed in the backyard; officers checked the perimeter and found none. According to an article by Charns published by the station KTLA, an LVMPD spokesperson said police later installed their own temporary cameras to ease the family's fear that someone would come to harass or harm them, and sources close to the investigation said they did not believe the call was a hoax; in Nevada a false 911 report is a felony.

Why it matters

The only part of the event that instruments recorded is the light. The police camera, the 23 AMS reports and their timing all fit a bright meteor. Lindley Johnson, NASA's planetary defense officer, told Motherboard in June 2023 that it was most likely a meteor less than a meter across. Given its speed, he said, it was high in the atmosphere and hundreds of miles from Las Vegas when it was seen: "So nothing from the meteor landed in anyone's backyard in Vegas." Motherboard noted that the fireball appears in the AMS logs but not in those of NASA's Center for Near Earth Object Studies (CNEOS); Johnson explained that the CNEOS list only covers objects larger than one meter.

The beings, by contrast, rest only on the account of the family, whose members saw them together. According to PolitiFact, the police camera videos KLAS obtained show something falling from the sky but no image of the beings; the officers found no UFO and the LVMPD closed the call as unfounded, although KLAS quoted police sources who believe something did crash in the yard. The light the camera recorded does not corroborate that anything came down there: according to NASA and the AMS it was hundreds of kilometers away. What it does corroborate is that a very bright light seemed to fall nearby to many people that night. In the AMS reports, a witness at Railroad Pass, south of Las Vegas, thought it might have hit the ground, and another in Goodsprings wrote that he was "pretty sure it crashed right there into the desert."

The "alien" images in circulation come from elsewhere. In June 2023 a video presented as security camera footage, showing a creature in a backyard over audio of the call, went viral; Reuters and PolitiFact found that the creature was computer animation. Its maker, the TikTok account @owltreestump, confirmed to PolitiFact that it had created the video, and the video carries the date May 26 and the tag "#cgi."

Separately from that fake, Angel Kenmore posted on YouTube a short video of his backyard that he presents as the moment the family saw the creature; he says a figure can be seen behind the fence. Scott Roder, of the crime scene reconstruction firm Evidence Room, analyzed it with motion-tracking software and said in April 2024 on NewsNation's Banfield program that it was "an original video," unedited, showing two beings; Fox News relayed his words in May. Ben Hansen, host of the program UFO Witness, sided with a British expert who holds that the figure is the shadow of a flashlight held by a family member.

The NewsNation and Fox News coverage debates the video Angel Kenmore posted and adds no new record of the event. What the records of the time do establish is the fireball, the 911 call with its time, and the visit of the two officers, filmed by their cameras.
