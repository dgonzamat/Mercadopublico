26:T43f,It is worth pinning down what that name means at the outset, because much of the case depends on it: 'November-5' is not a date, it is a facility callsign. The 44th Missile Wing organized its silos into squadrons designated by phonetic-alphabet letters and numbered each squadron's facilities 01 through 11; November-5 is, literally, the fifth site of the November flight. Woods describes that, once on Highway 79, the sky began to brighten to a glow he compares to sunrise, and that rounding a bend they came upon an enormous object hovering just above the ground; his habitual size comparison is a big-box store. From there the account enters classic abduction territory: small grey-skinned beings, an interruption of memory and a jump to finding himself elsewhere, with his partner as shaken as he was. Woods has testified about the episode to AARO, the Pentagon's anomalous phenomena office, and has characterized that appearance as a conversation in which he was briefed on what he could and could not disclose. His most widely circulated public telling was broadcast in April 2025.27:T5f4,A point of documentary hygiene is indispensable here, because anyone searching 'Ellsworth 1977' will find a forged document before anything else. A purported military message dated 16 November 1977 circulates, narrating an intrusion involving beings and damage at a silo called Site L-9. That paper is a proven hoax. Bob Pratt dismantled it in 'The Truth About the Ellsworth Case' (MUFON UFO Journal, January 1984); Tom Deuley, a former atomic weapons communications officer, verified that some of the personnel named were not stationed there and that ranks and names were garbled; Brad Sparks noted that the radiation readings —'1.7 to 2.9 roentgens'— carry a precision no instrument of the period delivered, and that the text confuses dose with rate. The message also lacks the classification a missing warhead would have required and is written, in its reviewers' words, like a news report rather than a nuclear incident report. It spread credited to Warren York, who supplied it to Vangard Sciences claiming he had obtained it through FOIA, without a release stamp, request number or responding agency ever being produced; Sparks named Richard Doty, stationed at Ellsworth between 1977 and 1978, as the prime suspect. NICAP catalogs it as a hoax. And the decisive fact for keeping the two matters apart is that L-9 and November-5 are different installations: Lima-9 and November-5 both belonged to the 68th Missile Squadron, but they are separate sites.
