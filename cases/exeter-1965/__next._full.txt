30:T4ed,Its strength lies in the witnesses. They are exactly the kind of witnesses —trained, sober, used to reporting precisely and with a professional reputation to lose— that make it hard to dismiss the sighting as casual confusion or a prank; and the reaction of the animals and the chain of indirect witnesses at the station reinforce that something happened. Its limit, however, is that of its time and must be weighed honestly: there was no radar, photograph or film to fix the object. The testimony, however convergent, describes what the witnesses perceived, not the physical nature of what was perceived, and the prosaic explanations —though the Operation Big Blast one failed on timing— cannot be entirely excluded in the absence of an instrumental record. That is why the case is Tier A and not S: it rests on convergent professional testimony, strong but not diagnostic.
