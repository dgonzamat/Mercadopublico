24:T485,The replies came within six days. On 11 March the embassy in Rabat reported that the newspaper Le Matin had said hundreds of Casablanca residents saw two greenish lights west of the city and, the next day, that the same phenomenon was seen over Rabat, Fez and northern Spain, which the paper put down to a conjunction of Venus with Sirius. On 13 March the State Department told Algiers that NASA had no knowledge of the origin of the phenomena and that the Defense Department had no knowledge of any U.S. military activity that could explain the 6 March radar sighting. No further correspondence and no Algerian closure are on record. An ephemeris calculation supports the astronomical lead Rabat pointed to: from January to March 1975 Venus shone in the west after sunset at magnitude −3.8, and on 6 March at 19:30 it stood about 9° above the western horizon as seen from Oran, setting around 20:15; on 17 February it passed 0.2° from Jupiter. That fits a dazzling light that returns at the same hour, lasts two hours and seems to 'land' as it sets.
