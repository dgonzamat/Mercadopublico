16:T561,Around one in the morning on August 23, 1954, Bernard Miserey, a 25-year-old shopkeeper's son from the Normandy town of Vernon —on the banks of the Seine, northwest of Paris—, had just put his car away in his garage, near the bridge, returning late from a trip, when, by his account, he saw over the Seine bridge an elongated, red, bright object 'resembling a big cigar', motionless at a fairly low height and in total silence. In the statement he gave the police he reckoned the object 'was perhaps a hundred meters long', but he told Le Figaro he had 'no idea' of the objects' size or altitude. What turned the sighting into a classic case was what happened next: from the lower part of the large object, one after another, several smaller luminous discs began to descend. According to his statement, the first fell freely, slowed, tilted and shot horizontally across the river towards him, becoming very luminous, surrounded by a halo, and vanished at prodigious speed to the southwest; a second, third and fourth followed, and after a somewhat longer interval a fifth, which dropped to the level of the new bridge, stopped for a moment oscillating slightly —its red glow more intense at the centre and fainter at the edges— and left towards the north.
