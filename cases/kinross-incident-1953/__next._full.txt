2b:T825,The file is not a Project Blue Book case but a USAF Directorate of Flight Safety Research accident report (Form 14). In its analysis (paragraph 6) it identifies the intercepted target as an RCAF Dakota (C-47), serial VC-912, flying from Winnipeg to Sudbury, crossing northern Lake Superior from west to east at 7,000 feet and about 30 miles south of its intended track. Its formal conclusions state that the F-89 "probably crashed in the Canadian waters of Lake Superior just prior to or at the time of interception", and the prevailing instrument flying conditions "could be a cause factor"; those same conclusions (2b and 2c) still call the target an "unidentified aircraft". The same file includes the statement of the investigating officer, Capt. David C. Collins, on his conversation with the Dakota's pilot, who said he was flying at 7,000 feet in the clear, did not know he was being intercepted and had neither seen nor heard the F-89. The disputes came later: in 1961 and 1963 RCAF spokesmen told NICAP such an intercept seemed unlikely, and in 2006 Gerald Fosberg, identified as the pilot of that flight, denied in the documentary "The Moncla Memories" that he had been the target.
