14:T468,Around 7:00 p.m. on February 23, 1975, in the Kamimachi neighborhood of Kōfu (Yamanashi prefecture), near the Hinode housing estate, two primary-school boys, Masato Kawano and Katsuhiro Yamahata, both about seven years old, were roller-skating when they said they saw in the sky two objects glittering orange and crackling. According to their account, reported by the Yamanashi Nichi-Nichi newspaper on February 25, the bigger one flew off toward Mount Atago and the other came down between the posts of a vineyard behind the estate and landed. It was a saucer about 5 meters across and 2.5 meters high, with four large illegible letters on the hull; a side hatch opened and an occupant of about 1.3 meters came out, with no visible eyes or mouth but with three fangs, its body glowing silver and a long rifle-like object over its left shoulder, while a smaller one stayed inside. The being walked about a meter from the craft and put a hand on Yamahata's shoulder; too scared to walk, he was carried on his friend's back some 30 meters to his house. Later accounts added that the occupant reboarded before the object took off.15:T40e,What distinguishes the case from an isolated child sighting is the corroboration around the event. According to the newspaper, the boys' mothers went back to the spot with them and saw from a distance an orange light glowing on and off over the vineyard for about five minutes, which flew away while they looked elsewhere. A classmate, Ichiro Minegishi, aged eight, said he had seen a shining object flying toward the housing estate half an hour earlier while riding in a car with his parents on the Kōfu bypass. The next morning two of the vineyard's concrete posts were found almost fallen down, which alarmed the neighbors again, and the school inspected the site with tape measures. UFO researcher Leonard Stringfield later wrote that there were landing traces and a ring pattern near the broken posts and that a schoolteacher found radioactivity within the circular patch; the period newspaper does not mention that measurement. The boys were questioned in depth by investigator Masaru Mori, who translated the article into English.16:T486,Authorities offered a prosaic explanation: the Civil Aviation Bureau of the Transport Ministry noted that an airliner often flies over Kōfu between 6:00 and 7:00 p.m. and that, in particular, the lights of the YS-11 propeller plane, flying at a thousand meters, are visible to the naked eye. The newspaper adds that the sky was clear that evening. The explanation has an obvious limit: it can account for the orange lights in the sky —and even the light the mothers saw— but not the landing, the occupant, the physical effect on the boy or the toppled posts. At the same time, none of those remaining elements has backing that raises it above testimony: the posts could have fallen from mundane causes before or after, the Geiger reading appears only in later accounts and would have been taken by a teacher with no forensic protocol or chain of custody, and the central component —the occupant— rests on two seven-year-old witnesses.
