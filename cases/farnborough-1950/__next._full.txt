The group's final report, DSI/JTIC Report No. 7, "Unidentified Flying Objects", of June 1951, devotes its paragraphs 13 and 14 to Farnborough, alongside a third case, that of a Meteor pilot who landed at Tangmere on 1 June 1950. Hubbard honestly described his own impression of what he saw", but that it finds it "impossible to believe" that so unconventional an aircraft could have crossed, at no great altitude, on a summer morning, a district as populous and air-minded as Farnborough without being seen by more than one observer; it concludes that Hubbard was the victim of an optical illusion or saw a normal aircraft and deceived himself about its shape and speed. 30:T410,The group's reading has concrete support in its own file. In August a single man saw the object, while the two officers beside him searched the same sky and saw nothing; estimates of size, height and speed for an isolated object in a clear sky depend on one another, and misjudging the distance is enough to turn a distant aircraft into a fast, low disc.
