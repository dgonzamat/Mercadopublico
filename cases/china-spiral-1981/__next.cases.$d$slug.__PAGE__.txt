11:T486,Unlike most mass sightings, this one received sustained scientific study. Wang Sichao, a planetary astronomer at the Purple Mountain (Zijinshan) Observatory of the Chinese Academy of Sciences, collected more than seventy witness reports over the years and devoted more than two decades to studying this and other sightings. He reconstructed the trajectory of the 1981 event: he placed the object at an altitude of roughly 650 kilometers and attributed to it a speed of about 1.6 km/s before it disappeared. Those figures are significant: 650 km is low orbital altitude, far above any aircraft or atmospheric phenomenon, and 1.6 km/s is the order of magnitude of an object in a space regime. It is worth being precise about Wang's stance, because it is often misrepresented: he held that the object moved in a way that did not fit a natural body, and across his career argued that some Chinese sightings deserved an 'intelligent, non-natural' explanation. In other words, the physical signature (orbital altitude and speed) comes from Chinese institutional science; the prosaic reading of that signature is the corpus's, not necessarily Wang's own conclusion.12:T41a,The combination of orbital altitude, speed and spiral morphology matches a mechanism well understood in astronautics: a rocket upper stage or a satellite venting propellant or gases while tumbling, forming a spiral lit by the Sun against an already-dark sky (the so-called 'twilight effect'). It is exactly the phenomenon later confirmed behind the 2009 Norway spiral (the failed launch of the Russian Bulava missile) and several subsequent Chinese 'UFO spirals' officially attributed to launches —a case for which Wang himself was quoted in the press when a sounding rocket was mistaken for a UFO. The specific attribution of the 1981 event to a particular launch was not closed in the public record, and that is where the case's honesty lies: the physics points robustly to a human object in a space regime venting propellant, but the documentary chain that would tie the 1981 spiral to a dated, named launch was never completed.
