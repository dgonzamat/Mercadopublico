14:T5dd,The report reached official U.S. channels by several routes at once. The FBIS sheet was filed in an Executive Office of the President folder titled "NASC Inquiry into Bahia, Brazil Incident, November 13, 1963," that is, an inquiry by the National Aeronautics and Space Council about the episode. On November 14, the embassy in Rio, in a cable drafted by Science Attaché André C. Simonpietri and signed by Ambassador Lincoln Gordon, asked the consul in Salvador to confirm or correct its understanding from a radiotelephone conversation the night before: that the consul gave an unqualified negative on a newspaper report that a "large metal manned balloon" had been found near Salvador around November 8, with a dead crewman aboard wearing a uniform or spacesuit with strange markings, and a four-meter crater. The cable added that an object had been sighted aloft in that area around that date and tentatively identified as a weather-type balloon, but that no such object had been found aground. It was sent unclassified, with copies to the Department of Defense and NASA. That same day, the air attaché in Rio sent message C-113 to the Air Force at Wright-Patterson AFB: the first and only information available in Rio was an O Globo item of November 8 saying it had received news from Salvador that a large metallic globe had fallen in Conde; the consul claimed no object had been found and that his information indicated a weather balloon, and the Rio press was also unable to confirm anything.15:T6d9,On November 20, 1963, the embassy sent Washington airgram A-628, titled "Negative Report on UFO," whose subject line misdates the sighting to November 1964. The consul in Salvador, Harold Midkiff, had replied with telegram No. 157 of that same November 14, with a line that closed the case: "Story of strange object descending in region of Conde Bahia apparently fabricated in Rio no such objects seen by anyone in the area according to newspaper reporters and others who rushed there before investigation reports were received Secretary Public Security speculated object might be weather balloon but no such sighting verified." Midkiff forwarded by pouch two Bahian press clippings backing that conclusion: the Diário de Notícias of November 9, in which the Ministry of Aeronautics categorically denied any "strange object" in Conde, and the Jornal da Bahia of November 11, headlined "A Nave que Não Estava Lá" ("The Airship That Wasn't There"). According to the latter's reporter, the only newsworthy aspect of the episode was an "astounding by-product": he sent a telegram to his paper from the federal telegraph office in Conde, by an old Morse code transmitter, and five minutes later he had a reply from Salvador. The joke is the speed of a small-town telegraph, not a debunking: the airgram does not say that reply refuted anything. On November 21, the air attaché relayed the consul's text to Wright-Patterson (message C-116) and added that there was no further mention in the Rio or other press.
