🇺🇸1958·Detroit, Michigan

United States.Detroit · a young man sees a circular object with a dome

An FBI memo dated April 17, 1958 records a call from a 23-year-old man who, at 4:08 in the morning, told the Detroit field office that on his way home from work he had seen “a circular object with a crystal-type dome” reflecting lights cross the city heading north. He had first tried to alert the Air Force at Selfridge Field, without success. The FBI opened no investigation: it recommended passing the information “to proper air force authorities,” as it did with such reports. There is a single witness and nothing to corroborate him. The memo was declassified in May 2026.

What happened

On Thursday, April 17, 1958, at 4:08 a.m., a 23-year-old man telephoned the FBI's Detroit office in Michigan. He was on his way home from work and had just seen in the sky, according to the memorandum Special Agent Robert Ross Reynolds wrote that same day, "a circular object with a crystal-type dome that reflected lights." The object was "passing in a northern direction from southwest" and crossed the city three blocks south of what the memo calls only "Six Mile," at Lamphere Street.

Before calling the FBI, the witness had tried to reach the Air Force at Selfridge Field, the air base next to Mount Clemens, northeast of Detroit, but could not get through. He said he was the son of a Detroit policeman and had previous experience with the Civil Air Patrol, the Air Force's civilian auxiliary, though, in the memo's words, he "was not too familiar with the identification of aircraft." The memo records neither the duration of the sighting, nor the object's height, nor whether the witness was alone, the weather or the length of the call; there is a single civilian witness, with no radar, photograph or other observers in the document.

Reynolds addressed the memo to the head of the Detroit office, under the subject "Unidentified Flying Objects," and closed it with a single recommendation: "Advise proper air force authorities." That was what the FBI's internal instructions required. Headquarters Bulletin No. 57, of October 1, 1947, had told the field offices that the FBI was discontinuing its investigations of "flying discs" and that future reports should be referred to the Air Force. In 1952 another instruction reminded the offices to pass each report promptly to the local office of the Air Force Office of Special Investigations (OSI), because investigating these objects was the responsibility of the Department of the Air Force.

That same April 17, the memo was filed in the Detroit office file (100-DE-18221, serial 844). A handwritten note at the foot reads "Lt Col (Bernie) E Prater advised 4/17/58 no letter nec.": Lieutenant Colonel Prater was advised that day and no letter was needed. The memo does not say which unit or office that officer belonged to, and it records no investigation of the FBI's own.

Project Blue Book, the Air Force program that studied such reports, has no file from Detroit for April 1958 in the collection of its records held today by the Internet Archive (archive.org): the only Michigan file from that month, "north of Selfridge AFB," concerns a different sighting, on April 12, of two large and six small orange objects, which its witness reported a month later. The sources consulted record no later Air Force investigation and propose no cause. The memo was declassified on May 8, 2026, in the first tranche of PURSUE, the series of UFO documents published on the Department of War's website.

Why it matters

The memo shows how the chain of UFO reports worked in the United States in 1958: the FBI did not investigate, it noted the call and passed it to the Air Force, and the note at the foot shows that this was done the same day. It is an official document, written hours after the call, with the time, the object's path and a concrete textual description.

Its content about the object is minimal: a single witness, with no instruments backing the account, who himself said he was not very familiar with identifying aircraft. There is no record of what the Air Force did with the report or of anyone speaking to the witness again, and with no later analysis the document does not allow saying what he saw.
