🇧🇷1996·Varginha, Minas Gerais, Brazil

Brazil.Varginha · three young women say they saw a creature by a wall

On January 20, 1996, three young women in Varginha said they saw a creature crouching by a wall, and local ufologists later claimed the fire brigade and the Army captured one or more beings. Two Army inquiries (1996 and 1997) attributed the account to confusion with a local man and were archived; the file includes the autopsy of military policeman Marco Eli Cherese, pointing to an infection by known bacteria. In 2026 it returned to the political stage with witnesses before U.S. members of Congress and the Superior Tribunal Militar's publication of the file.

What happened

On Saturday, January 20, 1996, around 3:30 p.m., three young women in Varginha (Minas Gerais, Brazil) said they saw a creature crouching by a wall on a vacant lot in the Jardim Andere neighborhood. They were sisters Liliane and Valquíria Silva, aged 16 and 14, and their friend Kátia Xavier, 22. The creature was about seven meters away and had three protuberances on its head, slimy-looking skin and huge red eyes. ISTOÉ magazine reported it that way in May 1996, in a clipping included in the military file. Over the following days, the ufologists Ubirajara Franco Rodrigues and Vitório Pacaccini gathered anonymous accounts, some from alleged soldiers. According to those accounts, the fire brigade had captured a being that same Saturday morning, before the young women's sighting, after an anonymous call, the Army had taken part in a second capture, and the creatures had passed through city hospitals before being taken to Campinas (São Paulo). A. J. Gevaerd (Revista UFO) and Stanton Friedman also investigated the case.

The accounts attributed the transport to trucks from the Army Sergeants School (EsSA), the Army's school for noncommissioned officers in Três Corações. Military policeman Marco Eli Cherese, who worked in the P2, the Military Police's intelligence section, died in mid-February 1996. His sister publicly attributed his death to a virus caught from the creature. The military file reproduces the reports of a private laboratory, the Instituto de Propedêutica e Diagnóstico, which issued them at the request of the Varginha Legal Medical Institute. The autopsy concluded septic pulmonary thromboembolism, incipient myocarditis and acute hepatic congestion. Cultures grew Staphylococcus schleiferi from an armpit lesion and Enterobacter aerogenes from the lung: known bacteria with nothing exotic about them (the latter a common hospital germ; the former uncommon in humans). The HIV test was negative.

The commander of the Military Police's 24th Battalion testified that Cherese had a cyst under his left armpit, had it operated on and died of a severe hospital infection, and that he had not been officially involved in any incident with extraterrestrials. The family asked the police to investigate possible medical negligence. Lieutenant Colonel Olímpio Vanderlei Santos, head of the EsSA's Command and Services Battalion, whom the 1996 book "Incidente em Varginha" named as leader of the capture, denied any involvement to investigators and put the story down to revenge by some subordinate.

In May 1996, after a report on the TV program Fantástico, the EsSA opened an internal inquiry (sindicância). It reviewed the guard's vehicle exit sheets: there was no unusual movement from January 22 to 24, and on the 25th and 26th trucks went to Varginha for routine maintenance at the Mercedes-Benz dealer, with a purchase order and invoice. It concluded that the soldiers named by the press took part in no transport. The commander of the Varginha fire company testified in 1997 that he was off duty that Saturday, that his rules forbid answering a call without confirming it, and that the company's logbook shows no animal capture in that period.

The Military Police Inquiry (IPM) No. 18/97 was opened by the EsSA in January 1997 and did not investigate the sighting; it asked whether the book "Incidente em Varginha," by Vitório Pacaccini and Maxs Portes, committed a crime by attributing to soldiers the capture and transport of a being. It runs to 357 folios in two volumes, with the 1996 sindicância and a dozen 1997 statements from soldiers, the Military Police commander, the fire company commander and the two ufologists. Its report, dated April 11, 1997, judged it more likely that the young women mistook for a creature a local man with a mental disability, known as "Mudinho," seen crouching and dirty by a wall. It also acknowledged that the fire brigade's presence in Jardim Andere, the Army trucks near the dealer and an EsSA vehicle trip to Jaguariúna for horse fodder were real events, which the authors reinterpreted.

The file has gaps. Those who testified do not include the three young women, the ufologists' informants (whom the ufologists declined to identify) or anyone from the Regional or Humanitas hospitals, where the ufologists say the creature was taken. The Military Police commander himself admitted the description matches "Mudinho" "except for the eyes, the horns and other small details." The report assumes heavy rain that day, while the ISTOÉ clipping in the same file speaks of a sunny Saturday. At the request of the Military Prosecutor's Office, which called the authors' conduct unethical but not a crime, the judge of the 4th Military Judicial District archived the case in 1997: she found no sign of an offense and deemed the claims about the Army untrue.

In January 2026, thirty years after the events, the case returned to the spotlight. Brazil's Superior Tribunal Militar (STM) made the digitized IPM available, with no access restriction, in ARQUIMEDES, its archive; the press summarized it as a finding of "error and rumor," not of a recovered craft. On January 15, three members of the U.S. Congress held a closed-door session in the Longworth House Office Building in Washington with Brazilian witnesses.

The neurosurgeon Ítalo Venturelli (then a physician at Varginha's Regional Hospital) described having seen, in 1996, an entity with a teardrop-shaped skull and large lilac eyes, different from the Silva sisters' description (red eyes). He said he decided to speak in detail after a near-fatal heart attack, gave an exclusive interview to Ross Coulthart (NewsNation, November 2025) and appeared in two 2025 and 2026 documentaries. According to The Debrief, he was first shown a brief black-and-white video and then spent three or four minutes at the bedside; the colleague who, he says, first treated the being died in 2018, and no medical records were kept. He spoke out nearly thirty years after the events, and no other doctor has publicly corroborated his account; he says the others are afraid to speak.

On January 20, James Fox and Leslie Kean (the New York Times journalist who revealed the AATIP program in 2017) hosted a press conference at the National Press Club in Washington with first-hand Brazilian witnesses, medical experts and U.S. insiders. On June 9, the press conference of David Grusch, a whistleblower who testified under oath, and four lawmakers on the Capitol steps (↗) listed the Varginha records among those to be declassified and raised the hypothesis that the beings reported here were transported to the United States.

The claims that emerged in 2026 are three: that several beings were recovered, some dead and at least one perhaps alive; that medical personnel handled a non-human body; and that there were anomalies in tissue samples from soldier Cherese "that current medicine cannot explain." They remain testimony: no peer-reviewed medical paper, no documentation of a chain of custody for the hypothesized transfer to the U.S., and no Brazilian diplomatic cable has been published.

On July 7, 2026, Rep. Eric Burlison (a member of the Task Force on the Declassification of Federal Secrets) sent an oversight letter to CIA Director John Ratcliffe. It demanded that within 30 days the agency hand Congress the records of a Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) request about U.S. government flights and the transfer of material from Brazilian military custody in January 1996.

Why it matters

In Varginha, an account of beings generated first-hand official documents: an Army internal inquiry in 1996, a Military Police Inquiry in 1997 archived by the Military Justice and, within it, the autopsy of the policeman whose death fed the legend. Those papers support a conventional reading: confusion with a local man, trucks on routine maintenance, a hospital infection by known bacteria after surgery. But they were produced by the same institutions accused of a cover-up, in a proceeding that asked whether a book committed a crime. Against that coherent official record stand anonymous accounts that nobody could test.

The claims made in 2026 point to a transfer to the United States, and the CIA had already answered the request Burlison cites with a Glomar response: it neither confirms nor denies that the records exist, citing Executive Order 13526. Such a response does not indicate that records exist: the agency gives it when confirmation alone would be classified, and the request asked about the Brasilia station and its liaison with Brazilian services.

Capitol Press Conference · Grusch + lawmakers (Jun 9, 2026)

On June 9, 2026, on the Capitol steps, Representatives Eric Burlison, Jared Moskowitz, Anna Paulina Luna and Tim Burchett held a press conference with whistleblower David Grusch, hosted by journalist Leslie Kean and documentary maker James Fox.

They set out three demands: presidential declassification of files Grusch had identified by name, passage of the UAP Disclosure Act, and stronger whistleblower protections with international coordination. The lawmakers cited Varginha 1996 among the records to be declassified. Grusch said the government is aware of several kinds of non-human intelligence, up to what he called "sentient plasmoid life." The conference presented no new material evidence.
