17:T5a5,At about 08:15 on 23 November 1993, shortly before sunrise, two cars carrying personnel of Air Surveillance Squadron No. 10 (EVA-10) were climbing Mount Iroite, in the Barbanza range between the Noia and Arousa estuaries, where the radar station occupies the summit at some 680 metres. In the first car, stopped at the entrance control hut, were a warrant officer and two sergeants first class; some two or three hundred metres behind, coming out of a bend in the road, came the official car of the lieutenant colonel commanding the squadron, driven by a soldier. All five saw, for three or four seconds, what the lieutenant colonel described that same day as a 'trace at great speed with a green luminous nucleus, its own light and a trail with white-yellow ignition'. The sky was clear in the direction of the sighting, with cloud and morning haze to the east according to two witnesses; the commander wrote 'CAVOK' and the squadron's own automatic weather station recorded dead calm, about 7 °C and 59 % humidity. The file's table puts sunrise at 07:10 universal time, but an ephemeris calculation places it at about 07:36, and the station's pyranometer records no radiation until after 07:50: at the time of the sighting the Sun was still some four degrees below the horizon, the commander ticked 'twilight' and one of the witnesses wrote that 'the sun had not yet appeared above the mountains'; the Moon did not rise until early afternoon.18:T6ed,On 1 December the Air Operations Command asked the general commanding the Central Air Command (MACEN) to appoint an investigating officer and opened file 01-MACEN-93; on the 3rd a captain of EVA-10 itself was designated. The messages of those days date the sighting and the report to October, and the commander's own questionnaire, dated 23 November, writes 'month 10' in the box for the date of the event; it is an error the rest of the file corrects: the letter reached the Air Operations Command on 29 November, the weather table is for 23 November, the commander's second questionnaire, from December, says November, and on 23 October the Sun would already have risen at that hour. The captain took questionnaires from the five witnesses in mid-December. Those in the first car agree on the essentials: a sergeant in the back seat saw 'a green, egg-shaped body, followed by a sparkling white trail', which 'recalled a flare in its intensity and colours, though with sharper edges and no halo'; the driver, a supply sergeant, at first thought it was a vehicle's reflection on the windscreen and described the trail as 'like that of festival rockets'; the third, beside the driver, saw it come in from the left 'getting bigger each time', cross at an inclination of about 30° and vanish behind a rise, and described it as 'solid', phosphorescent green and cone-shaped. The soldier driving the commander's car compared it to a ball or a tadpole with a tail, one twentieth of the full Moon, and wrote that he thought it 'could be a meteorite', although its colour and direction made him doubt it.
