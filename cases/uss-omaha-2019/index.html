🇺🇸2019·Restricted military area off San Diego, California

United States.USS Omaha · crew films a sphere descending into the sea

On July 15, 2019, off San Diego, the combat information center of USS Omaha recorded on an infrared camera a sphere that flew over the sea, stopped, and went down to the water. A slide from a Navy briefing, released by filmmaker Jeremy Corbell, says the ship saw "a possible UAS," an unmanned aircraft, assessed that it had sunk, and found no wreckage. In May 2021 the Pentagon confirmed that Navy personnel filmed it, without explaining it. Analyst Mick West attributes it to a heat source going behind the horizon.

Record of the event

What happened

On July 15, 2019, in a restricted warning area off the coast of San Diego (California), the littoral combat ship USS Omaha and other U.S. Navy units recorded a series of unidentified spherical objects. From the Combat Information Center (CIC, the ship's command hub, where sensor data are brought together) the crew used a FLIR (forward-looking infrared) camera and night-vision devices to film a dark sphere moving over the sea, stopping, and slowly descending toward the water until it vanished, with no wake, prolonged splash or visible debris; the literature on these phenomena calls the air-to-water passage attributed to it "transmedium."

In the clip's audio the crew is heard narrating the incident in real time: "It took off. It's booking it" as the object crossed the screen, and "It splashed! It splashed!" as it went down. The operators tracked it live and did not interpret it afterwards. The released clip has a cut: about six minutes are missing between the first part and the second.

The episode was part of a wave of "sphere" incursions over several Navy ships off San Diego in July 2019 (USS Omaha, USS Russell, USS Kidd, USS Rafael Peralta, USS John Finn, among others). According to an intelligence briefing Corbell says he obtained, there were at least 14 solid objects about two meters across maneuvering around the formation, with speeds between 74 and 254 km/h (about 40 to over 130 knots). From those same days comes the so-called "pyramid" video, filmed in night vision from the USS Russell, in which camera optical artifacts may explain at least part of the perceived triangular shape. Corbell also says that a submarine searched the area and found nothing; that claim has no official confirmation.

A search does appear on the Navy slide Corbell released in April 2021, in a comment on the incident: the Omaha "observed a possible UAS, spherical in shape" (an unmanned aircraft system) moving toward the water and disappearing, the ship "assessed the object had sunk," and "Attempts to search the water for wreckage were ineffective." The comment does not say who searched or with what.

The material came to light in 2021, released by filmmaker Jeremy Corbell alongside investigative journalist George Knapp; according to Corbell, a still from the video had been shown at an Office of Naval Intelligence briefing on May 1, 2020, and the video was recorded off a monitor in the CIC. In April 2021 the Pentagon confirmed the authenticity of the "pyramid" video and the photos, and in May, after the Omaha video was released, its spokesperson Susan Gough stated: "I can confirm that the video was taken by Navy personnel," adding that its task force on these phenomena (UAP Task Force, UAPTF) had "included it in their ongoing examinations." The confirmation covered the footage's provenance and authenticity, not an explanation of its content: the origin was left undetermined.

On September 15, 2021, answering a Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) request from The Black Vault, an online archive of official documents obtained that way, the U.S. Navy reported that USS Omaha "did not retain or create records for July 1–18, 2019": the deck log of that class of ship is a digital voice recording, and neither the Naval History and Heritage Command nor the Federal Records Center holds records for those dates.

Why it matters

The Navy has a sensor record of the incident made by the crew itself: the infrared camera video from the Omaha's combat center, with other ships of the formation involved in the wave. And the Pentagon officially confirmed that the material is genuine and was under analysis by its task force. It happened during the years, between 2017 and 2021, when the Navy formalized procedures for reporting these phenomena, serious press (including the New York Times) treated the topic as a flight-safety matter, and Congress began demanding reports.

The official confirmation is of authenticity and provenance: the Pentagon validated that the Navy recorded something, but did not say what it was. The slide comment called it a "possible UAS," a term that, according to skeptical analyst Mick West, sometimes includes balloons. On the Metabunk forum on May 14, 2021, West proposed a weather or scientific balloon with a suspended payload, and dropped it the same day: with the wind from the north, it was "impossible it was a balloon moving north." He wrote: "After studying this, I think I'll flip to my #2. flare," a heat source that glare makes look larger. Earlier, by watching the object and the sea together, he had shown that the fast start the crew remarked on ("It's booking it") is the camera moving, not the object.

The same day he noted that at the end the blob fades over several frames, and in June he argued that the object does not enter the water: the blob shrinks uniformly, like the glare of a smaller hot object, "like a jet engine," going very slowly behind the horizon. West found no matching aircraft in flight-tracking records; he allowed that it could have been an untracked flight, such as a military one. Against that reading stand the crew's live narration, shouting that it had splashed, and the ship's own assessment that the object had sunk, followed by a search that found no wreckage. Skeptics also point to the sensor's low resolution and the difficulty of judging distance and scale from a single camera. That final descent, whose interpretation is disputed, is what keeps the case open.
