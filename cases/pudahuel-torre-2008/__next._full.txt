2d:T8cc,On 10 March 2010, the head of CEFAA, Ricardo Bermúdez, and its executive secretary, Gustavo Rodríguez, visited the tower and interviewed the staff on duty that day. They saw a great many plastic bags caught on the fence protecting the perimeter of runway 17R. According to SEFAA's published report, the meteorologist described a 'fairly small object', 'not very striking to the naked eye', that 'reflected the sunlight', northeast of the tower at an elevation of about 45°. To the naked eye it looked rigid and geometric, but through binoculars 'it looked rather amorphous, it did not look like a pyramid'. In the roughly 20 minutes they watched it, it drifted north, 'very slowly', growing smaller and dropping in elevation, until it could no longer be seen. He himself suggested that, if it was debris, it might have been falling, and added that at the airport 'there is packaging material that looks aluminised, there are bags that blow away and can be seen up there and draw attention'. The tower shift supervisor told a different story: a small, solid, silvery object, which he associated with a radiosonde balloon, that 'started to rise from the ground' and stayed at about 200 feet 'for a good while', in all 'at least 3 minutes'. He saw it as round, 'not so much a triangle, as my colleagues saw it, my female colleagues', 1.5 to 2 metres across and between 500 metres and a kilometre away. He said the weather office and the rescue and firefighting service ruled out a radiosonde balloon or a bird-scaring device, and that there was no radar record, 'not even as a primary track'. That morning's weather reports gave clear skies, visibility of at least 10 kilometres and a light surface wind; higher up, the wind came from the southeast, at up to 8 knots, up to 172 metres, and from the south-southeast and south, at up to 14 knots, between 172 and about 830 metres. In 2010 the committee concluded that it was not an anomalous phenomenon as such, 'given that it was observed taking off from taxiway Delta, that is, from inside the airport', and added that along the runway perimeter fence, on its 'west side', a great many plastic bags piled up that 'from time to time are lifted by the prevailing wind'.
