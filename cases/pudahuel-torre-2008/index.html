Chile.Pudahuel · the silvery object over Santiago airport

On the morning of October 17, 2008, control tower staff at Santiago airport and a meteorologist saw a small silvery object over the airfield that reflected the sunlight; they watched it for about 20 minutes as it drifted slowly north, and through binoculars it looked amorphous to the meteorologist. Chile's official committee for these phenomena, then called CEFAA, interviewed the witnesses in 2010, found the runway fence full of plastic bags and attributed it to a bag lifted by the wind. A ufology group asked for the case to be reopened in 2018, and in 2022 the committee, by then called SEFAA, upheld that conclusion.

What happened

On Friday, October 17, 2008, between 10 and 11 in the morning, control tower staff at Arturo Merino Benítez International Airport, in Pudahuel (Santiago), saw something over the airfield and phoned the weather office to ask whether they had launched a balloon. They had not, and the meteorologists went outside to look. That morning's weather reports gave clear skies, visibility of at least 10 kilometers and a light surface wind; higher up, the wind came from the southeast, at up to 8 knots, up to 172 meters, and from the south-southeast and south, at up to 14 knots, between 172 and about 830 meters.

What they saw is known from the interviews the official committee carried out a year and a half later. According to SEFAA's published report, the meteorologist described a "fairly small object", "not very striking to the naked eye", that "reflected the sunlight", northeast of the tower and about 45° above the horizon. To the naked eye it looked rigid and geometric, but through binoculars "it looked rather amorphous, it did not look like a pyramid". In the roughly 20 minutes they watched it, it drifted north, "very slowly", growing smaller and dropping lower, until it could no longer be seen. He himself suggested that, if it was debris, it might have been falling, and added that at the airport "there is packaging material that looks aluminized, there are bags that blow away and can be seen up there and draw attention".

The tower shift supervisor told a different story: a small, solid, silvery object, which he associated with a radiosonde (a balloon carrying weather instruments), that "started to rise from the ground" and stayed at about 200 feet "for a good while", in all "at least 3 minutes". He saw it as round, "not so much a triangle, as my colleagues saw it, my female colleagues", 1.5 to 2 meters across and between 500 meters and a kilometer away. He said the weather office and the rescue and firefighting service ruled out a radiosonde or a bird-scaring device, and that the radar recorded nothing, "not even as a primary track" (the echo returned by the object itself).

The case reached the official office from outside. An email from an airport meteorologist ended up with a ufology group, CIFAE Chile: according to it, several people watched an "anomalous element" over the airport for half an hour, and one of them filmed it on his cell phone; the group said it had that video, "including the audio". Its representative asked the Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGAC) for information through the Office of Information, Complaints and Suggestions (OIRS), and the General Secretariat ordered a reply. It came from CEFAA, the DGAC committee that studied anomalous aerial phenomena, in a 2010 record that lists "No. of witnesses: 02".

On March 10, 2010, the head of CEFAA, Ricardo Bermúdez, and its executive secretary, Gustavo Rodríguez, visited the tower and interviewed the staff on duty that day. They saw a great many plastic bags caught on the fence protecting the perimeter of runway 17R. The committee concluded that it was not an anomalous phenomenon as such, "given that it was observed taking off from taxiway Delta, that is, from inside the airport", and added that along that fence, on its "west side", plastic bags piled up that "from time to time are lifted by the prevailing wind". It noted: "Resolved: plastic bag".

On May 6, 2018, the director of CIFAE asked for the "UFO CASE PUDAUEL (sic) AIRPORT 2008" to be reopened. The committee, by then called SEFAA, re-examined the file without being able to obtain the meteorologist's original images, and on October 12, 2022 concluded that there was no reason to change the 2010 conclusion: it was not an anomalous phenomenon, but "an inert body carried by the wind, very possibly some kind of garbage bag". The header of the case report classifies it as "Resolved"; the publication report says it was closed as "Probably explained".

Why it matters

Professional witnesses at an airport watched for minutes an object they could not identify, and the official committee found the most likely explanation on the runway fence. The bag fits several facts. According to the 2010 record, the object was seen taking off from taxiway Delta, inside the airport, and plastic bags that the wind lifts piled up on the west side of the fence. The upper wind came from the southeast and south, so it pushed toward the north and northwest, and the object drifted north, although the meteorologist placed it northeast of the tower. According to a calculation for this record, the Sun at that hour stood about 42° high to the east-northeast, close to where the meteorologist saw the object, which fits something small that "reflected the sunlight". Through binoculars it looked amorphous, and the meteorologist himself mentioned the bags and the aluminized packaging that the wind lifts at the airport.

Against it stands the supervisor's account: a "solid" object, 1.5 to 2 meters across, that rose from the ground and hung at about 200 feet "for a good while". The two testimonies do not describe the same thing: the meteorologist speaks of an object high up, to the northeast, watched for about 20 minutes and amorphous through binoculars; the supervisor, of a round one that stayed still for some three minutes, and he mentions female colleagues who saw it as triangular, whose accounts the report does not include. The interviews took place almost a year and a half later, and neither the video the ufology group said it had nor the meteorologist's photograph or recording reached the office.

The two SEFAA documents do not fully agree either: they attribute the first quotation to different people (the case report to a controller, the publication report to the meteorologist), the publication report's header dates the event to 2018 instead of 2008, and the classification shifts from "Resolved" to "Probably explained". None of this points to anything other than a light object carried by the wind, but it explains the "very possibly" in the conclusion: nobody recovered the object and there is no image showing it.
