Chile.Coyhaique · a Carabineros pilot sees a blue-green light

At around 20:00 on April 20, 2020, the pilot of a Carabineros Air Prefecture aircraft flying from Punta Arenas to Puerto Montt, at about 28,000 feet, saw abeam Coyhaique a large, oval, blue-green light that stayed above and in front of his aircraft; he took it for an airliner until it moved and came closer, and it then disappeared toward the northwest. SEFAA, Chile's official office for these phenomena, identified the flight from the flight plans, but the pilot stopped cooperating and handed over no images, and the case was closed as a "Report" (Relato) with no cause proposed.

What happened

On Monday, April 20, 2020, a 36-year-old pilot, identified in the report only by his initials, took off from Carlos Ibáñez del Campo airport in Punta Arenas bound for El Tepual airport in Puerto Montt. According to his report to what was then CEFAA, at around 20:00 local time, while flying at about 28,000 feet "abeam" Teniente Vidal aerodrome in Coyhaique, in the Aysén region, "a large light stayed above, in front of my aircraft; I thought it was an airliner, but as it moved and came closer it clearly was not". He described it as oval, blue-green in color, and placed it 1,000 meters from his aircraft. He could not say where it came from, but said it disappeared toward the northwest. The report records no weather ("N/A") and no other witnesses.

The pilot added a detail that the report records without comment: "Later that same light, on an outing with my son to Puerto Varas, appeared in some photos taken of Lake Llanquihue". Those photos never reached the office.

SEFAA, the section of Chile's Directorate General of Civil Aviation that studies these reports, began by establishing which aircraft it was. According to the flight-plan register, only two aircraft flew from Punta Arenas to Puerto Montt that day. The passenger manifest of one of them, a Piper Cheyenne belonging to the air-ambulance company Aerotac, showed a pilot and co-pilot whose names did not match the informant's; by elimination, the report came from flight C62 of the Air Prefecture of Carabineros de Chile, the national police. The report does not say what type of aircraft C62 was or whether it carried other crew or passengers.

The office asked the pilot for more details of the flight and any images he had of what he described. In an email of May 5, 2020 he replied that he found "the procedure for sending the information cumbersome" and that, although he had "had several episodes" with anomalous phenomena, he did not want to "get involved" in the subject, ending with "I'll leave it here". From May 8, 2020 the file was investigated by the director of what was then CEFAA. The user, the report notes, provided no material for study. After more than a year and ten months with no further communication, SEFAA closed the investigation under paragraph 2d of its technical procedure, which concerns supporting audiovisual material, because there was nothing that allowed it to go beyond the account, and classified the case as a "Report" (Relato). The report proposes no cause and does not mention any review of that night's sky, of radar or of visible satellites.

Why it matters

The whole case rests on a single short account by a single crew member, with no images, no radar data in the report and no other recorded witnesses, and its author declined to expand on it. SEFAA identified the flight by cross-checking flight plans and passenger manifests and inferred, by elimination, that the report came from Carabineros flight C62, which flew that route that day. That the pilot said he had had "several episodes" with anomalous phenomena, and that he later saw "that same light" in photos of Lake Llanquihue, some 480 kilometers to the north and on another occasion, are details of his own account that the report could not check: the photos were never handed over.

SEFAA proposed no cause. By this case's own calculation, not SEFAA's, made with the PyEphem ephemeris software for an observer at 8,500 meters above Coyhaique, Venus was to the northwest. If 20:00 was Aysén time (UTC−4, the same as Santiago in April 2020), its azimuth was about 305 to 307 degrees and it was setting: at 19:50 its geometric altitude was about −2.6 degrees and at 20:00 about −4.0. From 8,500 meters the apparent horizon lies about 3 degrees below the horizontal, and refraction along a grazing ray lifts objects a little further, so Venus was sinking behind that horizon at around 20:00, ahead and to the left of an aircraft heading almost due north. If the pilot gave Punta Arenas time (UTC−3), it was 19:00 in Aysén and Venus stood about 4 degrees above the horizontal, some 7 above the horizon visible from that height, also to the northwest. It was shining at magnitude −4.4, close to its peak brightness of late April 2020; the Sun was between 11 and 21 degrees below the horizon and the Moon, almost new, had not risen.

That coincidence identifies nothing, and the account itself has details that do not fit it. The pilot said the light was "above" and "in front of" the aircraft, not low on the horizon and off to one side; that it "moved and came closer"; that it was 1,000 meters away, a distance a witness cannot measure at night for a light with no reference points; and that it was oval and blue-green. The time is also approximate, and the report gives neither the aircraft's heading nor its exact position.
