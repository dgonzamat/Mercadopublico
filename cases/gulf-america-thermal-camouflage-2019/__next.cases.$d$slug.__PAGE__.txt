Reviewed frame by frame, the clip shows something the description does not say: the sensor points down at the sea surface (the swell is visible, with a north arrow in the upper left corner), and between seconds 1 and 4 a dark mass, which looks like low cloud or a smoke plume, crosses the lower right of the frame. The area of contrast next to the reticle is barely perceptible at normal resolution, and in the last second the image saturates to white, which matches the 'no content' segment.19:T405,What sets this case apart is an additional paragraph AARO attached under the heading 'AARO Comment,' a format that appears in only a minority of PURSUE records. The comment explains the physical mechanism behind the observed behavior: 'Infrared sensor systems capture information within a specific range of electromagnetic wavelengths. When a tracked source's temperature is similar to that of the surrounding environment, it can visually blend into the background or appear to flicker due to dynamic contrast adjustments applied by the system's auto-gain control filters.' In other words: AARO does not merely describe the object as 'flickering': it explains why an infrared sensor produces that effect when an object's thermal signature sits near the equipment's own detection threshold. The scope of the comment should be stated precisely: it is a general explanation of how an infrared sensor behaves, not an analysis of this specific target.
