🌍2013·Middle East (exact location undisclosed)

Middle East, CENTCOM area (exact country undisclosed; platform and release are U.S.).Middle East · a sensor records an eight-pointed star shape

In 2013, an infrared sensor on a U.S. military platform in the Middle East recorded for one minute and 46 seconds an area of contrast that the Pentagon office for these phenomena (AARO) describes as “an eight-pointed star with arms of alternating length.” It leaves a trail as it moves, exits the frame and, after an apparent cut in the video, reappears. Whoever recorded it left no description: everything comes from AARO's reading of the video, released in 2026. The official data index says “resolved as an aircraft” and the video's record “unresolved”; analyst Mick West attributed the star shape to sensor diffraction.

Record of the event

What happened

In 2013, on a date that has not been released, an infrared sensor aboard a U.S. military platform recorded one minute and 46 seconds of video in the area of U.S. Central Command (CENTCOM), which covers the Middle East and Central Asia. The Command sent the report to the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO), the Pentagon office that studies these phenomena. Whoever recorded it left no oral or written description of what they saw, and AARO notes this in the video's record. Everything known about the event comes from the reading the office made of the recording while preparing it for release. The video was released on May 8, 2026, in the first release of PURSUE, the U.S. government program that publishes its files on these phenomena, more than a decade after it was recorded and with no coordinates or platform identification.

That timestamped reading describes the shape in detail: an area of contrast "resembling an eight-pointed star with arms of alternating length," with four longer points and four shorter ones, alternating, rather than a regular star. At second 10, the sensor narrows its field of view to zoom in. Between seconds 11 and 29, the pattern moves within the image and leaves a visible trail. At second 30 it exits through the bottom right corner. After what AARO describes as "an apparent cut" in the video, the pattern reappears; from second 35 to 1:44 it stays mostly within the image, until it exits through the top left quarter of the screen.

The official record contradicts itself. The data file accompanying the release on war.gov, the Department of War's website (the Pentagon), titles the video "Resolved as an Aircraft, Middle East 2013," while the record on DVIDS, the Pentagon's media portal, and the video page call it "Unresolved UAP Report"; analyst Mick West pointed this out on the Metabunk forum on May 12, 2026. The file carries only that label, without saying what aircraft it was or who identified it, so it is a label and not an explanation.

The Metabunk thread, titled "The 'Chandelier' UFO," a nickname for the object, was opened in January 2024, when an image of the same object circulated, and in it West had already described it as a sensor diffraction effect around a small heat source ("a diffraction artifact - i.e. glare around a bright light"). In May 2026 another participant proposed a parachute flare, an idea others disputed.

Why it matters

AARO describes a very specific shape, eight points of alternating length, and that is why it draws attention. But without any witness, there is no way to know how far away the recorded object was, how large it was or whether anyone also saw it with the naked eye.

The cut that AARO itself acknowledges midway through the video indicates that what was released may not be one continuous take and may have gone through some editing or compiling before reaching the archive. Without knowing who made the cut or why, it cannot be ruled out that part of the pattern's behavior (moving, disappearing, reappearing) comes from the edit rather than from a single object in continuous flight.

If the star shape is diffraction, as West proposed, the points are drawn by the sensor's optics around an intense heat source and do not describe the shape of what was recorded. The trail it leaves as it moves fits either a moving sensor effect or a real object whose heat source leaves a trace behind it.

Without telemetry or a second sensor confirming the shape, none of these readings can be confirmed or ruled out with what is available.
