🇫🇷1967·Cussac, Cantal (France)

France.Cussac · two children see a sphere and four black figures

On August 29, 1967, in Cussac (Cantal, France), two shepherd children, François Delpeuch (13) and his sister Anne-Marie (9), said they saw a bright sphere (two to five meters across) and four black figures that climbed into it before it took off with a whistle. Gendarmes came that afternoon but drew up no report; in 1968 the gendarmerie confirmed they noticed a sulfurous smell, and one of them spoke in 1978 of a patch of yellowed grass. In 1978 GEPAN, a unit of the French space agency, reinvestigated and concluded in a confidential internal report that the children had seen an unknown vehicle; the skeptical reading is a helicopter.

What happened

On the morning of August 29, 1967, François Delpeuch, thirteen, and his sister Anne-Marie, nine, were tending the family's cattle beside a road in Cussac, in the rural department of Cantal, in central France, with their dog Médor. They said they saw, beyond a wall, a bright sphere, intense silver in color, and next to it four small figures "completely black," with a glossy look François compared to silk. One of the figures seemed bent over something on the ground; another held an object that shone like a mirror. The size varies with the source: the newspaper La Montagne, in 1967, described a craft four meters wide and two high; later accounts spoke of a sphere two to five meters across.

According to the children's account to the gendarmerie, the figures suddenly rose and entered the sphere (one of them, they said, seemed to draw the others inside), and the object ascended spinning on itself with a sharp whistle, leaving behind a sulfurous smell. The children ran to raise the alarm, and their father, the village mayor, called the gendarmerie.

Gendarmes from the Saint-Flour and Neuvéglise brigades arrived around four in the afternoon, made some brief checks and did not consider it necessary to draw up a report. According to GEPAN, they concluded that the sighting was real and probably involved a foreign military craft; a 2016 study of the GEPAN files adds that they had first leaned toward a helicopter. The La Montagne correspondent said at the time that no traces were found, and the local press described the figures as "little devils."

In 1968 the investigators Joël Mesnard and Claude Pavy interviewed the children separately and unannounced. They did not contradict each other, and the investigators recorded that François had watering eyes for several days. In some versions the sphere rested on legs or landing gear with rounded "feet"; according to Mesnard and Pavy, only Anne-Marie saw them, and François, who did not, thought his sister had taken the branches of the hedge for legs. They also learned that a neighbor, Mr. Delcher, who was unloading hay in his barn, heard the whistling as the sphere rose, and they wrote that the gendarmerie confirmed to them that the officers who arrived at four had noticed the sulfurous smell, carried by the west wind according to the children.

More than a decade later, GEPAN, the official unit of the French space agency (CNES) for the study of unidentified aerospace phenomena, reopened the case. In April 1978, four people led by Claude Poher interviewed François and Anne-Marie again, now adults, at their home and at the site, reformulating questions and cross-checking versions to test the account's consistency. They found no major contradictions or signs of fabrication.

One of the gendarmes then stated that he had noticed a strong odor that afternoon and a circular patch of yellowed grass, but GEPAN did not take that "trace" into account: in a test with Pantone color samples, the colors the gendarme picked had little contrast. The internal report, kept confidential at the request of the agency's scientific council, concluded that the witnesses had seen "the hasty departure of a completely unknown inhabited vehicle." GEPAN itself wrote that the only phenomenon that seemed to explain every aspect, including the four occupants, would be a helicopter, but ruled it out because of the object's shape, color and brightness.

Researcher Thierry Pinvidic carried out his counter-investigation between 1983 and 1985 with two other ufologists, Bertrand Méheust and Jean-Pierre Grangeon. According to the 2016 study, the three questioned some details of the event but could not agree on its nature; after interviewing François, they wrote that the physical reality of the observation did not seem established to them. The report, signed by Pinvidic alone, was published in 1993. In 1996 Éric Maillot, of the ufology association CNEGU, argued for the helicopter hypothesis: the only craft known in 1967 with a comparable appearance, whistle, vertical takeoff and flight behavior is a helicopter, whose bubble canopy would have reflected the sun behind the children.

GEPAN's assessment was cited in the COMETA report, the 1999 document signed by former senior French officials, and Poher, who had led the 1978 investigation, rejected the helicopter again in 2004.

The sources do not give a single version of the smell. According to Pinvidic's counter-investigation, François noticed a very fleeting smell around half past ten in the morning and a gendarme an acrid one around four in the afternoon. The rural constable told GEPAN that around eight in the evening, in a meadow more than five hundred meters from the site, a smell so strong nearly suffocated him; the 2016 study objects that such a smell had not bothered the children, the mayor or the gendarmes who had been there earlier, and recalls that a farmer had spread manure near the site that morning. To pin down the substance, GEPAN asked François, Anne-Marie, the gendarme and the rural constable to pick the smell out among four (sulfur dioxide, nitrogen dioxide, kerosene and acetone); the answers varied and none of the four really matched the smell they remembered.

Why it matters

Two children said they saw a sphere and four black figures climb into it, and their account passed through two official French filters, the gendarmes and GEPAN. Most accounts of humanoid figures never reach a state body; this one reached two.

What GEPAN checked was the coherence of the testimony, not the existence of the object. An account without contradictions, held for years by its two protagonists, points to sincerity or a firm memory, but does not prove that four black beings were there. The witnesses were minors, the account has a folkloric tone ("little black devils") that connects with the religious imagination of the countryside, and there is no instrumental record; suggestion, perceptual error and a story built between the two of them remain ordinary explanations.

The only physical traces claimed, the smell and the patch of yellowed grass, were not preserved for analysis. The smell was described differently depending on the hour and the witness, and the grass appeared in a 1978 statement that GEPAN itself did not take into account.
