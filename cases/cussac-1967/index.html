🇫🇷1967·Cussac, Cantal (France)

France.Cussac · two children see a sphere and four black figures

On August 29, 1967, in Cussac (Cantal, France), two shepherd children, François Delpeuch (13) and his sister Anne-Marie (9), said they saw a bright sphere about two meters across and four small black figures that climbed into it before it took off with a whistle. Gendarmes came that afternoon but drew up no report; in 1968 the gendarmerie confirmed they noticed a sulfurous smell, and one of them spoke in 1978 of a patch of yellowed grass. In 1978 GEPAN, the official French agency, reinvestigated and concluded in a confidential internal report that the children had seen an unknown vehicle; the skeptical reading is a helicopter.

What happened

On the morning of August 29, 1967, François Delpeuch, thirteen, and his sister Anne-Marie, nine, were tending the family's cattle beside a road in Cussac, in the rural department of Cantal, in central France, with their dog Médor. They said they saw, beyond a wall, a bright sphere, intense silver in color, and next to it four small figures "completely black", with a glossy look François compared to silk. One of the figures seemed bent over something on the ground; another held an object that shone like a mirror. The size varies with the source: the newspaper La Montagne, in 1967, described a craft four meters wide and two high; later accounts spoke of a sphere two to five meters across.

According to the children's account to the gendarmerie, the figures suddenly rose and entered the sphere (one of them, they said, seemed to draw the others inside), and the object ascended spinning on itself with a sharp whistle, leaving behind a sulfurous smell. The children ran to raise the alarm, and their father, the village mayor, called the gendarmerie. Gendarmes from the Saint-Flour and Neuvéglise brigades arrived around four in the afternoon, made some brief checks and did not consider it necessary to draw up a report. According to GEPAN, they concluded that the sighting was real and probably involved a foreign military craft; a 2016 study of the GEPAN files adds that they had first leaned toward a helicopter. The La Montagne correspondent said at the time that no traces were found, and the local press described the figures as "little devils".

In 1968 the investigators Joël Mesnard and Claude Pavy interviewed the children separately and unannounced. They did not contradict each other, and the investigators recorded that François had watering eyes for several days. In some versions the sphere rested on legs or landing gear with rounded "feet"; according to Mesnard and Pavy, only Anne-Marie saw them, and François, who did not, thought his sister had taken the branches of the hedge for legs. They also learned that a neighbor, Mr. Delcher, who was unloading hay in his barn, heard the whistling as the sphere rose, and they wrote that the gendarmerie confirmed to them that the officers who arrived at four had noticed the sulfurous smell, carried by the west wind according to the children.

The sources do not give a single version of the smell. The 2016 study, drawing on Pinvidic, records that François noticed a very fleeting smell around half past ten in the morning and that a gendarme noticed an acrid smell around four in the afternoon. The rural constable told GEPAN that around eight in the evening, more than ten hours later, in a meadow at the edge of the village more than five hundred meters from the site, he was hit by a smell so strong that it nearly suffocated him; the study objects that such a smell had not bothered the children, the mayor or the gendarmes who had been there earlier, and recalls that a farmer had spread manure near the site that morning.

More than a decade later, GEPAN, the official unit of the French space agency (CNES) for the study of unidentified aerospace phenomena, reopened the case. Around 1978 its investigators interviewed François and Anne-Marie again, now adults, at their home and at the site, reformulating questions and cross-checking versions to test the account's consistency. They found no major contradictions or signs of fabrication. One of the gendarmes then stated that he had noticed a strong odor that afternoon and a circular patch of yellowed grass, but GEPAN did not take that "trace" into account because of the low contrast of the colors the gendarme picked in a color-chart test. The internal report, kept confidential at the request of the agency's scientific council, concluded that the witnesses had seen "the hasty departure of a completely unknown inhabited vehicle". GEPAN itself wrote that the only phenomenon that seemed to explain every aspect, including the four occupants, would be a helicopter, but ruled it out because of the object's shape, color and brightness.

In 1993 Thierry Pinvidic signed alone a report on the counter-investigation he had carried out with Méheust and Grangeon, and judged the helicopter hypothesis "indefensible". From 1996 Éric Maillot of the Cercle Zététique argued for it: the only craft known in 1967 with a comparable appearance, whistle, vertical takeoff and flight behavior is a helicopter, whose bubble canopy would have reflected the sun behind the children. GEPAN's assessment was cited in the COMETA report, the 1999 document signed by former senior French officials. Claude Poher rejected the helicopter again in 2004, and the 2016 study of the GEPAN files developed Maillot's thesis; according to that study, GEPAN, Poher and Pinvidic did not agree on the nature of the event.

Why it matters

Two children said they saw a sphere and four black figures climb into it, and their account passed through two official French filters. Gendarmes came the same day, although they drew up no report, and some eleven years later GEPAN, within the space agency, interviewed them again, reconstructed the scene on site and concluded that they had seen an unknown vehicle. Most accounts of humanoid figures never reach a state body; this one reached two.

What GEPAN checked was the coherence of the testimony, not the existence of the object. An account without contradictions, held for years by its two protagonists, points to sincerity or a firm memory, but does not prove that four black beings were there. The witnesses were minors, the account has a folkloric tone ("little black devils") that connects with the religious imagination of the countryside, and there is no instrumental record; suggestion, perceptual error and a story built between the two of them remain ordinary explanations.

The only physical traces claimed, the smell and the patch of yellowed grass, were not preserved for analysis. The smell was described differently depending on the hour and the witness, and according to the 2016 study none of the four substances in the test GEPAN ran eleven years later truly matched what they remembered; the grass appeared in a 1978 statement that GEPAN itself did not accept. According to Maillot, the whistle, the vertical takeoff and the flight fit a helicopter, but the shape, color and brightness the children described were what led GEPAN to rule it out.
