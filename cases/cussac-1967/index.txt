29:T464,On the morning of 29 August 1967, François Delpeuch, thirteen, and his sister Anne-Marie, nine, were tending the family's cattle beside a road in Cussac, in the rural department of Cantal, in central France. Accompanied by their dog Médor, they said they saw, beyond a wall, a bright sphere, intense silver in color (La Montagne, in 1967, described it as a craft four meters wide and two high; later accounts spoke of a sphere two to five meters across, depending on the source), and next to it four small figures 'completely black', with a glossy look François compared to silk. One of the figures seemed bent over something on the ground; another held a mirror-like object. In some versions of the account the sphere rested on legs or landing gear with rounded 'feet'; according to Mesnard and Pavy, who interviewed the children separately and unannounced in 1968, only Anne-Marie saw them, and François, who did not, thought his sister had taken the branches of the hedge for legs. The same investigators recorded that a neighbour, Mr Delcher, who was shifting hay in his barn, heard the whistling as the sphere rose.2a:T619,The institutional response is what gives the case weight, and it is worth breaking down because it happens in two stages. First, gendarmes from the Saint-Flour and Neuvéglise brigades came to the site around four in the afternoon, made some brief checks and did not consider it necessary to draw up a report, although, according to GEPAN, they concluded the sighting was real and probably involved a foreign military craft; one of the gendarmes stated in 1978 that he had noticed a strong odor and a circular patch of yellowed grass, although the La Montagne correspondent said at the time that no traces were found, and GEPAN's own report notes that the low contrast of the colours that gendarme picked in the colour-chart test does not allow the 'trace' to be taken into account. Second, and more than a decade later, GEPAN —the official agency for the study of unidentified aerospace phenomena, part of CNES, the French space agency— reopened the case. Around 1978, GEPAN investigators interviewed François and Anne-Marie again, now adults, at their home and at the incident site, reformulating questions and cross-checking versions to test the account's consistency. The reconstruction found no major contradictions or signs of fabrication, and the internal report, kept confidential at the request of the agency's scientific council, concluded that the witnesses had seen 'the hasty departure of a completely unknown inhabited vehicle'.
