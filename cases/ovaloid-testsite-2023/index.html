United States.Military test range · two contractors see a bronze-colored object

In September 2023, at a U.S. military test site whose airspace had been closed for laser (LiDAR) trials, a contractor with fifteen years on military installations, who had closed the airspace herself that day, and a contractor with experience as a drone pilot saw a bronze-colored metallic object for a few seconds before it vanished. According to the description of the graphic the FBI produced, it was 40 to 60 meters long and emerged from a bright light. The location remains classified, and the case was released in the U.S. government's first batch of files, in May 2026.

Record of the event

What happened

In September 2023, a team of contractors was working with LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging, a laser distance-measuring technology) at a U.S. military test site whose airspace had been expressly closed for the trials. That day, the person in charge of managing and closing that airspace was a contractor with fifteen years of experience on military installations. According to the FD-302 form of her FBI interview, between 7:15 and 7:30 a.m. she was driving the first of three vehicles carrying contractors toward the site of some drone tests.

As she passed through an access gate that had failed three times before opening, she looked up and saw, to the southwest, a cigar-shaped object with an extremely bright light, 500 to 3,000 feet above the nearest treeline, a mile away. The object was already there, almost hovering, moving slowly from east to west. The light, an intense diamond white with a ring around it, was on its eastern end, pointed southeast, and looking at it "was like looking into the sun." She described it as "metallic bronze in color," the length of two or three Black Hawk helicopters nose to tail and the width of one and a half, and completely silent. At first she was annoyed, because she had restricted the airspace; then she realized the object "was not an aircraft or drone." They watched it for five to ten seconds and the object "just disappeared." The sky was clear, no one took photos or video, and she said she would not have reported it had she seen it alone.

She was not the only one who saw it. According to the FBI forms, another contractor, with ten to fifteen hours as a drone pilot, described a linear object, metallic or gray, with a super bright light on its east side, no wings or exhaust, smaller than a Boeing 737 but one to two Black Hawks long and "definitely bigger than a drone," about 5,000 feet up, moving east to west parallel to the ground. After five to ten seconds the light went out and the object vanished; he noted that the sun was in the east and visibility was good.

According to the main witness, a passenger in the second vehicle said he saw it too. Another of those present tried unsuccessfully to get a colleague to see it. In a third FBI interview, a contractor driving another of the vehicles saw only a bright white light over the horizon, stationary and then moving to the right, for about ten seconds and always the same size, and put it ten to twenty miles away. That interview places the drive at around 9:00 a.m., not between 7:15 and 7:30, and records that one of those present thought the light might have been a meteor coming straight toward them and burning up in the atmosphere. The names are redacted in the documents, and so is the place: the state and the name of the test site have not been released.

From the testimony, the FBI produced a composite sketch of the object above the treeline and a graphic that, according to the published description, shows a bronze-colored metallic ellipsoid 130 to 195 feet long (about 40 to 60 meters), which is said to have materialized out of a bright light in the sky and disappeared instantly. The forms, the sketch and the graphic were released on May 8, 2026, on the war.gov/ufo portal, in the first release of documents from the PURSUE system (Presidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP Encounters), along with nearly 200 declassified files.

Why it matters

Two contractors working at a military test site with closed airspace separately described the same object to the FBI, and one of them was the person who had closed that airspace that morning. Their accounts agree on the essentials (an elongated metallic object, with a very bright light on its east end, moving east to west and vanishing after five to ten seconds) and differ on color, size and height: bronze or gray, two or three Black Hawks long or one to two, 500 to 3,000 feet above the trees or about 5,000 feet up.

By this entry's own reasoning, not any source's, the location cuts both ways. Airspace closed for trials makes ordinary civilian traffic (light aircraft, balloons, recreational drones) unlikely, but a military test site is also where aircraft that are not publicly acknowledged are tested. And because the location is still redacted, nothing can be checked against flight or test records.

No instrument recorded the object, even though the site was set up for instrumented tests: what was released is a sketch and a graphic made from descriptions, and the sighting lasted five to ten seconds. The graphic's most striking features, the appearance out of a light and the instant disappearance, do not fully match the interviews: the main witness told the FBI the object was already there when she looked up, and the third contractor saw only a bright light. By the same reasoning, a very bright point can give the impression of appearing and vanishing all at once.
