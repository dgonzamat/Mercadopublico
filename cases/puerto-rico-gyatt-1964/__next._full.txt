2f:T4ff,On November 19, 1964, the guided-missile destroyer USS Gyatt (working with a Navy F-8 Crusader fighter at 35,000 feet inside the Atlantic Fleet Weapons Range) detected a high-speed, high-altitude object on radar. The object slowed and held position at the Crusader's nine o'clock, about nine miles out. The pilot selected afterburner and tried to close the distance; at about five miles, the object accelerated extremely in a 20-degree climb and pulled away rapidly. It remained on the Gyatt's radar for twenty-one minutes, and as the Crusader returned to Roosevelt Roads Naval Air Station, it closed again from astern to four miles. The pilot, under full moonlight and good visibility, described the object as delta-shaped, about the size of a jet fighter. The Gyatt's radar (an AN/SPS-49 undergoing an evaluation program, which is why a camera was recording the scope) registered, according to the initial report, a track in excess of 3,800 knots; the later analysis of the radar photographs, however, gave the object a course of about 165° relative to the Gyatt and a relative speed of about 485 knots. Five days later, on November 24, another Crusader flying at Mach .99 at 45,000 feet was rapidly left behind by a high-speed, high-altitude target held by the weapons range.30:T763,The case reached the highest levels of the CIA: briefing notes prepared for Walter N. Elder, special assistant to Director John McCone, summarize the episode with the same figures (speed, altitude, behavior) and record that the Office of Naval Intelligence (ONI) had no further information and asked to be notified of similar incidents going forward. A first memo of 1 February 1965, from the Assistant Director for Special Activities (the office running the A-12), records that the commanding officer at Ramey had identified the UFO as a YF-12A and that, through that office's efforts, Naval Intelligence was 'finally convinced' it was not a YF-12A or similar category Lockheed aircraft; it also asked the Office of Scientific Intelligence to analyse the original radar film. The reply memo, dated February 11, 1965 and handled under BYEMAN control channels with access restricted to Project OXCART (the A-12 spy plane program, predecessor to the SR-71) reviewed the radar photography and associated correspondence and reached a different conclusion from the initial reports: that there was 'in fact' no high-altitude ultra-high-speed UFO north of Puerto Rico in the 18-24 November period, but 'apparently valid tracks and/or observations of unidentified aircraft' on 16, 17, 18, 19 and 24 November.
