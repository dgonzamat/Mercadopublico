29:T441,On November 19, 1964, the guided-missile destroyer USS Gyatt —working with a Navy F-8 Crusader fighter at 35,000 feet inside the Atlantic Fleet Weapons Range— detected a high-speed, high-altitude object on radar. The object slowed and held position at the Crusader's nine o'clock, about nine miles out. The pilot selected afterburner and tried to close the distance; at about five miles, the object accelerated extremely in a 20-degree climb and pulled away rapidly. It remained on the Gyatt's radar for twenty-one minutes, and as the Crusader returned to Roosevelt Roads Naval Air Station, it closed again from astern to four miles. The pilot, under full moonlight and good visibility, described the object as delta-shaped, about the size of a jet fighter. The Gyatt's radar —an AN/SPS-49 undergoing an evaluation program, which is why a camera was recording the scope— confirmed a relative speed in excess of 3,800 knots.
