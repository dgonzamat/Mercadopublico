21:Ta0b,The explanation that ended up attached to the case came from J. Allen Hynek, an Ohio State University astronomer and Project Sign consultant, but his 1949 evaluation is more nuanced than usually told. Hynek wrote that 'there is no astronomical explanation if we accept the report at face value'; he added that phrases from the pilots themselves —'tremendous bursts of flame,' 'cigar-shaped,' 'disappeared into a cloud,' 'orange-red flame,' 'time in sight five to ten seconds'— could be satisfied by a brilliant, slow-moving meteor, that the passenger who was awake gave a description that agreed with a meteor rather than a ship, and he left it to psychologists to say whether the trail of a bright meteor could produce the impression of a ship with lighted windows. His conclusion was conditional: if the Robins time, an hour earlier, was correct, the object was some kind of aircraft; if the difference was a timekeeping error, it must have been an extraordinary meteor. In his case index he classed it as astronomical with 'fair or low' probability. The file's record card, a temporary ATIC form of 1952, also carries the handwritten note 'Prob Meteor'. And the Office of Special Investigations (OSI) report on the Georgia sightings concluded that what seven Augusta residents interviewed between 24 and 27 August had seen was 'not aircraft, but probably of meteoric nature.' The file also contains indications that favor a fireball and that this entry should not omit: around 2:30 a.m., two crews flying near Blackstone, Virginia, saw an 'unusual meteor,' brighter than any they had seen, traveling horizontally toward the south and southwest for three seconds; in Georgia, personnel at the Atlanta Naval Air Station, hunters near Covington and residents of Augusta described bright lights with trails that night or the following ones. Many scattered sightings of a horizontal light with a flame in the same window are the typical signature of a fireball, although the times do not match and Hynek treated the Virginia sightings as a separate incident.
