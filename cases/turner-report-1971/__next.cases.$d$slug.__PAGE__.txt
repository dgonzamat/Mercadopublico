14:T4b8,Oliver Harry Turner was by 1971 an Australian government physicist and analyst, head of the Nuclear Branch within the Joint Intelligence Organisation (JIO), the defense analytical intelligence body created in 1970 out of the former Joint Intelligence Bureau, with functions partly comparable to those of the U.S. Defense Intelligence Agency. His institutional role was to assess technical and scientific intelligence for the Department of Defence, not to investigate UFOs. The file now known as the Turner Report is in fact a JIO file with minutes going back to 1958, and it shows that the interest came mainly from Turner. In January 1970, after Blue Book's closure, the Royal Australian Air Force (RAAF) reported that it was considering reducing its own UFO investigation effort. In February 1970 the Director of Scientific and Technical Intelligence, R. H. Mathams, having discussed an earlier paper by Turner, wrote that he was not convinced the problem had a sufficient scientific intelligence component to warrant diverting Australia's very limited resources, and the civilian deputy director, while agreeing that the subject should be studied somewhere, concluded that JIO could not be that somewhere.15:T6db,On May 27, 1971, Turner signed the minute 'Scientific and Intelligence Aspects of the UFO Problem', addressed to the Director through the Director of Scientific and Technical Intelligence, who forwarded it with a handwritten note asking to discuss it. It attached two documents intended, he said, to focus on aspects of the problem 'that have tended to remain hidden.' The first, on the official U.S. attitude, was compiled, in Turner's own words, from official reports and statements made by the CIA, the USAF, Congressional hearings and Blue Book records; that is, from U.S. material already public, not from classified allied intelligence. It argues that early USAF intelligence analyses pointed to real phenomena of extraterrestrial origin; that the CIA's Office of Scientific Intelligence, alarmed by the overloading of military communications during the 1952 wave, used the January 1953 Robertson panel meeting to turn Blue Book into a public debunking instrument; that the Avro saucer and a crash anti-gravity program with six university centers and physicists such as Teller, Oppenheimer, Dyson and Wheeler were funded; that JANAP 146 threatened service personnel who spoke out with prison; and that the Condon report contradicted its own contents. It also criticizes the RAAF: of 15 identifications as Venus between 1960 and 1965, it says, none was valid. The second document draws from the catalogue of about a thousand landing reports compiled by Jacques Vallée with J. Allen Hynek three alleged 'weapon systems': electrical interference, paralysis and a heat ray.
