Record of the event

What happened

The NASA-UAP-VM6 plate belongs to the photographic archive of Apollo 17, in December 1972, the last crewed mission to land on the Moon. When the lower-right quadrant of the sky above the lunar horizon is magnified, three points of light appear, arranged in a regular triangle. For decades the photo circulated in amateur forums and catalogs, with no official standing.

During the mission, the astronauts remarked on bright particles drifting past the spacecraft ("we've got a few very bright particles or fragments or something that go drifting by as we maneuver," said Ron Evans) and speculated that they were ice or paint fragments shed by the S-IVB, the rocket's last stage. According to the air-to-ground transcript released with the plate, Evans described them as "very jagged, angular fragments that are tumbling," and Cernan as "flat, flakelike particles," "some may be 6 inches across," most of them apparently moving away from the spacecraft.

More than two days later, Schmitt said he thought he had the S-IVB spotted, behind and above the spacecraft, and that through the monocular it "sure looks like the S-IVB." Cernan looked at what Schmitt was pointing to: a bright, distant object that, he said, was "obviously rotating because it's flashing," in a regular rhythm. He said it was "definitely not a particle that's nearby," not one of the particles "that tends to look like a star," but "something physical in the distance," and placed it, looking back at the Earth, at about the 11 o'clock position and 10 or 12 Earth diameters away; Houston answered, "We don't doubt it, Gene." When Cernan described a bright flash followed by a dull one, Schmitt put them down to the side of the S-IVB and its engine bell. Those remarks concern what they saw out of the window, not this plate.

On May 8, 2026, the newly renamed Department of War included the plate in the first batch of PURSUE, the federal release of files on these phenomena ordered by the Trump administration and carried out with NASA, on war.gov/UFO. It also opened an official case on it ("DOW has opened a case," the caption says). The caption offers no explanation: it says "there is no consensus about the nature of the anomaly" and that "new preliminary US government analysis" suggests it is "potentially the result of a physical object in the scene." It adds that the government "has obtained the original film from the Apollo 17 mission" and that "the results of the full NASA and DOW analysis will be released when completed." The caption does not say how that analysis is being done; no results have been published, so the case remains unresolved.

Historically, skeptics and imagery analysts dismissed the formation as a photographic flaw: film emulsion errors, lens flare or light reflecting off the lunar module window. The government's preliminary analysis speaks of "a physical object in the scene"; the caption does not say how far away that object would be.

Why it matters

In 2026 the U.S. government opened a case on a 1972 plate that had circulated among amateurs for decades, wrote that a preliminary analysis finds it "potentially the result of a physical object in the scene," and obtained the original film to analyze it with NASA.

The crew itself saw bright fragments floating next to the spacecraft, and Schmitt said they had been seeing the distant S-IVB "for about 24 hours or so" and that until then he had taken it for "just some other particle out there." Those remarks concern what they saw out of the window, not the plate. The results of the full NASA and Department of War analysis have not been published.
