29:T48e,From the top of a rise, some 150 to 200 yards away, Zamora saw in a dry arroyo a shiny object that he first took for an overturned car, with two people in white coveralls next to it. According to his statement, he saw them only that once, for about two seconds: they "appeared normal in shape — but possibly they were small adults or large kids," and one of them "seemed to turn and look straight at my car and seemed startled." When he got closer they were no longer in sight. He described the object as oval, smooth, with no doors or windows, whitish like aluminum but not chrome, resting on legs, with a red insignia in the middle. As he approached in his patrol car and then on foot, he heard two or three metallic thumps; immediately afterward the object emitted a loud, rising roar, a blue-orange flame issued from below, raised dust, and the object rose slowly to about the level of his car, 20 to 25 feet by his statement, and then departed horizontally at low altitude and high speed to the southwest over the canyon, making no further sound and clearing the dynamite shack by about three feet. Frightened, Zamora took cover behind his car and radioed in.2a:T611,New Mexico State Police Sergeant Sam Chavez arrived about three minutes after the object disappeared and found four fresh indentations in the ground, arranged in a quadrilateral, and several charred bushes. According to the report of Blue Book's Sergeant David Moody, Chavez thought smoke was coming from one bush, but the charred parts were cold to the touch, and he found no tracks other than those of the patrol car. That same evening FBI agent Arthur Byrnes and Army Captain Richard Holder, of the Stallion Range center at White Sands, measured the marks and took samples. On April 26 Major William Connor of Kirtland and Moody visited the site, and Moody found no radioactivity with a Geiger counter. Hynek, a physicist and Blue Book's scientific consultant, arrived on April 28, when the site had already been heavily trampled; he interviewed Zamora and Chavez and concluded that Zamora "saw a tangible, physical object," in good daylight and at times as close as about 100 feet, and that hoax and hallucination were ruled out. Blue Book found no other witnesses to the object, no unidentified helicopters or aircraft in the area, no unusual radar returns, no radiation in the soil samples and no chemical traces of propellant in the brush. The case card (file 8766) is marked "Unknown" and notes that a lunar-module-type configuration was initially suspected but that no vehicle could be placed at the site; the USAF used that classification relatively rarely.
