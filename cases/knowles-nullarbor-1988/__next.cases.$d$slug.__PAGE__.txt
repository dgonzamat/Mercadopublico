A truck driver, Graham Henley, driving east ahead of the Knowles, said he saw a light 'too high up to be another truck or vehicle' that 'disappeared and reappeared', and later met the family at Mundrabilla. According to Brunt, of the vehicles within 30 or 40 km his was the only occupant to report an unusual light; that night the crew of a tuna boat in the Great Australian Bight, hundreds of kilometres away, also saw a vertically elongated light, and the Ceduna sergeant spoke of four reports in all (the family, the trucker, a tuna-spotter aircraft and a trawler). Henley did not see the 'lifting': according to Skeptoid, no witness to that episode was ever presented, and since Henley was some 10-15 kilometres ahead, the light he saw in his rear-view mirror may have been the Knowles car itself.16:T6d7,The family continued to Ceduna (South Australia), where they reported to the police station. But meteorologist A. T. Brunt, who reviewed the case in 1989, writes that the car was subjected to thorough inspections on several occasions by the police and by UFO research groups in Australia, and that "none of these inspections showed anything unusual about the car": "There was no black ash inside or outside the car", only a black deposit on the rims of the two front tyres consistent with brake-lining material; the rear right tyre "had burst in a normal manner", as expected at high speed; and "the dents on the roof were insignificant, and there was no proof that they were not there before the event." The dust samples yielded nothing either. The Australian Mineral Development Laboratory analyzed the external ash and found clay and salt particles, consistent with a vehicle crossing the Nullarbor; an inspection commissioned by a TV station observed 'no significant dust'; and although Dr. Richard Haines of NASA Ames Research Center concluded that the interior dust differed from the exterior dust, later analyses by other labs found no anomalous material. Skeptoid sums it up as "a claim of strange dust on the car which turned out to be nothing".
