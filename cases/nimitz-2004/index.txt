32:T444,Between November 10 and 16, 2004, the USS Nimitz carrier strike group was doing exercises to prepare for its deployment, in a training area off California and Mexico. The guided-missile cruiser USS Princeton, the first of its class with the upgraded AN/SPY-1B combat radar, on several occasions detected contacts the crew labeled "Anomalous Aerial Vehicles" (AAVs). According to the case's executive summary, an unsigned, undated document attributed to the Pentagon's AATIP program and made public in 2018, a senior chief with 17 years on cruisers of this type said the AAVs would descend "very rapidly" from about 60,000 feet to about 50 feet "in a matter of seconds," hover for a while and then leave at high speed. They were detected three times that week. The radar was in a mode for intercepting conventional aircraft: it never obtained an accurate track, meaning a continuous fix, and dropped the contacts as clutter, as it does with any other false target.
