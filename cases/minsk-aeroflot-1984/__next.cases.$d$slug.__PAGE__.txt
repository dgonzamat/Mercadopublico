14:T4bb,The episode caused a sensation when the trade-union newspaper Trud, one of the highest-circulation papers in the USSR, published it on January 30, 1985 under the title 'At 4:10 Sharp', making it the most famous Soviet UAP case. That an official daily devoted space to a pilots' sighting was notable in a controlled press system, and it had consequences for the paper: the trade-union secretariat (VTsSPS) declared the publication 'erroneous and harmful', reprimanded Trud's editor and removed him soon after, with no public explanation ever given of what was seen. Decades later, James Oberg proposed in the Skeptical Inquirer (2009), as a prosaic hypothesis, a naval missile test launch from the Murmansk area, over the Barents Sea, which he himself calls technically unproven until Russia releases the records: the phenomenology (a beam expanding into a cone, shape changes, simultaneous visibility from other aircraft in the region) fits an illuminated fuel plume in the upper atmosphere.
