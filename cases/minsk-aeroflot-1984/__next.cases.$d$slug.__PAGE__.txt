13:T44a,The episode caused a sensation when the trade-union newspaper Trud, one of the highest-circulation papers in the USSR, published it on January 30, 1985 under the title 'At 4:10 Sharp', making it the most famous Soviet UAP case. That an official daily devoted space to a pilots' sighting was itself notable in a controlled press system, and it forced an investigation: the case was referred to the state network for studying the phenomenon (the program later known as SETKA) and discussed by specialists. Decades later, skeptical analysis (among others, by specialist James Oberg and the Skeptical Inquirer) convincingly linked it to a rocket launch from the Plesetsk Cosmodrome that morning: the phenomenology (a beam expanding into a cone, shape changes, simultaneous visibility from other aircraft in the region) fits an illuminated fuel plume in the upper atmosphere.
