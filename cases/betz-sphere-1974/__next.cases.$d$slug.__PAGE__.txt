14:T8c4,The explanation, however, proved prosaic and convergent. In June 1974, a group of scientists examined the sphere and concluded it was made of a high-quality terrestrial stainless steel —a magnetic ferrous alloy of type #431, resistant to corrosion and heat. Already in April, the U.S. Navy had pointed out that the object was nothing more than a large ball bearing used as a check valve in the piping system of some chemical plant, and the Miami Herald reported an almost identical ball in Jacksonville identified as part of a valve from a paper mill; an industrial supplier displayed a Bell & Howell stainless steel ball of the same dimensions. Navy spokesman Chris Berninger described a hollow 8-inch ball with a half-inch shell of high-grade but common stainless steel, seamless and with one small triangular mark, inside which tiny beads of manufacturing residue rolled about; the report of metallurgist Fred Thomas, of Jacksonville Naval Air Station, gave it as hollow and empty, weighing 21.34 pounds, neither radioactive nor explosive. Berninger conceded that the sphere did move at the Betz house, and put it down to the uneven stone floors of the old house. In late April, J. Allen Hynek and four other scientists examined it in New Orleans, at a meeting sponsored by the National Enquirer, and said it gave no evidence of an extraterrestrial origin: it obeyed gravity and made a high-pitched sound when shaken, and Hynek called it "a Chinese puzzle", though he said he did not think it was a hoax. As for the 'movement', skeptical analyses noted that the sphere sat still on display for nearly two weeks and that, being almost perfectly balanced, the slightest tilt of the house's old, uneven stone floor was enough to make it roll or change course. The enigma dissolved into an industrial valve ball on a crooked floor. In 2012, writer Brian Dunning (Skeptoid) also traced its likely origin: James Durling-Jones, a sculptor from Taos (New Mexico), used 8-inch stainless steel ball check valves in his works, and around 1971 several rolled off the roof rack of his van near Jacksonville and were lost.
