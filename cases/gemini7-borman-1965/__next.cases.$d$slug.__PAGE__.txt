The brilliant body Lovell saw ahead of the capsule, 'slowly tumbling', was the second stage of the Titan II rocket that had placed Gemini 7 in orbit, but the crew had it in sight separately from the 'bogey', which James Oberg, from the trajectory, identifies as a piece of debris from the booster itself. The 'particles', for their part, have been linked to the phenomenon John Glenn had already observed on Mercury and that astronauts nicknamed 'fireflies': ice crystals and tiny droplets of effluent —water, vented urine, frost shed from the fuselage— that, lit by the Sun against the black of space, glint like a swarm of points around the craft; but Borman placed them three or four miles out, not next to the capsule.
