4c:T527,At about ten at night on 29 March 1980, the colonel who was director of studies at the General Military Academy, on the outskirts of Zaragoza, telephoned the chief of staff of the Air Transport Command. According to the letter that command sent to the Air Staff on 2 April, he reported that for 'about 20 minutes' he had been watching 'with binoculars and with the naked eye a bright object, motionless and with a glow around it'. In the statement he later signed before the investigating officer, the colonel, with 38 years of service and wearing prescription glasses, placed the sighting between 21:45 and 22:15, from a window at the Academy, and described it thus: 'seen through binoculars it seemed to have the shape of a lemon squeezer, very bright because of the intense light it gave off, golden in colour'. Its size was 'that of a large star', with no trail and no sound. He saw it at about 60° of elevation when it appeared and at 30° when it vanished; it 'remained motionless for about 30 minutes and disappeared from West to Northwest'. He added that 'more than eight members of the family' saw it, that 'their comments and those of neighbours agree that it had been appearing for several days', and that 'it gave the impression of a very close star, an impression that faded when it disappeared abruptly'.4d:T54b,The chief of staff passed the alert to the duty captain at Zaragoza air base, asking him to request from the tower and from the ground-controlled approach radar (GCA) any echo in the area of the Academy. According to the captain's report, at about 22:10 the GCA answered that it had two echoes, on radial 090 at 7 miles and radial 095 at 10 miles, moving 'from E. to W. very slowly without transponder and without determining altitude, estimating it was low'; they were seen for three minutes, disappeared for good ten minutes later and were not seen from the tower. At about 23:15 others appeared: one between radials 050 and 060, at 3 to 6 miles, flying circular circuits, and another on radial 220 moving out and in between 10 and 12 miles. Then came interference inside the 7-mile circle which, according to maintenance, 'was not from the radar, so it must have been external'. A handwritten note at the foot of the same report adds that the Academy saw 'the same bright lights' again the next day, over roughly the same place and for a similar time, and that it ruled out military helicopters because none were deployed at the San Gregorio training ground next to the Academy.
