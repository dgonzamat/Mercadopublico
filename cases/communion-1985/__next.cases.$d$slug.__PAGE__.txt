14:T474,The case was independently investigated by journalist Ed Conroy, who devoted a whole book ('Report on Communion', 1989) to checking the account, interviewing neighbors and witnesses, and examining the context, arriving at a nuanced conclusion rather than a validation or a debunking. Skeptics —including neurologist Steven Novella— note that the central features (waking paralyzed, presences in the bedroom, inability to move) fit hypnagogia and sleep paralysis, common and well-characterized neurological phenomena, compounded by the suggestion of hypnosis. The harshest accusation, given Strieber's track record as a fiction author, was fabrication: in his review for Skeptical Inquirer (fall 1987), Ernest H. Taves weighed four hypotheses and, invoking Occam's razor, opted for a horror writer 'having fun' by offering as fact what is fiction. Taves himself notes that Strieber had passed a polygraph test, which did not allay his suspicions. Strieber rejected that reading and maintained his account for decades.
