12:T4bc,In March 1952, Commander Edward J. Ruppelt — head of Project Blue Book at the Air Technical Intelligence Center (ATIC) of the USAF, based at Wright-Patterson AFB, Ohio — visited MIT Lincoln Laboratory at Hanscom AFB (Lexington, Massachusetts) to deliver a lecture on 'flying saucer' phenomena. MIT Lincoln Lab had been founded in 1951 as a federally funded research and development center (FFRDC) with an air defense mission, and its scientists were key interlocutors for aerial threat analysis. The lecture was filmed on reel-to-reel film by the ATIC team and catalogued as 'AF-ATIC-FILM, 03/52.' Ruppelt, who would coin the term 'UFO' in 1952 and serve as Blue Book director until 1953, represented at that time the USAF's most systematic effort to analyze and catalog sightings of unidentified aerial phenomena. The context is significant: in March 1952, the ATIC was processing a wave of military and civilian sightings that would peak four months later with the 'Invasion of Washington' of July 1952 — sightings over D.C. airspace that sent F-94 interceptors after radar-visible objects over the White House and Capitol, generating the largest UAP media coverage in the United States up to that point.13:T5af,The 'AF-ATIC-FILM, 03/52' is the first known filmed record of an official USAF briefing on flying saucers delivered to civilian defense scientists. Its analytical weight operates on three dimensions. First, it places ATIC and Ruppelt in active intelligence exchange with the FFRDC ecosystem in the pre-Washington 1952 phase: the USAF was not only cataloging sightings internally but formally presenting them to its principal defense contractors four months before the largest public UAP episode of the period. This suggests that in March 1952, ATIC already considered the phenomenon substantive enough to warrant a filmed briefing at MIT Lincoln Lab. Second, the film's custody at MIT Lincoln Lab for 74 years — without publication or transfer to the National Archives — is consistent with the documented pattern of UAP records retention by FFRDC contractors that Burlison has been investigating since May 2026.
