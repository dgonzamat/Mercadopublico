34:T6e1,Redmond shows how a case inflates when records that the documentation itself keeps apart are merged into one. The usual account combines the low descent the officer saw, a two-hour radar track climbing and descending nearly 15 km, and a simultaneous scramble of six fighters. The Blue Book file separates them. The visual sighting has two witnesses, Dickerson and Wertz, and both described it to the Air Force as a very bright light, low in the northeast, that for more than an hour stayed around one point though it seemed to move rapidly toward and away from them. The radar return was, according to the directors on duty from 15:00 UTC, a fixed target next to a mountain, which the operators saw again on later days; nobody gave a statement covering the first hours. The altitude figures come from the Seattle log, which relayed them second-hand, and the fighters took off in four waves over five hours, some of them inside an exercise. The military response was large, but it was aimed at a radar return, not at the light the officer saw. The astronomy supports the identification: at 12:00 UTC Venus, at magnitude −4.4, had risen about half an hour earlier and stood about 5 degrees high, almost due east, with Regulus close by; by 12:59 it was about 16 degrees up. The witnesses spoke of the northeast, a difference of some 20 to 40 degrees that is easy to make without a compass. The card says both bodies were "just below horizon," which is not accurate: they had already risen, which strengthens the explanation rather than weakening it. But there is a tension the identification does not resolve: over the next hour and a half Venus climbed from about 5 to about 21 degrees, while the witnesses described a light that stayed very low on the horizon.35:T503,A residue remains. The FAA log records things Venus does not explain well: a light that descends fast and stops, the shift to the northeast, the mushroom shape with a flame when an aircraft approached, and the disappearance into clouds. The time in the FAA log (12:59 UTC) also does not match the teletype and the card (12:00 UTC). Add to that ATIC's own summary, in which Wertz saw the object zoom straight up at the end. But the file shows an investigation that, however slow, did interview the witnesses and the radar operators, not a concealment.
