🇪🇸1968·Puente Almuhey and the Guardo road (León)

Spain.Puente Almuhey · villagers see lights and a disc that splits

Between November 24 and December 10, 1968, residents of Puente Almuhey (León) saw two kinds of lights: a reddish disc that split into three and faded to the southwest, and a small, slow white light that stayed up to two and a half hours in the same direction. The stationmaster wrote to the military aerodrome at León, two captains took six statements and the head of the First Air Region pointed, "in a very remote way," to the setting Moon and to a planet. The ephemerides confirm that the Moon was setting in the southwest at that hour on November 24, and that Venus shone in that direction on the December evenings.

What happened

On December 13, 1968 the military aerodrome at León received a letter from the stationmaster of the La Robla Railway at Puente Almuhey, a village in the north of the province near the border with Palencia. He informed the head of the air sector that an unidentified flying artifact had been seen in the village. The aerodrome opened an inquiry under two captains of the Air Arm, who on December 14 took statements from six people with the same printed questionnaire: day, place, time, size, shape, height, noise, direction, speed, duration, color, lights and whether the witness knew what a shooting star or a comet was.

The statements describe two different episodes. The first took place on the night of November 24, at about ten. A 32-year-old woman from the village said that on the road from Cervera to La Magdalena, at kilometer 33, she saw for about ten minutes a disc-shaped object some two meters across, almost level with the road, with a continuous reddish light, silent and moving very slowly; she added that it broke up into three lights identical to the first, which joined back into one, and that it faded slowly.

A 19-year-old mechanic stated in León that the same night, at ten, on the road from Guardo to Puente Almuhey, near the border between León and Palencia, he saw a reddish circular object about ten meters off the ground and five hundred to a thousand meters away, for ten to fifteen minutes; it seemed to him to move southwest and to change into a kind of horn with its point upward, whose base slowly rose as its brightness faded until it vanished.

According to the aerodrome's report, three people saw it that night: a married couple and a third person unconnected with them, whom the couple stopped on the road. There is no statement of the husband's own. The woman's, recorded as married, is written in the plural ("they saw it coming from Guardo"), and at its foot there is a handwritten note, cut off at the edge, of which only "the same" can be read. In her statement the place ends, also cut off, at "the boundary of la Pe…"; the mechanic places his at the boundary between León and Palencia. The file does not say how they met.

The second episode took up the evenings of December 8 and 10, between seven and eight, and was seen by several villagers, always in a group. A 44-year-old pointsman spoke of a white light, spherical or ovoid, about thirty centimeters across, four to five hundred meters up, which lasted two hours the first day and an hour and a half the second and left toward the southwest; a 41-year-old woman saw it for five minutes, about three hundred meters up, above a spot about five hundred meters south of the station, between yellow and white; a 42-year-old industrialist saw it for about two minutes on the 10th, round and white; and the stationmaster, aged 46, described it as "a lantern with six panes," white, with some flashes, coming from the southwest and "swaying slowly," two and a half hours the first day and an hour and a half the second.

On January 14, 1969 the León aerodrome sent up its conclusions. It placed the observations in the immediate surroundings of the village, between seven and ten in the evening; it distinguished a strong, small light, between white and yellow, motionless "for up to an hour and a half" according to some observations (the statements go up to two and a half hours) and disappearing to the southwest, from a circular object about two meters across that, according to the couple, "came considerably closer" to them, that split into three discs and that changed into "a kind of triangle," the horn of the mechanic's statement; and it noted that the aerodrome's meteorologist found no similar atmospheric phenomenon on those days.

On January 22 the lieutenant general commanding the First Air Region wrote to the Air Staff that, "given the confusion in the information and the discrepancies in the statements," the nature of the objects could not be determined, and that, "in a very remote way," it might be the setting of the Moon in the first case and some planet in the second, always under special conditions of poor visibility or low cloud. In March 1993 the intelligence officer of the Air Operations Command (MOA) summarized the file with the same reading, naming Venus, though he warned that the scarcity of data made it impossible to hold any hypothesis with sufficient reliability, and proposed declassifying it.

Why it matters

The astronomical causes the air region pointed to, as a "very remote" possibility, were not checked at the time. Done today with ephemerides, by a calculation made for this case, the match is close. On November 24 there was a crescent Moon, 28 percent lit, which at ten in the evening local time stood in the southwest less than one degree above the horizon and set at 22:09: a reddish, low light in the southwest that lasts a few minutes and vanishes, as the two witnesses said.

The horn with its point upward whose base rises as it fades is the shape of a crescent Moon going down behind the terrain. On the evenings of December 8 and 10, Venus, the brightest body in the sky after the Sun and the Moon, stood in the southwest about ten degrees up between seven and eight and set at about a quarter to nine: a white light, still for hours, that twinkles and seems to sway near the horizon, in the direction everyone who saw it gave.

Not everything fits as cleanly. The disc that split into three lights and came back together, and the approach the aerodrome's report attributes to the couple (the woman's statement says only that it came from Guardo and moved very slowly, although the report says the officers, besides taking the statements, questioned the witnesses "ampliamente," at length), do not follow from the Moon on its own, though a low Moon seen through cloud or behind the line of a hill can break up and reform to the eye; the air region itself made its explanation conditional on poor visibility or low cloud.

The heights and sizes the witnesses gave, such as the thirty centimeters and the four to five hundred meters of height estimated by the pointsman, are estimates of a light with no reference, which cannot be measured. And the head of the region himself stressed the confusion and discrepancies in the statements. The 1968 inquiry nonetheless left an orderly record: six statements taken with the same questionnaire, two episodes the village was mixing up separated by date, the meteorologist consulted, and an explanation the air region judged remote and the ephemerides support.
