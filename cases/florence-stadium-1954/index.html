🇮🇹1954·Stadio Artemio Franchi, Florence

Italy.Florence · 10,000 fans see objects over the stadium

On October 27, 1954, during a Fiorentina football match, about 10,000 people at the Stadio Artemio Franchi in Florence saw objects over the stadium, and the game was halted. A filamentous substance, so-called “angel hair,” fell, and Professor Giovanni Canneri of the University of Florence analyzed it by spectrography: boron, silicon, calcium and magnesium, not radioactive. Skeptic James McGaha attributes it to migrating spiders, and the debate remains open.

What happened

On October 27, 1954, a typically crisp autumn day, Fiorentina was playing its local rival Pistoiese in front of about 10,000 people at Florence's municipal stadium, today the Stadio Artemio Franchi. Just after half-time the stadium fell eerily silent and then a roar went up: the spectators stopped watching the match and pointed at the sky, and the players stopped playing. According to the referee's match report, play was suspended because spectators saw something in the sky (BBC, 2014).

The witnesses described different things. Fiorentina player Ardico Magnini, who had played for Italy at the 1954 World Cup, recalled it sixty years later to the BBC: "something that looked like an egg that was moving slowly, slowly, slowly," while silver glitter came down from the sky. Fan Gigi Boni saw several objects "like Cuban cigars" that moved very fast and then stopped, in a scene lasting a couple of minutes; he believes they were extraterrestrial. Another player, Romolo Tuci, said they saw them "directly, for real."

La Nazione journalist Giorgio Batini said in 2003, on an Italian television program, that he received hundreds of phone calls about sightings that day; he went up to the newspaper's roof and saw shiny balls moving fast toward the Cathedral dome. According to the BBC, there were sightings in many Tuscan towns that day and the following ones. La Nazione's headline said "glass fibres" were falling on Tuscan cities after "globes and flying saucers" passed by; the photograph of the object that ran with the headline has been lost.

A silvery, filamentous substance also fell, the so-called "angel hair" (capelli d'angelo). Roberto Pinotti, president of Italy's National UFO Centre, who was ten years old at the time, recalled that for an hour the rooftops of Florence were covered in a white substance that then evaporated like snow. Witnesses compared it to cotton wool or cobwebs, and it disintegrated on contact. Batini gathered samples in a wood outside the city by rolling them onto a matchstick and took them to the Institute of Chemical Analysis at the University of Florence, where others had done the same. The laboratory, led by Professor Giovanni Canneri, ran a spectrographic analysis: the substance contained boron, silicon, calcium and magnesium and was not radioactive. The analysis destroyed the material.

James McGaha, an astronomer and former U.S. Air Force pilot, told the BBC that the whole episode, angel hair included, was nothing more than migrating spiders: young spiders spin very thin webs that they use as sails, link together into large globs that glisten in the sun, and some of it falls to the ground. He had earlier considered a fireball, a meteor breaking up. The BBC adds that September and October are the months when spiders in the northern hemisphere migrate. Pinotti rejects the hypothesis, which he calls nonsense, because spider silk is a protein, an organic compound, and not the elements found in the samples; for him it was an intelligent, technological phenomenon. Science writer Philip Ball agrees that those elements don't seem to come from spiders: magnesium and calcium are fairly common in living bodies, but boron and silicon much less so.

Why it matters

It was a public event with thousands of witnesses. The records from the time that the sources cite are the referee's match report, the La Nazione headline and the analysis of the sample at the University of Florence. The descriptions of the objects come from witnesses who gave them between forty-nine and sixty years later, and they differ from one another.

Angel hair is the only physical element, and the sample was destroyed in the analysis, so it cannot be repeated. Ball told the BBC he wouldn't trust any reports of an old and strange event like this unless he had seen the data. The BBC concludes that it remains a mystery and that those who were there are convinced that what they saw was unlike anything on Earth.
