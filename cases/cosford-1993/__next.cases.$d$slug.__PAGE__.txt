11:T45f,Between the night of 30 March and the early hours of 31 March 1993, the UK experienced a wave of sightings that spread across much of the country —from Devon and Cornwall in the southwest to the Midlands—, with dozens of witnesses —about 70 in Great Britain according to David Clarke—, among them police officers and military personnel; weeks later similar accounts arrived from Ireland, northern France and Spain. The core of the sightings, timed between 1:10 and 1:15 a.m., tallied remarkably: two bright white lights speeding towards the south-eastern horizon and leaving luminous trails; some saw a third light that gave the impression of a triangle, and a few reports from other times and places did not fit the pattern. At the RAF base in Cosford (Shropshire), an MoD police patrol saw the two lights with their trails and phoned a warning to the Met Office observer at nearby RAF Shawbury, and the incident escalated within the British Ministry of Defence (MoD), which still kept an official channel open to receive and assess reports of aerial phenomena. Radar tapes were checked and showed no intruder.12:T459,The episode that gave the case weight occurred later at nearby RAF Shawbury: according to the Met Office log, at 01:40 GMT, that is, 2:40 a.m. local time. A Met Office observer —Wayne Elliott—, a witness familiar with military air traffic, recounted seeing a large object projecting a narrow beam of light at the ground at about 400–500 feet, and estimated its size at somewhere between a C-130 Hercules transport plane and a Boeing 747 when it passed over his head at an estimated 4,000 feet. He described the object moving slowly while the beam swept the field beyond the perimeter fence 'as if it were looking for something', then accelerating away at high speed with a humming sound. In his 1996 account for the Met Office magazine he added that he had worked at military fast-jet and helicopter airfields and that the object was moving too quickly to be a helicopter, 'which seemed to be the obvious answer'.
