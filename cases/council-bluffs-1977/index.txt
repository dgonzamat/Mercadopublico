2f:T4f9,Council Bluffs is a good case study of how materials science works on UAP casework: an event with witnesses, a preserved physical trace, and a peer-reviewed re-analysis decades later. It matters for its result, not in spite of it. When Garry Nolan and Jacques Vallée measured isotopes and composition, they found not an anomalous signature but signs of an incomplete industrial process. It is the disciplined counterpoint to the temptation to treat every unexplained molten metal as evidence of non-human technology: here a theorist of anomalous materials studied the sample and honestly reported that it pointed to something terrestrial. That willingness to publish the mundane result is exactly the corpus's standard. It is also worth being precise about the scope of 'unresolved': the contemporary phase ruled out the most trivial explanations (meteorite, re-entry, hoax), so the residue is genuinely anomalous in the sense of lacking an identified source that night; but 'unidentified source' does not equal 'non-terrestrial origin', and the isotopic re-analysis pushes hard toward the terrestrial.
