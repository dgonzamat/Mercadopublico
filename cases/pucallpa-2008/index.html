Peru.Pucallpa · villagers see lights shooting beams at people

In April 2008, residents of San Juan, a hamlet near Pucallpa (Ucayali, in the Peruvian Amazon), reported “lights” that flew over the area at night and shot beams at people, spreading fear. According to a reconstruction from popular ufology, the Peruvian Air Force office that studied these phenomena (OIFAA) got the report through Commander Julio César Chamorro, though other sources place his departure from the office years earlier. Three Communication students from the University of Lima carried out the civilian inquiry. Nearly all the record is testimony and secondary sources.

What happened

In April 2008, according to the reconstruction published by the site Exploración OVNI (which takes it from tudiscovery.com), the residents of the San Juan hamlet, on the outskirts of Pucallpa (Ucayali department, in the Peruvian Amazon), were frightened by "lights" that flew over the place at night and shot beams of light at them. The text says the phenomenon kept repeating in the same area. It gives no names of witnesses, no dates for the nights and no further description of the lights beyond the beams aimed at people.

According to the same reconstruction, the OIFAA (Office for the Investigation of Anomalous Aerial Phenomena) of the Peruvian Air Force received the complaint through Commander Julio César Chamorro and already had personnel in the region, where it began the investigation. The text also presents a document bearing an official seal, which it calls the first and only one made public in Peru showing that the Air Force knew of a specific complaint; it asks the commanding general of the IV Territorial Air Region for support in investigating. The text neither reproduces nor dates it.

Other sources do not fit the 2008 version (see ↗). The profile of Chamorro at the International Coalition for Extraterrestrial Research (ICER) describes him as a retired Air Force commander (born in 1957, joined in 1975, a radar-operation and air-defense specialist) who founded the OIFAA in 1999; it adds that the office reopened in 2013 as the Department of Investigations of Anomalous Aerial Phenomena (DIFAA). El Comercio's historical archive dates the office to 2001, names Chamorro as its first head, says he left it after about two years in charge, during which an average of three sighting cases a day were received, and adds that in 2004 its computers were stolen and much of its files lost. The state news agency Andina wrote in 2013 that the unit had been shut for five years. The Air Force's official role in Pucallpa therefore remains unconfirmed.

The civilian part of the inquiry was carried out by three Communication Sciences students from the University of Lima: Teresa Lucía Chamorro, the commander's daughter, Martha Wong and Luciana Masías. They took the case as a project for the course "Fundamentals of Information". According to Exploración OVNI, they obtained material "that very few experienced researchers have achieved in all their years of career", and the experience completely changed how they saw the phenomenon; the text does not say what the material is.

The case was made public in late April 2012, at the congress "Ovni – Conciencia, La Información Clasificada" held in Lima, under the name "the Pucallpa UFO". The Exploración OVNI article, from July 2012, speaks of "last April" and describes it as an open case that remains under investigation.

Why it matters

The case reaches us only through a ufology outreach reconstruction and a university coursework project, not through an investigation file: the sources consulted publish no radar data, independent photographic analysis, chain of custody for the material or verifiable technical file.

What sets it apart is the link to the Air Force, and that link is unconfirmed: the reconstruction says the OIFAA received the report and cites a document with an official seal that has not been shown, while El Comercio and Andina contradict its chronology. What is documented is Chamorro's personal link to the inquiry: one of the three students who carried it out was his daughter.

None of the sources consulted proposes a cause for the lights.
