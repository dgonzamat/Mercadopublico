18:T5bd,On the evening of 4 August 1990, two men (by the account, two cooks from a nearby hotel walking on the moors above the hamlet of Calvine, in Perthshire, Scotland) observed a large diamond-shaped object hovering silently in the sky. They took shelter under some trees and from there shot a series of six photographs; according to the witnesses, the object hovered silently for four or five minutes and then slowly ascended vertically, gradually gaining speed. The handwritten note that opened the Ministry of Defence file records another version: the object hovered for about 10 minutes before ascending vertically at high speed, while an RAF aircraft, believed to be a Harrier, made a number of low-level passes for 5 to 6 minutes. In at least four of the six images, alongside the diamond-shaped object, appears what has been interpreted as a military jet (probably a Harrier) flying nearby, a decisive detail because it suggests military aircraft were present in the scene. According to the MoD file quoted by Clarke (DEFE 24/1940/1), its specialists established that the jet was a Harrier and identified "a barely visible second aircraft, again probably a Harrier"; yet, according to the ministry's own briefing, there was "no record of Harriers operating in the area at the time" the photographs were taken. A former defence intelligence official later told Clarke the two jets were US and UK aircraft that were "escorting the object", a claim no document supports.19:T422,After years of research, the academic, journalist and former adviser to the British archives David Clarke located former RAF press officer Craig Lindsay, who on his own initiative had kept for more than 30 years a copy of one of the prints together with the envelope and the photocopies he had once handled for the MoD. In 2022 Lindsay showed him that material and the image was made public for the first time. A photographic analysis by Andrew Robinson of Sheffield Hallam University concluded that the copy is "without doubt" of the original photograph the MoD analysed, that the image is a genuine photograph of a scene in front of the camera, and that if any manipulation or construction took place, it occurred in the scene rather than in camera or in post-production, though he found no evidence of it.
