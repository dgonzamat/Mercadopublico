Record of the event

What happened

In 2023, on a date that has not been made public, an electro-optical and infrared sensor system aboard a U.S. military platform recorded 13 seconds of video. Such a system combines a visible-light camera with one that builds its image from the heat objects give off; the official description does not say which of the two produced these images. U.S. Central Command (CENTCOM), which commands U.S. forces in the Middle East, sent the video as a report of an unidentified anomalous phenomenon to the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO), the Pentagon office that studies these cases. AARO filed it under the title "DOW-UAP-PR169, Unresolved UAP Report, Middle East, 2023."

The official description of the video is a single sentence: from second 0 to 13, "The sensor pans to track an area of contrast, keeping it generally within the center of the frame." An area of contrast is a patch that stands out from the background. AARO adds that the description is for information only and should not be read as an analytical judgment, an investigative conclusion or a determination about the event's validity, nature or significance. It gives no coordinates, altitude, type of platform or time of day.

A review of the frames of the released video, made for this case, shows a featureless gray background, lighter at the top and darker at the lower left: no horizon, ground, clouds or other reference can be made out. The sensor's symbols appear over the image in light blue: a cross in the center, four corner marks framing a central rectangle and a letter N. Several black rectangles cover the edges of the screen and an area just below the cross, and the official description does not say what they hide. The audio track is silent.

The only thing that stands out in the image is a bright point a few pixels across. It is visible, faintly, from the first frame, near the bottom edge of the screen. From about second 3 to the end it stays inside the central rectangle, in its right half, and never reaches the cross. The official description does not say where the area of contrast is, but the point is the only visible thing that stands out from the background, and its position fits an area the sensor keeps "generally" in the center. Because the background offers no reference, the video cannot show how much of the point's motion is its own and how much comes from the sensor's panning.

DVIDS, the service that distributes imagery from the U.S. armed forces, posted the video on November 20, 2026, in the eighth batch of PURSUE, the program run by the Department of War (the name the Department of Defense has used since 2025) to publish files on anomalous phenomena, which has released them in batches since May 2026. The page gives no location ("undisclosed location") and lists November 18, 2026, as the date taken, the same date the video file carries as its creation date. It cannot be the date of the recording, which the official description places in 2023 without giving the day, and the match suggests it is the date the file was prepared.

Why it matters

The video shows that a military sensor tracked something that stood out from the background for 13 seconds, and nothing more. Without a date, location, altitude, distance or type of platform, the point's size and speed cannot be worked out, and there is no way to tell whether the gray background is sky, sea or land. Without knowing whether the image is in visible light or in heat, it is not clear what the point's brightness means either. A point a few pixels across shows no shape, and its path across the screen may come from the sensor's motion as much as from the object's.

No source proposes what it was. AARO's annual report for fiscal year 2024, which despite its name covers reports received between May 1, 2023, and June 1, 2024, gives the share for similar cases. Of the 57 reports sent in that period by U.S. forces deployed to the Middle East, AARO resolved 13 as balloons, drones or satellites and had a preliminary assessment of two more as possible satellite flares. It placed the remaining 42 in an archive for lack of information to analyze them. The report does not list the cases one by one, so it is not known whether this video is among those 57.

The same report says that, of the 757 reports AARO received in that period from all regions, it closed 118 cases as conventional objects, and that 174 more, which were awaiting final approval on May 31, 2024, were closed the same way by the time the report came out; they included balloons, birds, drones, satellites and aircraft. According to the report, most of the cases AARO holds remain unresolved for lack of the data needed to analyze them, and compression and pixelation in sensors often turn a bird into a blob or an orb. A video released as unresolved means that the case was not closed, not that the object showed anything unusual.

Another CENTCOM video, the small thermal signature recorded over the Middle East in 2020 ↗ Middle East, released in the first PURSUE batch, in May 2026, has a description in the same format and a similar image: a point a few pixels across that the sensor follows, also released as unresolved.
