🇺🇸1966–1967·Point Pleasant, West Virginia

United States.Mothman · Point Pleasant residents see a winged creature

Between November 1966 and November 1967, residents of Point Pleasant, West Virginia, said they saw a large gray winged creature with red eyes, mostly near the “TNT Area,” a former explosives complex. On November 15, 1966, two couples said it chased their car; a sheriff's deputy went back with them and found nothing. A biologist put it down to a crane in 1966, and Joe Nickell to a barred owl. On December 15, 1967, the Silver Bridge collapsed, killing 46 people, after a corroded link cracked, but the coincidence fused the two events into the Mothman legend.

What happened

The sightings began in November 1966 around Point Pleasant, a small West Virginia town on the banks of the Ohio River. According to the first report by Mary Hyre, the Point Pleasant correspondent, witnesses described a gray winged figure as tall as a man ("about six feet tall"), with large widely spaced red eyes and a big wingspan, able to rise almost vertically and to follow a car at high speed. Many sightings were near the "TNT Area", a former World War II explosives manufacturing and storage complex, full of abandoned bunkers and surrounded by woods and marshland that is now a wildlife reserve.

The most cited episode occurred on November 15, 1966, when two young couples, Roger and Linda Scarberry and Steve Mallette and his wife, were driving through the area and said they saw a winged creature beside the road and were chased by it. According to Hyre, the four fled toward Point Pleasant along Route 62 at 100 miles an hour (about 160 km/h) with the creature behind them. According to John Keel, who recorded it in Flying Saucer Review in 1968, the four said the creature flew over their car without flapping its wings; they reported it to the police, and Deputy Millard Halstead went back to the spot with them without finding it, although his radio then gave out, he said later, a strange sound "like a speeded-up phonograph".

Keel visited Point Pleasant five times from December 1966 and interviewed many witnesses. He tabulated 26 sightings between November 1966 and November 1967, ten of them near the "TNT Area", and wrote that all the witnesses agreed the wings did not flap. One of them, Thomas Ury, described it on November 25, 1966 as about six feet long with an eight-to-ten-foot wingspan. Keel also recorded that in late December 1966 a Gallipolis Ferry farmer shot a snowy owl two feet tall with a five-foot wingspan, and that the witnesses who went to see it said it in no way resembled what they had seen. He granted, however, that in three cases in his table (nos. 7, 11 and 15) the witnesses may have seen a large bird; in one of them, on December 4, 1966, five pilots at the Gallipolis airport saw a giant "bird" that at first looked like a plane to them.

On December 15, 1967, the Silver Bridge, a chain suspension bridge linking Point Pleasant with Kanauga (Ohio), collapsed without warning during rush hour: 31 of the 37 vehicles on it fell, 24 into the Ohio River and seven onto the Ohio shore, and 46 people died. The official investigation determined a structural cause: the fracture of a single link of the chain ("eyebar 330") by stress-corrosion cracking, a flaw that the routine inspections of the era could not detect. The National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) report, adopted on December 16, 1970, attributes the collapse to the fracture of that eyebar.

The closeness between the sightings and the tragedy led some people to interpret the creature as a harbinger of the disaster. Keel's book "The Mothman Prophecies" (1975) tied the episode to sightings of lights, to "men in black" and to omens. According to Daniel Reed (Skeptical Inquirer, 2022), some say the creature caused the collapse and others that it came to warn of it. Point Pleasant today has a statue of Mothman and holds an annual festival. The sources cited record no photograph or physical remains of the creature; the account rests on testimony of the time and on press coverage.

Why it matters

The NTSB report on the Silver Bridge does not mention any sightings. Don Karol, an NTSB investigator, wrote in 2017 that no evidence was ever found connecting Mothman to the collapse.

Every explanation proposed for the sightings is a bird. In 1966, biologist Robert L. Smith of West Virginia University told the Associated Press that the descriptions fit a sandhill crane, with a wingspan of more than seven feet and reddish skin around the eyes. According to Reed, Ohio University officials said at the time that none had ever been seen in the region (Athens Messenger, November 20, 1966), and Audubon notes that the species isn't native to West Virginia and that the witnesses described a creature with no neck. Reed found that claim inaccurate: the GBIF database holds 51 sandhill crane records since 1938 within 60 miles of Point Pleasant, although the species does not usually stop in the area, against 5,576 great blue heron records since 1907. Reed considers the heron more likely.

Joe Nickell attributes the sightings to barred owls. According to Audubon, a healthy population of the species lives in the reserve that now occupies the "TNT Area"; its eye reflects an intense red in headlights and its flight is silent. Nickell also points out that at night it is very hard to judge the size of something at an unknown distance: if you misjudge the distance, you misjudge the height by the same proportion. Against the eyeshine explanation, Reed cites Benjamin Radford: of nearly a dozen people who claim to have seen the creature or to have some personal connection to it, only one mentions glowing red eyes, and she did not see them herself but relayed her father's account.

Keel, who interviewed the witnesses, concluded in 1968 that the creature was tied to UFOs "in some very special and terrifying way". The original testimonies survive mainly in the press of the time and in Keel's interviews.
