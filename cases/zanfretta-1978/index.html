🇮🇹1978–1981·Marzano, Torriglia (Genoa, Italy)

Italy.Zanfretta case · a night watchman reports meeting beings

On the night of December 6 to 7, 1978, night watchman Pier Fortunato Zanfretta radioed his control room during an encounter with an object and a being about three meters tall at Marzano, Torriglia (Genoa). The Carabinieri telex of December 8 recorded no witness to the encounter, a horseshoe-shaped imprint about two meters across on the ground and a reliability rating of "buono" (good); the January 1979 report added more than 50 residents who had been seeing luminous objects for months, and the Genoa magistrates' court shelved the file for lack of any offense. Eleven "abductions" (1978–1981) recovered under hypnosis followed.

What happened

On the night of December 6, 1978, Pier Fortunato Zanfretta, a 25-year-old night watchman for a private security firm, was on his usual round in Marzano, a hamlet of the municipality of Torriglia in the mountains above Genoa. According to their report, around 00:33 on the 7th Zanfretta saw flashlights behind a shuttered villa known as "Casa Nostra" and, thinking of thieves, went into the neighboring meadow. Reaching the back corner, he felt a blow to his back and, turning, faced a humanoid being about three meters tall, "grosso e orrendo" (bulky and horrendous), and a strongly luminous object that soon took off vertically with a whistle.

The telex the Carabinieri sent on December 8 tells the sequence differently. Behind the villa he briefly saw a very strong light source and, coming closer, an oval, red, dazzling object more than ten meters across. Frightened, he headed back toward his car, parked about a hundred meters away, alerting the control room, and right afterward he was struck by a "luminous form" of human appearance about three meters tall, which climbed into the object; the object took off vertically with a loud whistle and vanished at high speed toward the southwest. Later accounts added wrinkled skin and triangular yellow eyes. His colleagues, who went looking for him after about an hour of radio silence, found him on the spot unconscious, agitated and in a state of severe shock.

At noon on December 7, almost twelve hours after the encounter, Dr. Cereda, owner of Val Bisagno, the Genoa security company Zanfretta worked for, notified the Torriglia Carabinieri station. The telex, sent by the Carabinieri company at Chiavari to the Interior Ministry and the Air Force General Staff, recorded a clear sky, no witness to the encounter and a horseshoe-shaped imprint about two meters across on the ground, and rated the reliability "buono" (good). The same telex recorded that earlier that evening, around 19:30, someone on the terrace of their home at Albora di Propata had briefly seen a luminous flying object, with "fair" reliability.

On January 3, 1979, brigadiere Antonio Nucchi, commander of the Torriglia station, sent his report to the Genoa magistrates' court. He had inspected the site as soon as he heard the news and describes in the meadow beside the villa a horseshoe-shaped imprint about 2 meters across, as in the telex, and 3 meters long, a measurement the telex did not give. He says Zanfretta always appeared very hot and sweaty, especially on his head, despite the hoarfrost at Marzano, and that the car's bodywork was hot "as if it had been left in the summer sun." An examination at the neurology department of San Martino Hospital found him "sanissimo di mente" (perfectly sane), apart from slight hypertension, and he did not drink or use narcotics. Nucchi had known him personally since 1971, and he had always been considered a serious person.

The report also records a second episode. On the night of December 27, at Scoffera, Zanfretta's car stopped responding and drove some three kilometers on its own; there, in the earth at the roadside, two heeled shoe prints 45 to 50 centimeters long with concave soles could be seen. It adds that in the Torriglia area, for some months, many residents had been seeing very large, luminous flying objects, among them gamekeeper Barbacetto and a Guardia di Finanza brigadiere named Esposito, with a list of more than 50 people. None of them witnessed that night.

Meanwhile, journalist Rino Di Stefano, who covered the case from the start and later published a book in several editions, proposed putting Zanfretta under regressive hypnosis to "remember" what had happened. The first session, on December 23, 1978, was conducted by medical hypnotist Mauro Moretti before journalists and ufologists. Under hypnosis, Zanfretta said the beings had taken him aboard and examined him, and that he had communicated with them. Between 1978 and 1981 he claimed eleven such encounters, with ever more detail: a mother object, a glass sphere supposedly handed to him as "proof," messages. The magistrate ordered the file shelved for lack of any offense on December 31, 1979, and the closure was registered on January 11, 1980.

Why it matters

Zanfretta's first night ended up in official papers written within weeks, something rare for a contact account: an alert to the control room during the event, a Carabinieri telex that rated the account's reliability "buono," an imprint measured in the grass, a neurological examination that found him healthy and the report of a brigadiere who had known him for years and considered him a serious person. Nobody else saw the encounter, and the residents who had been seeing lights for months were not there that night.

Each physical trace has an ordinary explanation. The skeptical researchers summarized in the English Wikipedia hold that the imprint in the grass matched the spot where the villa's owners tied their horse during the day, that the heat of his clothes was consistent with his having sat in the car for a while, that the footprints of the second episode were consistent with simple puddles, and that third-party sightings were never authoritatively verified. The National UFO Center (CUN) stated on several occasions that it did not consider the story reliable.

Nearly everything else comes from hypnosis. Critics point out that the sessions were done without precautions, which invites suggestion and false memories, and most of them, with no doubt about Zanfretta's good faith, concluded that the hypnosis had invalidated his reconstruction of events. They also showed that several supposed pieces of evidence had prosaic explanations that outlets with a commercial interest left out or presented in a biased way. The press turned Zanfretta into a celebrity, and the later encounters, told under hypnosis, can be neither confirmed nor refuted.
