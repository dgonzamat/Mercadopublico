19:T409,On the night of 6 December 1978, Pier Fortunato Zanfretta, a 25-year-old night watchman for a private security firm, was on his usual round in Marzano, a hamlet of the municipality of Torriglia in the mountains above Genoa. According to the Torriglia Carabinieri report, around 00:33 he saw torchlights behind a shuttered villa known as 'Casa Nostra' and, thinking of thieves, went into the neighbouring meadow; reaching the back corner he felt a blow to his back and, turning, faced a humanoid being about three metres tall, "bulky and horrendous", and a strongly luminous object that soon took off vertically with a whistle. According to the chronology published later, Zanfretta spoke by radio with the company's dispatch during the event, and later accounts added wrinkled skin, triangular yellow eyes and a red, oval object more than ten metres across that radiated heat. His colleagues, who went looking for him after about an hour of radio silence, found him in a state of severe shock, unable to speak coherently for a while.1a:T945,What sets Zanfretta apart from most contact accounts is the institutional response. On 8 December the Carabinieri company at Chiavari sent a telex to the Interior Ministry and the Air Force General Staff: it recorded no witness to the encounter, noted a horseshoe-shaped imprint about two metres across on the ground, and rated the reliability 'buono' (good). On 3 January 1979, brigadiere Antonio Nucchi, commander of the Torriglia station, sent the Genoa magistrates' court a report describing the imprint in the meadow beside the villa, about 2 metres across and 3 long; it says Zanfretta always appeared very hot and sweaty, especially on his head, despite the hoarfrost at Marzano, and his car's bodywork was hot "as if it had been left in the summer sun"; that in a second episode, on the night of 27 December at Scoffera, the car stopped responding and drove some three kilometres on its own, and that there, in the earth at the roadside, two heeled shoe prints 45 to 50 centimetres long with concave soles could be seen; that an examination at the neurology department of San Martino Hospital found him 'sanissimo di mente' (perfectly sane), apart from slight hypertension; and that he did not drink or use narcotics; the brigadiere adds that he had known him personally since 1971 and that he had always been considered a serious person. It adds that in the Torriglia area, for some months, many residents had been seeing very large, luminous flying objects, among them gamekeeper Barbacetto and a Guardia di Finanza brigadiere named Esposito, with a list of more than 50 people: they are not witnesses of that night. The magistrate ordered the file shelved for lack of any offence on 31 December 1979, and the closure was registered on 11 January 1980. The sceptical researchers summarised by Wikipedia hold that the grass circle matched the spot where the villa's owners tied their horse during the day, that the heat of his clothes was consistent with his having sat in the car for a while, that the footprints of the second episode were consistent with simple puddles, and that third-party sightings were never authoritatively verified; the National UFO Centre (CUN) stated on several occasions that it did not consider the story reliable.
