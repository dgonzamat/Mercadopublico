Record of the event

What happened

The video reached the public under the title "4 UAP Formation Iran 26 Aug 2022 over water" (UAP stands for unidentified anomalous phenomena). According to AARO, the Pentagon office that studies these phenomena, that title was given by whoever uploaded the file to a classified network in June 2024, and it is not an official designation. The office only assesses as likely, without confirming it, that the footage comes from an infrared sensor aboard a U.S. military platform within Central Command's (CENTCOM) area of responsibility in 2022, a region that includes the Persian Gulf and waters near Iran. It confirms neither the exact location nor the country.

The DVIDS page gives August 26, 2022 as the date taken and "IR" as the location code. The clip was uploaded there on May 22, 2026, in the second drop of PURSUE, the Pentagon's program for making its files on these phenomena public. It was released with no coordinates, no platform identification and no statement from any crew.

According to AARO's description, between seconds 1 and 4 of the video four distinct areas of contrast cross the sensor's field of view together. They enter from the lower third of the left side of the screen and exit near the center of the bottom, within about three seconds. Between seconds 5 and 6, a fifth area of contrast enters separately from the upper left corner. AARO describes a 20-second video, but what was released is shorter: 9 seconds according to the page on DVIDS, the Pentagon's public media archive, and about 10 according to the French amateur blog OVNI 66.

That blog reproduced AARO's description on June 1, 2026 and published its own analysis of the clip on June 7. It explains that in these black-and-white infrared videos only areas of contrast, hotter or colder, are visible, with no detail of what is filmed. In the footage it first makes out three ships with their wakes, which AARO's description does not mention, and then three or four grouped dark shapes crossing the screen diagonally, from the lower-left quarter to the center of the bottom, with no wake. The blog takes it for a formation flight.

Slowing the clip to 50, 25 and 12.5%, the blog's author believes the shapes undulate "as if their movement were caused by flapping wings." The blog concludes that with color film a group of birds could probably have been identified. According to the blog, the shapes seem to move faster than the ships because they are closer to the sensor. It adds that in an "original version," which it does not identify, another shape, perhaps a bird or an insect, crosses the screen even faster.

The clip is part of a batch of 50 videos that AARO found on that classified network while searching for 51 records that eight members of the House of Representatives had requested on March 6, 2026. Separately, on March 31, 2026, in a letter distinct from that request, a task force of the House Oversight Committee devoted to the declassification of federal secrets asked Secretary of War Pete Hegseth for 46 videos by April 14. The first on the list was "4 UAP formation – Iran, 8/26/22," nearly the same label as this clip's title.

The Metabunk forum posted the letter on April 1 and attributes it to Rep. Anna Paulina Luna. On June 13, 2026, its administrator, Mick West, compared the list with AARO's data. He concluded that the list follows the order of the drop's 50 videos and that its first line is this clip, which also matches by title. Some lines group several clips and a few match only by position. The match is West's cross-check: the official sources cited do not mention it.

Why it matters

The only word that places the video in Iran, and the one that presents it as a "formation" of objects, was written by an unidentified person when uploading the file. That label reached the House letter almost intact, and the letter requested the video by that name on March 31, 2026, seven weeks before the clip was made public. The uploader's reading thus reached a congressional request before the public could see the footage.

AARO itself warns that many materials in the batch lack a substantiated chain of custody, without saying which, and that its description reflects no analytical judgment or factual determination. The office released the clip without a conclusion, and the only analysis among the linked sources was done by an amateur working from the public footage. No official source supports the amateur's reading.

That analysis also shows the sensor's limit. In a black-and-white infrared image, a flock of birds and a group of unknown objects can look the same: areas of contrast with no detail. The clue the blog proposes for telling them apart, the wing beat it believes it sees when slowing the video, can be checked in what was released, but not in the rest of the 20 seconds AARO describes.
