2a:T55a,On 31 July 1995, at night, between 20:00 and 21:00 local time, two aircraft from Buenos Aires were converging a few minutes apart on San Carlos de Bariloche's Teniente Luis Candelaria International Airport: the Boeing 727 of Aerolíneas Argentinas flight AR 674, with Captain Jorge Polanco, First Officer Carlos Dortona, Flight Engineer Jorge Allende and a company pilot riding in the cockpit, and a Gendarmería Nacional Piper PA-31T Cheyenne, registration GN-705, with Commanders Juan Domingo Gaitán and Rubén Cipuzak and a mechanic. The tower told them the city had no power and the airport was running on its generator. On the outbound leg of the approach procedure, the 727 crew saw a light 'at eleven o'clock' and reported it to the tower, which answered: 'OK, received, that traffic should now be forty miles from Bariloche'. Already on final, about ten miles out, the 727 transmitted: 'Yes, we have an object on our right flying in formation with us, and above we can see the other aircraft. The object has two bright flashing lights... I don't know, we think it's a flying saucer'. To the airport chief, Polanco later stated that he saw 'green lights at its ends and a strong orange light in the centre', and over the years he has described a sighting of about seventeen minutes, with the object by the wingtip. The episode became known as the 'Polanco Case'.2b:T6b1,The night had two separate blackouts, and they should be kept apart. The first hit the whole city. The head of the Bariloche district of Energía Río Negro, engineer Etchegoyen, told researchers Heriberto Janosch and Virgilio di Pelino on 2 August 1995 that it was caused by a switching error at the transformer station that receives 132,000 volts from Alicurá, which overloaded the two transformers and tripped them; the Bariloche Electricity Cooperative also attributed it to human error, and the local press and the Air Force date it to 20:17 (Janosch placed it between 20:05 and 20:15). That ordinary cause is on record. The second blackout is the one that coincided with the approach: at 20:47, with the 727 on final, the airport's generator went out, and with it the runway lights, and the aircraft had to go around and climb to hold.
