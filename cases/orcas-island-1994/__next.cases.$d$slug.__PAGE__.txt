13:T434,The central account comes from a group of six to eight people who, on the night of Saturday, September 24, 1994, had gathered on a beach on Orcas Island, the largest of the San Juan Islands, in the Puget Sound of Washington State. They had assembled to watch the starry sky. According to the report collected by the National UFO Reporting Center (NUFORC), to their left and high in the sky two or three bright, brief blue flashes appeared, which those present described with a color comparable to that of old photographic flash cubes. According to the same summary, a bright light with a small fiery tail then shot out and zigzagged down towards the earth, disappearing from view about 60 degrees above the horizon. According to the testimony, the flashes were not accompanied by a clearly defined solid object; according to the summary of the NUFORC record reproduced by search engines (the page could not be consulted directly), the group instead heard a low hum, as if an outboard motor were running out on the lake, which lasted about an hour or until they left the beach.14:T42b,What distinguishes the episode from an isolated report is corroboration. NUFORC (the civilian report-intake center based in Washington State, directed by Peter Davenport) recorded, according to that summary, five additional reports from independent sources about the same event. The NUFORC index, as captured in a public copy of its database, dates the sighting to 20:00 on 24 September 1994, gives it a duration of an hour and a half, summarizes it as 'Bright blue flashes in the sky and zigzagging light' and lists it as posted on 26 November 2003, nine years later. That concurrence of uncoordinated observers describing a simultaneous luminous phenomenon lifts the case above single testimony: whatever the cause, something visible happened in the area's sky that night.
