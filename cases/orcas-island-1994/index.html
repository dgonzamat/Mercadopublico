🇺🇸1994·Orcas Island, San Juan Islands, Washington

United States.Orcas Island · stargazers on a beach see blue flashes in the sky

On the night of September 24, 1994 (an approximate date), a group of six to eight people stargazing on a beach on Orcas Island, in Washington's San Juan Islands, saw two or three bright blue flashes, the color of old flashcubes, and then a light that zigzagged down. NUFORC, a civilian sighting center, received, according to its director, five more independent reports of the same event, and, by the same account, at least one house in West Sound had electrical problems at the same time. No instrument recorded the event.

Why no ordinary cause is identified
No source proposes a cause: the NUFORC record (no. 33261, posted in 2003) describes blue flashes, a light that zigzagged down and a hum like an outboard motor, and its director's note mentions five independent reports and electrical anomalies in a West Sound home. These details come from the database copy cited in the document; the original page could not be checked again. The transformer arc and the bolide are readings made for this case, not a source's. The absence of a published analysis is not evidence against an ordinary cause: in 2024 AARO resolved 174 cases, all "to prosaic objects," though "many other cases remain unresolved." (source ↗)

What happened

The central account comes from a group of six to eight people (women, according to one member's report) who, on the night of Saturday, September 24, 1994 (an approximate date), had gathered on a beach on Orcas Island, the largest of the San Juan Islands, in Washington State, to watch the starry sky.

According to the report collected by the National UFO Reporting Center (NUFORC), the civilian report-intake center based in Washington State and directed by Peter Davenport, to their left and high in the sky two or three bright, brief blue flashes appeared, in a color comparable to that of old photographic flash cubes. Then a bright light, which the witness took for a satellite, shot out with a small fiery tail, zigzagged down toward the earth and disappeared from view about 60 degrees above the horizon.

The flashes were not accompanied by a clearly defined solid object. The group heard instead a low hum, which the witness likened to an outboard motor running out on the lake, and which lasted about an hour or until they left the beach.

One of the witnesses wrote the report from memory and sent it to NUFORC on November 20, 2003; the center posted it on November 26, nine years after the event. The date is approximate: Davenport's note says he set it as September 24, 1994. That note adds that the center received five other reports from independent sources about the same event and that at least one home in West Sound, a locality on the island, had electrical anomalies at the same time.

The NUFORC index, as captured in a public copy of its database, summarizes the report as "Bright blue flashes in the sky and zigzagging light," places it at 8 p.m., with the shape listed as "light," and gives it a duration of an hour and a half, without saying whether that covers the whole event or only the hum.

Why it matters

The account's strength is that it does not rest on one person: several saw the flashes at the same time from the beach, and the center says it received five more reports of the same event. Its weakness is the record: what is known comes from a report written from memory nine years later, with a date set by someone else, and no instrument captured the flashes or the light that zigzagged down.

No source proposes a cause: the NUFORC record describes the flashes, the hum and the zigzagging light without explaining them. Two conventional causes could be considered, though no source proposes them: the flashover of a distribution transformer, which can produce brief blue-white bursts and at the same time disturb the power supply of connected homes, and a bolide, a bright meteor entering the atmosphere, which can be seen at the same time from scattered points. Neither could be confirmed or ruled out, for lack of a technical record.
