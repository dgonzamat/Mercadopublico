25:T4d7,The Pine Gap Joint Defence Facility was established through a bilateral treaty signed between Australia and the United States on 9 December 1966, on a desert plain about 18 km southwest of Alice Springs (Northern Territory). The facility's initial public name was 'Joint Defence Space Research Facility', and its declared justification was 'space research' and upper-atmosphere research — a characterization later sources describe as deliberately misleading, intended to avoid revealing its true mission. The treaty stipulated that, after an initial nine-year period, either party could cancel the agreement with one year's notice; in practice it has been continuously renewed for nearly six decades. The base became fully operational on 19 June 1970 with the launch of its first associated satellite, code-named Rhyolite, dedicated to intercepting Soviet transmissions, including the telemetry of the Soviet missile program. For its first decades the station was run under CIA direction. Today it is one of the central nodes of the 5-Eyes Signals Intelligence (SIGINT) satellite network — Australia signed the UKUSA signals-intelligence sharing agreement in 1956 — along with facilities such as Menwith Hill (UK) and Misawa (Japan).26:T503,The UAP dimension of the case is of a different nature and far weaker epistemically, and it is worth marking that clearly. The region around Pine Gap is one of Australia's most sustained UAP hotspots: Wycliffe Well, about 380 km to the north, self-styles as the 'UFO capital of Australia' and has accumulated reports since World War II. In December 1989, three men returning from a hunting expedition near Pine Gap reported observing a 'camouflaged door' opening on a rock formation, revealing a metallic grey disc that departed at high speed — a purely testimonial account, without instrumentation or independent corroboration. Australian journalist Ross Coulthart has publicly maintained in his investigations that the base has been the scene of recurring UAP incidents, citing anonymous Australian Defence Force sources. None of these incidents has been officially acknowledged by the Australian government or the Pentagon.
