25:T6c0,The Pine Gap Joint Defence Facility was established through a bilateral treaty signed between Australia and the United States on 9 December 1966, on a desert plain about 18 km southwest of Alice Springs (Northern Territory). The founding agreement (Australian Treaty Series 1966, No. 17) calls it a «joint defence space research facility» and commits both governments to operate «a facility for general defence research in the space field», on land that remains Australian and «shall be considered a secure area». By 1998, the national interest analysis presented to Parliament to extend the agreement already described it as «an intelligence gathering facility», according to the October 1999 report of the Joint Standing Committee on Treaties. The treaty stipulated that, after an initial nine-year period, either party could cancel the agreement with one year's notice; in practice it was amended in 1977 and 1988, and the exchange of notes of 4 June 1998 extended it for ten years from 16 November 1998 and thereafter until terminated, on three years' notice. Its first associated satellite, RHYOLITE 1, was launched on 19 June 1970; the GCAT catalog assigns it to the NRO and CIA. Its job was to intercept Soviet transmissions, especially missile-test telemetry, which according to Professor Desmond Ball before the Treaties Committee in 1999 remains one of the four categories of signal the base collects, along with radars, satellite links and microwave emissions.
