32:T57d,The Pentagon advised against shooting it down over land because of its size: according to Dalton, the balloon was about 200 feet (60 meters) tall with a jetliner-sized payload; the White House compared the payload to three school buses. Lieutenant General Douglas Sims of the Joint Staff testified that the risk of Chinese collection was assessed as low to moderate and the risk to people on the ground as moderate to significant, which is why it was brought down in territorial waters off South Carolina. On February 1, with the balloon over Montana, commanders considered downing it there and ruled it out because of the ground risk. On February 4, at about 14:39 Eastern time, an F-22 Raptor from the 1st Fighter Wing (Joint Base Langley-Eustis, Virginia) fired a single AIM-9X Sidewinder missile from about 58,000 feet at the balloon, which was flying between 60,000 and 65,000 feet, and brought it down about six miles offshore, off Myrtle Beach; it was the first air-to-air kill in the F-22's history. Recovery was led by the Navy, with the dock landing ship USS Carter Hall (LSD-50) as lead ship, the destroyer USS Oscar Austin (DDG-79), the cruiser USS Philippine Sea, divers from Explosive Ordnance Disposal Group 2 and the Coast Guard. The wreckage lay in about 45 feet (14 meters) of water, and the recovered material was handed to federal agents and analyzed at the FBI laboratory in Quantico.33:T625,China acknowledged the craft on February 3: its Foreign Ministry said it was a civilian research airship, used for 'mainly meteorological' purposes, which because of the Westerlies and its limited self-steering capability had deviated from its course, and regretted its unintended entry due to force majeure. Washington rejected that. Kirby, the National Security Council's coordinator for strategic communications, said there was no question the system was designed to surveil, that the balloon had sped up, slowed down and maneuvered a little to get a look at sensitive military sites, and that it belonged to a PLA-linked high-altitude balloon program that had overflown dozens of countries; the State Department, according to press reports, described antennas able to collect and geolocate communications and solar panels large enough to power several sensors. Dalton testified that balloons from the same program had briefly transited the continental United States at least three times during the prior administration (the Pentagon added another early in Biden's) and Kirby said that administration had not detected them. The press later reported that some of those episodes had been logged at the time as unidentified phenomena; that link to the UAP category comes from the press and officials it quoted, not from any AARO document we have located.
