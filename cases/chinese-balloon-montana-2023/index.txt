Lieutenant General Douglas Sims of the Joint Staff testified that the risk of Chinese collection was assessed as low to moderate and the risk to people on the ground as moderate to significant, which is why it was brought down in territorial waters off South Carolina. On February 1, with the balloon over Montana, commanders considered downing it there and ruled it out because of the ground risk. On February 4, at about 14:39 Eastern time, an F-22 Raptor from the 1st Fighter Wing (Joint Base Langley-Eustis, Virginia) fired a single AIM-9X Sidewinder missile from about 58,000 feet at the balloon, which was flying between 60,000 and 65,000 feet, and brought it down about six miles offshore, off Myrtle Beach; it was the first air-to-air kill in the F-22's history. Recovery was led by the Navy, with the dock landing ship USS Carter Hall (LSD-50) as lead ship, the destroyer USS Oscar Austin (DDG-79), the cruiser USS Philippine Sea, divers from Explosive Ordnance Disposal Group 2 and the Coast Guard.
