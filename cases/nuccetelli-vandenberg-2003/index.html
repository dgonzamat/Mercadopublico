United States.Vandenberg · contractors see a glowing red square over the base

Jeffrey Nuccetelli, an Air Force military police officer for sixteen years, testified under oath before the House of Representatives in September 2025 that between 2003 and 2005 there were five incidents with unidentified objects at Vandenberg Air Force Base, California, home of the missile defense program. In the most cited, on October 14, 2003, Boeing contractors reported a huge glowing red square hovering silently over two missile defense sites. That night he responded to another incident, a rectangular craft seen by five witnesses, and on another occasion, off duty, he saw a blue-and-white orb that appeared and disappeared.

What happened

Jeffrey Nuccetelli served sixteen years on active duty as a U.S. Air Force military police officer. According to the written testimony he submitted under oath in 2025, between 2003 and 2005 there were five incidents with unidentified objects at Vandenberg Air Force Base, California, home of the National Missile Defense Project. They happened, he says, during a series of launches the National Reconnaissance Office deemed "the most important in twenty-five years." He states that each incident had multiple witnesses and was documented, investigated and reported up the chain of command: "we sent information up, but no guidance came down." He says he witnessed one and investigated others, and that six other service members, all direct eyewitnesses, provided the information he presents.

The first came on October 14, 2003: Boeing contractors reported a massive, glowing red square hovering silently over two missile defense sites. After several minutes it drifted east and vanished behind the hills. According to Nuccetelli, this episode, later known as the "Vandenberg Red Square," was referenced by Representative Anna Paulina Luna at the first public hearing on the topic, and the official Air Force records documenting it are now held by AARO, the Pentagon office that investigates these phenomena, and by the FBI. That claim comes only from him: no known independent source corroborates it.

That same night, while Nuccetelli was on duty, security guards at a critical launch site reported a bright, fast-moving object. As he responded, panic erupted over the radio: "It's coming right at us… now it's right here!" and moments later: "It took off — it's gone!" When he arrived, he found five shaken witnesses who described a rectangular craft larger than a football field: it had hovered silently for about 45 seconds and then shot away at incredible speed.

About a week later, another patrol reported a strange light over the ocean heading toward the base. Taking it for an unannounced aircraft, they declared an emergency and launched an armed response. Before forces arrived, the object descended, hovered briefly or landed, and vanished instantly. According to Nuccetelli, the witness was later threatened and pressured to stay quiet, which discouraged further reports. Things stayed quiet until 2005, when a patrol reported a massive triangular craft, larger than a C-130 transport plane, hovering low over the main base, which drifted silently across the installation and disappeared into the night.

The fifth incident is his own, and the testimony gives it no date. One night, off duty, he was in his backyard with two friends when they noticed what first looked like a satellite, but with an unusual rhythmic pulse. It began maneuvering "like a butterfly," vanishing and reappearing in different patches of sky. It dropped until it was about 200 feet over his house: a large orb of blue-white light that appeared solid, with the light not seeming to radiate from its surface. It vanished again, reappeared, and finally accelerated and receded over about 40 seconds until it became indistinguishable from the stars.

Nuccetelli told all this on September 9, 2025, before the House Task Force on the Declassification of Federal Secrets, at the hearing "Restoring Public Trust Through UAP Transparency and Whistleblower Protection," alongside Dylan Borland and Navy Senior Chief Petty Officer Alexandro Wiggins, who testified about their own cases (↗, ↗). In 2026 he joined Vanguard Enterprise (↗) as development director, the nonprofit UAP whistleblower support organization co-founded by Borland and Matthew Brown.

Why it matters

According to Nuccetelli, the five incidents took place over the facilities where the United States was testing its missile defense, each with several witnesses identified by role (Boeing contractors, guards, patrols), and were reported up the chain of command at the time. But everything known about them comes from his word, given twenty years later: there is no photo, video, radar record or declassified document that a third party can check, and the "official Air Force records" he places with AARO and the FBI have not been released.

In 2003-2005 Vandenberg was an active missile test range, and Nuccetelli himself places the events amid major launches. That opens an ordinary explanation specific to this site: reentry vehicles, decoys or debris from intercept tests can produce strange, unexpected shapes and lights for anyone unfamiliar with that technical context. It is a general hypothesis, not an explanation anyone has proposed for any one of these incidents.

The allegation that a witness was threatened into silence is, if true, more serious than any of the sightings, and it has no independent verification either. The only incident Nuccetelli saw in person, the orb over his house, is also the least verifiable: a light at night seen by three people, off duty, with no record of any kind.
