🇺🇾2026·Southern coast of Uruguay, from the Río de la Plata to the Atlantic

Uruguay.Uruguay · residents in nine departments see lights in formation

Between January 13 and February 15, 2026, the Uruguayan Air Force commission that takes UFO reports (CRIDOVNI) logged 25 reports of white lights crossing the night sky in nine departments, mostly to the south, over the Río de la Plata and the Atlantic. Residents and tourists in Punta del Este and elsewhere shared photos and videos. On February 16, after checking them against aerospace data, the Air Force concluded they were Starlink satellites and other low-orbit networks. A radio station in Maldonado spoke of about 30 reports.

What happened

The Commission for Receiving and Investigating Reports of Unidentified Flying Objects (CRIDOVNI) is part of the Uruguayan Air Force and was created on August 7, 1979. According to the Air Force, it receives, analyzes and evaluates reports of unidentified aerial phenomena, and in each case considers whether there could have been a hoax, a conventional phenomenon or an illusion. Between mid-January and mid-February 2026, at the height of summer and of the tourist season, it received a series of reports of lights crossing the night sky.

One hot spot was Punta del Este, in the department of Maldonado. Listeners of the local radio station Cadena del Mar told the station's journalist Marcelo Umpiérrez that they saw up to five points of light per sighting, moving from the southwest toward the south. According to the accounts the station gathered, the lights seemed to move erratically, vanished at times and reappeared in other formations, which is why many people did not take them for airplanes or for the satellites they knew.

According to the EFE news agency, tourists and residents posted photos and videos on social media and sent them to the local press. As early as January 21, Montevideo Portal reported that residents of the beach town of Las Flores, also in Maldonado, had been photographing lights they had not seen before for weeks. For several days, the station said, people speculated that they were UFOs.

On February 16, the Air Force released the CRIDOVNI summary, which EFE and the Uruguayan press reported. The commission had received 25 reports of "avistamientos nocturnos de luces en vuelo" (nighttime sightings of lights in flight) between January 13 and February 15, from nine departments: Montevideo, Canelones, Maldonado, Rocha, San José, Rivera, Colonia, Lavalleja and Cerro Largo. Seven are in the south and east of the country; Rivera and Cerro Largo are in the north and northeast. According to the Air Force, the witnesses agreed in describing "luces de color blanco en desplazamiento" (moving white lights), seen toward the south, southeast and southwest, mostly over the Río de la Plata and the Atlantic Ocean. They saw them low or midway up the sky, between 10 and 40 degrees above the horizon.

The commission evaluated the accounts, photos and videos from the witnesses and checked them against "los registros y datos aeroespaciales disponibles" (the available aerospace records and data); according to Cadena del Mar, it compared orbital paths and times. It concluded that the lights were passes of satellites of the Starlink system, run by the U.S. company SpaceX, and of other platforms in low orbit. According to the newspaper El Megáfono, the commission added that such reports tend to rise when constellations of satellites make visible passes, which surprise people who are not familiar with the sight. The reports that reproduce the summary do not say which satellite matches each report, or why some lights seemed to go out and come back.

The figures differ between outlets. EFE, the Medios Públicos news site and El Megáfono, which reproduce the Air Force summary, give 25 reports, and Infobae attributes the same figure to the Canal 10 newscast Subrayado. Cadena del Mar wrote "alrededor de 30" (around 30), without saying where the number came from. The gap remains unexplained; 25 is the figure in the official summary as those reports quote it.

In those five weeks, SpaceX launched ten batches of Starlink satellites, according to the launch catalog kept by astronomer Jonathan McDowell. Three lifted off from Cape Canaveral, Florida, on January 14, 18 and 30; seven from Vandenberg Space Force Base, California, between January 22 and February 15. Each batch carried 24 to 29 satellites, which at first travel together, one behind another, at about 250 kilometers up, before climbing to their working orbit.

The orbits of the Florida batches reach farther south than Montevideo, and the California batches pass over almost the whole planet, so both could pass over Uruguay. Launches on those dates do not prove that every light was a satellite: the satellite also had to be above the witness's horizon at the time of the report and still lit by the sun, even though it was already night on the ground. According to Cadena del Mar, the commission compared times, but the news stories that carry its summary do not publish that check for each report.

Why it matters

The case shows how a state UFO office works when a wave of sightings arrives through social media. The Air Force gathered five weeks of reports along with the witnesses' photos and videos, checked them against orbital data and released a conclusion on February 16, one day after the last report, while the images were still circulating. The Uruguayan and regional press ran it under similar headlines: they were not UFOs, they were satellites.

It was not the first time. Cadena del Mar recalled that three years earlier there had been similar reports in the east of the country. In 2023 the Air Force published a case of the same kind, though in the west: lights seen at around 12:30 a.m. on February 11, 2023, from the Almirón hot springs and the Paysandú airport, to the south and about 15 degrees above the horizon. In May 2023, CRIDOVNI attributed them to Starlink satellites, as it did other reports from various towns between February 10 and 13. What has been published does not show whether the reports in the east that the station recalled were the Paysandú ones, in the west, or others.

The weak point of the case is the detail. The conclusion groups 25 reports from nine departments and five weeks under a single explanation, and the published summary does not allow it to be checked one by one: it gives neither the dates nor the times of the reports, nor the witnesses' names, nor the images the commission analyzed. Accounts such as the lights that vanished and reappeared over Punta del Este are explained only as a group. The explanation is the one put forward by the only institution that saw all the material, and no published analysis contradicts it.

In those same days, according to the Telenoche newscast as cited by Infobae, the Air Force reached a preliminary agreement with the U.S. All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) to exchange analysis software and equipment and, possibly, to set up observation stations. In July 2026, the Air Force reported working meetings and technical workshops between CRIDOVNI and AARO on methods for ruling out conventional phenomena.
