14:T627,Between 21 January and 26 February 1984 the first organized field campaign, Project Hessdalen, was mounted, led by Erling Strand with support from researchers at the Norwegian Defence Research Establishment and the universities of Oslo and Bergen: volunteers (about 22 on the test weekend, sometimes only one or two) operated radar, a seismograph, a magnetometer, a spectrum analyser, diffraction-grating cameras, Geiger counters, a laser and infrared viewers. They gathered 188 reports of lights; 53 were scored as leaving a non-conventional explanation open, but only 6 a score of F9 or higher, and the report judges that "too little to draw any conclusion". The spectra photographed were "too weak to say what kind of spectrum it is". The radar recorded 36 echoes, of which only three were probably also seen as lights, and the report cites a speed of about 8,500 m/s tracked by radar; the magnetometer disturbances were perhaps due to the phenomenon, "we have to do more measurements". The Geiger counter, seismograph and IR viewer recorded nothing. The most striking result was the laser: pointed nine times at a flashing light, eight times the light changed its flashing sequence, a result Strand himself calls "very surprising" and a short sample. His conclusion: "We have not found out what this phenomenon is".
