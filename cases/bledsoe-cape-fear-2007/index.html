🇺🇸2007·Cape Fear River, near Fayetteville, North Carolina

United States.Chris Bledsoe · orange lights by the Cape Fear River

On January 8, 2007, while fishing by the Cape Fear River in North Carolina, contractor Chris Bledsoe and four companions, his 17-year-old son and three subcontractors, reported three orange objects that appeared to land, and that same night an alleged encounter with an entity at his home. The civilian group MUFON investigated with regressive hypnosis, a psychiatric evaluation and a polygraph that flagged signs of deception in part of Bledsoe's answers. He has since published books and appeared in documentaries, and claims NASA investigated him for eight years, which no document or named official has corroborated.

What happened

On January 8, 2007, Chris Bledsoe Sr., a construction contractor and commercial pilot, was fishing on the banks of the Cape Fear River, outside Fayetteville, North Carolina, with his 17-year-old son, Chris Bledsoe Jr., and three men who worked for him as framing subcontractors: Donny Ackerman, Gene Robinson and David McDonald. By his account, Bledsoe walked about 70 meters from the group to be alone for a while and saw two bright orange objects, which he described as "two setting suns" with fire shooting off them, joined by a third that "took its place" beside them. Per the summary of the MUFON investigation, the objects descended and appeared to land some 100 to 150 yards away, across the river. The frightened witnesses fled.

Bledsoe estimated he had been away from the group for about 20 minutes; his companions said he had been gone 3 to 4 hours. His son went looking for him after waiting two hours and was himself missing for another two hours before reappearing. That night, per the family's account, there were two more episodes: the son said small creatures watched and paralyzed him in the woods near the house, and the father described an encounter on his property with a "glass-like" being roughly a meter tall with red eyes.

The report Bledsoe himself filed shortly afterwards with MUFON's case system, reproduced by researcher Lon Strickler, tells another version. The group had spent the afternoon fishing; he walked up the track alone, saw three large orange balls come down and hover over the trees "maybe a mile away or closer", and watched them for ten to fifteen minutes before going back. His son came out of the woods saying he had been hiding from "aliens". Then the five saw six to eight lights converge in the sky and three white lights come down across the river; as they fled they saw a white egg-shaped object with spikes hovering about 20 feet over the road, and other drivers stopped to look at lights. Back home, around 1 or 2 a.m., he and his son saw by the kennel a figure at most four feet tall, with a clear covering and what looked like red goggles.

That report mentions no lost hours. It says the family had the two of them committed to a psychiatric hospital for two days, and that his father later told him he had heard on the radio that a Soviet satellite had fallen that night. Bledsoe offered to take a polygraph and be hypnotized.

Jonathan McDowell's GCAT catalog of space objects records the re-entry, on January 8, 2007, of the third stage of a Soviet Tsiklon-3 rocket (1990-104B, 1,600 kg), with no time or place, so it is not enough to link it to what was seen.

The case reached MUFON (Mutual UFO Network), the largest civilian UFO-investigation organization in the United States, which took statements from the five witnesses; according to B. J. Booth, it took some time to locate the three fishermen, and their accounts of the lights were almost identical. On July 14, 2008, a year and a half later, Dr. Michael O'Connell led a regressive hypnosis session with Bledsoe that produced an account of abduction by tall beings; memories recovered under hypnosis are disputed, because they can blend with unintended invention and with what the interviewer suggests. A psychiatric social worker brought in by MUFON, Debbie Gioia, evaluated him and concluded he was "sound of mind". A retired FBI polygraph examiner, Bob DrDak, gave him a test that flagged "deception" on some of his answers; the polygraph's reliability for establishing facts is technically disputed, and the result does not settle the matter on its own.

From there Bledsoe built a public career: the book "UFO of God", documentaries and lectures (UFO Congress, Contact in the Desert, the Shawn Ryan Show podcast) where he describes repeated contact with luminous "orbs", of which he says he has recorded over 2,000 videos over his property, and with a female being he calls "the Lady". On that circuit he has repeatedly claimed NASA "started looking into his claims in 2008 and kept doing so for around eight years", and he names as part of that circle of investigators the physicist Hal Puthoff, a former SRI researcher tied to the Pentagon's AATIP program, and former CIA officer Jim Semivan, who wrote the foreword to his book.

Why it matters

The sighting of the lights by the river has five witnesses whose statements MUFON took, although they were together and knew one another, and Bledsoe's evaluation found no pathology. The lost hours come from the gap between Bledsoe's own estimate and what his companions said, and they do not appear in his first report. The encounters with beings that night are told by him and his son, and the abduction came out of a hypnosis session a year and a half later, the weakest evidence in the case.

The polygraph flagged deception on some of his answers. On its own it does not prove a hoax, but it carries weight alongside what followed: a book, documentaries and paid lectures, which began after the first report.

The NASA investigation Bledsoe claims has no backing: there is no NASA document or public statement naming a responsible official, and the report of NASA's UAP Independent Study Team (September 2023, 16 experts, chaired by David Spergel), the only publicly documented NASA institutional process on the subject from that period, does not mention him. The claim leads back to Bledsoe himself and to outlets that reproduce his account without checking it.
