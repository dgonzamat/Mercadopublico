🇺🇸2007·Cape Fear River, near Fayetteville, North Carolina

United States.Chris Bledsoe · orange lights by the Cape Fear River

On January 8, 2007, while fishing by the Cape Fear River in North Carolina, contractor Chris Bledsoe and four companions, his 17-year-old son and three subcontractors, reported three orange objects that appeared to land, and that same night an alleged encounter with an entity at his home. The civilian group MUFON investigated with regressive hypnosis, a psychiatric evaluation and a polygraph that flagged signs of deception in part of Bledsoe's answers. He has since published books and appeared in documentaries, and claims NASA investigated him for eight years, which no document or named official has corroborated.

What happened

On January 8, 2007, Chris Bledsoe Sr., a construction contractor and commercial pilot, was fishing on the banks of the Cape Fear River, outside Fayetteville, North Carolina, with his 17-year-old son, Chris Bledsoe Jr., and three men who worked for him as framing subcontractors: Donny Ackerman, Gene Robinson and David McDonald.

By his account, Bledsoe walked about 70 meters from the group to be alone for a while and saw two bright orange objects, which he described as "two setting suns" with fire shooting off them, joined by a third that "took its place" beside them. B. J. Booth summarized the investigation by MUFON (Mutual UFO Network), the largest civilian UFO-investigation organization in the United States, on the UFO Casebook site. Per that summary, Bledsoe went back to the bank and pointed the lights out to his companions, who saw the three objects descend and appear to land some 100 to 150 yards away, across the river.

Bledsoe estimated he had been away from the group for about 20 minutes; his companions said he had been gone 3 to 4 hours, and that two of them had searched for him in the truck without finding him. Bledsoe's own account, which Booth quotes, orders events differently: his son had set out on foot to look for him two hours earlier. He found him hiding under some bushes, and the young man told him that two creatures had been watching him and that he could not move when they looked at him. When they got back to the bank, still according to Bledsoe, three lights came down across the river, and the five, frightened, fled in the truck. Back home, the father described an encounter, in the woods by the house, with a "glass-like" being roughly a meter tall with red eyes, and the son said he saw small beings come out of the woods near the house.

The report Bledsoe himself filed shortly afterwards with MUFON's case system, reproduced by researcher Lon Strickler, tells another version. The group had spent the afternoon fishing; he walked up the track alone, saw three large orange balls come down and hover over the trees "maybe a mile away or closer," and watched them for ten to fifteen minutes before going back. His son came out of the woods saying he had been hiding from "aliens."

Then the five saw six to eight lights converge in the sky and three white lights come down across the river; as they fled they saw a white egg-shaped object with spikes hovering about 20 feet over the road, and other drivers stopped to look at lights. Back home, around 1 or 2 a.m., he and his son saw by the kennel a figure at most four feet tall, with a clear covering and what looked like red goggles.

That report mentions no lost hours. It says the family had the two of them committed to a psychiatric hospital for two days, and that his father later told him he had heard on the radio that a Soviet satellite had fallen that night. Bledsoe offered to take a polygraph and be hypnotized.

Jonathan McDowell's GCAT catalog of space objects records the re-entry, on January 8, 2007, of the third stage of a Soviet Tsiklon-3 rocket (1990-104B, 1,600 kg), with no time or place, so it is not enough to link it to what was seen.

MUFON took statements from the five witnesses; according to Booth, it took some time to locate the three fishermen, and their accounts of the lights were almost identical.

On July 14, 2008, a year and a half later, Dr. Michael O'Connell led a regressive hypnosis session with Bledsoe that produced an account of abduction by tall beings; memories recovered under hypnosis are disputed, because they can blend with unintended invention and with what the interviewer suggests.

A psychiatric social worker brought in by MUFON, Debbie Gioia, evaluated him and concluded he was "sound of mind." A retired FBI polygraph examiner, Bob DrDak, gave him a test that flagged "deception" on some of his answers; the polygraph's reliability for establishing facts is technically disputed.

From there Bledsoe built a public career: the book "UFO of God," documentaries and lectures (UFO Congress, Contact in the Desert, the Shawn Ryan Show podcast) where he describes repeated contact with luminous "orbs," of which he says he has recorded over 2,000 videos over his property, and with a female being he calls "the Lady." On that circuit he has repeatedly claimed NASA "started looking into his claims in 2008 and kept doing so for around eight years," and he names as part of that circle of investigators the physicist Hal Puthoff, a former SRI researcher tied to the Pentagon's AATIP program, and former CIA officer Jim Semivan, who wrote the foreword to his book.

Why it matters

The sighting of the lights by the river has five witnesses whose statements MUFON took, although they knew one another and saw the lights together, and Bledsoe's evaluation found no pathology. The lost hours come from the gap between Bledsoe's own estimate and what his companions said, and they do not appear in his first report. The encounters with beings that night are told by him and his son, and the abduction came out of a hypnosis session a year and a half later, the weakest evidence in the case.

The polygraph result does not on its own prove a hoax, but it carries weight alongside what followed: a book, documentaries and paid lectures, which began after the first report.

The NASA investigation Bledsoe claims has no backing: there is no NASA document or public statement naming a responsible official, and the report that NASA's independent study team on unidentified anomalous phenomena (UAP), 16 experts chaired by David Spergel, published in September 2023 does not mention him either, though it came years after the period Bledsoe cites. The claim leads back to Bledsoe himself and to outlets that reproduce his account without checking it.
