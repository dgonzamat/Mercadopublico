Spain.Talavera la Real · three soldiers on guard see a “green man”

In the early hours of November 12, 1976, three soldiers on guard in the fuel area of Talavera la Real Air Base (Badajoz, Spain) described interference, a whistle, a partial blackout and an agitated dog. José Manuel Trejo described a figure some three meters tall made of points of green light before losing consciousness; his companions fired on it, it vanished, and no casings or impacts were found. The file declassified in 1996 contains no report of the incident: the Air Staff says it has no “record of the event.”

What happened

In the early hours of November 12, 1976, at Talavera la Real Air Base (Badajoz, Extremadura), three conscript soldiers were on guard in the fuel area: José Manuel Trejo, who by his own account was the corporal of the guard, José Hidalgo, on guard with the dog Nerón, and Juan Carrizosa Luján. According to Extremadura Misteriosa, Trejo and Carrizosa were each in his own sentry box, about sixty meters apart. According to the testimonies, the sequence began with an anomalous noise, first comparable to radio interference and then to a high-pitched whistle that lasted several minutes, together with a partial blackout of the perimeter lighting and interference in communications; Trejo also recalled "an explosion of very white light." The dog became intensely agitated and, according to Trejo, led them toward the center of the poplar grove, where there was "a very large whirlwind of air."

When the whirlwind stopped, Trejo described beside the wall a large figure (close to three meters tall), apparently formed by small points of green light, with its arms outstretched in a cross, of anomalous proportions and with no defined hands or feet. His weapon jammed; he felt "an energy that went through his chest" and fell unconscious. His two companions opened fire on the apparition (two 20-round magazines according to Trejo, "up to 50 shots" according to COPE) and the figure, still according to the account, vanished. The next day more than fifty servicemen combed the area and found neither the casings nor bullet impacts on the wall. Trejo spent twenty-five days in hospital in Badajoz with convulsions and a month in a military hospital in Madrid, and says he was in a coma for thirteen days; he and his companions were accused of having taken drugs and invented the testimony, which he denies. Hidalgo and Carrizosa have died; Trejo is the only living witness.

The account became public through the press: J. J. Benítez published it in the magazine Mundo Desconocido (no. 17, November 1977), and in 1981 the Diario de Caracas reprinted it. The file the Ministry of Defense declassified in 1996 (no. 761112, Air Operations Command, 28 pages, now in the Virtual Library of Defense) grows from there, not from a base report: it is opened by the defense attaché in Caracas, who in April 1982 forwards the article in case the Intelligence Section had not seen it, and it is completed by letters from private citizens asking for information. The official answers say little.

In 1984 the Air Staff's second-in-command sets the reply to a citizen: to establish the cause of the events, the judicial authority "appointed an Examining Judge" who sent it the summary information, "there being no record of the event at the Air Staff," and all information on UFOs remained classified. In 1985 the Defense Information and Social Relations Directorate (DRISDE) repeats to another citizen that "an Examining Judge was appointed, who forwarded the relevant conclusions to the Judicial Authority." In 1991 the acting commander of Wing 23 writes that no document on the matter exists, and the Air Operations Command's enquiries to the base and to DRISDE found neither that report nor any other. The case sheet the Command itself drew up on declassification marks "file reliability: missing!" and summarizes the event from the press feature. The file, then, documents that there was a military judicial investigation whose outcome has not survived, not a report of the alarm, and it does not establish what happened that morning.

Why it matters

All that remains of the incident is the sentries' testimony: no material trace, no photograph, no radar contact, and the examining judge's report has not survived.

Vicente Juan Ballester Olmos (a researcher who took part in the declassification of the files) points to a possible "hallucinatory delirium by a person with a psychiatric history, combined with contagious hysteria," which, added to fear and confusion, degenerated into the account. He also holds that the base command never regarded it as a UFO-type event and that the file was opened because of letters reaching the Ministry asking for information.

Planvex (2017), which records Trejo's testimony, calls the fact that neither the forty bullets from the two magazines nor their casings turned up "one of the great unknowns of this case"; Extremadura Misteriosa adds that military experts certified that the weapons had been fired.
