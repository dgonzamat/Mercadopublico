27:T430,According to the report, instead of striking them the object 'hesitated momentarily' over the helicopter and continued slowly west, then accelerated sharply, passed west of Mansfield Airport and turned 45° to the northwest. The physical description came later: to UPI, two weeks afterwards, Coyne spoke of a 'big, gray, metallic-looking' hull about 60 feet long, shaped like an airfoil or a 'streamlined fat cigar', with a red light in front, a centre dome and a green light at the rear that swivelled like a spotlight and bathed the cabin in green. The uncontrolled spinning of the magnetic compass appears in later crew accounts gathered by Zeidman, not in the report or the 1973 press. The most striking point is in the report: Coyne saw the altimeter showing a 1,000 feet-per-minute climb and 3,500 feet with the collective in the full-down position. In Zeidman's reconstruction, when Coyne raised the collective the helicopter kept climbing to 3,800 feet, where he felt he had regained control. He told UPI there had been no noise, turbulence or noticeable g-forces.28:T41e,Coyne radioed Mansfield tower to ask whether there were any high-performance aircraft in the area; the tower acknowledged and then gave no further response. According to Zeidman, contact was lost on both UHF and VHF and was later restored with Akron-Canton. The Air National Guard F-100s based at Mansfield were not airborne, Coyne told UPI.
