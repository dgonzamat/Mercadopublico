🇺🇸2014·Tucson, Arizona

United States.Boyd Bushman · a posthumous video with photos of an alien

On October 8, 2014, a video of Boyd Bushman, a retired Lockheed Martin senior scientist, was released; recorded that August, shortly before his death, it showed him claiming the U.S. worked with extraterrestrials at Area 51 and showing photos of an alien as proof. In the following weeks, forum users and the press pointed out that the "alien" closely resembled a plastic Halloween doll of the kind sold at Kmart and Walmart. His employment at Lockheed Martin appears in an FBI file, but nothing he said has been verified.

What happened

Boyd Bushman (1936-2014) was an engineer with a verifiable career in the American aerospace industry. According to his Wikipedia biography, he worked at Texas Instruments, Hughes Aircraft and General Dynamics, whose Fort Worth division became part of Lockheed Martin in 1993, and ended as a senior scientist; he worked on weapons and sensor programs and held several patents (28, according to the October 2014 version of that biography). An FBI file released in 2016 (FOIPA 1306337) confirms his employment: in 1999, the security office of Lockheed Martin Tactical Aircraft Systems described him as a senior specialist in the Special Programs department, hired on July 23, 1982, with a Top Secret SCI/SAP clearance.

The same file shows his interest in anti-gravity years before the video. In October 1999 Bushman asked the company's legal department for proprietary-information agreements so he could receive from two Czech contacts "scientific data and video recording of experiments" on supposed gravity generators. He said he would wait until the agreements were in place before receiving the data, and at the company's direction the matter was not pursued. In November, the company's security office asked the FBI whether it had developed any information indicating an "elicitation danger" associated with those contacts; a review of his personnel and security folders found nothing remarkable beyond "some classified security procedure and classified information mishandling incidents."

In August 2014, shortly before his death, at 78, Bushman recorded a long video interview in Tucson, Arizona, with aerospace engineer Mark Q. Patterson. It was published on YouTube on October 8, 2014, some weeks after his death. The press picked it up in late October: TVQC (Quebec) on the 26th, the British paper Metro on the 27th and the San Antonio Express-News on the 30th. Metro spoke of a deathbed video in which a "dying scientist" said he had reverse-engineered flying saucer technology for Lockheed Martin and showed photos of aliens that, he claimed, had been taken on disposable cameras inside American military facilities.

According to Snopes, in the video Bushman said he had worked on projects involving anti-gravity, UFOs, aliens, Area 51 and Roswell. He described very human-looking beings, between four and a half and five feet (1.4 to 1.5 m) tall, with three cartilage backbones and able to communicate telepathically. He spoke of two groups, one friendlier and one of cattle thieves, some of which would live about 200 years, from a planet called "Quintumnia."

In the copy of the video kept by archive.org, some figures do not match Snopes's summary: Bushman says one or two of those beings were 230 years old, that at least 18 "exist and operate" with the American facilities and that their planet is 68 light-years away, a trip that, he said, they made in 45 years, against the 68,000 that in his view Einstein's physics would require. According to Snopes, the only evidence he offered was some photographs of those beings.

On October 28, a thread about the video was opened on Mick West's Metabunk forum. One participant wrote that, when the video appeared, he had traced the "alien" photo to a 2008 Above Top Secret thread whose poster claimed it was a K-Mart Halloween decoration from the late 1990s; he added that he was not sure that thread was the photos' origin. On October 31, Snopes rated the claim false. According to Snopes, a Reddit user located a plastic toy alien doll that "very closely resembled" the image Bushman showed, and a Quebec news outlet pointed out that it could be purchased at Walmart. The sources consulted offer no institutional or documentary corroboration of what Bushman claimed in the video.

Why it matters

The only thing Bushman offered as proof was photographs, and the identification with a plastic doll rests on three findings from the time: a claim by an Above Top Secret poster in 2008, a very similar doll that a Reddit user located and a Quebec outlet that placed it on sale at Walmart. The Metabunk participant who traced the photo acknowledged he could not be sure that thread was its origin.

His career is real, and the FBI file shows him with a Top Secret clearance, but the sources consulted offer no corroboration of what he said in the video. Snopes cites Stuart J. Robbins, who wrote for SWIFT that such a confession provides no information that can be verified or reproduced.
