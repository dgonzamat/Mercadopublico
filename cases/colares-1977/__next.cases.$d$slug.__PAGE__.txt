16:T420,From late August 1977 and throughout 1978, residents of Colares island and of coastal municipalities in the State of Pará, in the Brazilian Amazon, reported night-time incidents with luminous objects that emitted beams of light aimed at people. Locals called them 'chupa-chupa', because they believed the beam drew blood or energy from the victims. The Operação Prato mission report (ACE 3252/83) records the cases seen by Dr Wellaide Cecim Carvalho, aged 24, in charge of the Colares health unit: she told the team she had seen four patients with paresis, headache, asthenia, dizziness and tremors, all with first-degree burns and marks of small perforations, on the neck in men and on the breast in the single case of a woman; she added that she had observed no changes indicating anaemia and did not believe the victims had been 'sucked', though she accepted they might have been struck by a beam of light of unknown nature. She herself appears in the Air Force register as a witness to a metallic cylindrical object over the town on 16 October 1977.17:T408,Faced with the panic, the I Regional Air Command (I COMAR), based in Belém, sent its intelligence section under Captain Uyrangê Hollanda. The team interviewed witnesses, examined injuries and watched the sky at night. The report describes, among others, the case of Claudomira Rodrigues da Paixão, who on 18 October felt a light moving over her body and settling on her left breast, where the forensic institute's examination recorded a light burn, and that of Raimundo Galvão Trindade, with an oval mark of about 20 by 12 centimetres on his thigh that looked like a burn. The I COMAR 'Registro de Observações', held at the National Archives, numbers more than a hundred sightings between 2 September 1977 and November 1978, with sketches and some photographs: dozens were observed by the team itself, by Hollanda or by the unit's sergeants, mostly distant lights crossing the sky, some at low altitude.
