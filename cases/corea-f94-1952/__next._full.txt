32:T5d8,The two statements tell what happened next in a different order. According to the operator, after the last pass, at about 7,000 feet, Bromide announced that they had something on their tail; he turned and thought he saw a plane, without being sure, because it lasted a second. According to the pilot, it was the operator who, on the last pass, looked back and said he thought he saw an object on their tail, and he gave it little credence until Bromide called to say it was detecting something behind the fighter on its scope. The pilot asked for an intercept vector and, with the light still in sight, turned to starboard. On a northeasterly heading, the operator locked on to a target with the airborne radar, an APG-33, at 7,000 yards, nearly dead ahead and slightly above, and the fighter began to close at 50 knots faster than the target. At 6,000 yards the target began to pull away. The pilot lit the afterburner, from about 250 knots, and followed the target in a gradual climb, past 10,000 feet, with the pip nearly centred on the scope, while the range opened at a steady rate: to about 26,000 yards in 12 to 15 seconds, according to the pilot, or about 24,000 in about 14, according to the operator. There the lock broke, and they did not pick it up again for the rest of the mission. Neither man saw evasive action or exhaust.
