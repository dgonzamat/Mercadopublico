1e:T578,The identification that closed the case came in 2013. According to Calcutta's The Telegraph (24 July 2013), as picked up by The Register and UAS Vision, the Army called in two astronomers from the Indian Institute of Astrophysics in Bangalore, among them Tushar Prabhu, to determine whether the objects were celestial or terrestrial. Between August 2012 and February 2013 the Army had logged 329 sightings at Thakung, 155 of them as violations of the Line of Actual Control. According to the same coverage, the astronomers were told that the first object, seen from a post at about 4,715 m near Thakung, appeared on the horizon around 6 pm and stayed visible until about 5 am, and that the second appeared around 4 am and faded around 11 am; a regular observer at the post had noticed a four-minute shift in the appearance of one of them from one day to the next. At the Army's request, the two astronomers told military personnel how to measure the lights' position with a theodolite (horizontal angle and elevation), measurements taken from 17 to 22 February 2013 at Point 4715, and on analysing them concluded that the object observed from there was Jupiter —the measurements matched the planet's diurnal motion— and the morning one, Venus. 1f:T49c,The conventional explanation is especially strong here. The Indian Institute of Astrophysics' identification of Jupiter and Venus rested on theodolite measurements the Army took on their instructions, and fits the main features of the sighting: nightly persistence of hours, great brightness, rising in the east, absence of radar echo and absence of emission. Two loose ends remain that the public record does not tie up: in September 2012 the Hanle astronomers observed the objects for several days and ruled out meteors and planets, and the identification rests on measurements from February 2013; for August–October 2012, Venus fits the description, although nobody measured it. Neither weighs enough against a direct measurement, but they prevent treating the identification as settled for each of the hundreds of sightings, which is why this entry puts the planetary explanation at 72% and keeps 16% as undetermined.
