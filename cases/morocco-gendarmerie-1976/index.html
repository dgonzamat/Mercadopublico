122

1976·Morocco (national reports; Royal Gendarmerie, Rabat)

Morocco.Morocco · wave + Royal Gendarmerie (US cables)

On the night of September 18-19, 1976, around 01:00-01:30, a luminous object was reported almost simultaneously from Agadir, Casablanca, Rabat, Kenitra, Marrakech, Essaouira, Meknes and Fez: a silvery circular shape trailing sparks and fragments, silent, on a SW→NE course. King Hassan II ordered the Royal Gendarmerie to investigate; a Major gave the U.S. defense attaché a summary and showed him witness drawings, and the episode ended up in declassified State Department cables. The time and track match the computed re-entry of a piece of a Soviet rocket; its value lies in the head-of-state-level institutional response.

Part 01

The night in question

Between 01:00 and 01:30 on the early morning of September 18-19, 1976, Morocco's Royal Gendarmerie received almost simultaneous calls from widely separated points across the country —Agadir, the Marrakech region, Kalaa-Sraghna, Essaouira, Casablanca, Rabat, Kenitra, Meknes and the Fez area— reporting the same phenomenon: a circular, silvery, luminous shape crossing the sky on a general southwest-northeast course, emitting intermittent trails of sparks and bright fragments, and moving without making any noise. The embassy cable specifies that the times ranged from 01:00 to 02:00, most between 01:00 and 01:30; that there was general agreement on an approximately south-to-north course, parallel to the Atlantic coast, at an estimated altitude of 1,000 meters; and that descriptions fell into two types: a silvery, flattened luminous ball, disc-shaped, or a large luminous tube-shaped object that emitted sparks from the rear. That witnesses hundreds of kilometers apart described an identical object at the same time is the feature that most impressed the authorities and, at the same time, the clearest clue to its true nature.

King Hassan II took a personal interest in the matter and ordered the commander of the Royal Gendarmerie to handle it. On September 23, four days after the episode, that commander, Colonel Housni Benslimane, asked to see the U.S. ambassador in Rabat, conveyed the King's personal interest and asked for any available information. The next day Major Mohamed Lissaoui of the Gendarmerie gave the defense attaché a summary of the sightings and let him examine drawings prepared by various witnesses, including himself: Lissaoui had seen it around 01:15 while returning from Kenitra, flying parallel to the coast at a relatively slow speed, "as if it were an aircraft preparing to land", first as a disc and, as it came closer, as a luminous tube. Ambassador Anderson wrote that he frankly did not know what to make of the sightings, although he found the similarity of descriptions from widely dispersed locations intriguing. All of this was recorded in U.S. State Department cables —the embassy's, 1976RABAT05209, and Washington's October 5 reply, STATE 247538, released years later by WikiLeaks within the so-called 'Kissinger Cables'. The reply went out, like every Department cable, over the signature of then-Secretary of State Henry Kissinger: a king asking a superpower to explain a sighting is a level of involvement that rarely leaves a documentary trail.

The corpus framing is deliberately skeptical about the phenomenon. The description —an object crossing an entire country in minutes, fragmenting and trailing sparks, seen simultaneously over a huge area and at low angular speed— is the classic signature of an atmospheric re-entry of space junk. Washington's reply already pointed that way in 1976: it was difficult to offer a definitive explanation, the government was unaware of any U.S. aircraft or satellite activity that might have been mistaken for the sightings, and the flat southwest-to-northeast trajectory "could conceivably be compatible with a meteor, or decaying satellite", although meteors are usually visible around 100 km up, not 1 km; pending further descriptions or photographs, the cable leaned toward a meteor, probably a spectacular one, or, given the slow speed, silence and burning fragments described, a decaying satellite part of which there was no precise re-entry record. In October 2012, satellite observer Ted Molczan searched the USSTRATCOM tracking database for decays on those dates and found one that fit: object 1976-074C, the BOZ ullage motor assembly ejected from the third stage of the Molniya rocket that orbited the Molniya 1-35 satellite in July 1976. Harro Zimmer's numerical calculation placed its re-entry between 01:19 and 01:22 UT on September 19, with the terminal descent near the Strait of Gibraltar, which would also explain the absence of sightings from Spain. One tension with the cable itself remains: some reported times run as late as 02:00, while a re-entry lasts minutes. What makes the case worthy of the corpus is therefore not the object's nature but the institutional chain it set off: a head of state mobilizing his gendarmerie and opening a diplomatic channel with a superpower, all preserved in a declassified file. It is a foreign-government UAP file in the full sense.

Part 02

Why this case moved the needle

It is Morocco's first corpus case and one of the cleanest examples of institutional over phenomenological value. The phenomenon itself matches in time and track the re-entry of a piece of a Soviet rocket computed in 2012, so as a sighting it contributes little. What makes it a first-rate record is the state's response: a monarch personally mobilizing the Royal Gendarmerie, a Major meeting with the U.S. defense attaché, witness drawings shown and a request for information that reached the U.S. State Department and got an answer, all preserved in now-declassified diplomatic cables.

For the corpus, that documentary chain is exactly the kind of evidence that matters: it shows how a foreign government, outside the Anglo-American circuit, processed an unidentified aerial phenomenon with institutional seriousness and left a written record. It is included on the same criterion as Petrozavodsk —documented and officially acknowledged— without elevating the object to an anomaly. And it works as a double calibrating reminder: on the one hand, that the authorities' reaction is not proof of the stimulus's strangeness (a piece of rocket was enough to mobilize a king); on the other, that declassified government files are valuable for what they reveal about institutional behavior, not necessarily for the nature of the phenomenon that prompted them.
