26:T472,Between 01:00 and 01:30 on the early morning of September 18-19, 1976, Morocco's Royal Gendarmerie received almost simultaneous calls from widely separated points across the country —Agadir, the Marrakech region, Kalaa-Sraghna, Essaouira, Casablanca, Rabat, Kenitra, Meknes and the Fez area— reporting the same phenomenon: a circular, silvery, luminous shape crossing the sky on a general southwest-northeast course, emitting intermittent trails of sparks and bright fragments, and moving without making any noise. The embassy cable specifies that the times ranged from 01:00 to 02:00, most between 01:00 and 01:30; that there was general agreement on an approximately south-to-north course, parallel to the Atlantic coast, at an estimated altitude of 1,000 meters; and that descriptions fell into two types: a silvery, flattened luminous ball, disc-shaped, or a large luminous tube-shaped object that emitted sparks from the rear. That witnesses hundreds of kilometers apart described an identical object at the same time is the feature that most impressed the authorities and, at the same time, the clearest clue to its true nature.27:T554,King Hassan II took a personal interest in the matter and ordered the commander of the Royal Gendarmerie to handle it. On September 23, four days after the episode, that commander, Colonel Housni Benslimane, asked to see the U.S. ambassador in Rabat, conveyed the King's personal interest and asked for any available information. The next day Major Mohamed Lissaoui of the Gendarmerie gave the defense attaché a summary of the sightings and let him examine drawings prepared by various witnesses, including himself: Lissaoui had seen it around 01:15 while returning from Kenitra, flying parallel to the coast at a relatively slow speed, "as if it were an aircraft preparing to land", first as a disc and, as it came closer, as a luminous tube. Ambassador Anderson wrote that he frankly did not know what to make of the sightings, although he found the similarity of descriptions from widely dispersed locations intriguing. The description —an object crossing an entire country in minutes, fragmenting and trailing sparks, seen simultaneously over a huge area and at low angular speed— is the classic signature of an atmospheric re-entry of space junk. Washington's reply already pointed that way in 1976: it was difficult to offer a definitive explanation, the government was unaware of any U.S. aircraft or satellite activity that might have been mistaken for the sightings, and the flat southwest-to-northeast trajectory "could conceivably be compatible with a meteor, or decaying satellite", although meteors are usually visible around 100 km up, not 1 km; pending further descriptions or photographs, the cable leaned toward a meteor, probably a spectacular one, or, given the slow speed, silence and burning fragments described, a decaying satellite part of which there was no precise re-entry record.
