4c:T63c,At about 23:10 the corporal alerted the duty NCO. The sergeant went up with binoculars onto a mound behind the garages and from there described something different: an object shaped like an inverted cup, with white lights on the top and bottom and flashing amber and white lights on the sides, of a size he compared to a lorry's. In his statement he added that the object stayed some thirty minutes over the range, until 23:25, and that as it moved 'a high-power spotlight lit up the ground from underneath'. The other five witnesses, from their post, saw at that moment a very intense white light that, after some five minutes stationary in the same place where the first had appeared, rose slowly, headed for the auxiliary tower and vanished at great speed to the north-east. In the version the patrol gave the range commander, that second object, with white and amber lights 'of incalculable intensity', lit up some hundred metres around it and let the firing targets be seen clearly. Nobody heard any noise or felt any movement of air. The range commander, who carried out a reconnaissance from 23:30, saw nothing abnormal and wrote the next day that 'the reddish patch that came into view was the Moon, which was becoming visible as the mist that existed in that part of the Range cleared', so that 'it was thought that the mist hiding the Moon would have been the cause of the Patrol's alarm'.
