🇨🇳2010·Xiaoshan International Airport, Hangzhou

China.Hangzhou · an unidentified light shuts the airport for an hour

On the night of July 7, 2010, an unidentified luminous object caused Hangzhou's Xiaoshan International Airport to close for about an hour, and several flights were diverted or delayed. The press later attributed the closure to an unauthorized private flight (China Daily) or to a military test at a nearby base (South China Morning Post). There was never a conclusive official explanation, and the most widely circulated photographs were not taken at the time of the closure and were attributed to aircraft.

What happened

Around 8:40 p.m. on July 7, 2010, according to ABC News, the crew of a flight preparing for descent into Hangzhou's Xiaoshan International Airport (Zhejiang province) detected an unidentified object and notified air traffic control. Less than an hour earlier, according to the same report, Hangzhou residents had seen a flying object emitting red and white rays of light. Within minutes, aviation authorities grounded outbound flights and diverted inbound ones to the airports of Ningbo and Wuxi: 18 flights were affected (more than 20 delayed by 56 minutes, according to Beijing Review). The length of the closure varies by source: ABC News says operations returned to normal an hour later, and the Christian Science Monitor (July 16, 2010) that the airport was shut for about two hours.

Several photographs circulated, but they were not from the time of the closure. Hangzhou residents released images taken in the afternoon of an object bathed in golden light with a comet-like tail, which the city's meteorological authorities attributed to sunlight reflecting off an airplane. A resident, Ma Shijun, who was out for a night stroll with his wife, said he felt a beam of light over his head, looked up and photographed at 8:26 p.m. a streak of bright white light that, according to Beijing Planetarium curator Zhu Jing, looks just like a plane shining its strobe lamps; Ma himself told Xinhua he did not know whether it was a plane or the airport's object.

According to later summaries not checked against sources of the time, the investigation was joint between the Civil Aviation Administration of China (CAAC) and the regional military command's air force; the head of CAAC air traffic control in Zhejiang, Wang Jian, told Xinhua that "no conclusion has yet been drawn", and the reports consulted do not say that radar recorded the object. No closed official explanation was published, and the ones the press gave contradict each other: on August 5 China Daily blamed the closure on a "black flight", a private plane or helicopter flying without authorization, and later the South China Morning Post reported that it was due to a military test at a nearby air force base.

An investigation group from the Beijing UFO Research Society and the Shanghai UFO Research Center visited the airport on July 14 and on the 25th concluded that there was no evidence of an alien craft, but that private or military aircraft activity could not be ruled out; no reading was officially confirmed. Before the visit, the director of the Shanghai UFO Research Center, Lou Jinhong, told ABC News he could not offer an opinion on the case because they had not yet collected all the relevant documents and data.

According to ABC News, the event set off a firestorm of speculation among Chinese internet users: one wrote that it was "a hidden U.S. bomber flying toward China"; another, that it was clearly a man-made phenomenon and that the Chinese military was probably testing a new missile or aircraft.

Why it matters

What is documented is the institutional response: the airport closed for an hour, according to ABC News, or about two, according to the Christian Science Monitor and the press published figures for the flights affected. For the object itself there is less: the crew's detection, according to ABC News, and the testimony of residents, with no radar record in the reports consulted.

The press's two explanations, an unauthorized private flight (China Daily) and a military test at a nearby base (South China Morning Post), are both aircraft, contradict each other, and neither was officially confirmed. That the airport closed does not say what the crew detected.
