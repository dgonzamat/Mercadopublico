🇲🇽2023·Chamber of Deputies, Mexico City

Mexico.Mexican Congress · Maussan displays two Nazca mummies

On September 12, 2023, broadcaster Jaime Maussan presented to a hearing of Mexico's Chamber of Deputies two small mummified bodies with elongated heads and three-fingered hands, which he said had been found in Nazca, Peru, and were not part of “our terrestrial evolution.” Mexico's National Autonomous University (UNAM) clarified that it had only carbon-dated one sample, without drawing any conclusion about its origin, and anthropologist Andrew Nelson told Live Science the bodies “have been debunked on the basis of anatomy.” In 2017 Peruvian prosecutors had already described similar mummies as recently made dolls.

What happened

On September 12, 2023, the Mexican journalist Jaime Maussan presented two small mummified bodies at a public hearing on unidentified anomalous aerial phenomena held in the Chamber of Deputies of the Mexican Congress. At the start of the session, the participants swore to tell the truth. The bodies were in boxes and looked humanoid: elongated heads, three-fingered hands and grayish skin. According to the science news site Live Science, they were no more than three feet (one meter) tall.

Maussan said they had been found in the Nazca region of Peru and that their DNA showed they "are not human beings" and "are not part of our terrestrial evolution." He added: "we do not want to call them extraterrestrials because we do not know." He and another presenter, military physician José de Jesús Zalce Benítez, also said the abdomens held eggs and that carbon-14 dating put the bodies at about 1,000 years old. Maussan attributed that dating to the National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM).

UNAM republished its 2017 statement, according to which it had only carbon-dated the sample it received, without concluding anything about its origin. The sources do not say whether that sample came from these two bodies or from another of the "Nazca" bodies. Julieta Fierro, a researcher at UNAM's Institute of Astronomy, said the matter "has neither head nor tail," according to the Mexican newspaper El Financiero of September 13. Anthropologist Andrew Nelson of Western University in Ontario told Live Science that the bodies "have been debunked on the basis of anatomy" and that Maussan has not submitted his CT, carbon-14 and DNA evidence to peer review.

According to Nelson, earlier studies show that some of the bodies “are human mummies that had been deliberately manipulated to appear alien”; in 2017 Peruvian paleontologist Rodolfo Salas-Gismondi wrote that the feet could have been made by mutilating the foot of a human mummy.

Deputy Sergio Gutiérrez Luna, of the Morena party, who organized the session, said the Chamber neither endorsed nor denied the theses put forward and only wanted to hear "all voices." The fact-checking site Snopes notes that Congress at no point confirmed that the material came from extraterrestrials.

The case was not new, and Maussan himself told Live Science that the same bodies had made headlines in 2017 and 2018 and that further tests since then show they are not human. In 2017, when similar "Nazca" figures first circulated, Peru's prosecutor's office concluded, on the basis of a report by the Institute of Legal Medicine, that they were "manufactured dolls of recent date," covered with a mixture of paper and synthetic glue to simulate skin, and that animal bones had been modified to build their internal structure (El Financiero).

According to the same paper, the bodies were never shown physically in Peru: Maussan only showed photos at a conference. In 2015 Maussan had led the "Be Witness" event, where he unveiled a supposed alien body that turned out to be the mummy of a human child (Snopes). El Financiero reports that scientific voices called the presentation a fraud.

Why it matters

The hearing gave Maussan's claim an official stage: a session of the Chamber of Deputies, with participants sworn in and its recording posted on the Congress's YouTube channel. But an oath obliges one to say what one believes, not to prove it, and the session put the bodies through no examination.

Nelson also raised another question: if the remains were 1,000 years old and from Peru, one would need to know whether they were looted and how they left the country. The case's sources document no independent examination, with full access, of the two bodies shown in Congress. That their DNA is not human could only be supported by a genetic analysis published and replicated by laboratories independent of those who exhibit them.
