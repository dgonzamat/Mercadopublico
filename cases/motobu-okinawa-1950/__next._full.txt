2d:T63e,The set was an SCR-584 "B" radar, serial number 754, which the report says was "in good condition"; the card calls it a "Gun Radar", that is, a fire-control radar. When it was picked up, the object was stationary. Then it moved on a heading of 220°, to the south-west, at about 507 miles per hour, a figure the report explains as follows: 16.9 miles between the first and last points, covered in two minutes. The altitude at first contact was 13,500 feet (the card says 13,000); there was only one object, and the last position was at 26° 26′ N and, as far as can be read, 127° 48′ E, at 1040. According to the report, the object was "observed" for about ten minutes and tracked by radar for about two minutes after it started to move. The 1963 card says it stayed stationary for ten minutes before moving; with first contact at 1030, the last at 1040 and two minutes of movement, the stationary phase in the report lasts at most about eight minutes (our own calculation). The card also explains how it was lost: the radar tracked it south-west "to point over a mountain. Could not track beyond this point", and it adds "Additional Radar in area did not pick up object. Speed faster than Operator could track". A heading of 220° held for 16.9 miles from the first point ends at 26° 26′ N, 127° 46′ E, close to the report's last position and about 6.6 miles from the radar itself.
