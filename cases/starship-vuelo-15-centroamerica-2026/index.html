🇭🇳2026·Honduras, the rest of Central America and central and southern Mexico

Honduras.Honduras · witnesses in many places see a light cross the sky

On the night of Friday, November 13, 2026, people in Honduras, El Salvador, Guatemala, Puerto Rico and Mexico saw, and many filmed, a bright point surrounded by a cone-shaped cloud of light crossing the sky slowly, and shared it as a possible UFO. It was SpaceX's Starship on its 15th test flight, launched that afternoon from south Texas. That same night, in this order, civil protection authorities in Chiapas, Honduras's civil aviation authority and El Salvador's presidency linked the light to the launch.

Record of the event

What happened

On Friday, November 13, 2026, at 4:47 p.m. central Mexico time (22:47 UTC), SpaceX launched the 15th test flight of Starship, the world's largest rocket, from Starbase, its site in south Texas. The ship carried 26 third-generation Starlink satellites and was to circle the Earth about 14 times over more than 22 hours before returning to Texas. The booster, the first stage, did not return to the tower as planned: its guidance system diverted it to the Gulf of Mexico, where it was destroyed. The ship kept going and reached orbit.

A little over an hour and a half after liftoff, with night already fallen on the ground, the ship passed over Mexico and Central America and the videos began. In Mexico, the reports that spread place the pass between 6:25 and 6:30 p.m. central Mexico time. In Puebla, residents of San Salvador el Seco, Tepeaca, San Nicolás Buenos Aires and the state capital's metropolitan area filmed it. In Chiapas, where there were videos from Nicolás Ruiz, people described a bright, cone-shaped formation. The footage shows a bright point surrounded by a cloud of light that widens as it moves; the Puebla newspaper Puntual compared it to a jellyfish.

In Honduras, reports came from Catacamas, in Olancho; San Lorenzo and San Marcos de Colón, in the south; Omoa, in Cortés; and San Marcos de Ocotepeque. According to the news site TuNota, hundreds of Hondurans went through moments of uncertainty and panic, and social media talk turned to a UFO and even the end of the world. Radio HRN spoke of dozens of witnesses. The San Pedro Sula newspaper El País reported that the light was also seen in Guatemala, El Salvador and Puerto Rico.

At 8:16 p.m. that night, the Honduran Civil Aviation Agency (AHAC) posted on X a statement dated that day in Comayagüela. It said that the luminous object observed "in various parts of Honduran territory" would be related to the Starship launch, which had reached orbit. It added that the U.S. Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) had issued a notice to air missions that "contemplated a trajectory over the Central American region, including Honduras." According to the channel Abriendo Brecha, the agency's director, Belkis Escobar, added that the object was on an orbital trajectory, outside the airspace used by commercial aircraft, and posed no risk to commercial aviation.

An hour later, at 9:18 p.m. local time, the Press Secretariat of the Presidency of El Salvador posted on X an 11-second video. The text read: "Starship flight 15, from the aerospace company @SpaceX, was visible from El Salvador. The ship lifted off from Starbase, in south Texas, U.S., and its trajectory allowed it to be observed from various points in Central America." The video shows the bright point at the tip of the cone-shaped cloud and, beside it, smaller separate points of light; El País of Honduras wrote that the satellites the ship had just deployed could be seen along with it. In Puerto Rico, according to El País of Honduras, the Caribbean Astronomy Society had warned in advance that the ship might be visible and that its path would pass south of the island.

The ship completed its mission. It deployed the 26 satellites and stayed in orbit. On Saturday, November 14, about 22 hours after liftoff, the ship returned to Starbase and the tower's mechanical arms caught it in midair, the first such catch of a Starship coming back from space.

That return, in daylight, also crossed Mexico: according to an information card from Mexico's air navigation service (SENEAM) and its Federal Civil Aviation Agency (AFAC), the ship entered Mexican airspace at 3:08 p.m. central Mexico time, left it at 3:15 p.m. and landed at 3:20 p.m. SENEAM notified aircraft operators after confirming with the FAA that the operation posed no risk to Mexican airspace.

Why it matters

A rocket in orbit looks like this because of a difference in light. At dusk the ground is already dark, but hundreds of kilometers up the Sun is still shining. The gases the ship releases at that height expand and form a cloud that the sunlight illuminates, and from below it looks like a cone-shaped patch of light, with a bright point inside it, moving across a black sky. That is how the newspaper Puntual explained it; El País of Honduras attributed the glow to sunlight reflecting off the ship itself. According to Abriendo Brecha, in the footage the light seemed to move slowly and could give the impression of being close to the ground. That shape, which looks like neither an airplane nor a star, is what makes people think of something unknown.

El País of Honduras added that the ship and the 26 satellites it had just deployed could be seen in the sky. The timing is consistent: according to the planned timeline published by the tracking site Space Launch Schedule, deployment was to end one hour and 18 minutes after liftoff, around 6:05 p.m. in Honduras and central Mexico, about 20 minutes before the Mexican videos.

What sets this case apart is the speed of the official response. At 7:42 p.m. local time, less than three hours after liftoff and a little over an hour after the Mexican videos, the Chiapas state civil protection department wrote on X that the light seen over the state's coast "corresponds to SpaceX's Starship, after its launch from Texas." Half an hour later, Honduras's aviation authority published its statement, and El Salvador's presidency confirmed it with its own video an hour after that.

AHAC relied on facts anyone could check: that day's launch and the FAA notice to air missions, which traced the route over Central America. AHAC was cautious and wrote that the phenomenon "would be related" to the flight. Abriendo Brecha noted that the official explanation makes it possible to link the glow to the flight, although the videos shared on social media have not been publicly analyzed one by one.

The case also shows how a single launch can set off a wave of reports across a whole region. It was not the first time a Starship had been seen from the Caribbean. In January 2025, the ship on the seventh flight exploded minutes after liftoff, and its debris crossed the sky as colored streaks. According to audio reviewed by the U.S. public radio network NPR, pilots flying near Puerto Rico told air traffic control in San Juan about lights they could not identify. This time the ship did not break up. It was enough for it to pass at night, lit by the Sun, over several countries for videos of a supposed UFO to circulate in all of them within hours.
