4c:T4af,What turned a dubious account into a historic episode was what came next. Dahl said that the next morning a man dressed in a dark suit met him for breakfast, recounted in detail what he had seen (despite not having been there) and warned him not to talk about it for his own and his family's sake. That scene is recurrently cited as the founding brick of what popular culture would later call 'men in black'. Fred Crisman, presented in the story as Dahl's superior and described by the FBI as his business partner, went to the island to 'verify' and said he too had seen an object. According to the signed statement both later gave the FBI, in early June they had picked up some rock formations from a gravel pit on Maury Island and sent a cigar box of them to Ray Palmer, an editor in Evanston (Illinois), only for analysis; days after the flying-disc stories began, Palmer called them and offered to pay for an exclusive story if the material was fragments of a disc. In that same statement they said they had told officers Davidson and Brown how they got the rock formations and that these had no connection with any flying disc.
