15:T5b0,The teletype reached Wright-Patterson on September 12 and Washington, and the Air Force Director of Intelligence, Major General C. P. Cabell, ordered an on-site investigation. Lieutenant Jerry Cummings and Lieutenant Colonel N. R. Rosengarten interrogated operators, instructors and the two T-33 crewmen, and found that no other radar in the area had picked up anything unusual. Their analysis recorded that the Evans Signal Laboratory at Belmar had released two large balloons at 11:12 EDST, painted silver for radar, which rose until bursting at about 104,000 feet with a 39-foot diameter; that the two pilots disagreed radically about the track and could not fix it to better than 10 nautical miles; and that the object, always on their left during the turn, could have been a nearly stationary balloon inside their turning circle. The 15:15 target was another balloon: according to Ruppelt, some officers had seen it launched and were betting on its height. The Blue Book card closes the file as "balloon" for the 10th and "anomalous propagation" for the 11th, and the Sandy Hook card as "balloon released from Evans Signal Laboratory at 1112 EDST". The file's initial summary left a caveat worth keeping: a single one of the radar returns "cannot be explained".
