35:["$","p","6",{"className":"text-lg leading-relaxed text-text md:text-xl","children":"The FAA's explanation and the others put forward have problems of their own. 36:["$","p","7",{"className":"text-lg leading-relaxed text-text md:text-xl","children":"The weather data cut against it: that afternoon's O'Hare weather report recorded 12 °C at the surface, overcast at 1,900 feet and haze with 5 miles' visibility; the sounding (measurements taken by an instrument balloon) launched that evening at Davenport shows 8 to 13 °C in the lowest 2,000 feet and does not drop below 0 °C until about 10,200 feet (3,100 m), and NARCAP puts the freezing level at 10,000 feet (about 3,000 meters). The cloud in which the hole opened was therefore made of water droplets that were not supercooled, and for that reason the report rules out any freezing mechanism.
