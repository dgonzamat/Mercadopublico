2a:T738,The Project Sign file (incident 172) shows far more limited corroboration than is usually told. The two tower controllers, L. D. Jensen and Manuel E. Johnson, saw a round white light the size of a tail lamp for "several seconds", in horizontal flight or a single turn, and saw it leave in a straight line; the oculist A. D. Cannon, who was flying in the Cub with his passenger Einar Neilson and later went up to the tower, saw it "at intervals for seconds only", also in a straight line. No one but Gorman saw the maneuvers; Ruppelt summarized that the other witnesses "did not witness all the maneuvers". Sign's investigators flew to Fargo within hours and ran a Geiger counter over the aircraft, whose slightly higher radioactivity was later attributed to cosmic rays from a recent flight; their October 1948 summary stated that meteorological balloon releases had been discredited, and Ruppelt found in the file an old memo on the meeting held upon the ATIC team's return which concluded that "some weird things were taking place". The explanation came later: in January 1949 the Air Weather Service listed incident 172 among the cases where a synoptic balloon "could have been" at the location, and an Aero Medical Laboratory memorandum of April 28, 1949, by then under Project Grudge, concluded the light may simply have been rising steeply on a relatively straight course, as a lighted weather balloon would, and that the Fargo weather station had released one within 10 minutes of the first sighting. The same memo added that at night, chasing a point of light with no other reference, a pilot can hardly tell which is moving, himself or the object.
