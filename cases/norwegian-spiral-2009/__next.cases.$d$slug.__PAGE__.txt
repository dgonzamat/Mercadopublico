4f:T4a6,The explanation, however, came quickly and was unequivocal. On 10 December 2009, barely a day later, Russia's Defence Ministry acknowledged that it had conducted a test of the submarine-launched intercontinental ballistic missile RSM-56 Bulava, and that it had failed. According to the statement, the first two stages worked normally, but the third suffered a technical malfunction: the engine deflected and expelled its fuel sideways, sending the missile into an uncontrolled spin. At high altitude (above 100 kilometres, already at the edge of space) the vented fuel particles were lit by the Sun, which was still below the horizon for ground observers; the missile's spin distributed that reflective fuel into the perfect spiral seen from the ground. The blue beam corresponded to the failed rocket's visible propulsive phase. The apparent absence of atmospheric drag confirmed the phenomenon occurred in the upper atmosphere, consistent with the missile's trajectory.
