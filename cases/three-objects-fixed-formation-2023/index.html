INDOPACOM area of responsibility (exact location undisclosed).Western Pacific · a sensor tracks three patches in formation

In 2023, an infrared sensor on a U.S. military platform in the Indo-Pacific Command area recorded nearly two uninterrupted minutes of three areas of contrast that, according to the description by the Pentagon office for these phenomena (AARO), “appear to maintain a fixed position and orientation relative to one another” while the sensor keeps them in the center of the frame. AARO warns that the description is not a judgment on the nature of the event. Whoever recorded it left no description, and no coordinates, platform or exact date were released; the official data index gives Japan as the location. It was released in May 2026.

Why no ordinary cause is identified
No source proposes what the areas of contrast were: AARO's record (DOW-UAP-PR47) only describes them and warns that this is not an analytical judgment, and no Metabunk or other analysis of this clip was found. The absence of a published analysis is not evidence against an ordinary cause: in its FY2024 report AARO closed 292 cases (118 during the period and 174 before publication), all as prosaic objects "including balloons, birds, UAS, satellites, and aircraft." The same report adds that the majority of its cases "remain unresolved due to a lack of data." (source ↗)

Record of the event

What happened

In 2023, an infrared sensor aboard a U.S. military platform recorded one minute and 59 seconds of video within the area of U.S. Indo-Pacific Command (INDOPACOM), which covers the Pacific and East Asia. An infrared sensor builds its image from the heat of what it points at. The command sent the video to the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO), the Pentagon office that studies these phenomena, which cataloged it. Whoever recorded it left no spoken or written description of what they saw.

AARO's official visual description is brief but precise on one point: for nearly the full two minutes, "the sensor tracks three distinct areas of contrast, maintaining their positions generally within the center of the frame. The areas of contrast appear to maintain a fixed position and orientation relative to one another." An area of contrast is a patch lighter or darker than the background; here there are three of them. The description mentions no crossing, separating or change of arrangement at any point in the recording. Like every description in the series, it warns that it is not an analytical judgment or a determination about the nature of the event.

The data index of the Department of War, the current name of the Department of Defense, gives Japan as the location of the recording. Metabunk is a skeptical forum run by Mick West, where members look for ordinary explanations of UFO photos and videos. In May 2026, in the forum's thread on another video from the same release, a participant remarked that the index gave "South China Sea" for this video and said he did not know how much to trust that column, because in other records it gave locations that did not match. Beyond that, nothing more was published about the location.

DVIDS, the public media archive of the same department, lists January 1, 2023 as the date taken, as it does for other videos in the series whose records state only the year. The video was published on DVIDS under the title "Unresolved UAP Report, INDOPACOM, 2023," as part of the first release of PURSUE, AARO's publication program, on May 8, 2026.

Why it matters

Everything known about the event comes from the video and AARO's description, which says what can be seen in the image and not what the person operating the sensor thought they saw.

That the sensor tracks the three patches means it turns to keep them in the center of the frame. The video therefore shows how they sit relative to one another, but not whether the group was moving relative to the sea or the ground, in which direction or how fast, and AARO's description does not say either.

No source proposes what they were: AARO only describes them, and no analysis of this video has been published on Metabunk or elsewhere. As a reading made for this case, not AARO's, three patches in fixed formation could be a group of drones or uncrewed aircraft flying in coordination, seen from so far away that the sensor cannot make out each craft, only its heat. Stars, planets or satellites in apparent conjunction could also produce three points that keep the same positions relative to one another. Without flight data, without a distance estimate and without knowing whether the three points share the same thermal signature (the same level of heat), there is no way to choose between those readings.

That nobody has published an analysis does not rule out an ordinary cause: in its 2024 report, AARO closed 292 cases, all as conventional objects including balloons, birds, drones, satellites and aircraft, and added that many other cases remain unresolved.
