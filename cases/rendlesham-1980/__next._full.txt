30:T971,Early on the 26th, around 3:00 a.m., a security patrol by Woodbridge's east gate (John Burroughs and Bud Steffens) saw red and blue lights among the trees and feared an aircraft had crashed. With permission from Central Security Control, Sgt. Jim Penniston, Burroughs and Airman Edward Cabansag went in on foot. The written statements the witnesses handed in over the following days (Master Sgt. J. D. Chandler's is dated 2 January 1981) are the record closest to the events. In his, Penniston describes a yellow light above the trees with a blinking red light and a blue one, and states that the object was "definitely mechanical", but specifies that he was never closer than about 50 metres and that it moved off in a zigzag through the trees; he does not mention touching it, engraved symbols or a notebook, details that entered his account decades later. Burroughs wrote that the woods lit up and that all three hit the ground, and Chandler, who relayed by radio from the edge of the forest, noted that he himself saw nothing at any time but that, after talking to the three, he was "sure that they had observed something unusual". Burroughs and Cabansag also say that, past a farmhouse, they followed a rotating beacon for about two miles before concluding it was a lighthouse. Penniston, however, did not take it for the same light: according to Chandler's statement, he said at the time that "this was not the light or lights he had originally observed", and according to that of Lt. Fred Buran, who directed the operation from Central Security Control (both dated 2 January 1981), when Buran asked whether he could have been mistaken, Penniston replied that had Buran seen the other lights he would know the difference. Buran added that, after talking to him face to face that morning, he was "convinced that he saw something out of the realm of explanation for him at that time", and called him "a totally reliable and mature individual", not overly excited. At 4:11 a.m. the base alerted the Suffolk police; the two officers who attended found nothing and, according to the Chief Constable's 1983 letter, "the only lights visible" to them were those of Orford Ness lighthouse.
