The written statements the witnesses handed in over the following days (Master Sgt. J. D. Chandler's is dated 2 January 1981) are the record closest to the events. In his, Penniston describes a yellow light above the trees with a blinking red light and a blue one, and states that the object was "definitely mechanical", but specifies that he was never closer than about 50 metres and that it moved off in a zigzag through the trees; he does not mention touching it, engraved symbols or a notebook, details that entered his account decades later. Burroughs wrote that the woods lit up and that all three hit the ground, and Chandler, who relayed by radio from the edge of the forest, noted that he himself saw nothing at any time but that, after talking to the three, he was "sure that they had observed something unusual". Burroughs and Cabansag also say that, past a farmhouse, they followed a rotating beacon for about two miles before concluding it was a lighthouse. Fred Buran, who directed the operation from Central Security Control (both dated 2 January 1981), when Buran asked whether he could have been mistaken, Penniston replied that had Buran seen the other lights he would know the difference. 34:T783,Read together, those documents support the conventional explanation better than the popular account suggests, without closing it. Astronomer and science writer Ian Ridpath, who has investigated the case since 1983, proposes converging mundane causes: a bright fireball seen over southern England around 3 a.m. on the 26th as the light that seemed to fall; the Orford Ness lighthouse, about eight kilometres away, as the flashing light seen through the trees, which matches what Burroughs and Cabansag wrote and what the police saw; and bright, low stars as the objects of Halt's second night. Computing positions for 3:15 a.m. on 28 December puts Vega and Deneb about 8 to 10 degrees above the horizon between north and north-east, and Sirius about 11 degrees towards the south-west, close to the height Halt recorded, although the rapid movements, colours and beam described on the tape require assuming twinkling, refraction and effects of the night scope.
