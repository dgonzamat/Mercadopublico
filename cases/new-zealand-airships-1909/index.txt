30:T660,The best-investigated episode is Kelso. According to the stationmaster, Mr Gibson, who telephoned the Otago Daily Times, at noon on Friday 23 July 1909 the schoolchildren saw in the air 'a strange machine' shaped like a boat, with what seemed the figure of a man seated in it, which came from the Blue Mountains, circled high over the school and went back the way it came; the correspondent added that it had a pontoon-shaped part above and a short mast, and that at least five people saw it. It was the dinner hour and nearly everyone was indoors; of the adults, Mrs Russell and George McDuff saw it. A special reporter from the paper went to the town on 28 July and, in an exercise remarkable for the time, interviewed the schoolchildren one by one and asked them to draw what they had seen, without seeing each other's drawings. He obtained six drawings whose resemblance he called 'nothing short of dumbfounding to all sceptics': one showed the craft from beneath with two sails on each side, another a wing with a revolving propeller at the rear. Thomas Jenkins said he watched it for about ten minutes, entirely black, with a wheel revolving very fast at the rear and a box underneath. That same week, the Kelso correspondent reported that a father, to test his son, had him draw what he saw and compared it with a picture in the Windsor Magazine, and the Mataura Ensign wrote that the children's sketches were reported to have been made from those published in a recent magazine article.
