Blue Book's files have been at the National Archives since 1976, open to public consultation; half a century later, the 2024 National Defense Authorization Act created Record Group 615 there — the Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena Records Collection — and obliged federal agencies to transfer digital copies of all their records on the subject. NARA issued formal guidance on identifying, preparing and transferring that material, and agencies have been delivering it in tranches that are published on a rolling basis in the online catalogue. The architecture is recognisable: a central, mandatory, searchable repository, which is exactly what Blue Book was for its era. The difference lies in the mandate — Blue Book existed to close cases, and its 1969 shutdown was justified on the grounds that nothing in it threatened national security; RG 615 exists to accumulate them and make them available, without the task of resolving them.
