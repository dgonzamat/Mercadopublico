🇧🇷1985·Sky over Belo Horizonte, Minas Gerais, Brazil

Brazil.Belo Horizonte · a military pilot follows and photographs lights

On the night of November 30, 1985, the control tower in Belo Horizonte, Brazil, watched a luminous object that radar did not detect, and several residents called in to report it. Approach control alerted Major Marco Aurélio de Magalhães Rocha, who was flying in on an Air Force training plane. Rocha radioed that he could see it too, closed to about four miles, described two points of light and another that moved below them, and took three photos. The radio transcript, his report and prints of the photos reached the Air Force Staff without any analysis.

What happened

On November 30, 1985, Major Marco Aurélio de Magalhães Rocha, an Air Force pilot, was flying from Uberaba to Belo Horizonte, in the state of Minas Gerais. He was at the controls of a T-25, a two-seat propeller training plane, with a lieutenant along as a passenger. The night was clear, with more than 10 km of visibility. According to the report Rocha wrote afterward, shortly after passing over Pará de Minas, west of Belo Horizonte, he saw a light in the sky a little to the left of his course.

At 23:56 universal time, which was 9:56 p.m. in Belo Horizonte under the daylight saving time then in force, approach control called the plane on the radio. The controller said the city's control tower was watching an object about 7 miles south of Belo Horizonte, that his radar was not showing it, and asked him to take a look. "Eu estou observando visualmente também," Rocha answered: he could see it too. He placed it first a little to his left and then almost straight ahead, and the controller told him the tower had been watching it for about ten minutes. Rocha described two points of light, the brighter one on the left and the dimmer one on the right, and said he would circle near the object to photograph it.

After passing over the city, Rocha asked to climb to 7,000 ft, about 2,100 meters, to keep a wider margin above the terrain. The controller cleared him and told him there was no other known aircraft in the area. As he closed in, Rocha said the object was lit all over, with a point of light beneath it, and that it had three clearly separate lit tips. At 6,800 ft, with the object ahead and a little to his right, he estimated it was about four miles away, though he said it was hard to judge. "Para quem acredita em disco voador, esse é decididamente um..." (for anyone who believes in flying saucers, this is definitely a...), he said, and the end of the sentence was unintelligible on the recording. He added that the point of light underneath kept moving.

Rocha then asked for the position of the nearby mountain range, and the controller told him the Pico da Piedade was to his right, two and a half miles away. Shortly afterward, the pilot said he had nothing more to watch, that there were some scattered cloud layers, and that he was continuing to Belo Horizonte reporting a "provável OVNI" (probable UFO). Before he landed, the controller asked whether he had taken photos. "Foram batidos tres fotografias" (three photographs were taken), he replied. The controller asked him, once he landed at Pampulha airport, to log the event in the occurrence book.

In his written report, Rocha said the light that had at first looked like a single one was two larger sources of light. The bigger one had two lit sections: one halfway up and a smaller one at the bottom, "como se fosse a luz indireta da boca de um grande balão" (as if it were the indirect light from the mouth of a large balloon). Below it and a little to the side was a smaller light, a strong orange point, which moved up and down and sideways. Around and below the object there was a bank of low stratocumulus cloud. He wrote that after the photos he turned away toward Belo Horizonte, and he attached a sketch of the object and the three negatives.

The approach control's written log differs on two points: the direction and the colors. It says that at 10:00 p.m. local time, when the shift began, the plane was already over Belo Horizonte, turning east to photograph the object, which radar did not detect. At the same time, the tower was receiving calls from people who were watching it. According to that log, the pilot placed the object east of the city and described it as two blue lights and a red one that moved up and down and sideways. The time fits the recording.

That direction contradicts the first one the controller gave over the radio, to the south, which is also the one in Rocha's report. But it fits the end of the recording, where the controller places Belo Horizonte to the plane's left and the Pico da Piedade to its right. The colors do not match the orange point in the report, and none of the documents explains that difference.

Why it matters

The case papers are kept in the UFO collection at Brazil's Arquivo Nacional: the approach control log, the transcript of the radio recording, the pilot's signed report, his sketch and copies of the photos. In 1986 they went up the Air Force chain of command to the Air Force Staff, with two cover letters that neither analyze the event nor offer an explanation.

The transcript records what the pilot said while he was watching, before he wrote his report, so the two versions can be compared. They agree on the essentials: two sources of light, a point moving underneath, a distance of about four miles and three photos. The report adds the comparison with the mouth of a balloon, which does not appear on the recording. The approach control radar did not detect the object, and the documents do not mention that any other radar was checked. Nor do they name the people who saw it from the tower or the residents who called.

The photos do not settle the case. Of the three photos, only one shows the light, and the file holds three prints of it. One shows the inside of the cockpit and the clear canopy. Another shows an elongated, out-of-focus patch of light with a fainter one below it, among reflections. The third shows the canopy frame with reflections on the glass. None of them shows the shape Rocha described. The sketch, initialed by him, is the only image of the object as he said he saw it, and no later analysis of the photos or the negatives has been published.

Rocha himself used the image of a balloon to describe the lower part of one of the lights, but he did not conclude that it was one: over the radio he reported a "provável OVNI." Six months later, on the night of May 19, 1986, the Air Force sent fighters after lights and radar returns over São Paulo and Goiás ↗ Official UFO Night. The Belo Horizonte case is smaller: a single training plane that approached an object seen only by eye, and what remains of it is above all what the pilot said over the radio at the time.
