🇦🇶1956·Coppermine refuge, Robert Island, South Shetland Islands (Antarctica)

Antarctica · Robert Island (Chilean expedition).Robert Island · four Chileans say they saw two "cylinders"

In January 1956, four Chileans of the 10th Antarctic Expedition, isolated in a refuge on Robert Island with a broken radio, said they saw for about 36 hours two metallic, cylinder-shaped objects standing vertical and still in the sky, which at times maneuvered in zigzags. The account became known eleven years later, in a ufological bulletin using false names; the only published first-hand testimony is that of the assistant Abelardo Báez, who told it in 2022. The sources disagree on the date, and one of the scientists' official report ends the stay before the day on which the account has them rescued.

What happened

In the southern summer of 1955-1956, the 10th Chilean Antarctic Expedition left four men at the Coppermine refuge on Robert Island, in the South Shetlands, to carry out scientific work for a month: the glaciologist Jorge Moder Jorquera, of the Catholic University; the marine biologist Celestino Castro Alvarenga, of the University of Chile's marine biology station at Montemar; his assistant, Abelardo Báez, aged 18; and the Navy medical sergeant Héctor Juan Adofacci. According to Báez, the radio they used every afternoon to talk to the Arturo Prat base, on Greenwich Island, broke down after a few days and they were left isolated.

What happened there is known only from later accounts. The first appeared in October 1967, unsigned, in issue 2 of the bulletin UFO Chile, under the headline "Scientists observe UFOs for two days over Antarctica". It used false names "at the request of the protagonists" and placed the events on Robertson Island, in the Weddell Sea. The printed text gives the day, January 8, but not the year: it only says the expedition took place "during the Second International Geophysical Year - 1956 to 1958". The year 1956 appears only in a handwritten note by an unknown hand on the scanned copy: "(January 8 to 10, 1956)".

According to the bulletin, on the morning of January 8, before seven, two of the expedition members saw two metallic, cigar-shaped objects, vertical and motionless, the apparent size of the full Moon, and soon all four were watching them. At about nine, one of them supposedly turned horizontal and zigzagged for about five minutes, changing color, before returning beside the other. A Geiger detector allegedly showed radioactivity forty times above normal; with a theodolite, using some cirrus clouds as a reference, one of the scientists supposedly calculated a height of about 8,000 meters, a length of about 150 meters and a speed of 40,000 kilometers per hour. When one of them pointed a polarizing lens at them, an object supposedly descended, and his companion, overcome by nerves, kicked the lens to pieces.

The objects supposedly disappeared during a storm, at about two in the morning two days later, after some 43 hours, and a helicopter, of unstated nationality, rescued the men on January 20. The bulletin added that they took many photos, that they told a senior Navy officer, who was not disturbed, and that the U.S. Air Technical Intelligence Center (ATIC) sent them a questionnaire.

The only published first-hand testimony is that of Abelardo Báez, who in 2022, aged 85 and the only survivor, gave his version to the newspaper El Porteño. According to him, everything happened "on January 4, 1956": Castro saw the objects first, then Moder and the sergeant came out, and he came last; they were "two zeppelins, like pencils", vertical and static, changing color, and they stayed "36 hours there, static". Moder took measurements with an altimeter and a radioactivity meter, and when he signaled with a lens the objects came closer, until Castro, frightened, smashed the mirror with a stick. According to Báez, the objects were always oriented toward the Prat base, and in their maneuvers they moved off in the direction of other bases.

Báez said that, back on the ship, the commander confiscated their rolls of film and forbade them to talk; that, as he later learned from Moder, the Navy sent the documentation to the United States; that they were picked up "around January 20", and that they managed to stay "about three weeks" on the island. He attributes the figures (about 150 meters long and "almost forty-five thousand kilometers per hour") to a document by Moder that he says he keeps. That year he also told his version on La Red's program Así Somos, after the Chilean researcher Patricio Abusleme located and interviewed him.

Báez also keeps the scientific reports that Moder and Castro handed to the commander-in-chief of the Tenth Antarctic Division, at Bahía Chile, on February 13, 1956. In his, Castro writes that the flag flew at the refuge "for 25 days, from December 19, 1955 to January 12, 1956". That report was published in the Chilean Navy's Anuario Hidrográfico in 1980. Both have been cited only for their dates and place, so it is not known whether they mention the phenomenon.

The account then circulated with changes. The official Chilean committee that studies these phenomena, CEFAA, published it on its website in 2015 under the title "Caso Isla Robertson en la Antártica", but it is a reissue of the 1967 text, with the same false names. Donald Johnson's chronology, citing an article by Gordon Creighton in Flying Saucer Review (March-April 1968, p. 20), says the Chilean expedition photographed at nine in the morning two cigar-shaped objects, standing vertical, that stayed in the sky for 64 minutes and whose presence was reportedly confirmed by radar; it does not mention the radioactivity, the zigzags or the lens. Larry Hatch's catalog, which also refers to Flying Saucer Review, records four Chilean scientists, two vertical metallic cigars 150 meters long, radioactivity, a photograph, 40,000 kilometers per hour and a duration of 64 minutes, with no radar. Creighton's article could not be consulted, so it is not known where the 64 minutes and the radar come from.

In 2016, an article by Pablo Villarrubia Mauso on the Mystery Planet website, which does not say where it takes the account from, dates the case to January 8, 1957, a year compatible with the bulletin's printed text, reduces the witnesses to two scientists who saw the objects "separately", limits their maneuvers to four hours and has "a British navy helicopter" pick them up on January 20. It adds that the scientists handed the photos to ATIC, which did not return the negatives and claimed they "were fogged", according to what one of the witnesses told the physicist Rafael Vera Mege, of the University of Concepción, years later. The book Alienígenas americanos (2022) dates the case to 1955.

The real names appeared in the second edition of Los ovnis de la Antártida (2018), by the Argentine Rubén Morales: those of the two scientists came from Pablo Petrowitsch, a friend of one of the witnesses and the origin of the CEFAA report, and he found the other two in documents of the time. Morales also placed the events on Robert Island, in the South Shetlands, not on Robertson Island.

Why it matters

The four witnesses are named, their presence on the island is recorded in documents of the time and one of them told the story in person; but everything that describes the phenomenon is late. The first text, from 1967, is anonymous, uses false names, sets the scene on another island and in another sea, and carries details that are hard to sustain: 21 degrees below zero in the middle of the Antarctic summer, a calculated speed of 40,000 kilometers per hour, a reference to theories of saucer propulsion and a "Second International Geophysical Year, 1956 to 1958" that never existed (the Geophysical Year was 1957-1958). Yet it is right about what could later be checked (two scientists, an assistant and a medical sergeant; a month's stay; the broken radio), and its division of roles matches Báez's: the one who sees first and breaks the lens is one man, the one who measures and signals is the other. That points to a source close to the witnesses.

The 1967 and 2022 versions agree on the essentials: two vertical cylinders, motionless for hours, that at times maneuver, seen for nearly two days (some 43 hours in the bulletin, 36 according to Báez); the radioactivity; the lens that draws them in and the companion who breaks it; and material sent to the United States. They do not agree on the date (January 8 in the bulletin, the 4th in Báez's memory) nor on what happened afterward: a confidence to an officer who was not disturbed, or confiscated film and an order of silence. The figures Báez attributes to a document by Moder almost match those of the bulletin: either they corroborate it, or Báez's memory has gradually adjusted to the published version.

A contemporary record clashes with both: the report Castro signed in February 1956 says the flag flew at the refuge until January 12, whereas both accounts have the rescue arrive around the 20th. If the flag was lowered on leaving the refuge, the departure came about eight days earlier than the story says; Báez himself reckons they stayed "about three weeks", closer to the report. Both sighting dates fall within that stay: the clash is only with the day of the rescue.

The later versions shift the date and the number of witnesses, Johnson's and Mystery Planet's shorten the duration, and what they add by way of backing, the radar and the negatives handed to ATIC, is not in the 1967 bulletin, which speaks of photos taken by the expedition members and of an ATIC questionnaire. Each step, from an anonymous text with pseudonyms to the page of an official body and a book chapter, gave it an appearance of backing the previous one lacked. No photo of the phenomenon has surfaced, the compilation of Chilean press coverage of the International Geophysical Year published in 2006 does not mention it, no Navy or ATIC file has been found, and no Antarctic case from 1956 appears in the digitized Blue Book collection. The Canal 13 interview with Moder that Báez remembers, and that El Porteño dates to April 1968, would be the testimony closest to the events from one of the scientists, and it has not been located.
