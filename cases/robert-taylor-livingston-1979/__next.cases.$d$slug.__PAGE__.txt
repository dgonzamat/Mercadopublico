11:Ta0b,What distinguishes the case is the institutional response. Because of Taylor's injuries, Lothian and Borders Police opened a file and recorded the matter as a common assault —unusual among UFO sightings, and the reason the BBC singles it out, because the police investigated it. At the site, officers documented physical marks: two parallel sets of ladder-rung-like tracks (about 2.5 meters) and between 32 and 40 circular holes about 9 cm in diameter, arranged consistently with the account of the smaller objects. According to the BBC, citing the police report of the time, the marks indicated that an 'object of several tons had stood there but there was nothing to show that it had been driven or towed away', and PC William Douglas wrote: 'There appeared to be no rational explanation for these marks'; Taylor's trousers were sent for forensic examination, which said they seemed to have been damaged by something hooking them and moving up. Taylor's personal credibility was a central factor: those who knew him described him as an honest man not given to fantasy —his employer, Malcolm Drummond, stated flatly that he was not one to make up stories—, he did not drink, and he held to the same account without changing it until his death in 2007. Prosaic explanations point to a medical episode. Physician Patricia Hannaford, who advised the sceptic Steuart Campbell, proposed an isolated attack of temporal lobe epilepsy, with the reported objects explainable as resultant hallucinations; she relied on Taylor's previous meningitis, the strong smell nobody else could detect, his headache, dry throat, the paralysis of his legs and his loss of consciousness. The BBC notes, however, that no evidence of an epileptic seizure was gathered at the time, and that his wife told police he had no history of mental illness, though he had had meningitis fourteen years earlier. Campbell suggested the attack might have been stimulated by a mirage of Venus, and in 2013 local businessman Phill Fenton suggested that Taylor 'may have suffered a mini-stroke' and that 'the UFO he believes he saw could have been a saucer-shaped water tower nearby'. For the ground marks, Campbell saw PVC pipes in an adjoining field, learned that the water authority had laid a duct within 100 metres of the clearing, and concluded that stacks of pipes stored there might have left them; that explanation was never confirmed and there is no agreed alternative.
