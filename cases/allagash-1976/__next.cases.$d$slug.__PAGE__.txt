1b:T746,The decisive blow to the case's credibility came in 2016, when Chuck Rak, one of the four, admitted in an interview that the abduction part had been made up and that he had gone along with the story: he supported it at first 'because I wanted to make money', and called it 'brilliant storytelling', not 'the truth'. He added that his own hypnosis produced no recall, although he had publicly claimed otherwise. Rak did not retract the light: he maintained that he saw 'a very, very bright globe of light' changing from white to red to green, and said the group also saw the craft two nights earlier, on Chamberlain Lake. He also said a park ranger told them at the time that what they saw was men operating a searchlight from the back of a pickup, for a hardware store grand opening in Millinocket; Rak rejects that explanation because the store was about 75 miles away and it was nine at night. The same interview gives the retraction's context: according to Rak, the group stayed together speculating it could make 'millions of dollars for each of us', made 'very little', and he began telling people the abduction never happened after falling out with the others; even so, he declined to call it a hoax. The other three (Jim and Jack Weiner and Charlie Foltz) have held to their version to this day. That internal fracture leaves the case in an awkward position: it is no longer a fourfold concordant testimony, but three witnesses who maintain an account and a fourth who says it was invented. It does not prove the other three are lying (sincere hypnotic confabulation is perfectly compatible with their conviction) but it removes the piece that made the case "exceptional".
