17:T402,Wanaque Reservoir, New Jersey's largest water reservoir, was in 1966 the scene of one of the most cited collective sighting waves on the U.S. East Coast. The most intense episode began on the night of 11 January 1966. At 6:20 PM a Pompton Lakes newspaper editor, Howard Ball, 36, saw from the intersection of Hamburg Turnpike and Colfax Road, in Wayne, a circular, pea-sized, blue-white light 'resembling a burning magnesium' and at times pulsing like a strobe light, which stopped, moved sideways and toward him, and went off at jet speed. According to the period press, as cited later by compilations, the Wanaque and Pompton Lakes police received more than 150 calls that night; the military telex, more cautious, notes that the Pompton Lakes and Ringwood police received six between 6:30 and 7:30 PM. A New York journalist who called Blue Book at 5:15 AM the next day, after speaking with the mayor, placed the object over the reservoir from 7:30 to 8:30 PM and again from 2:20 AM on 12 January until the end of the night.18:T56e,That file does record the military response. The New York Air Defense Sector, at McGuire, used two Nike missile batteries to scan the area: number 74 saw nothing, and number 75 saw an object moving at aircraft speed, a bright light that dimmed, northeast of the battery and heading east. There was no radar contact. The sector's intelligence officer, Major Harold J. O'Connell Jr., rated the sighting 'unknown'. Blue Book closed it as 'probable aircraft', citing the density of New York air traffic 'without additional information to eliminate' that possibility, and for the reservoir report it proposed from its office Saturn and, for the early morning, Jupiter. The activity returned in October: the file dates the main episode to 11 October around 9:00 PM, and the press quoted Sergeant Ben Thompson of the reservoir police describing an intensely bright light over Windbeam Mountain. According to a January 1967 memo, the officers claimed that after that sighting six or seven helicopters and ten to twelve jets came into the area; Stewart AFB said none of its aircraft were there, and McGuire that it had no helicopters and none of its aircraft were flying in the area. A bright point light seen over frozen water, with reflections and no distance cues, 'rocks' and 'dances' through the autokinetic effect, and battery 75 described what it saw as a light moving at aircraft speed in the densest air corridor in the country.
