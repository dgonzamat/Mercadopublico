2e:T50c,In the early hours of 1 January 1975, four soldiers of the Army Engineers Academy, in Burgos, were driving back from New Year leave to report for duty. They were travelling in a Mini 850 owned by the driver, a 22-year-old soldier from Torrelavega, a waiter by trade, who according to his statement had been serving in a dance hall until about three in the morning; the others were two 22-year-old soldiers, from Santander and from Polanco, and an 18-year-old from Vega de Pas. At about six, at kilometre 14 of the Burgos to Santander road, at the spot known as El Andrinal, the driver saw through the left-hand window 'an intense light that was falling towards the ground'. He braked and alerted the others; on getting out they saw the light already stopped, on the ground or very close to it, at a distance they reckoned at three or four hundred metres: yellowish white, two to three metres high and wide, shaped like an inverted bell or a truncated cone. They drove on and, a kilometre further on, at kilometre 13, got out again: in the same direction they saw three or four much fainter lights, also stationary and at ground level. Frightened, they carried on towards Burgos. None of them heard any noise, and according to their statements at no time did they see an object, 'only a glow'.2f:T4b3,On 10 January the Chief of the Air Staff ordered by telephone that an inquiry be opened, and the lieutenant colonel who was air military commander of Burgos, with the permission of the captain general of the 6th Military Region, summoned the four soldiers to Villafría military aerodrome on 15 January, two weeks after the events. The statements agree on the essentials and show their limits: the soldier from Santander got out on the right and only saw the light once it was fixed; the one from Polanco only saw it when his companions alerted him; the one from Vega de Pas had been asleep in the back seat, did not get out at the first stop and at the second saw three or four lights without being able to say their size, shape or distance. None of them took photographs or made drawings at the time. When giving his statement, the driver, 'prompted and helped by the Investigating Officer', made some pencil strokes that show 'more or less a balloon'; another drew something like an inverted bell, and the youngest said he could not draw it, but that his companions' strokes resembled what he had seen. Those drawings are not in the file.
