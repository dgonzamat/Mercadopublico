🇧🇷1986·Skies over São Paulo, Rio de Janeiro and Goiás

Brazil.Official UFO Night · Brazilian jets chase lights seen on radar

On the night of May 19–20, 1986, Brazilian controllers and radars logged unidentified lights and contacts over São Paulo and Goiás; the press later spoke of some 21 objects. The Air Force scrambled five fighters from Santa Cruz (Rio) and Anápolis (Goiás): three picked something up on their radar or saw it, one of them something that pulled away even at supersonic speed, and two detected nothing. The Minister of Aeronautics, Octávio Moreira Lima, confirmed it at a press conference with the pilots, and the confidential air defense report concluded that the phenomena were "solid" and reflected "in some way intelligence."

Why no ordinary cause is identified
No named source proposes a cause. That the PT-MBZ pilot saw lights that "looked like large red stars" and that Mars was rising in the east-south-east (an ephemeris calculation) is not a proposal; the COMDA report, for its part, interprets the phenomena as "solid and reflecting, in a certain way, intelligence." The absence of a published analysis is not evidence against an ordinary cause: in its 2024 report AARO stated that "all 174 cases have been finalized as resolved to prosaic objects including balloons, birds, UAS, satellites, and aircraft." The same report adds that "many other cases remain unresolved," so the figure is the rate among resolved cases, not among all cases. (Arquivo Nacional (Brazil) — COMDA “Relatório de Ocorrência” (2 Jun 1986), ARX.0.0.249, in Portuguese · AARO — Fiscal Year 2024 Consolidated Annual Report on UAP (14 Nov 2024), base rate ↗)

What happened

During the night of May 19, 1986, the Brazilian air defense system recorded unusual activity. According to the report of the Aerospace Defense Command (COMDA), the first alert came at 23:15 UTC (20:15 Brasília time): the Brasília Area Control Center reported that the São José dos Campos tower operator (named in the press as Sérgio Mota da Silva) was seeing lights moving over the city, mostly red but changing to yellow, green and orange, and São Paulo approach control confirmed radar contacts in the area.

At 00:08 UTC the pilot of aircraft PT-MBZ saw, south-southeast of the São José dos Campos radio beacon (on its 150 radial), lights that "looked like large red stars" moving from east to west, and could not get closer to them. Later, Anápolis (Goiás) approach control reported blips on its radar with defined heading and speed. The figure of some twenty-one objects spread by the press does not appear in the COMDA report, which speaks of "some plots" (the marks an operator initiates on the scope) and of blips without giving a number. Among the civilian witnesses, according to the press, was Colonel Ozires Silva, founder of Embraer, who was flying to São José dos Campos after meeting President José Sarney in Brasília.

The Brazilian Air Force (FAB) responded with an interception operation that scrambled five fighters: F-5Es of the 1st Fighter Group from Santa Cruz Air Base (Rio de Janeiro) and Mirage F-103s (Mirage III) of the 1st Air Defense Group from Anápolis (Goiás). The COMDA report sets the takeoffs: the first F-5 from Santa Cruz at 01:34 UTC (22:34 local time), the first Mirage from Anápolis at 01:48, the second F-5 at 01:50 and two further Mirages at 02:17 and 02:36; the pilots named by the press were Lieutenant Kleber Caldas Marinho and Captains Armindo Sousa Viriato de Freitas, Márcio Brisolla Jordão, Rodolfo Silva e Souza and Júlio Cézar Rozemberg.

According to the report, the first F-5 pilot, leveled at 17,000 feet, saw a white light below him that climbed until it held about 10 degrees above him, followed it to 33,000 feet with his onboard radar marking it at 10 to 12 miles, and chased it out to sea for some 180 miles before breaking off for fuel.

The first Mirage made five approaches in which its onboard radar caught and followed something, but saw nothing; once it came within 2 miles of a blip that zigzagged or turned sharply, varied its speed and suddenly pulled away even though the fighter was flying at supersonic speed. The second F-5 saw a red light where ground radar indicated, which did not come closer and went out just as the contact was lost; at one point thirteen radar marks appeared behind the aircraft, which turned 180 degrees without gaining visual or onboard-radar contact. The last two Mirages had no contact at all.

What turned the episode into "the Official Night" was its recognition by the state. On May 23, 1986 the Minister of Aeronautics, Lieutenant Brigadier Octávio Júlio Moreira Lima, called a press conference with the pilots at his side and confirmed that the FAB had chased objects it could not identify, with no conclusive technical explanation.

On June 2 the acting commander of COMDA, Brigadier José Pessoa Cavalcanti de Albuquerque, sent the General Air Command a confidential eight-page "Relatório de Ocorrência." After warning that, for lack of time and specialized knowledge, it confined itself to a "simple narration of the facts," the report lists constant features: the phenomena produced blips simultaneously on ground radars and on the interceptors' radars, varied their speed from subsonic to supersonic and could hover, changed altitude between below 5,000 and above 40,000 feet, sometimes showed white, green or red lights and at other times none, accelerated and decelerated abruptly, and made turns sometimes of constant radius and sometimes of undefined radius.

It concludes that the phenomena "are solid and reflect in some way intelligence, through their ability to follow and keep distance from the observers as well as to fly in formation, not necessarily crewed." According to the press, the National Archive also holds audio of that night's pilot-controller communications.

Some of the arguments against are in the COMDA report itself: the network's radars were air-traffic-control sets, not specific to air defense, detections had to be initiated by hand by an operator and the contacts "were not strong enough" to be tracked; the marks that prompted the first scramble "did not constitute defined radar returns"; the Anápolis approach blips did not show on the military center's equipment; and two of the five fighters detected nothing. Other analysts have proposed anomalous propagation (an atmospheric effect that fools radar), mis-correlated air traffic and astronomical sources.

Computing positions shows that Mars, reddish and at magnitude −1.2, was rising in the east-southeast around midnight UTC and stood about 21 degrees high when the first Mirage took off, with Saturn and Antares high in the east; they may explain some lights the pilots saw, but not the 20:15 observation over São José dos Campos, when Mars had not yet risen, nor the onboard-radar contacts.

Why it matters

The Official Night combines radar detection at several control centers, an interception operation with five fighters, onboard-radar contacts in two of them, professional pilot witnesses, the public acknowledgment of the phenomenon by the Minister of Aeronautics with the pilots present, and an official report that speaks of "intelligence." The conventional explanations proposed (anomalous propagation, mis-correlated air traffic, astronomical sources) do not fully account for the convergence the report describes: ground radars, onboard radars on two fighters and visual observations within the same window of time.

The COMDA report also matters for what it does not do. It does not count the objects or offer a hypothesis, relies on what controllers and pilots said and on earlier reports by I CINDACTA (the integrated air defense and air traffic control center) that it does not attach, and states that it confines itself to narration so as not to feed speculation; its conclusion about "solid" and "intelligent" phenomena is an operational judgment by a military command based on those accounts, not the result of analyzing raw data. That is why the figure of twenty-one objects, which does not appear in the report and which the press attributes to Ministry of Justice records, and the specific speeds in popular accounts should be treated with caution, whereas the takeoff times, the onboard-radar contacts and the two fighters' lack of contact are in the document.
