4f:T497,During the night of 19 May 1986, the Brazilian air defence system recorded unusual activity. According to the report of the Aerospace Defence Command (COMDA), the first alert came at 23:15 UTC (20:15 Brasília time): the Brasília Area Control Centre reported that the São José dos Campos tower operator (named in the press as Sérgio Mota da Silva) was seeing lights moving over the city, mostly red but changing to yellow, green and orange, and São Paulo approach control confirmed radar contacts in the area. At 00:08 UTC the pilot of aircraft PT-MBZ saw, on the 150 radial of the São José dos Campos VOR, lights that "looked like large red stars" moving from east to west, and could not get closer to them. Later, Anápolis (Goiás) approach control reported returns on its radar with defined heading and speed. The figure of some twenty-one objects spread by the press does not appear in the COMDA report, which speaks of "some plots" and of returns without giving a number. Among the civilian witnesses, according to the press, was Colonel Ozires Silva, founder of Embraer, who was flying to São José dos Campos after meeting President José Sarney in Brasília.50:T600,The Brazilian Air Force (FAB) responded with an interception operation that scrambled five fighters: F-5Es of the 1st Fighter Group from Santa Cruz Air Base (Rio de Janeiro) and Mirage F-103s (Mirage III) of the 1st Air Defence Group from Anápolis (Goiás). The COMDA report sets the takeoffs: the first F-5 from Santa Cruz at 01:34 UTC (22:34 local time), the first Mirage from Anápolis at 01:48, the second F-5 at 01:50 and two further Mirages at 02:17 and 02:36; the pilots named by the press were Lieutenant Kleber Caldas Marinho and Captains Armindo Sousa Viriato de Freitas, Márcio Brisolla Jordão, Rodolfo Silva e Souza and Júlio Cézar Rozemberg. According to the report, the first F-5 pilot, levelled at 17,000 feet, saw a white light below him that climbed until it held about 10 degrees above him, followed it to 33,000 feet with a contact on his onboard radar at 10 to 12 miles, and chased it out to sea for some 180 miles before breaking off for fuel. The first Mirage made five passes with contact and lock-on on its radar but saw nothing; once it came within 2 miles of a return that zigzagged or turned sharply, varied its speed and suddenly pulled away even though the fighter was flying at supersonic speed. The second F-5 saw a red light where ground radar indicated, which did not come closer and went out just as the contact was lost; at one point thirteen plots appeared behind the aircraft, which turned 180 degrees without gaining visual or onboard-radar contact.
