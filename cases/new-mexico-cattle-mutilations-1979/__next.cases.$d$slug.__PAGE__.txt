4d:T72b,The case that set the pattern began on the night of June 13, 1976, when Manuel Gomez reported a dead cow on his ranch, 13 miles east of Dulce according to the police report; State Police officer Gabe Valdez examined it the next morning and described a three-year-old cow without its left ear, tongue, udder or rectum, removed with what appeared to be "a sharp precise instrument", and a trail of circular tripod marks, about 4 inches across, that followed the cow for some 600 feet. According to his report, the State Police laboratory and the medical examiner's office analyzed skin samples and reported that the skin "had been cut with a sharp instrument". He also noted a yellow oily substance that no laboratory was able to identify, and radiation readings twice normal background, taken three days later by Howard Burgess, a retired Sandia scientist. He also wrote that "the tripod marks had returned and removed the left ear", which was intact when Gomez found the animal. His report attributed the mutilations to a "sophisticated and secretive operation", not to cults or predators. The second wave came in 1978: an eleven-month-old Gomez bull on April 24, its liver "all white" and "like mush"; several cows in May with legs Valdez judged to be broken and marks he read as clamps; and another Gomez cow on June 14. Los Alamos examined tissue from the bull and replied on May 9, 1978 that a heart culture grew Clostridium, but that possible contamination prevented the conclusion that infection was the cause of death; according to Rommel, the scientist who ran the test informally thought the animal had probably died of blackleg.
