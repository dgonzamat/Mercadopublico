26:T4e9,The episode belongs to a tradition of less-documented Salvadoran sightings. The most cited antecedent is that of January 15, 1969: around 3:30 in the afternoon, under a cloudy sky, a bright plate-shaped object flew over San Salvador, according to El Diario de Hoy's front-page story the next day, "many thousands of meters up"; two carpenters working on the terrace of El Diario de Hoy alerted journalists, and witnesses described it as a fast disc that at times released black smoke before moving off toward Cerro de San Jacinto. Among the calls the newsroom received, someone suggested it might be the Soviet crewed mission Soyuz 4, launched on January 14 of that year. There was also, decades later, a wave on February 29, 1996, when numerous people across the country reported lights crossing the sky at high speed, triggering a flood of calls to the media and even to the meteorology office at Ilopango airport itself; according to Flotilla Aérea's compilation, some experts attributed it at the time to the TSS-1R tethered satellite, which had broken away from the shuttle Columbia during the STS-75 mission.
