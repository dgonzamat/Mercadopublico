12:T6cf,On Monday 16 August 1954 —Campagnac wrote in 1964 that he did not remember the exact date, only that it was a Monday in August, at six in the evening and already at dusk (other versions say five); the date comes from a Malagasy policeman's calendar—, the capital of Madagascar — then Tananarive, in the French colony — was the scene of one of the most documented mass sightings of the era. Staff at the Air France office, among them Edmond Campagnac, head of the company's technical service on the island, saw a large sphere of an «electric green» falling from the east in a straight line, inclined at about 45 degrees, which, Campagnac wrote in 1964, «immediately made us think of a meteorite»; it seemed to pass by the Queen's Palace and disappeared behind the hills to the south. Half a minute later, according to that account, it reappeared over the Governor's Palace, came down toward the centre and travelled along the Avenue de la Libération some 100 metres up and at about 400 km/h: in front, a large, very bright green «lens» and, some 40 metres behind, a cigar-shaped «fuselage» the size of a DC-4 with an aluminium sheen, followed by a few orange sparks, all in complete silence; the observation lasted about two minutes. Later versions, such as the one compiled by Patrick Gross, speak of a football-shaped craft with bluish flames, 50 to 150 metres up. Campagnac estimated in 1964 that some 20,000 people saw it. The next morning, at Ivato airfield, a friend told him he had had to ask for help to round up his herds, scattered the previous evening around six by the craft's passage over La Sakay, some 100 kilometres away in a straight line; later versions speak of 150 kilometres to the south and a farm school.13:T611,What sets Tananarive apart from an ordinary mass sighting are the reported physical effects and the quality of the witnesses. Campagnac wrote in 1964 that when the craft entered the avenue the lights of the whole city went out. After a 1969 television broadcast in which he told the story, another witness, former warrant officer Grimout, wrote that he was at a dentist's on the same avenue and that the blackout began three or four seconds after he saw the craft and lasted about ten minutes, although he described a dark mass with some ten lights like portholes; he believed the press had only spoken of a general power failure. After that broadcast four viewers phoned to confirm the sighting, and in 1988-1989 a Malagasy witness came forward, former policeman Razafimahatratra, on duty outside the Métro cinema, who saw a «flying saucer» over Analakely at six; his report burned in the 1975 town-hall fire. But Grimout placed the passage between 6:30 and 7 p.m. and gave it another path —north to south, then a 90-degree turn following the Tamatave railway—, and Joël Mesnard, who searched the Madagascar press, found no report of the event. The animals in the park where peasants kept livestock for the markets panicked as the object passed, and at La Sakay the herds scattered, a detail suggesting a real, physical stimulus, not merely a visual one.
