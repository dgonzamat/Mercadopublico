🇦🇺1972·Moorooduc Road, between Frankston and Dromana, Victoria, Australia

Australia.Puddy · a driver sees a blue light over her car

On July 3, 1972, around 9:00 p.m., Maureen Puddy, a housewife from Rye (Victoria, Australia), was driving home from visiting her son in the hospital when a blue light lit up Moorooduc Road. She said she stopped, saw an object shaped like two saucers placed together and, on her form, that it followed her as she left. On July 25 she said another blue light stopped her car and a voice in her head asked her to tell the media. The Royal Australian Air Force (RAAF) found no aircraft in the area the first time and left the second event as "Unknown"; three other reports of lights that second night disagree on time, place and description.

Why no ordinary cause is identified
None of the cited sources proposes an ordinary cause: the RAAF left the second event as "Unknown" and did not rule on the first; Magee, who dealt with the witness, asks whether it was hypnosis by the beings, and Norman whether it was hypnosis "by someone." The absence of a published analysis is not evidence against an ordinary cause: the Pentagon office AARO's 2024 report finalized "all 174 cases" as "resolved to prosaic objects," though "many other cases remain unresolved." Against it, the RAAF found no helicopters or aircraft in the area on the first night, and Cox recorded that Beale saw a similar blue light, though the Beales' form speaks of a pinkish glow. (RAAF file A703 580/1/1 · Magee (1997) · Norman (1980) · AARO, Fiscal Year 2024 Consolidated Annual Report on UAP (November 14, 2024), base rate ↗)

What happened

Maureen Puddy, 37, was a housewife in Rye, in the Australian state of Victoria. Her son was in a Melbourne hospital with a broken leg from an accident at school; he had been taken there in an air ambulance helicopter. She visited him on Mondays, Wednesdays and Fridays. On the night of Monday, July 3, 1972, she was driving home alone in her 1963 Holden station wagon along Moorooduc Road, between Frankston and Dromana. (The Victorian UFO Research Society, VUFORS, said in October 1972 that it was July 5.)

Soon after she crossed the railway line, a blue light lit up the road. She thought it was a helicopter, like the ones that had carried her son, and sped up to get out of the light, but the light stayed with her; when she slowed to about 20 miles an hour (30 km/h), it slowed too. Finally she pulled over, thinking the helicopter was in trouble, and got out of the car.

According to the form she filled in, the object hovered about 100 feet (30 m) off the ground and was wider than the road. She heard a soft purring sound, which she compared to an elevator. She froze for about two minutes, got back in the car and drove off at high speed. The form notes that the object "followed car" and that she last saw it at the junction with the Nepean Highway. In a handwritten note she added that it followed her about eight miles (some 13 km), a figure she attributed to the Rosebud police, where she reported at about 9:15 p.m. She later described the object to Garry Little and Bill Stapleton, two Melbourne investigators who interviewed her at the time: two saucers placed lip to lip, with no seams, windows or antennas, and an iridescent blue light that lit the road. Researcher Keith Basterfield summarized their report on his blog in 2012.

Four days later, on July 7, she signed the Royal Australian Air Force (RAAF) form. On July 11 an officer noted that no military helicopter was operating; he had checked with civil aviation and local operators, and the only traceable helicopter was based at Sale, in eastern Victoria. In the unit's evaluation, nearby military and civil aircraft are listed as "Nil," and on the probable-cause question neither option was marked.

On the night of Monday, July 24, at about 8:00 p.m., she thought she heard someone calling her by name; neither her husband nor her daughter had heard anything. As she later told Little and Stapleton, the voice kept calling her through the night, and later she thought it had been inside her head.

On Tuesday, July 25, the date her forms give, she was driving home from the hospital again, on the same road and at about the same time, according to Little and Stapleton's account. She braked at the railway crossing to let a man leading a cow go by. A few hundred meters farther on, the road lit up blue again. She said she thought "My God, it's back," decided not to stop for anything and sped up. Then the engine cut out, as if she had turned the key, and the car glided to the edge of the road by itself. The steering wheel, the brakes and the gear lever did not respond; the lights stayed on.

According to that same account, all sound "drained out of the air" and she felt as if she were sitting "in a tube, a completely closed, vacuum tube." At first she picked up impressions she could not understand. Then she heard them, inside her head, in "perfect English," in a voice that seemed to her like a mechanical recording: "All your tests will be negative," a pause of two or three seconds, "Tell the media. Do not panic. We mean no harm," another pause of about a minute, and "You now have control."

She estimated it all lasted a minute and a half. The engine started by itself, the light and the object were gone, and another vehicle approached. Judith Magee, vice president of VUFORS, who spoke with her by phone, wrote in an unsigned piece in the society's October 1972 bulletin that the voice first spoke in a foreign language and then in "almost perfect English."

She went to the Rosebud police station and made a report. According to the note of the RAAF officer who evaluated the case, she also phoned the RAAF duty officer that evening. She told him the object, silvery blue, "took control of the car": she pulled over at the roadside and could not open the doors or restart the engine. She "sensed," without hearing it, a message that the note gives as "Tell the media not to panic." When the object vanished, she started the car and headed home.

The form for this second event, dated July 28, says less: it does not mention the stopped car or the voice. It gives 9:15 p.m. and an iridescent blue light above the car, at about 100 feet, that "did not move" for five minutes, made no sound and "just vanished." Asked how many witnesses there were, she answered "1 man said he saw the light, other reports of light being seen."

The officer who wrote the evaluation on August 21 noted that what she stated in the form "differs markedly" from what she told the duty officer. He recorded that the cause "cannot be determined" and classed the object as "Unknown." He added that Senior Constable Cox, of the Rosebud police, knew her and said she seemed a rational woman not given to great flights of fancy, and that Cox was amazed when she asked him the meaning of the word "media," which she said she had never heard.

According to Little and Stapleton's report, on July 26 she phoned three television stations, one of which interviewed her; she also spoke on a call-in program on 3AW, a Melbourne radio station. The wife of the man with the cow called the program to say he had seen a blue light and had not looked again.

Along with Puddy's form, the RAAF filed those of two other witnesses who also placed their sighting on July 25: William and Mary Beale, of Main Ridge, and Florence Toal, of Montrose. The Beales saw at about 10:00 p.m., for two minutes, an object with a pinkish glow that rose quickly toward the east; Toal, after 8:00 p.m., a bluish-white glow to the south-southwest for 50 seconds. Mrs. Davis, who phoned but never sent in a form, described a very fast object with blue, red and yellow lights, visible for about three seconds between 9:00 and 10:00 p.m. Mrs. Beale, Toal and Davis said they came forward after hearing Puddy on the radio or a news item about the light.

Cox also told the officer, according to his note, that William Beale went out at about 10:00 p.m. to check his poultry sheds, after a fluctuation in the electric power, and saw over the hill an object like Puddy's that lit up the whole area in blue. By phone, Mrs. Beale told the officer that her husband saw at that hour a light crossing from west to east at high speed with a humming sound, and did not mention the power fluctuation.

The sources give two timelines for the second event. According to the forms and to Little and Stapleton, it happened on Tuesday, July 25, and she called the TV stations the next day. According to the officer's note, it happened on a Monday night, and Mrs. Beale heard Puddy on the radio on the morning of the 25th. The 1972 VUFORS bulletin gives July 27.

The third episode is known only from accounts published years later. Magee was there with Paul Norman, also of VUFORS, and told it in 1980 and in 1997. She places it "about six months" after the second event, in summer, which in Australia means early 1973, although her 1997 text says all three episodes took place in 1972. Little and Stapleton's version, as Basterfield summarizes it, dates it around February 22, 1973.

According to Magee, Puddy phoned her agitated: for days she had been hearing a voice saying "Maureen, Maureen, return to the meeting place." Magee and Norman arranged to meet her at about 8:30 p.m. Puddy arrived running and said she had nearly gone off the road. When Magee got into Puddy's car, she felt a tingling "like a mild electric shock"; Puddy told her that a small figure in a suit like foil (tin in 1980, gold in 1997) had appeared on the front seat. At the meeting place she said she saw the figure beside the car, though Magee and Norman saw nothing. She said it moved aside to let Norman pass and beckoned her to follow; she refused.

Then she described a round room, with no doors or windows, with a central object "like a mushroom," jelly-like, with lines like Roman numerals, and she cried: "I can't get out." Magee urged her to close her eyes and felt her relax as if in a trance; later Puddy said "I'm back in there again" and, at the end, "he's gone." When Magee asked whether the object could be a gyroscope, a word Puddy did not know, someone suggested "compass," and she took from her dashboard a compass on a suction cup that was, Magee wrote, "exactly what I had envisaged."

In Little and Stapleton's version, the figure was a "man" with long blond hair in a suit like white satin; one of the investigators, on getting into the car, said there seemed to be "a presence" in it, and Puddy, on coming out of the trance, remembered nothing.

Basterfield wrote in 2015 that in the early 1980s, about ten years after the events, he heard from her, that she had moved out of Victoria, and that he knew of no one who had ever re-interviewed her. In 1988 a press report reprinted in the VUFORS bulletin said the society had lost touch with her; Magee wrote in 1997 that Puddy addressed a quarterly VUFORS meeting in 1995; the year may be a misprint.

Why it matters

What she said, and when, was written down in 1972: two forms filled in within days of each event, the note of an officer who cites the constable who took her report, and the unit's evaluations. That record does not confirm what she described: the RAAF worked from the forms and by phone.

The most delicate point is the difference between versions of the second event. To the duty officer she spoke of a stopped car and a message she "sensed" without hearing it; to the investigators, of a voice inside her head; the form records neither. They also differ on whether she could move: she told the officer she could not open the doors, and told Little and Stapleton she believed she could have moved but chose to stay still. Nothing in the record says which version is right: she may have written less than what happened, or the account may have grown. The message, with its instruction to address the media, already appears in the officer's note of August 1972.

None of the other people confirms the core of the account, which is the stopped car and the message: William Beale's blue light comes only from Cox, at second hand, and the other lights were seen in other places. The man with the cow, the witness closest to the second event, was not interviewed, according to Basterfield. There is no known radar record or photograph.

Those who knew the witness took her seriously. Magee wrote in 1972 that she believed the account genuine and saw no reason for her to invent it. In 1997 she said that, for her and Norman, the case looked more like mind control or hypnosis than an abduction, and she wondered whether the beings Puddy described had hypnotized her, and how many people "they" could hypnotize. Norman suggested in 1980 that Puddy may have been under hypnosis "by someone" and asked by whom; in 1993 he denied it was an abduction. There is no known published skeptical critique of the case, nor any documented clinical hypnosis session or regression.

The sources also disagree on the hospital where her son was (Heidelberg according to Basterfield, the Royal Children's according to Magee) and on what the object did on the first night: according to her form, her note and Magee in 1997, it followed her when she drove off; according to the 1972 bulletin, it stayed above the car while she sped up and slowed down, but "flew off at terrific speed" as soon as she stopped.

Primary documents

RAAF file A703 580/1/1, part 17: Puddy's first report (July 1972)

Pages 23 to 31 of the RAAF file on unidentified aerial objects. It gathers the July 11, 1972 memo, the form Puddy signed on July 7, her handwritten note, the sketch and Officer Gibbs's note: no military helicopter operating, and the only traceable helicopter on an instrument flight plan was based at Sale. The answers and notes are handwritten.

RAAF file A703 580/1/1, part 18: second report and evaluation (July and August 1972)

Pages 91 to 105. It includes the August 21, 1972 memo that treats the reports of Puddy, the Beales and Toal as one, Puddy's July 28 form, those of the two other witnesses, and the RAAF officer's handwritten note that classes the object as "Unknown" and records what Puddy said by phone.

VUFORS bulletin of October 1972: "I've been chased by a flying saucer"

Unsigned first-person account by VUFORS vice president Judith Magee, to whom Puddy told her story by phone and in person; the oldest civilian text known on the case.

It dates the first event July 5 and the second July 27. In the first, the light stays above the car while she speeds up and slows down, and the object flies off at great speed once she stops; at the police station, Puddy said she had been chased by a flying saucer. According to Magee, the RAAF told her there were no aircraft in the area and asked her not to tell anyone, which decided her to notify the media. Magee writes that she believes the account genuine.

Magee, "The Close Encounter of Maureen Puddy," Australian UFO Bulletin (September 1997)

Magee's 1996 text, presented in June 1997 at the "50 Years On" conference in Sydney. It is the account of the third episode by someone who was present, with the suction-cup compass and her reflections on a hypnosis she attributes to the beings. It says she believes Puddy did not return the RAAF questionnaire; the file shows both forms, the second received August 17.
