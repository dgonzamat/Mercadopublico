Chile.Antofagasta · an aircrew films three flashing lights

Shortly after midnight on November 23, 2023, the crew of a flight from Iquique to Santiago with a Chilean Air Force call sign saw over the Antofagasta Region three lights that flashed on and off like stars and at one point began to move, and filmed them with an iPhone. SEFAA, Chile's official office for these phenomena, analyzed the video's 1,110 frames, fixed the time and the aircraft's position, and concluded that "what was reported was" the overflight of three Starlink satellites and the remains of the Chinese satellite Fengyun 1C, without assigning each light to one of them. It classified the case as "Probably Explained."

What happened

The notice reached SEFAA on November 24, 2023. SEFAA is the section of Chile's Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGAC) that studies anomalous aerial phenomena. The notice said that on a flight the day before, over the Antofagasta Region, three white lights had been seen, with no size given, for more than a minute and always to the south. According to the report, there were more than three witnesses, all crew members of flight FACh922, whose call sign, FACH, is that of the Chilean Air Force. It was flying from Iquique to Santiago at 35,000 feet and, when the sighting began, it was directly over Antofagasta airport, heading 190° (almost south, slightly west). The testimony read: "During the flight, three lights are seen that switch on and off intermittently (like a star). However, at one point they begin to move."

The notice arrived without images, but the crew later handed over the footage, and the office analyzed each of its 1,110 frames. Frame 428, reproduced in the report, shows two of the luminous objects. According to the metadata, the file had kept its digital integrity and was recorded at about 00:21 local time on November 23 (03:21 UTC, universal time) with the main camera of an iPhone 11.

The aircraft did not appear in the public records of transponder traffic, so SEFAA asked for help from an adviser in the standards and development office of the Department of Aerodromes and Aeronautical Services (DASA). According to the adviser, the radar record placed the flight over Andrés Sabella airport, Antofagasta, at 03:07:54 UTC, thirteen minutes earlier than the notice indicated. The report's radar figure shows two tracks over the airport, neither with the flight's call sign, and the report does not say which of them is the aircraft. It adds that when filming began, at 03:21 UTC, the aircraft was roughly next to the ISEKA control point, south of the city, some 40 kilometers from the airport. By this case's own calculation, an aircraft at cruise covers far more than that distance in thirteen minutes, and the report does not explain the gap. With those data the office fixed the aircraft's position and altitude: 11,278 meters above sea level, heading 190°.

The reconstruction of the sky put the Sun at azimuth 201° 15′, ahead of the crew, some 43° below the horizon, "which allowed there to be light that could be fleetingly reflected by space objects orbiting near the horizon." Seconds before filming began, according to the text of the report, the space traffic in that sector included the Chinese weather satellite Fengyun 1C and three satellites of the Starlink network, SpaceX's internet constellation. According to the report, all four were on the horizon described, in orbits on different planes, and made movements similar to what the crew saw and to what was captured on video.

The conclusion, dated December 15, 2023, states that "it was concluded that what was reported was the overflight of three satellites of the Starlink project and the remains of the Fengyun 1C satellite," and classifies the case as "Probably Explained."

Why it matters

The case had a video with a verified time and camera and a radar record that, according to the DASA adviser, placed the aircraft over the airport at 03:07:54 UTC. With that the office could compare what was filmed with specific satellites and propose which they were, though without saying which light corresponded to each.

The report leaves loose ends. The testimony speaks of three lights and the conclusion of four objects, three satellites and the remains of another, without saying which one corresponds to each light or whether one was not seen; the frame it reproduces shows two. It does not analyze separately the moment when the lights "begin to move," beyond stating that the satellites' movements were similar to what was filmed. Nor does it make clear which of the radar tracks is the flight, or the aircraft's path between the airport and the ISEKA point.

The text calls Fengyun 1C a "weather satellite," but Jonathan McDowell's GCAT satellite catalog lists that satellite as destroyed on January 11, 2007, the date of the Chinese anti-satellite test. The report's own figure labels it as a cataloged fragment (37047), and the conclusion speaks of its "remains."

Five-page report by the official anomalous aerial phenomena section. The notice gives the flight as FACh922, from Iquique (SCDA) to Santiago (SCEL) at 35,000 feet, and its initial position as "Vertical SCFA, heading 190" (SCFA is Antofagasta airport). The radar figure labels the two tracks over the airport "SOBERBI 371 370" and "AVISPA 79 A025", neither with the call sign FACH922. The position fixed for the filming is 23° 47′ 51″ S, 70° 22′ 34″ W, at 11,278 meters. The text numbers as "3096" a Starlink that the figure labels Starlink-3696 (51965); the other two appear as Starlink-30109 and Starlink-2102, and Fengyun 1C as "FENGYUN 1C DEB (37047)", a cataloged fragment. The space-traffic figure carries the stamp "23-12-2023 03:20:55Z", a month after the event, although its caption and the Sun position correspond to November 23. The header says "Resolved", a label SEFAA also puts on cases it closes as a "Report" (Relato). A DGAC work with no open license, so it is linked rather than hosted.
