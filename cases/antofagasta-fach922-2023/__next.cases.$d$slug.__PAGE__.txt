4d:T59b,The report arrived without images, but the crew later handed over the footage, and the office analysed each of its 1,110 frames. Frame 428, reproduced in the report, shows two of the luminous objects. According to the metadata, the file had kept its digital integrity and was recorded at about 00:21 local time on 23 November (03:21 UTC) with the main camera of an iPhone 11. The aircraft did not appear in the public records of transponder traffic, so SEFAA asked for help from the adviser of the Standards and Development Office of the Department of Aerodromes and Aeronautical Services (DASA). According to the adviser, the radar record placed the flight over Andrés Sabella airport, Antofagasta, at 03:07:54 UTC, thirteen minutes earlier than the report indicated. The radar figure the report uses to illustrate this shows two tracks over the airport labelled 'SOBERBI 371 370' and 'AVISPA 79 A025', neither with the call sign FACH922, and the report does not say which of them is the flight. It adds that when filming began, at 03:21 UTC, the aircraft was roughly next to the ISEKA control point, south of the city, some 40 kilometres from the airport; it does not explain how an aircraft at cruise covered so little distance in thirteen minutes (the distance and the objection are this record's). With that the office fixed its position at 23° 47′ 51″ S and 70° 22′ 34″ W, 11,278 metres above sea level, heading 190°.4e:T4cf,The reconstruction of the sky put the Sun at azimuth 201° 15′, ahead of the crew, some 43° below the horizon, 'which allowed there to be light that could be fleetingly reflected by space objects orbiting near the horizon'. Seconds before filming began, according to the text of the report, the space traffic in that sector included 'the weather satellite Fengyun 1C' and Starlinks 30109, 3096 and 2102. The figure that identifies them a few seconds later labels the first 'FENGYUN 1C DEB (37047)', a catalogued fragment, and the others as Starlink-30109, Starlink-2102 and Starlink-3696 (51965): the 3096 in the text is a typo, since the figure labels 3696. According to the report, all four were on the horizon described, in orbits on different planes, and made movements similar to what the crew saw and to what was captured on video. The conclusion, dated 15 December 2023, states that 'it was concluded that what was reported was the overflight of three satellites of the Starlink project and the remains of the Fengyun 1C satellite', and classifies the case as 'Probably Explained'.
