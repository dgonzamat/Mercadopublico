2d:T644,The 1963 memo matters as a datum of institutional history, not as evidence of the phenomenon. It describes no sighting and provides no proof of extraterrestrial life; it is a reflection paper its own author calls 'miscellaneous thoughts'. Hence it enters as a tier-B document-case, valued for its provenance and rarity, not for probative content. Three things make it valuable to the corpus. First, the level: it emanates from the National Aeronautics and Space Council, within the Executive Office of the President, and circulates to the State Department —that is, the question of what to do upon a contact was discussed, however tentatively, at the heart of the U.S. space-policy apparatus, not at its margins. Second, the date: 1963, barely three years after Project Ozma and two decades before formal post-detection protocols took shape (which the corpus records in `seti-post-detection-protocol-2026`); the document is an early link in the genealogy of the disclosure question. Third, the tone, which is itself a datum: the blend of rigorous scientific consensus, speculative humor and honesty about the discomfort of both extremes —neither the saucer advocates nor the skeptics' 'total impossibility'— portrays how a senior technical official thought about the problem when he could do so in writing and under classification.
