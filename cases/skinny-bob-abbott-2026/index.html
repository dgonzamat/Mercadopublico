United States.Skinny Bob · 2026 viral revival of a fabricated 2011 video

In April–May 2011 the anonymous YouTube account 'ivan0135' uploaded four grainy videos, presented as leaked film of interviews with a grey being from Zeta Reticuli between 1942 and 1969, showing an extremely thin being —nicknamed 'Skinny Bob'— blinking and moving. Crowdsourced analysis (Metabunk, skinnybob.info) identified in them a commercial stock film-damage overlay and a timecode in the Consolas typeface, released in 2006-2007, applied digitally: the footage is a modern creation, although its maker never came forward. In 2025 Edward 'Eddie' Abbott, who presents himself as a former Army intelligence analyst, said he had seen the complete footage in a SCIF in 2008, without verification. Since May 25, 2026 the channel 'qtecqot', opened a month earlier, has uploaded four new videos structured as an alternate-reality game. A technical verification bench (ExifTool, C2PA, SynthID, FFmpeg) returned no conclusive signal; the signs of AI generation come from observers, not from a published forensic analysis.

Part 01

The night in question

The video known as 'Skinny Bob' appeared online in April–May 2011: four black-and-white videos uploaded on April 14 and May 2, 9 and 18 by the anonymous account 'ivan0135', created on April 13 (the third carries no footage, only text messages), presented as leaked film from a series of interviews, conducted between 1942 and 1969, with a grey being from Zeta Reticuli. They showed an extremely thin being, bald, with a disproportionate head, narrow mouth and large blinking eyes, seated at a table, full-length, and lying in a kind of pool. The often-repeated KGB attribution is not a later accretion: the uploader added it himself, since the first 2011 video opens with a still of a 'TOP SECRET KGB ARCHIVE' logo lifted from the 1998 documentary 'Secret KGB UFO Files'. For years the community analyzed it frame by frame. On the Metabunk forum and on skinnybob.info, the scratch-and-grain layer was identified as a film-damage overlay sold on commercial stock sites (Pond5) and available online by about 2009; the on-screen timecode typeface turned out to be Consolas, released by Microsoft in 2006-2007, and the aging is consistent with Boris FX's Sapphire plug-ins for After Effects. In addition, the aging effects also degrade the timecode, so they were applied last, the opposite of what happens with film shot and then transferred. That does not identify the maker or the base material —no one has confessed or shown the set, the puppet or the studio—, but it does rule out the clips being unaltered archival film.

In 2025 the case gained a testimonial layer. Edward 'Eddie' Abbott, who presents himself as a former U.S. Army intelligence analyst (2007-2009, with a deployment to Iraq) and had been known in UFO circles since a 2021 phone interview with Linda Moulton Howe, stated in July 2025 on the UndercoverETuk channel that he saw the complete, unedited Skinny Bob video inside a SCIF (Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility) in 2008: 'in color', with a being —'a pilot'— that moved, blinked and rotated, 'alive', and two craft flying in a staggered formation. He further claims that parts of that footage no longer circulate. Abbott adds other unverified accounts to that claim: photographing a V-shaped craft chased by jets and helicopters, and taking part in artifact recovery in the Middle East, plus an alleged hybrid program. None of his stated credentials, claimed access, or the material he describes has independent documentary corroboration.

On May 25, 2026 the YouTube channel 'qtecqot' —whose namesake X account was created in April 2026 and had about 28 followers when the clips began circulating— uploaded the first of a series of videos presented as a continuation of the original material: 'Confidential leaked ufo-ebe footage continuation of disclosure', followed on June 15 by the recovery of the same being ('ET crew recovery site D… skinny Bob'), in the last week of July by a walkthrough of a craft interior, and around late August by a fourth clip, now in color, titled 'Species 4, 13 @qtecqot ≠ @ivan0135'. The uploader attributes the material to a range from 1942 to 1957; the frames carry a burned-in timecode in the lower-left corner with a black block right over the date digits. The channel's descriptions narrate the uploads as numbered installments 'of eight', with formulas such as 'source anonymity is maintained' and a line giving ivan0135 as 'incapacitated' and whereabouts unknown since April 2026: a self-sealing alternate-reality game (ARG) structure, because it explains in advance why the original maker can never confirm anything. By late September 2026 the channel had about 13,700 subscribers. Media coverage speculated, without evidence, about a connection between this revival and the simultaneous wave of orb and sphere videos from Brazil, Miami, Japan, Finland, Indonesia and California.

Part 02

Why this case moved the needle

It is worth being precise about what the available technical analysis found and what it did not, because its finding is often cited beyond what it says. The Singhfinity article, titled 'A verification bench that returns nothing on the new Skinny Bob clips', assembled a full bench on the qtecqot videos: yt-dlp to keep native resolution and frame rate, ExifTool for container metadata, a C2PA manifest check, Google's SynthID detector, FFmpeg for grain and specular-highlight tests, and reverse search for commercial film-damage overlays. Its conclusion is the one in the title: YouTube strips almost all metadata on re-encoding, the absence of a C2PA manifest proves nothing and SynthID only recognizes Google's generators, while Veo, Kling, Runway or a local model leave no signature. Specular highlights that stay fixed while the geometry moves and background incoherence —repeated cones, morphing foliage, an implausible gait— appear in the article as the most promising test targets and as what 'people in the replies claim to see', not as measured results. The judgment that the new material is synthetic rests on something else: the newly created account, the self-sealing ARG packaging, the convenient blacking-out of the date, and the view of the person who cut the most widely shared compilation, who describes it as AI-generated video mimicking the 2011 film.

The case matters as an early example of a pattern likely to recur: the reactivation of an old viral hoax with video-generation tools, in a format —grainy classified 8mm film— that hides precisely the defects that would give synthesis away. The credibility accumulated over fifteen years of unresolved mystery transfers to two-month-old clips simply by appearing next to them on a single timeline. Abbott's testimony fits the tradition that pattern 8d catalogs: 'insider' claims and documents of doubtful provenance —like Boyd Bushman's 'confession' or the MJ-12 papers— that raise public interest without delivering verifiable evidence. There is no proof that Abbott and qtecqot are coordinated; the channel itself declares it is not ivan0135, and its alleged prior contact with him is uncorroborated.

The classification is adjusted after reading the sources. mundano_natural, with mundanoType fraude, rises from 0.88 to 0.92 because the technical evidence on the 2011 clips —a stock damage overlay, a 2006-2007 typeface applied digitally— is firmer than the entry said, and because the new channel's structure is that of a serialized fiction. indet drops to 0.07 and the non-human mass to 0.01: who made the 2011 base material and with what technique remains unresolved, but not whether it is authentic archival film. The probability goes from 8 to 5. What would move the entry is qtecqot publishing the original files with their metadata intact, or someone documenting the access Abbott claims to have had.
