Part 01

The file in question

The video known as 'Skinny Bob' appeared online in early May 2011: grainy black-and-white footage, presented by its uploader as U.S. military archive material from the 1940s and 1950s, showing a grey-like being —extremely thin, bald, disproportionately large head, narrow mouth, large blinking eyes— moving inside what appeared to be an enclosed facility. The clip circulated widely in the UFO community; some speculated it was promotional material for an unannounced film or series. In thirteen years, no one claimed authorship or produced a technically conclusive debunking, and the video sat in the usual 'neither confirmed nor refuted' limbo that characterizes much of the found-footage UFO genre.

In 2025 the case gained a testimonial layer. Edward 'Eddie' Abbott, who presents himself as a former U.S. Army intelligence analyst (2007-2009, with a deployment to Iraq), stated in interviews that he saw the complete, unedited Skinny Bob video inside a SCIF (Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility) in 2008: in color, with the being —whom he describes as 'a pilot'— moving, blinking and, in his account, alive. He further claims that parts of that footage no longer circulate in the public version. Abbott adds other unverified accounts to that claim: photographing a V-shaped object and taking part in artifact-recovery missions in the Middle East tied to an alleged hybrid program. None of his stated credentials, claimed access, or the material he describes has independent documentary corroboration.

Between May 25 and the last week of July 2026, the YouTube channel 'qtecqot' uploaded three new videos presented as a continuation of the original material: 'recovery footage' of the same being, unidentified symbols, the interior of a craft, and military personnel next to an object, with internal dates attributed to a range from 1942 to 1957. The channel's descriptions frame the uploads as numbered installments of a larger archive, preserve the uploader's anonymity, and deliberately replicate the 2011 video's classified 8mm-film aesthetic — an alternate-reality game (ARG) structure rather than a one-off leak. Media coverage speculated, without solid evidence, about a connection between this revival and the recent wave of unidentified orb and drone sightings in Brazil, Miami, Japan, Finland, Indonesia and California during the same period.

Part 02

Why this case moved the needle

The strongest contribution to this case is not testimonial but technical. An independent analysis assembled a full verification bench on the new qtecqot videos: yt-dlp to preserve native resolution and framerate, ExifTool for container metadata, a C2PA content-provenance manifest check, Google's SynthID detector, FFmpeg to examine grain and specular highlights frame by frame, and reverse image search for commercial film-damage overlays. Most of those tests were inconclusive by the material's own design —YouTube strips most container metadata on re-upload, the absence of a C2PA manifest proves nothing, and SynthID only detects Google's own generators, missing Kling, Runway, Veo or local models. But the pixel-level analysis did produce a strong signal: specular highlights that stay fixed while the underlying geometry shifts, a generation failure characteristic of current AI video models, along with background incoherence —repeated cones, foliage that morphs between frames, an implausible body gait. The reasonable conclusion is that the new material is synthetic fabrication disguised under the aesthetic filter of 1960s classified film.

For the corpus, the case matters as the first cataloged example of a pattern likely to recur: the reactivation of an old viral hoax through AI video-generation tools, propped up by an unverified 'insider' testimony that surfaces just before the new material starts circulating. The sequence —Abbott's 2025 statement, qtecqot's 2026 uploads, an ARG structure with numbered installments— suggests the two pieces feed each other rather than coincide independently, and it fits the tradition running parallel to the institutional corpus that pattern 8d catalogs: insider claims and documents of doubtful provenance —like Boyd Bushman's 'deathbed confession' or the MJ-12 papers— that raise public interest without delivering verifiable evidence about the phenomenon's nature. The posterior stays concentrated on media fabrication (mundano_natural), with an indeterminacy tail that acknowledges the impossibility of a definitive technical verdict on the original 2011 video, whose authorship remains unresolved thirteen years on.
