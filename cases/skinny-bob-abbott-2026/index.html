🇺🇸2011·No known location (videos posted on YouTube)

United States.Skinny Bob · videos of a gray being presented as a leak

In April and May 2011, the anonymous YouTube account "ivan0135" uploaded four videos presented as leaked film of interviews held between 1942 and 1969 with an extremely thin gray being ("Skinny Bob"). A collective analysis on online forums found a stock film-damage overlay and a timecode in the Consolas typeface, from 2006-2007, applied digitally: it is not untouched archive film, although its maker never came forward. In 2025 Eddie Abbott, who says he is a former Army intelligence analyst, claimed without verification to have seen the full footage in 2008. Since May 2026 another channel has posted four videos it presents as a continuation.

What happened

In April and May 2011, an anonymous YouTube account, "ivan0135," created on April 13, uploaded four black-and-white videos, according to the community archive skinnybob.info, which gives the date and time of each upload: on April 14 and May 2, 9 and 18. The third has no footage, only text messages. They were presented as leaked film from a series of interviews, held between 1942 and 1969, with a gray being from Zeta Reticuli. They showed an extremely thin being, bald, with an outsized head, a narrow mouth and large blinking eyes, seated at a table, standing full-length and lying in a kind of pool.

According to skinnybob.info and the Metabunk forum, the often-repeated KGB attribution came from the uploader himself: the first video opens with a still of a "TOP SECRET KGB ARCHIVE" logo taken from the 1998 documentary "Secret KGB UFO Files." Singhfinity instead treats it as an accretion of later retellings, grown out of the account's Russian-looking handle, and does not mention that still. The maker never came forward or claimed the work.

For years, enthusiasts analyzed the videos frame by frame on the Metabunk forum and the skinnybob.info site. They identified the scratch-and-grain layer as a film-damage effect sold on commercial stock sites (Pond5) and available online by about 2009. The typeface of the on-screen timecode turned out to be Consolas, released by Microsoft in 2006-2007, and the aging is consistent with Boris FX's Sapphire plug-ins for the editing program After Effects.

The aging effects also degrade the timecode, so they were applied last, the opposite of what happens with film shot and then transferred. That does not identify the maker or the base footage, since no one has confessed or shown the set, the puppet or the studio, but it rules out the clips being unaltered archive film.

In July 2025, Edward "Eddie" Abbott, who presents himself as a former U.S. Army intelligence analyst (2007-2009, with a deployment to Iraq) and had been known in UFO circles since a 2021 phone interview with Linda Moulton Howe, said on the UndercoverETuk channel that in 2008 he had seen the complete, unedited video inside a SCIF, a secure room for handling classified information: "in color," with a being ("a pilot") that moved, blinked and turned, "alive," and two craft flying in a staggered formation.

He claims parts of that footage no longer circulate. He adds other unverified accounts: photographing a V-shaped craft chased by jets and helicopters, taking part in recovering artifacts in the Middle East, and an alleged hybrid program. Neither his credentials, nor the access he claims, nor the material he describes has independent documentary corroboration.

On May 25, 2026, the YouTube channel "qtecqot," whose namesake X account had been opened in April 2026 and had about 28 followers when the clips began to circulate, posted the first of a series of videos presented as a continuation of the original material: "Confidential leaked ufo-ebe footage continuation of disclosure." It was followed on June 15 by the supposed recovery of the same being ("ET crew recovery site D… skinny Bob"), in the last week of July by a walkthrough of a craft's interior, and around late August by a fourth clip, now in color, titled "Species 4, 13 @qtecqot ≠ @ivan0135."

The channel dates the material to 1942 to 1957, but that cannot be checked: the frames carry a burned-in timecode in the lower-left corner, with a black block right over the date digits. The most widely shared compilation was cut by the content creator @mario1973p, who joined the 2011 ivan0135 material and the 2026 qtecqot material into a single chronological video. He himself describes the new clips as AI-generated video imitating the 2011 film.

The channel's descriptions present the uploads as numbered installments "of eight," with formulas such as "source anonymity is maintained" and a line saying ivan0135 has been "incapacitated" and his whereabouts unknown since April 2026. This is the structure of an alternate-reality game (ARG, an interactive fiction presented as real), which seals itself off because it explains in advance why the original maker can never confirm anything. By late September 2026 the channel had about 13,700 subscribers. Some media speculated, without evidence, about a link between this revival and the simultaneous wave of orb and sphere videos from Brazil, Miami, Japan, Finland, Indonesia and California.

The most thorough technical analysis of the 2026 clips is a Singhfinity article of July 31, 2026, which predates the fourth clip, titled "A verification bench that returns nothing on the new Skinny Bob clips." It reviewed the videos at their original quality along with their metadata, looked for a C2PA provenance manifest and the SynthID watermark Google puts on what its own tools generate, ran grain and highlight tests, and searched commercial stock sites for film-damage overlays.

It found nothing conclusive: YouTube strips almost all metadata when it re-encodes, the absence of a C2PA manifest proves nothing, and SynthID only recognizes Google's generators, while Veo, Kling, Runway or a local model leave no signature. Two signs remain unmeasured: highlights that stay fixed while the geometry moves, and inconsistencies in the background (repeated cones, foliage that changes shape, an implausible gait). The article presents them as the most promising tests and as what "people in the replies claim to see," not as results.

Why it matters

The conclusion that the 2026 material is synthetic does not come from any measurement. It rests on other signs: a newly created account, the self-sealing ARG packaging, the date blacked out right where it mattered, and the view of @mario1973p, who cut the most widely shared compilation. For the 2011 material, by contrast, digital manipulation is proven, even though no one knows who did it.

The case shows how an old viral hoax is revived with video-generation tools. The format it imitates, which the Singhfinity article describes as "grainy 1960s 8mm classified film," hides precisely the flaws that would give synthesis away. The credibility built up over fifteen years of unsolved mystery passes to clips posted only months ago simply because they appear next to them on the same timeline.

Abbott's testimony raises public interest without providing verifiable evidence. There is no proof that Abbott and qtecqot are coordinated: the channel itself says it is not ivan0135, and nothing corroborates that it knows anything about him, as it implies by calling him "incapacitated." It remains to be seen whether qtecqot will publish the original files with their metadata intact, or whether anyone will document the access Abbott claims to have had.
