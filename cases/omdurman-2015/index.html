🇸🇩2015·Wadi Sayidna military zone, north of Omdurman (Khartoum)

Sudan.Omdurman, Sudan · air defense fires at a bright target

On the night of May 5, 2015, around 10 p.m., Sudan's air defense fired at a bright object flying low over the Wadi Sayidna military zone, north of Omdurman. Residents heard explosions and saw fire in the sky. The army spoke first of a "bright target," later that day of an unidentified reconnaissance target and the next day of a reconnaissance drone of unknown origin. The information minister, by contrast, blamed the firing on a miscommunication during military exercises.

What happened

On Tuesday, May 5, 2015, around 10 p.m. local time, residents of Omdurman heard at least one explosion coming from the north, from the Karari area. Omdurman is Khartoum's twin city, across the Nile. A witness who lived nearby told the "Sudan Tribune" he had seen "a flame in the sky" and that the house shook. He said it all started around 10:30 p.m., and he heard no airplanes in the area. A resident also called the police station to report seeing "a glowing entity in the sky," according to a police official.

That night, the air defense of the Wadi Sayidna military zone had fired at something. Rumors of a coup against President Omar al-Bashir spread through the city. The newspaper "Al-Araby al-Jadeed" reported, citing military sources, that "foreign planes" had struck a target in Omdurman. The Hezbollah-affiliated channel Al-Mayadeen quoted an army message saying an Israeli drone had been shot down.

On Wednesday the 6th, army spokesman Col. Al-Sawarmi Khaled Saad said in a statement that the Wadi Sayidna air defense had "confronted a bright target" the night before. He denied there had been a coup attempt, clashes or a foreign attack. On Sky News Arabia he described it as a "moving object that resembles a plane or a rocket," and said they intercepted it because it looked suspicious.

That same day, in another statement, he said an air base had received notice of a bright target flying at low altitude: "We engaged with the aerial target, which is a reconnaissance imaging target whose identity has not yet been uncovered." According to Reuters, the target was destroyed and its debris fell on residential areas of Omdurman. The officers who went to the scene had not yet determined what it was, according to Ynet. On Thursday the 7th, the army said it was a reconnaissance drone, without saying where it came from.

The same Wednesday, Information Minister Ahmed Bilal Osman gave another version to Radio Tamazuj. He denied there had been any bombing in the capital and explained that there were military exercises north of Omdurman, near the air defense barracks. In his account, the units opened fire because of a miscommunication.

Why it matters

An army fired at an object it could not identify and said so in public the next day. The record, however, comes down to what the army and the government said, and their versions do not match: for the spokesman it was an aerial target that was shot down and later a reconnaissance drone; for the minister, a miscommunication during exercises, with no bombing. The contemporary sources do not include photos of the debris or radar data.

The suspicion of a foreign drone had a background. Sudan had accused Israel of bombing an arms factory in Khartoum in October 2012, and in March 2009 air strikes that the press attributed to Israel destroyed a convoy of trucks in the west of the country. Israel, according to Reuters, either refused to comment or said it neither admitted nor denied involvement. This time, witnesses quoted by an Arab newspaper thought the aircraft had come from Israel, and the Israeli military did not respond immediately, according to its own radio station. According to Reuters, the rebels in Darfur and along the border with South Sudan were not believed to have air capability.

That the spokesman spoke of a "reconnaissance imaging" target suggests the army believed the object was surveilling the military zone, but no source explains how it knew. If the minister was right, the object may have been one of its own aircraft or drones, or a target in the exercises themselves. The case resembles the 2025 drones over European airports ↗ “Drones” over Europe: armed forces that did not identify the object, although here they said they shot it down and no one showed the debris.
