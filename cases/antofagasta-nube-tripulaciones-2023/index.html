🇨🇱2023·Airspace north of Antofagasta, Chile

Chile.Antofagasta · two airline crews see a glowing, rising cloud

On the night of September 6, 2023, the crew of a Sky Airline flight north of Antofagasta told Iquique control of a light to the southwest that it took for the Moon behind a cloud, which rose vertically and flashed "like a beacon"; the crew of a JetSMART flight 321 miles to the south said it saw it pass over its aircraft. SEFAA, Chile's official office for these phenomena, put it down to military flights; five months later, with four photos from those minutes that the captain handed over, it concluded it was the Chinese rocket carrying the Yaogan 33-03 satellite, though by this case's own calculation it was not in view of Chile.

What happened

At 00:30:48 UTC on September 7, 2023 (21:30 on the 6th in Chilean summer time), the captain of Sky Airline flight SKU329, which had taken off from Iquique for Santiago, called the Iquique Area Control Center. He was flying at 35,000 feet, some 85 kilometers north of Antofagasta airport. The recording of the call, which Chile's official anomalous aerial phenomena office (SEFAA) transcribed, reads: "We have… a light up there, we thought it was the Moon, it looks like a flash… we can't really tell the altitude… We thought it was the Moon, covered by clouds, but it's moving vertically." Asked whether it was above his position, he said no, to the southwest. The controller told him she had no traffic, that the nearest was on the same airway 113 miles away. Almost two minutes later he confirmed he still saw it: "it's fading, but in the center I'd say you can see every so often a light that shines like a beacon or a strobe light, but at very high speed, very fast. Very high too."

At 00:32:58 JetSMART flight JAT026 broke in: "we see the same… Right now it's above us," and confirmed it was overhead. At 00:33:16 SKU329 said it had now disappeared, that it had been "three or four flashes." The controller reported that the two aircraft were 321 miles apart, and JAT026 repeated that the light had passed over them "and had a kind of beacon."

The Iquique air traffic operations officer sent SEFAA, the anomalous aerial phenomena section of the Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGAC), the controller's handwritten report and the synchronized radar and audio video. The office placed both aircraft with the tracking system; its map puts JAT026 off the coast, north of La Serena. In the radar image it highlighted the possible presence of at least three military aircraft in the line of sight of the SKU329 crew; Iquique's flight plans did not record them, but the Air Force liaison officer confirmed night operations by institutional aircraft in the area. With that, on October 10, 2023 it closed the case as "Resolved": what was reported arose from "the sporadic sighting of institutional air operations." It had not obtained the crews' identities, requested by email three times in September.

On February 11, 2024 the SKU329 captain himself filed a report with his account and five images. He wrote that, already at cruise, he saw to the southwest, out over the Pacific, something that looked like a cloud glowing as when the Moon is behind a cloud, although, he wrote, that night "we never could see the moon"; that his first officer first thought it was the Moon and then that it was not; that the cloud grew vertically, expanded in all directions and formed a halo that came to stretch "from Argentina to the Pacific," with a flash at its center, sometimes white and sometimes red, and at the end three white lights almost in a triangle. By his account it all lasted about 30 minutes; the recording with the controller covers less than three minutes (00:30:48 to 00:33:16) and the photos were taken within a span of two. By this case's own calculation, the Moon, which SKU329 mentioned in the call and which, according to the captain, the other traffic also mentioned, was some 63° below the horizon.

SEFAA examined four of the images with error level and copy-move analysis (techniques that detect retouching): they had not been altered, came from a Samsung S22 Ultra and were taken between 21:28 and 21:30 local time on September 6. They showed a luminous object of gaseous appearance through which a star could be seen. SEFAA's report on the captain's submission, filed under number 1967, says the photos are from September 6, "one day before what was reported," but by this case's own calculation there is no such gap: 21:28 in Chile on summer time was 00:28 UTC on the 7th, two minutes before SKU329's call. The gap lies in the captain's account, which dated the sighting the 7th in local time.

The office searched the United Nations launch register, found that of the Chinese military satellite Yaogan 33-03 (2023-136A) on that September 6, on a Long March 4C rocket from Jiuquan into a sun-synchronous orbit, and on February 14, 2024 it reissued the report and concluded that what was reported was the launch of that rocket. The new version keeps the paragraph on the possible military aircraft but concludes something else, without explaining why it drops the first conclusion.

The report does not say who took the photos, although the captain supplied them; he attached a fifth image, which he attributed to a JetSMART aircraft flying along airway UQ814 toward Calama, and which the office did not analyze; he also mentioned photos taken from the ground at Tongoy that the report does not examine.

Why it matters

Two crews from two airlines, 321 miles apart, spoke live with Iquique control about the same thing; the recording preserves it timed to the second, and four phone photos, found unaltered by SEFAA's analysis, capture it in those same minutes. That an object should be seen to the southwest from one aircraft and directly above another almost 600 kilometers away only fits something very high or very large, which is what the photos show and what the second conclusion holds; that is this case's own deduction. The photos and the description of a cloud that grew and let stars show through led the office to change its explanation and attribute it to the rocket's third stage. A propellant plume at great altitude glows like this when lit by the Sun; by this case's own calculation, at that hour the Sun was about 28° below the horizon to the west, so something hundreds of kilometers up in that direction could be lit, as the captain described.

The identification with the Chinese rocket has a problem the report does not address. It speaks of the "launch," but according to Jonathan McDowell's satellite catalog the satellite separated at around 18:26 UTC on September 6 (the catalog's estimate), some six hours before the photos: what was seen over Chile would not be the rocket's ascent but its third stage already in orbit, which the catalog places at 496 to 683 kilometers up. By this case's own calculation, that stage could not have been in view. Using the February 2024 orbital elements published in the report itself, taken back in time with the precession of a sun-synchronous orbit (one that always passes over a given place at the same solar time), the plane of the satellite and the stage passes over Jiuquan at about 18:15 UTC on the 6th, which confirms the calculation.

At 00:30 UTC on the 7th, that plane lay some 48° of arc from JAT026 and some 53° from SKU329, whereas something 500 or 680 kilometers up is only visible within about 25 to 29°. The figure with which SEFAA estimates the trajectory draws it over the Pacific, far to the southwest and running west-southwest to east-southeast, hardly compatible with an orbit inclined at 98°, and the text concedes it may have followed "a parallel course further north" without checking.

McDowell's catalog offers another launch that matches the time; this is this case's own deduction, not SEFAA's, and not an identification. At 23:42 UTC on September 6, a Japanese H-IIA lifted off from Tanegashima with the XRISM observatory and the SLIM lunar probe, into an orbit inclined at 31°. According to the Japanese space agency, SLIM separated about 47 minutes and 33 seconds after lift-off, and the catalog dates that separation at 00:29:50 UTC on the 7th: between the photos, from 00:28:55 to 00:30:02, and SKU329's first call, at 00:30:48. By this case's own approximate calculation, the second stage's track was crossing the southeast Pacific toward Chile in those minutes and was above the horizon of both aircraft. Without the stage's precise position or the exact direction of the photos, this is a coincidence of time and of approximate geometry; but, unlike the Chinese rocket, this one could have been in view.

Documents and sources

Documents

SEFAA (Chilean DGAC) report, case no. 1918, final version, 7 September 2023

Eight-page report by the official anomalous aerial phenomena section, reissued in February 2024. It transcribes the audio between Iquique control and flights SKU329 and JAT026, places the aircraft with radar, reproduces enlarged the four examined photos from case 1967 (the captain's report) and concludes that what was seen was the Long March 4C rocket of the Yaogan 33-03 satellite.

Read more

Its first version, of October 2023, attributed the same sighting to military flights. It has errors and inconsistencies of detail: the flight appears as SKU329, SKU239 and SKU234, and the waypoint as PUNAP and PUNAB; the handwritten report says fewer than 10 seconds passed between the two aircraft's reports, but in the transcript about 20 seconds separate SKU329's second description from JAT026's, and two minutes from its first call. The captain recalled the controller telling him the only traffic was 300 miles to the south and belonged to LATAM, but the recording says 113 miles and the aircraft that answered was JetSMART's; his account says the other traffic saw the cloud "to the west of its position," whereas JAT026 said it was passing "over us," and the summary of his report says the sighting "lasted more than a minute," whereas the account speaks of about 30 minutes. A DGAC work with no open license, so it is linked rather than hosted.
