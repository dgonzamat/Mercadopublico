Record of the event

What happened

In 2020, an infrared sensor aboard a U.S. military platform recorded 58 seconds of video over the Middle East. The U.S. Department of the Air Force sent it to the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO), the Pentagon office that studies these phenomena. Whoever submitted the video left no oral or written description of the sighting: there is no witness account and no crew report. The official record says only "Middle East" and does not disclose the exact location. No coordinates, platform identification or exact date within 2020 were released.

The official timestamped description of the video lays out a simple sequence. The camera locks its crosshairs on an area of contrast, a patch that stands out from the background, and follows it as it becomes more and more distinct. At second 31, the sensor narrows its field of view to zoom in, and through second 56 the patch "increases in apparent size and distinctiveness." At the end it leaves the center of the frame and passes out of view through the bottom right corner. The record states that this description is for information only and is not an analytical judgment.

Viewed frame by frame, the video also shows that the background is layers of cloud and that the sensor is looking roughly horizontally; a tracking box labeled "1" stays on the patch. Up to second 30 it is a barely visible point. From second 35, now zoomed in, it becomes an irregular hot blob that around seconds 40 to 45 at times looks like two bright lobes side by side, and at the end like an elongated shape. The resolution does not show wings, rotors or any other recognizable structure.

The record also carries a paragraph headed "AARO Comment": "the area of contrast's apparent increase in size is likely to be at least partially attributable to the U.S. platform closing the distance between itself and the source of the detection." AARO thus offers a conventional cause for the clip's most striking feature, an object that seems to grow on the screen: the camera was moving toward it. The comment is cautious: it says "likely" and "at least partially," it does not explain why the patch also grows sharper, and it does not say what it was. It speaks of the platform closing in and does not mention the zoom at second 31, so the record does not separate how much of the growth is due to each.

The report was released labeled as unresolved. The video was posted on DVIDS, the Pentagon's media portal, on May 8, 2026, in the first release of PURSUE, the U.S. government's publication of files on these phenomena.

Why it matters

An object that seems to grow on screen suggests it is approaching or expanding, and that is what makes the clip striking. But perspective is enough to produce that effect: any stationary or slow object fills more and more of the screen if the observer moves toward it, without the object changing at all.

The comment does not close the case. In its 2024 annual report, AARO finalized "all 174 cases" as "resolved to prosaic objects including balloons, birds, UAS, satellites, and aircraft," and acknowledged that "many other cases remain unresolved"; so the lack of a published analysis of this clip does not show that there is no ordinary cause. With a single sensor and no further data to check it against, what remains is a video with a partial explanation of its most striking effect and an object that is still unidentified. The Metabunk thread on this release, for its part, only discusses a location mismatch in the official table, not what the object was.
