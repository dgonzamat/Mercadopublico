78

🇾🇪2024·Red Sea, off the western coast of Yemen

Yemen.Yemen · drone films a missile striking an orb

According to Congressman Eric Burlison, a U.S. MQ-9 Reaper drone filmed off the coast of Yemen, on October 30, 2024, a Hellfire missile fired by a second drone of the same type striking an unidentified orb, knocking off debris and glancing away without detonating, while the object kept going. Burlison showed the video at a House hearing on September 9, 2025, saying it came from a whistleblower and that an independent review was under way; according to The War Zone, it would be the first known instance of a Reaper engaging an airborne object during an operation.

What happened

On October 30, 2024, according to Congressman Eric Burlison, who presented the video, two U.S. MQ-9 Reapers operating off the coast of Yemen tracked an unidentified orb which, per the footage presented, was moving along a steady path. Since October 2023, the Houthis, an Iran-backed militia, had been attacking ships in the Red Sea and targets in Israel with missiles and long-range kamikaze drones, and U.S. forces had often been responding to those threats (The War Zone). Burlison explained that the Hellfire missile in the footage was fired by a second MQ-9 not seen in the video; the image carries the label "LRD LASE DES," which according to The War Zone would point to the filming MQ-9 laser-designating the target for the other.

The video, about 50 seconds long, shows the projectile entering from the upper left and striking the orb, which sheds some debris; the missile deflects away without detonating and the object continues on its path until the clip ends. NBC News describes the object as deforming and tumbling, with smaller objects, one of them very faint, coming off it. "It kept going, and it looked like the debris was taken with it," Burlison said. "I'm not going to speculate what it is." According to The War Zone, at times the sensor shows the object as more cylindrical than orb-shaped, perhaps because of the video quality.

Burlison, a Missouri Republican, obtained the video from a military whistleblower and presented it publicly for the first time on September 9, 2025, at the hearing of the House Oversight Committee's Task Force on the Declassification of Federal Secrets titled "Restoring Public Trust Through UAP Transparency and Whistleblower Protection." There and in a social media post that day he stressed that the "footage [is] presented as received from a whistleblower" and that an "independent review is ongoing." The War Zone asked the Pentagon and Central Command for comment, and a defense official told it: "We do not have anything to provide on this."

The War Zone notes that a laser-guided Hellfire was designed as an air-to-ground weapon and that a grazing blow without a detonation would point to a fuze (the mechanism that makes the missile explode) not modified for aerial targets. It adds that it is unknown whether the MQ-9s were tasked with an air-to-air mission. In its view, the use of that missile might indicate an impromptu engagement against a target of opportunity, and it is very plausible the decision to fire was based on the threat the object posed to warships or other friendly assets.

For a Reaper to track the object and a Hellfire to hit it, the object had to be moving relatively slowly and steadily, and parallax (the optical effect by which an object seems to move at a different speed depending on the viewpoint) can make it look fast.

Conventional readings circulated after the hearing. Harvard astrophysicist Avi Loeb, far from endorsing an exotic origin, proposed in a Medium article in September 2025 that the object was "comparable with a Samad drone" flown by the Houthis, whose Hellfire would have missed the main body and not detonated. According to a table of opinions compiled by a user on the Metabunk forum, retired pilot Chris Lehto called it "obviously a balloon." The skeptic Mick West, an administrator of that forum, wrote there: "I don't think it's moving fast at all. The debris trailing it indicates it's just drifting with the wind, and the apparent motion is from parallax."

The same thread cited an objection from Marik von Rennenkampff, a former Defense Department official: the wings of such a drone would be visible to that camera. The table counts him among those who saw an anomalous phenomenon, because three pieces of debris emerge from the object one after another. The War Zone, without committing, says what is seen is broadly consistent with an attempted shootdown of a subsonic cruise missile or kamikaze drone, and that the way the target changes aspect and reacts to the missile grazing it is "very balloon-like, although this is not definitive."

Why it matters

The video shows something unusual in such cases: a weapon fired at the object. The War Zone noted that it would be the first known instance of a Reaper engaging an airborne object in an operational setting, and that the video and its circumstances are unconfirmed by any other source. Burlison said his reason for showing it was to ask "why are we being blocked from this information consistently?"

Until the independent review publishes results, the video's authenticity and context rest on a whistleblower's word, and the Pentagon's silence neither confirms nor denies anything.
