🇧🇳1953·Seria, an oil town on the coast of Brunei

Brunei.Seria · hundreds watch a bright object trailing two vapor jets

Hundreds of curious onlookers watched a bright, star-like object over Seria, the oil town of Brunei. It seemed to rotate and gave off two jets of vapor. The account comes from a text written like a press dispatch, kept in a U.S. Air Force document dated February 25, 1953. According to the text, they put it at about 30,000 feet (9,100 m) and watched it for 40 minutes. It gives no time, no news outlet and no witness's name, and Project Blue Book closed it for insufficient data.

What happened

In 1953, Brunei was a British protectorate on the northwest coast of Borneo, and Seria was the town of its oil field, by the sea. In Project Blue Book, the U.S. Air Force program that collected reports of unidentified flying objects, the account of the event fits on a single sheet. It is document 108383 of the Air Force's Air Technical Intelligence Center (ATIC), dated February 25, 1953, and titled "Flying Saucer." Its text is one paragraph written like a press dispatch, but it does not say which newspaper or agency it came from or who sent it to ATIC.

The paragraph says that "today" hundreds of curious people in Borneo craned their necks to watch a bright, star-like object over Seria that seemed to rotate and gave off two jets of vapor. The onlookers called it a "Flying Saucer," the text says, "for want of a better explanation." According to the same text, they estimated it was flying at about 30,000 feet, some 9,100 meters, and said it was in sight for 40 minutes. The paragraph ends with a sentence about the region, not about Seria: similar phenomena had been reported over Borneo "several times recently."

The sheet records nothing else about the event. The text gives no time of day, no direction in which the object stood, no size and no shape beyond its looking like a star, and no witness's name; the hundreds of onlookers are the dispatch's figure, not a count of statements. Nor does it say what day it was written: "today" is the day of the dispatch, which the sheet does not date.

Years later, someone at the archive summed up the case on a Blue Book record card. The card is printed on an edition of the form dated September 1963, so it was filled in at least ten years after the event. It gives the date as February 25, 1953, the date of the ATIC document, and the place only as "Borneo." It records a civilian source, one object, 40 minutes of observation from the ground, no photos and no physical evidence. Its summary says that a bright, star-like object was seen appearing to rotate at an estimated altitude of 30,000 feet, with twin vapor trails, and that it had been "observed several times on previous occasions." The conclusion, typed twice in the box, is "Insufficient Data for Evaluation."

Why it matters

The case rests on a paragraph with no author. Neither the ATIC sheet nor the card names a witness, the outlet the text came from or the time of the event, and Blue Book's conclusion, insufficient data, proposes no object. No document in the file, which holds only those two sheets, explains what was seen. With no time and no direction, the account cannot be checked against any record from the period.

The card changes two things in the text it sums up. The dispatch said that similar phenomena had been reported over Borneo; the card writes, right after describing the object, "Observed several times on previous occasions," turning other, undescribed sightings into repeats of the one at Seria. And in the course box it types "Maneuvering," when the text only says the object seemed to rotate and describes no change of course. Both of the card's interpretations add more to the case than the text it sums up.

The date is not certain either. The card gives February 25, which is the date of the ATIC document; the text says "today" without dating the dispatch, so the sighting was on the 25th only if ATIC dated the document on the same day the news was written. In the Blue Book archive at archive.org, which titles each file by place, this is the only one with Borneo, Brunei or Sarawak in the title. The other sightings over Borneo that the dispatch mentions have no file of their own there under that name.

DOD DIR 5200.10." In the second line, a stroke running down from the line above crosses "ed to be"; it may be a deletion or a stray mark, and the card keeps the phrase ("appearing to rotate").
