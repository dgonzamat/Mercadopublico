Emiratos Árabes Unidos.'It may be a reflection': the observer himself doubts his own report over the UAE

In June 2024, an infrared sensor aboard a U.S. military platform recorded 21 seconds of an object over waters off the United Arab Emirates. AARO's visual description speaks of an area of contrast shaped like an 'inverted teardrop' with a vertically linear mass hanging below; the mission report the record cites (DoW-UAP-D8) describes an object with a vertical pole or bar attached to the bottom, and adds that the observer recorded it might instead be 'a reflection from an object in the water.' As mission report D27 is worded, what might be a reflection in the water was the bar, not the object. A curious detail in the record: AARO attributes the report's submission to Northern Command (NORTHCOM), even though the United Arab Emirates falls within Central Command's (CENTCOM) area of responsibility — an administrative inconsistency in the official record itself.

Part 01

The night in question

In June 2024, an infrared sensor aboard a U.S. military platform recorded 21 seconds of footage over waters off the United Arab Emirates. Per AARO's official record, it was U.S. Northern Command (NORTHCOM) that submitted the report — an administratively unusual detail, given that the United Arab Emirates falls within Central Command's (CENTCOM) geographic area of responsibility, not NORTHCOM's. The record does not explain the discrepancy; it may reflect a cataloguing error within AARO's own system, or a platform administratively assigned to NORTHCOM temporarily operating in the region.

The mission report accompanying the video, identified as DoW-UAP-D8, describes the object in unusual detail: it consists of 'an object with a vertical pole or bar attached to the bottom of the object.' The observer himself recorded a possible mundane explanation: per the record, 'the UAP may instead be a reflection from an object in the water.' The D8 report cited by the record appears in the same release as a 2025 report from Djibouti; the one matching this description is DoW-UAP-D27, from Central Command, dated 7 June 2024: a 'glowing hot spherical' object with a vertical cylindrical pole or bar beneath it, 'poss reflection from the object in the water', 'flying straight just over the water' at about 140 knots, seen from an aircraft at 23,999 feet. Read that way, the reflection explains the bar, not the object. The same report records that the sighting took place during the return to base, that the observer assessed the object as 'benign', that it showed no reaction to observation, and that the form marks 'no' for advanced capabilities and for interrogation by other sensors.

AARO's official timestamped visual description is consistent with both readings: for the full 21 seconds, 'an area of contrast visually resembling an inverted teardrop, with a vertically linear trailing mass suspended below, remains generally within the center of the sensor field-of-view.' The description mentions no change in shape; that the area stays in the centre of the field of view indicates the sensor was tracking it, and mission report D27 gives the object a speed of about 140 knots.

The material was released in PURSUE's first drop (May 8, 2026), with no exact coordinates, platform identification, or precise date within June 2024.

Part 02

Why this case moved the needle

This file's value lies in something uncommon: the observer himself, at the time of the report, already considered and wrote down a mundane alternative to what he was seeing. That does not happen with most files from this program, where the witness describes the phenomenon without offering an explanation, leaving the entire interpretive burden to a later analyst. Here, whoever was in front of the sensor already recorded the reflection hypothesis in the report itself, a sign of analytical sobriety at its origin.

The instrumental description says the area of contrast stays centred in the sensor's field of view for the 21 seconds, but that reflects the sensor's tracking, not the object being stationary: according to mission report DoW-UAP-D27, the object was flying straight just over the water at about 140 knots, seen from an aircraft at 23,999 feet. Read that way, the possible reflection explains the vertical bar that seems to hang from the object, not the object itself, which no one has identified.

The detail of the NORTHCOM-instead-of-CENTCOM attribution, while minor, is a reminder that even the PURSUE program's institutional metadata is not free of cataloguing inconsistencies — an added note of caution when reading any record in this corpus as a perfectly reliable document in every field, even when the chain of custody (unlike the Release 02 batch; see ↗) is institutionally traceable.
