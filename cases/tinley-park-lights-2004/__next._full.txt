28:T5b1,On the night of August 21, 2004, over Tinley Park (a southern suburb of the Chicago metropolitan area) numerous residents reported three reddish-orange lights arranged in a triangular or linear formation, moving slowly and in complete silence. The distinctive feature was the scale: more than a hundred people, in Tinley Park and neighboring towns such as Oak Forest, Mokena, Orland Park, Frankfort and Evergreen Park, described the same phenomenon simultaneously. One factor that aided the mass scale was the context: an Ozzfest festival concert had just ended at the local amphitheater (the then Tweeter Center), so thousands of people were out on the street, heading home and looking at the sky. Several witnesses captured the lights in photographs and video. Bill Dooley, a Tinley Park resident, recalled in 2011 that around 10:00 p.m. he saw three lights coming at him in single file for about twenty minutes, which at one point seemed to him stopped and together despite the wind, with no sound, went from single file to stacked to a triangle before moving away turning white, and that about 45 minutes later another red light came down the same path and did the same thing. T. J. Japcon, who saw them arrive from the southwest and stop in a triangle over a block party, filmed them with his camcorder, and recalled that a passing police officer said he did not know what was going on but was keeping his radio open because dispatch was getting calls.29:T680,The case was investigated by MUFON, in particular by Sam Maranto, director of the Illinois chapter, who compiled the testimonies and the visual material and has publicly argued the event was not staged, stressing that the observing conditions (clear sky, a crowd of sober, dispersed witnesses) were optimal. The conventional theories reported by the local press were model aircraft, a hoax using flares and balloons, and military aircraft, in an area with heavy traffic to O'Hare and Midway and on the same day as the Chicago Air and Water Show; others have proposed sky lanterns, which the blog Enigmatic Ideas rejects because they do not maintain rigid geometric formations. The UFO Hunters team tried to recreate the lights with flares and balloons in Indiana and, according to one participant, the result looked very different; in that same episode, some witnesses maintain it was a single triangular craft over 1,500 feet wide.
