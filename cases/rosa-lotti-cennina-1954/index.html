🇮🇹1954·Cennina, Bucine, Tuscany, Italy

Italy.Rosa Lotti · a farm woman sees two beings beside an object

On November 1, 1954, the Tuscan farm woman Rosa Lotti said she found in a clearing at Cennina an object shaped like a spindle or double cone, of polished metal and with a small glass door. Beside it were two beings a meter tall, in gray suits and helmets, who took five carnations and a black item (a cup or a stocking, depending on the version) from her; the carabinieri found no traces at the spot. The case took place during the wave of sightings of autumn 1954 in Italy.

What happened

In the autumn of 1954 there was a wave of sightings in Italy and other European countries. On the morning of November 1, 1954, Rosa Lotti, a 40-year-old farm woman and mother of four, was walking from her farm toward Cennina, in Tuscany, with a bunch of carnations: for the village church, according to her complaint; for the cemetery, according to later versions. The complaint she filed with the carabinieri gives 7:30; later versions say around 6:30. On reaching a clearing along the path she stopped dead before an unusual object.

According to the newspaper L'Unità of November 3, 1954, in her complaint to the carabinieri she wrote that at Poggio d'Ambra, in the municipality of Bucine, two strangers a meter tall, in a gray suit "shaped like a diving suit," a leather helmet, a normal face and very small teeth, took five red carnations and "a black cup" from her. Some five meters away, according to the complaint, stood a metal double-cone device about two meters tall, with a glass door and two small seats.

Lotti described it as "an enormous cigar, very pot-bellied in the central part and very pointed at the ends," and said the two beings uttered a few incomprehensible words. The paper added that in the village she was regarded as "perfectly sane, calm and serious," that, after freezing with fright, she ran to the village shouting, and that the carabinieri and villagers who went to the spot found neither the device nor its occupants, and that "a careful examination of the ground did not yield even a small trace" of the device or of their footsteps.

Later versions of the story are more detailed. They describe the object as a large spindle fixed vertically in the ground, a kind of double cone ("like two bells joined at their bases") of very polished metal, with an open glass door in the lower cone, through which two small seats could be seen, and a kind of rounded glass in the central, widest part.

According to those versions, two beings a little over a meter tall emerged from behind the object, of friendly appearance (the elder one laughing), dressed in suits resembling a pilot's, who spoke an incomprehensible language, with sounds that seemed Chinese to her: "liu, lai, loi." They snatched the carnations and one of her black stockings; when Rosa protested timidly, the elder returned some flowers but kept five, examined them curiously while laughing, wrapped them in the stocking and threw them inside the spindle through the small opening. Frightened, Rosa fled; when she returned with neighbors, the object and the beings had vanished.

According to the entry that the Italian Center for UFO Studies (CISU), a private association founded in 1985, published on its site ufo.it in 2015, which draws on the 1954 press and the later investigations, those who went to the spot half an hour later found a hole about ten centimeters across and fifteen deep, like one left by a thick pointed stake; L'Unità does not mention it, and CISU notes that those traces were seen by few people and never photographed. Rosa Lotti's account had some indirect corroboration from other neighbors regarding the location and sightings in the area, though, apart from two children, no one claimed to have seen the occupants.

CISU qualifies that corroboration: the first investigation of the case was probably the one carried out in 1972, eighteen years later, by the G.R.S.F. group of Prato, led by Siro Menicucci; two children, Ampelio and Marcello Torzini, said months later that they had seen Lotti talking with the "little men," but contradicted each other before journalists and their account grew out of a school exercise. Besides, flying saucers had been discussed at Lotti's home the night before, and an anonymous postcard sent to the Giornale del Mattino attributed the episode to a prank staged against her.

Why it matters

The entire extraordinary episode (the object and, above all, the beings) rests almost entirely on a single witness. Only two children, Ampelio and Marcello Torzini, aged 6 and 9, saw Lotti talking with the "little men," as became known months later; CISU judges their testimony "very ambiguous," because they contradicted each other before journalists and it grew out of a school exercise. According to Lotti's husband, a gamekeeper some two hundred meters away, with no view of the clearing, heard a conversation that he took for women chatting. The only physical trace is a hole that, according to CISU, few people saw and no one photographed, while L'Unità wrote that the examination of the ground found nothing.

The 1954 wave filled the press with similar encounters. The CISU entry records two readings: a hallucination, which it judges rather implausible given the features of the account, and the prank the anonymous postcard spoke of. It concludes that settling on an interpretation is not reasonably possible.
