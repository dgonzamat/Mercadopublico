29:T4d0,On 26 June 1959, around 6:45 p.m., at the Anglican mission of Boianai, on the shore of Goodenough Bay in eastern Papua New Guinea, the Reverend William Booth Gill —a 31-year-old Australian missionary holding a Licentiate in Theology— walked out of the mission house and saw a bright white light in the north-western sky, over the sea. As he later told J. Allen Hynek, on stepping out he saw Venus and, in addition to Venus, a bright white light somewhat above the planet; a mission teacher, Stephen, arrived at 6:52 p.m. and confirmed it was not a star. On a large, disc-shaped form, four humanoid-looking figures could be made out, appearing and disappearing from the upper deck as if busy with some task; from time to time a bluish beam of light projected upward. According to the signed sketch, the main object was clearly visible between 6:55 and 7:22 p.m. to 38 witnesses, Papuans of the mission who included teachers and medical assistants; at 7:10 p.m. the cloud ceiling covered the sky at about 2,000 feet, and Gill noted objects passing in and out of the clouds and casting a halo on them. The activity, with up to five lights at once, lasted about four hours, and in his notes that night Gill even wrote 'satellites?'.2a:T507,The following night, 27 June, the phenomenon repeated. Gill's first note is at 6:02 p.m.: the sun had already set behind the mountains, but it stayed quite light for fifteen minutes. On the large object four figures appeared, two of them bending over and raising their arms as though adjusting something, while two smaller objects hung motionless further off. Gill raised his arm and waved; to the surprise of the group, about a dozen people, the figure did the same; another witness waved both arms, the two outside figures followed, and all four seemed to wave back. With a torch, Gill directed a series of long dashes at the object, which answered with a pendulum-like swaying and seemed to come closer for half a minute; then the figures disappeared 'below deck'. At 6:30 p.m. Gill went in to dinner; at 7:00 p.m. the object was still there, somewhat smaller, and the observers went to church for Evensong. Asked by Hynek, Gill said he sometimes wondered about this himself, and that he had thought it might be 'just a new device of you Americans'; the next day, by the same account, the local people asked a trader whether he had seen 'the American Air Force'.
