🇵🇪2001·Chulucanas, Piura, Peru

Peru.Chulucanas · orange lights filmed over a religious procession

Between October and November 2001, orange and red lights were videotaped over Chulucanas (Piura, northern Peru) on three dates; the one of October 13, over the procession of the Señor Cautivo de Ayabaca, shows, according to El Comercio, "orange and red lights moving in an organized way through the air". They were investigated by the Peruvian Air Force's Office for the Investigation of Anomalous Aerial Phenomena (OIFAA), today DIFAA, with the lawyer Anthony Choy. Its 84-page report, presented to the high command in November 2002, remains classified, and no published explanation of the lights is known.

What happened

Between October and November 2001, in the Chulucanas district (Morropón province, Piura region, northern Peru), orange and red lights in the sky were videotaped on three dates: October 13 and 25 and November 15, according to El Comercio's historical archive. The most-cited episode took place on October 13, during the procession of the Señor Cautivo de Ayabaca, in which, according to El Comercio, more than a hundred people took part. The newspaper attributes that recording to Iván Iza Nanfaro, who filmed "orange and red lights moving in an organized way through the air". According to Infobae's interview with Anthony Choy, who later investigated the case, the head of the Air Force office that studied these phenomena told him that the municipality's cameraman had recorded UFOs, on a tape about two and a half hours long.

The most widely circulated description is Choy's own, in his public report of October 2005: "red-orange craft 150 to 200 meters long floating in complete silence for two and a half hours", which formed geometric figures and then merged "into a single sphere of light". Those sizes are estimates with no distance reference. According to that report, on October 25 craft were recorded landing at three points in the fields facing Cerro Pilán, and on November 15 one of them came down over the town and caused panic. Choy presents alongside the recordings witness accounts that are not part of them (beings two meters tall, people and cattle who vanish on Cerro Pilán, a "circle of fire" fifteen to twenty-five kilometers across seen by FAP pilots), which the report does not back with documents.

Television crews came later: Panamericana Televisión and Telemundo in early 2003 and Univision in August 2004. What they recorded were testimonies and lights seen in the Pilán area on those dates, not the events of 2001. On the Univision night, by Choy's account, those present debated whether it had been an airplane, a helicopter, a comet or a shooting star; Choy suggested, unconvinced, a light plane flying in circles, and dropped the idea when the object began moving erratically.

The official investigation was conducted by the Peruvian Air Force's Office for the Investigation of Anomalous Aerial Phenomena (OIFAA), created in 2001 after a light was filmed over La Molina (Lima) on April 26 of that year, which Choy later attributed to the planet Venus (El Comercio), and relaunched in 2013 as DIFAA (Department for the Investigation of Anomalous Aerial Phenomena). Choy, brought in as an investigator in January 2002, traveled to Chulucanas in February of that year, his first field destination; in April he delivered a field report to a special committee of the office, and the final report, a classified 84-page document analyzing the three recordings, was presented to the FAP high command at its headquarters on November 19, 2002. It was never published, so its conclusion is not known. Choy kept following the case on his own and by October 2005 had made 17 trips to the area.

Why it matters

The OIFAA studied a single set of recordings and took it to a formal report before the Air Force high command; El Comercio counts it among the first official Peruvian files on UFOs.

The public material rests on nighttime footage from civilian sources and an official report that remains classified; no instrumental analysis of distance, size or speed has been published, and the figures of 150 to 200 meters are estimates with no reference. The account in circulation comes mainly from the investigator, who presents it alongside witness accounts of Cerro Pilán that are not part of the recordings. None of the sources consulted proposes a specific conventional cause for the 2001 lights. One possible cause, such as paper sky lanterns or flares over a night procession, is neither proposed nor tested in them.
