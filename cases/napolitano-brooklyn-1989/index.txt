29:T4bc,What set this case apart from other abductions —typically single-witness and uncorroborated— was the claim of independent witnesses. Linda contacted investigator Budd Hopkins, a central figure of the abductionist movement, who documented the case in interviews and, above all, in his 1996 book 'Witnessed: The True Story of the Brooklyn Bridge UFO Abductions', which he called the most important of his career. In February 1991, fifteen months later, Hopkins received a letter signed only with the first names 'Richard' and 'Dan', who said they were police officers and had watched through binoculars, from under the FDR Drive, three figures and a woman in a white gown float from a window up to the craft. Hopkins found no one matching them in the New York police, and a later letter admitted they were not police officers but escorts of a very important 'third man' whose car had stalled; Hopkins came to believe it was UN Secretary-General Javier Pérez de Cuéllar. To those witnesses he added a woman driving across the Brooklyn Bridge, and in the book he went on to speak of more than twenty observers. For many in the ufology community, that made Napolitano 'the greatest abduction case in the world'.2a:T5d0,The case's apparent solidity eroded under scrutiny. Hopkins acknowledged in 1992 that he had never met 'Richard' or 'Dan': he only received letters and a tape on which a voice read a prepared script, and Linda was the only person who claimed to have met them. Linda also said the two men had kidnapped her twice in broad daylight and that one had tried to kill her, but she refused to report it to the police. MUFON investigators Joseph Stefula and Richard Butler, who had worked in VIP security, pointed out that the terminology of the letters was not that of bodyguards and brought a VIP security specialist, 'Mr. X', who also questioned it; George Hansen proposed that the kidnapping and attempted murder be reported to the police, and at a meeting at Hopkins's home in October 1992 the majority agreed to wait six months before investigating further. The key identities remain protected by pseudonyms in the sources consulted. Much of Linda's account was reconstructed through regression hypnosis, a technique psychologists associate with the creation of false memories. From the 1990s on, scientists and journalists questioned the reliance on hypnosis, the witnesses' anonymity and the lack of verifiable physical evidence.
