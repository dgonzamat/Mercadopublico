1c:T652,The cable matters as an institutional and provenance datum, not as evidence of the phenomenon. Its most striking claim —two purported alien corpses displayed before legislators— is exactly the kind of low-credibility claim the corpus treats with maximum caution: the document itself records the disagreement over their validity, provides no chain of custody or analysis, and the State Department withholds any judgment. That is why this file enters as a document-case and not an incident: its object of analysis is not 'what the bodies were' —a question the material does not even allow to be seriously posed— but the fact that a 2003 U.S. diplomatic cable documented the episode and that PURSUE declassified it in 2026. Three things make it valuable to the corpus. First, provenance: it is primary reporting from a chancellery, an institutional source with accuracy incentives distinct from those of the press or activism. Second, independence: it is a 2003 event distinct from and prior to Maussan's 2023 hearing, so it does not duplicate `maussan-congreso-mexico-2023` but adds a two-decade precedent, showing that Mexican UAP politics has a long, recurring arc. Third, it illustrates PURSUE's reach: the system releases not only military-sighting material but also the diplomatic record with which the U.S. state observed, without endorsing them, the UAP dynamics of third countries. The calibration lesson is clear: the presence of an extraordinary claim in an official document attests that the claim was made and recorded, not that it is true.
