14:T45c,Kovalyonok had just finished an exercise session when, through an observation porthole, he saw in front of the spacecraft an object flying on a parallel trajectory. The station was overflying southern Africa heading toward the Indian Ocean. In his later accounts, Kovalyonok stressed that the object was small in angular appearance (comparable to the size of a finger held up before the eye), initially elliptical. By his description, as he watched, the body became translucent, showed an internal structure, and then separated into two connected pieces, 'a bit like dumbbells' (dumbbell/barbell). At one end a gas- or plasma-like emission appeared; Kovalyonok described it as 'reactive object'-like. In the account he gave Michael Hesemann (2002) he tells it differently: the elliptical object flew alongside them, there was an explosion 'of golden light', a second explosion one or two seconds later and two golden spheres appeared; then he saw only white smoke, and when the station entered the Earth's shadow he could no longer see them. There he spoke of 'something impossible according to the laws of Physics'.15:T4df,The strongest skeptical interpretation comes from U.S. space journalist James Oberg, a specialist in the Soviet program: Oberg argues that many cosmonaut 'sightings' correspond to distant rocket launches or missile tests, whose fuel and exhaust clouds in vacuum, lit at grazing angles, produce translucent, shape-shifting objects that dissipate within minutes, exactly the described profile. Kovalyonok himself, in later interviews (among them the Russian documentary 'Космонавты и НЛО'), maintained he believed he had observed an unaccredited artificial terrestrial object, not an anomalous entity, though he could not identify it; the cross-check of Soviet and U.S. launch schedules for that date and trajectory, per the available accounts, found no recorded compatible test, and Jonathan McDowell's GCAT catalog, which includes ballistic-missile tests, lists no orbital or missile launch between 4 and 6 May 1981, only meteorological and research sounding rockets elsewhere; the next orbital launch is Kosmos-1269, on 7 May from Plesetsk.
