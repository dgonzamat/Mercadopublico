🇺🇸1969·On the way to the Moon (Apollo 12 spacecraft)

United States / cislunar space.Apollo 12 · astronauts see flashes of light while trying to sleep

In November 1969, on the way to the Moon, Apollo 12 astronauts Pete Conrad, Richard Gordon and Alan Bean saw streaks and flashes of light while trying to sleep, starting on the first night. They described them in the post-mission medical debriefing, whose audio the U.S. government declassified on May 22, 2026. On the same tape, one of them says they were happening inside the eye, and NASA later concluded that they were internal to their vision and did not come from an external light. They are usually attributed to cosmic rays passing through the retina, although no source confirms this for these particular flashes.

What happened

In the medical debrief that followed the Apollo 12 mission of November 1969, astronauts Pete Conrad, Richard Gordon and Alan Bean described seeing streaks and flashes of light while trying to sleep en route to the Moon. On the tape, titled "Apollo 12 Medical Debriefing - Tape 12" and 7 min 50 s long, one of them explains that they noticed them from the first night out and that there were two kinds: a pinpoint flash or a streak, depending on whether the particle struck the eye head-on or parallel; another says his were horizontal, a little above the center, and went from right to left. In the same conversation a crew member agrees that it was the same phenomenon all three of them saw and that it was not something external but something happening inside the eye.

The crew and medical staff also discussed whether the flashes were random or directional and whether they appeared in one eye or both, and one astronaut remarked that, after observing long enough, he could tell which eye had registered each flash. The medical staff reply on the same tape that they had long been looking for cosmic-ray and heavy-nucleus strikes, and that since the Mercury program they had photographed retinas without finding any tracks.

The official description accompanying the audio on DVIDS, the government portal where it was posted, adds that the medical team considered whether similar phenomena reported by Buzz Aldrin on Apollo 11 were due to exposure of the retina to cosmic rays, and that NASA later determined that what the Apollo 12 crew described was internal to their vision rather than an external light source. The audio was declassified on May 22, 2026, as part of the second release of PURSUE, the name under which the U.S. government publishes its declassified UFO files.

The transcript of the mission's radio exchanges with the ground records another episode, on the lunar surface about two and a half hours before liftoff. From Intrepid, the lunar module, lunar module pilot Alan Bean tells Houston that looking through the alignment telescope into the dark quadrant one can see "particles of light," flashes that come from behind on the left and sail off into space; he first thought they were dropping from what he called his "water boiler," a term the transcript does not explain, but it seemed to him that some were escaping the Moon and heading off toward the stars. Neither the transcript nor the official description of the audio links these particles, which Bean saw through the telescope, to the flashes the crew saw while trying to sleep.

The phenomenon known as light flashes or cosmic phosphenes, described by astronauts in deep space, is attributed to high-energy charged particles (galactic cosmic rays, mostly protons and heavy nuclei) passing through the eyeball and interacting with the retina and optic nerve, producing the perception of flashes with no external light source. Beyond Earth's magnetic field, which shields the planet's surface from much of this flux, exposure is far greater.

The hypothesis was already raised in the 1950s (Cornelius Tobias) and documented in later missions (the rest of the Apollo program, Skylab, and later onboard experiments such as ALTEA on the International Space Station), as well as partly reproduced in particle accelerators. The exact neuronal mechanism (direct ionization versus Cherenkov radiation in the vitreous humor) remains under study.

Why it matters

On the tape, three astronauts describe flashes of light that, according to Fox News, were discussed in the medical debrief itself as possibly tied to cosmic rays or heavy particles passing through the eye. Even so, coverage of the declassification presented them as "unexplained" flashes: Fox News headlined on May 22, 2026 that the Pentagon was declassifying Apollo 12 audio of astronauts describing unexplained streaks of light.

OANN reported that scientists have long noted that the flashes were likely caused by cosmic rays interacting with the astronauts' retinas in deep space. It is the general explanation of the phenomenon, which the debrief raises as a possibility; no source cited treats it as settled for these particular flashes.
