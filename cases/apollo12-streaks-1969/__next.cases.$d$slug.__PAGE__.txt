16:T47c,Unlike the military-sensor cases, this one has a robust, long-standing scientific explanation: the phenomenon known as 'light flashes' or cosmic phosphenes, reported by astronauts in deep space, is attributed to high-energy charged particles (galactic cosmic rays, mostly protons and heavy nuclei) passing through the eyeball and interacting with the retina and optic nerve, producing the perception of flashes with no external light source. Beyond Earth's magnetic field, which at the surface shields us from much of this flux, exposure is far greater, which explains why the effect is notable precisely on the translunar leg. The hypothesis was already raised in the 1950s (Cornelius Tobias) and was systematically documented in later missions (the rest of the Apollo program, Skylab, and later onboard experiments such as ALTEA on the ISS), as well as partly reproduced in particle accelerators.
