🇲🇩2023·Soroca region, northern Moldova

Moldova.Soroca · a balloon-like object shuts Moldova's airspace

On February 14, 2023, at about 12:30 p.m., Moldova's Defense Ministry received word of a small object resembling a weather balloon in the Soroca region. Because of the weather and the impossibility of tracking it, Moldova closed its airspace from 1:24 to 2:46 p.m. At the same hour, Romania's air surveillance system detected an object moving like a balloon at about 11,000 meters over southeastern Romania, and two fighters failed to find it. The next day, Moldova's Defense Ministry spoke of an unpowered aerostat, and the speaker of Parliament attributed it to Russia, citing Ukrainian information.

Ordinary cause the sources propose: balloon
On February 14, 2023, Romania's Defense Ministry described what its air surveillance system detected as a small object with flight characteristics resembling a weather balloon. The next day, the Moldovan Defense Ministry told Radio Free Europe it was "an object without propulsion, in the shape of a balloon." The class covers a civilian balloon: neither authority said whose it was. On February 15, the speaker of Moldova's Parliament, Igor Grosu, said on Ukrainian information that the object was believed to be Russian, "either a drone or a device that takes measurements," which points to another state's device. (Romanian Ministry of National Defence — "Precizări MApN," statement No. 45 (Feb 14, 2023) ↗)

What happened

On Tuesday, February 14, 2023, several governments were watching their skies nervously. Ten days earlier, the United States had shot down a Chinese spy balloon off South Carolina, and between February 10 and 12 its fighters downed three more objects over Alaska, the Yukon and Lake Huron (see ↗ Lake Huron). In Moldova, Ukraine's neighbor, President Maia Sandu had accused Russia the day before of plotting to overthrow her government.

At about 12:30 p.m. local time, Moldova's Defense Ministry received a report: in the country's airspace, in the region of the town of Soroca, in the north near the Ukrainian border, a small unidentified object resembling a weather balloon had been detected. That is how the Civil Aviation Authority (AAC) put it that afternoon, in a statement carried by the state news agency Moldpres. The statement does not say who detected the object or by what means.

The Interdepartmental Commission for Airspace Management met in haste. According to the Romanian news agency Agerpres, it brings together the Defense Ministry, the AAC, the state air traffic control company MoldATSA and the Infrastructure Ministry. At 1:24 p.m. it decided to close the airspace temporarily, "given the weather conditions and the impossibility of monitoring and identifying the object and its flight path," to protect the public.

At 2:46 p.m., on the Defense Ministry's proposal, the airspace reopened "after checking the information and making sure there was no danger to public safety." The closure lasted an hour and 22 minutes. The AAC added that flights were operating safely and that the authorities had followed the procedures set for similar situations.

Romania, to the southwest, reported a similar episode the same day. According to its Defense Ministry, at about 12:30 p.m. the Air Force's air surveillance system detected, in the southeast of the country, a small object whose flight characteristics resembled those of a weather balloon, at about 11,000 meters. At about 12:40 p.m., two MiG-21 LanceR fighters of the air policing service that Romania provides under NATO command took off from the 86th Air Base at Fetești. Their crews did not confirm that the object was there, either visually or on their onboard radar. The two aircraft stayed in the area for about 30 minutes until the situation was clear, and then returned to base.

The Romanian statement does not mention Moldova or give the exact place of the detection, and the Moldovan one does not mention Romania.

The next day, two versions emerged in Chisinau. Officials of the Moldovan Defense Ministry told Radio Free Europe that it was an aerostat, "an object without propulsion, in the shape of a balloon." That same Wednesday, reporters asked the speaker of Parliament, Igor Grosu, whether it had been confirmed that a Russian drone caused the closure. Grosu said that the object was believed to be Russian and had come through Ukraine: "Yes, it is either a drone or a device that takes measurements at a certain altitude. That is the information we received from our Ukrainian colleagues." According to him, Moldova's National Army identified the device and then passed the information to the Romanian military, because it was moving southwest.

On February 15, the Kyiv military administration announced that six Russian balloons had crossed the sky over the Ukrainian capital and that air defenses had shot down most of them. It said they were carried by the wind and might be fitted with radar reflectors, devices that make a small object look large on a radar screen, and reconnaissance equipment; their purpose was likely to locate and wear down air defenses. The Ukrainian Air Force spokesman, Yurii Ihnat, said Russia was using the balloons to spare its reconnaissance drones.

Another version circulated that day: according to Ukrainian Telegram channels, two drones launched from Belarus had entered Ukraine and Moldova. The Chisinau newspaper Ziarul Național picked it up, and Agerpres cited it as information from those channels, not from any authority. In the United States, Fox News ran a headline that same day saying Romania and Moldova were reporting "potential UFOs" in their airspace.

Why it matters

Whether it was the same object remains to be seen, though the times argue against it. Grosu said Moldova passed the information to Romania because the device was heading southwest. But both detections came at about 12:30 p.m., Moldova and Romania share a time zone, and Soroca lies some 300 km or more from southeastern Romania. Even with a very strong wind of 150 km/h, a balloon would need about two hours to cover that distance. For it to be the same object, the report would have had to reach Moldova when the balloon was already over Romania; otherwise, the Romanian detection was of a different one.

Grosu was the only official to attribute an origin to the object. He himself presented it as Ukrainian information and wavered between a drone and a measuring instrument, while the Moldovan Defense Ministry spoke of an object without propulsion in the shape of a balloon, which fits poorly with a drone. According to Kyiv's announcement on February 15, Russia was launching balloons over Ukraine at the time; a civilian weather balloon carried by the wind fits just as well with what the two ministries described. The published sources do not allow a choice between the two explanations.

The case also shows how much the moment weighed. A slow, unidentified object was enough to close a country's airspace and send two NATO fighters aloft, in the week of the North American shootdowns and a day after Moldova accused Russia of preparing a coup. The word "UFO" came from the foreign press; the two governments spoke of an object resembling a balloon.
