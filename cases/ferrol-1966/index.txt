28:T558,The most striking thing about the case is how it reached paper. The corporal, 'considering that the matter was of no importance', did not report it to his superiors, but he did tell comrades and civilians and showed them the photograph, and that is how the La Voz de Galicia correspondent in Ferrol heard of it; he phoned that day to ask for authorisation to publish the story, because its author was a Navy corporal and the corporal would not agree without his superiors' permission. That call prompted the letter from the lieutenant commander in charge of the department's radiotelegraph station, addressed to the admiral captain general of the Naval Department and dated 8 August 1966, four months after the events, with the sketch and the photograph attached. The Intelligence Section of the Air Operations Command summarised it at Torrejón on 24 May 1993: the letter described 'an apparent observation period of 45 minutes', with the object's shape, colour and size in the sketch, but 'no movement of any kind is mentioned'. Its conclusion was that 'there is no record of any later investigation' and that 'the lack of the most elementary data makes it impossible to venture any kind of hypothesis about its possible explanation'.
