Spain.Ferrol · four men on watch see a light and photograph it

On the night of April 2, 1966, an electrician corporal, a line keeper and two sailors on duty at the La Carreira radio receiving station, near Ferrol, watched for some 45 minutes a luminous object "of very intense opaque light" that changed shape every five minutes, to the left of Mount Campelo. The corporal photographed it with a 68-second exposure. Nobody told the commanders until August, when a correspondent of La Voz de Galicia asked for permission to publish it. The Air Operations Command put forward no hypothesis; Ballester Olmos and Plaza del Olmo propose the Moon, on a different date.

What happened

On the night of April 2, 1966, an electrician corporal, the line keeper and two sailors were on duty at the La Carreira radio receiving station, a communications facility of the El Ferrol del Caudillo Naval Department located between Narón and Valdoviño. According to the letter the station commander wrote months later, between 23:30 and 00:15 on the 3rd the four of them saw in the sky "a luminous object of very intense, non-reflective opaque light, changing shape approximately every 5 minutes". It was "to the left of Mount Campelo", a coastal height that lies to the northwest of the station.

Because it caught his attention, the corporal went to fetch a camera and took a photograph with a 68-second exposure at f/1.9, which according to the letter corresponds to "the last phase of the sketch". The same document adds that at 23:00, half an hour before the station's observation begins, a sailor posted there who was off duty saw "the same luminous object" over the station from Jubia, in Narón.

Everything known about the sighting comes through that letter and a hand-drawn sketch; there are no witness statements, and the witnesses' names are blanked out in the published copy. The sketch shows a series of figures, from a crescent of about ten meters labeled "stationary" to a circle and a rectangle of about two meters, with the notes "intense opaque light" and "approximate height 200 meters". The photograph survives only as a photocopy: an almost black sheet on which, with the contrast pushed, one can make out an isolated light spot and, lower down, a vertical row of small spots. The file's summary had already warned that "both the photo and the photocopy are very dark".

The corporal, "considering that the matter was of no importance", did not report it to his superiors, but he told comrades and civilians and showed them the photograph, and that is how the La Voz de Galicia correspondent in Ferrol heard of it. On August 8, 1966, four months later, the correspondent phoned to ask for authorization to publish the story, because its author was a Navy corporal who would not agree without his superiors' permission. That call prompted the letter from the lieutenant commander in charge of the department's radiotelegraph station, dated the same day and addressed to the admiral captain general of the Naval Department, with the sketch and the photograph attached.

The Intelligence Section of the Air Operations Command summarized it at Torrejón on May 24, 1993: the letter described "an apparent observation period of 45 minutes", with the object's shape, color and size in the sketch, but "no movement of any kind is mentioned". Its conclusion was that "there is no record of any later investigation" and that "the lack of the most elementary data makes it impossible to venture any kind of hypothesis about its possible explanation". It proposed declassification, and the file was declassified on May 31, 1993.

Why it matters

Four Navy men saw and photographed a shape-changing light from a communications station, and the event reached the chain of command only four months later, because a journalist wanted to publish it. The letter holds no separate statements and no measured bearing or elevation, and nobody checked the date by other means; that is why the Air Operations Command, in 1993, declined to put forward any explanation. The four witnesses at the station saw the light together and count as a single testimony, the Jubia sailor's account arrives second-hand through the same letter, and the almost black photocopy does not allow what they describe to be checked.

Vicente-Juan Ballester Olmos and the physicist Julio Plaza del Olmo analyzed the photograph and concluded that the object seen and photographed could be the Moon, and that the date of the event would differ from the one the report gives. In the Spanish version of their summary they call it "an unexpected outcome that explains everything", but the summary does not say which date they propose or on what grounds. On the letter's date the proposal does not fit: according to an ephemeris calculation for this record at La Carreira, at 23:30 on April 2, 1966 the Moon was nearly full, about 59 degrees high and to the south, in the opposite direction from Campelo.

At the end of March and the end of April that year, by contrast, a crescent Moon stood low in the west-northwest at those hours, to the left of Campelo, and a crescent setting through cloud or haze, about which the letter says nothing, changes shape and brightness from one minute to the next, like the series of crescents in the sketch. That the letter's date is wrong is plausible, since it was written four months later without anyone having reported the sighting at the time; but the proposal depends on that correction. Nor does it on its own account for the Jubia sighting: from there the station lies to the north-northwest, and a crescent Moon at that hour would have stood some 60 degrees further to the left, not "over the station".

Years later, several publications tied the case to the story of an object said to have fallen into the sea near Ferrol, picked up by fishermen and kept at the La Graña naval station with the letters NASA on its fuselage. The letter mentions no movement, no fall and no wreckage, and El Confidencial Digital, which spread the NASA-prototype version in 2012, placed it on "that winter night of 1966". Ballester Olmos, Plaza del Olmo and Mercedes Pullman concluded that the story mixed a minor sighting with the finding of some NATO buoys in the North Sea and other events from the 1970s, and the Navy's Central Archive replied to them that there was no record of the finding at sea of a NASA device in 1966.
