2b:T6ce,The episode extended into the following day. A T-33 Shooting Star took off from Howard Field to intercept one of the objects; according to the record, each time the T-33 closed in, the object 'would move several miles away, then stop.' The radar observations were described as strong returns, with moments of erratic and even evasive movement. Several search and tracking radar installations were involved, along with anti-aircraft artillery personnel. The file adds details that complicate the picture. Personnel at the sites reported "seeing red and green lights", with no noise and only for a short period; a commercial flight that volunteered to investigate was vectored "within a hundred yards of target" and reported a negative sighting; and on March 10 the Taboga Island tracking radar calculated the target's speed, as it moved away from two jets, at about one thousand miles per hour. The movement and identification section log gives the sequence hour by hour: at 23:45 on March 9 the object was 4,870 yards from D Battery at 3,500 feet, and a searchlight was to be used from a crash boat; at 23:55 it was at 6,000 feet moving away very rapidly to the southwest; at 24:00 radar advised that 'as soon as searchlight was employed, the object became evasive', now at 10,000 feet; Braniff Flight 400, about 100 yards away, saw nothing, and contact was lost at 02:10.
