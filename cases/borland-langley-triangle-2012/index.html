United States.Langley · Dylan Borland sees a silent triangle

In the summer of 2012, Air Force geospatial intelligence specialist Dylan Borland said he saw, near the NASA hangar at Langley Air Force Base (Virginia), an equilateral triangle about 100 feet long that interfered with his phone, made no sound, rose in seconds to commercial-jet altitude and moved slowly east, out over the Atlantic. He recounted it under oath before Congress on September 9, 2025, alleging professional retaliation. In August 2026 he claimed in an interview that a non-human intelligence “controls” the cover-up, his own interpretation of a secondhand comment, with no new verifiable data.

What happened

Dylan Borland served in the U.S. Air Force from 2010 to 2013 as a geospatial intelligence specialist (the 1N1 specialty), analyzing imagery taken from the air to identify military targets. From 2011 to 2013 he was stationed at Langley Air Force Base, Virginia, on operations that ran around the clock. After leaving the service he worked as a senior analyst at BAE Systems and Intrepid Solutions, specializing in video, radar and electro-optical imagery.

One night in the summer of 2012, around 01:30, Borland saw a white light appear roughly a hundred feet up near the NASA hangar on the base. At first he "thought it was a weather balloon", adding "I've seen tests from there before". As it approached, the light "manifested" into an equilateral triangle about 100 feet long and "between one to two stories thick", with a light on each corner and a larger one in the center, which stopped "about 100 feet in front of me and approximately 100 feet above me". The surface looked to him like black metallic flake paint with something resembling "gold lava plasma" flowing across it; the material, in his words, looked "fluid or dynamic" rather than solid. His phone, he said, "got extremely hot and completely froze."

After two or three minutes, the center light flashed and the object immediately shot up to what he took to be the cruising altitude of a commercial jet, with no sound or perceptible air disturbance. Borland said he felt static electricity all over his body and smelled "the smell of after a thunderstorm". From below he could see only the center light ("If I didn't actually see it take off, I would've thought it was a star"), which hovered and then began "to slowly move due east out over the Atlantic Ocean". He put the whole episode at about 15 minutes.

Before going public, in March 2023, he met with AARO, the Pentagon office that studies these phenomena, though he said he distrusted its conclusions and held back details. In August 2023 he filed a complaint with the Intelligence Community Inspector General (ICIG), in a video-recorded interview under oath.

His first public sworn testimony came on September 9, 2025, before the House Task Force on the Declassification of Federal Secrets, at the hearing "Restoring Public Trust Through UAP Transparency and Whistleblower Protection," alongside George Knapp, Jeffrey Nuccetelli and Navy Senior Chief Petty Officer Alexandro Wiggins. Asked by Rep. Mace whether anyone else saw it, he answered "Not to my knowledge". Asked whether he thought it was U.S. government technology, he said "I did once upon a time", but that knowing what he knows now he would have to answer in a secure room for classified information (a SCIF). He also stated that his career was "deliberately obstructed" after he reported the encounter: manipulation of his security clearance, alleged medical malpractice within the veterans' health system, workplace harassment and, as of the testimony, unemployment.

On August 6, 2026, in an interview with journalist Jesse Michels on the American Alchemy podcast, Borland said that "the deepest secret isn't the craft, it's the cover-up," and that the cover-up serves crime and money, not national security. Asked directly whether a non-human intelligence is in control of the cover-up, he answered "absolutely yes," adding that an ICIG official he did not name had "extremely hinted" to him, in an unclassified setting, that humans might not be "fully running the show." By his own description, this is his interpretation of a secondhand hint from an anonymous official, not something he witnessed.

On August 11, 2026, journalist Jeremy Corbell publicly claimed that a source inside the Office of the Director of National Intelligence (ODNI) had disclosed to him details of Borland's protected testimony to the ICIG, including the codename of an alleged UAP program operating without congressional oversight, "Rubik's Cube". Corbell had already mentioned it in May 2026 at the McMinnville festival, where Borland, on stage, refused to confirm or deny it without prior amnesty. Borland corroborated that the disclosure was intentional.

Asked by Liberation Times, the Office of the Intelligence Community Inspector General responded with a formal statement: it said it was aware of a journalist's public claim about testimony allegedly given to the office, noted that the law requires it to protect the identity of confidential whistleblowers, kept to its policy of neither confirming nor denying ongoing investigations, and warned that any unauthorized disclosure of protected information may be grounds for removal from Federal service and may warrant referral to the FBI or other investigative authorities. It is a legal warning prompted by a public claim: it neither confirms that the testimony exists nor validates its content.

Under Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard, ODNI had created the Director's Initiatives Group (DIG), a unit for sensitive inquiries that took on UAP matters. Matthew Brown, the whistleblower behind "Immaculate Constellation" (↗), said he went to the Liberty Crossing campus expecting a session on whistleblower protection and left with the impression that the group cared more about which classified compartments he had accessed than about assessing what he was reporting; he said he declined the ICIG route after Borland warned him that his own interview had felt like a measurement of how much he knew. Corbell added that an ODNI official described Brown in antisemitic terms inside a SCIF, an accusation Brown reads as an attempt to discredit him. An ODNI spokesperson replied that the agency is focused on carrying out presidential mandates on UAP, without addressing the disclosure allegations. Apart from the ICIG statement, all of this consists of claims by the parties without independent corroboration.

In 2026 Borland co-founded, with Matthew Brown, the nonprofit Vanguard Enterprise (↗), which provides legal, medical and financial support to UAP whistleblowers from the national-security sector; whistleblower Jeff Nuccetelli later joined as its development director.

Why it matters

Borland describes concrete physical effects: a large object that makes no sound and moves no air, a phone that heats up and freezes, static electricity on his body and a climb to cruising altitude in seconds. The account comes from an imagery analyst with a verifiable military record, under oath and at real risk of perjury, and his written testimony is published on the official site of the House Oversight Committee (oversight.house.gov).

But he is a single witness. Asked whether anyone else saw it, he answered "Not to my knowledge"; no radar or other instrument record has been published, and the account became public thirteen years after the fact, with the ordinary risk that memory rebuilds the details. None of the case's sources (the written testimony to Congress, the hearing transcript, the press coverage) proposes a concrete ordinary explanation for the object; Borland himself said he first took the light for a weather balloon, before seeing it up close.

What happened in 2026 adds no data about the night in 2012. The claim that a non-human intelligence "controls" the cover-up is Borland's interpretation of a secondhand, anonymous comment. The ICIG statement is an official, citable document with explicit legal consequences, but all it says is that the office neither confirms nor denies anything: it does not establish that Borland's testimony exists, much less what it says about that night. What would add new evidence is a second witness to the event or the declassification of the ICIG file itself.

What the 2026 sequence does show is how whistleblower protection works. If the leak happened as alleged, the body charged with safeguarding confidential identities would have been the channel through which protected material got out, and the codename "Rubik's Cube" entered public debate with no way to verify it. Together with Brown's account of the DIG, that describes a setting where reporting is costly and where the line between investigating a whistleblower and profiling one grows blurry. It helps in reading Borland's own retaliation claims, but it says nothing about the object, and for now the leak and Brown's account are crossed allegations, not proven facts.
