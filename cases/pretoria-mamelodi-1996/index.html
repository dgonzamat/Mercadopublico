🇿🇦1996·Pretoria, Mamelodi and Cullinan, Gauteng (South Africa)

South Africa.Pretoria · a police helicopter chases a glowing disc

Early in the morning of August 28, 1996 (around 4 a.m., according to later accounts), Sergeant Nico Stander of the Adriaan Vlok police station in Pretoria filmed a pulsing luminous disc; Johan Becker, a Pretoria resident, also saw it. At 5:25 a police helicopter flown by Superintendent Fred Viljoen took off and chased it from Mamelodi toward Cullinan without closing in, giving up when fuel ran low; the radar at Waterkloof Air Base showed only diffuse clutter. The time and bearing match Venus, in the east-northeast, and Stander himself mentioned the morning star on his recording; against this stands the 70° elevation Viljoen gave.

What happened

Very early on August 28, 1996 (around 4:00 a.m. according to later accounts; the account of the time gives no hour), Sergeant Nico Stander, on duty at the Adriaan Vlok police station in Pretoria, observed and videotaped a pulsating luminous disc in the sky. Stander, a UFO enthusiast (he showed the reporters a stack of "Encounters" magazines), shot it with a camera borrowed from his father, through the patrol car's windshield; on the tape an officer says the light is "pulsating like a little heart" while Stander himself remarks that "it could be a balloon, the morning star or an eclipse of the sun and moon."

It was a full-moon night. According to the account the Afrikaans newspaper "Rapport" published on September 1, the object drifted over the Erasmuskloof suburb, southeast of the capital, until, in the account's words, "police appeared on the scene," without saying which officers; according to the same account, Stander was one of the policemen on patrol when the object appeared. Later accounts describe it as a throbbing disc that included a red triangle and at one point emitted bright green "tentacles"; those details are not in the 1996 account.

The sighting escalated into a chase. The police Bo-105 helicopter took off at 5:25 with Superintendent Fred Viljoen at the controls and four passengers, according to the same account. Viljoen stated: "A bright light was above Mamelodi, at 70 degrees above the horizon. I followed the white disk, with the unusual flight pattern, in the direction of Cullinan. It moved at a high speed. We could not keep up with it and eventually had to give up when lack of fuel became a problem." He added that the disk was easily observable even in broad daylight. The account says the object "shot off at an unearthly speed" until it disappeared over the horizon at Cullinan.

The figures that circulate today (some 200 police on the ground, a top speed of about 270 km/h, the chase abandoned at 10,000 feet and a final vertical ascent) come from later compilations and do not appear in that account. The public broadcaster, SABC, aired the video on August 28 itself. On its news, which credited the video to a "Sgt. Nico Bekker," not to Stander, Viljoen said the craft outran him with ease and that what struck him most were its undulating movements, and resident Johan Becker, interviewed in another part of the city, said it traveled silently and in an undulating fashion. Many South Africans took it for a joke or a promotion for "Independence Day," which, according to "Rapport," started showing in cinemas the following Friday, August 30; the distributor, Sterland, denied any involvement.

Viljoen stated that he did not ask for an Air Force Mirage fighter to be scrambled, but that he kept radio contact with the control tower at Waterkloof Air Force Base, whose operators told him their radar showed "clutter" (a diffuse signal) in the area where the disk was flying. That is not the sharp signal of a solid object that would fix its position, speed and altitude. Viljoen, a veteran pilot, said it was the first time in his life he had seen anything like it and that he had always been skeptical about UFOs.

The morning star was there: on August 28, 1996 Venus had just passed its greatest morning elongation (its widest apparent separation from the Sun before dawn) and shone at magnitude −4.2; at 4:00 a.m. local time it was rising in the east-northeast of Pretoria, and by about 5:30 it stood some 21° high toward the northeast, roughly the bearing of Mamelodi and Cullinan from the city.

Why it matters

It brings together a police videotape, a helicopter crew with a named pilot, and real-time contact with an air base. But the version in later accounts, such as UFO Casebook's, is more spectacular than the 1996 account: the figures that sustain the exotic reading do not appear in the report published four days later. The witness who filmed the object was a UFO enthusiast who, on the recording itself, mentioned the morning star among the possibilities. The Waterkloof radar showed only diffuse clutter, not the signal of a sharp object, and the most striking visual descriptions (red triangle, green tentacles), which only later accounts give, are what one would expect from a 1996 nighttime recording of a very bright point source.

The elevation is what does not fit Venus: Viljoen gave 70° above the horizon, whereas Venus stood at about 21° when he took off; if that estimate is good, what he chased was not the planet. Everything else fits Venus. The time given by later accounts matches the rising of the brightest planet in the sky, the bearing of the chase toward Cullinan matches its azimuth (its direction along the horizon), the helicopter never closed in and gave up for fuel, and the object remained visible in daylight, as Venus is at that magnitude. A light that cannot be caught and seems to undulate through a car windshield or from the cabin of a helicopter in flight is the classic pattern of chasing a celestial body.

No source proposes an explanation: the sergeant only listed possibilities, Venus fits almost everything by a PyEphem calculation of its position for Pretoria, and Viljoen's elevation estimate is the unresolved objection.
