31:T58e,What makes the case a documentary landmark is what came afterward. Vilas-Boas wrote two letters to journalist João Martins, of the magazine O Cruzeiro, and Fontes and Martins decided to pay for his trip to Rio de Janeiro. There, on 22 February 1958, physician Olavo T. Fontes (a correspondent of the U.S. group APRO) questioned him in Martins's presence and examined him, psychological tests included. The case history he signed records insomnia and nightmares, nausea and headache in the first days, burning and watering eyes, a month of drowsiness and, from the eighth day, small wounds on the forearms and legs: the first after a knock and the rest spontaneous, reddish nodules with a central opening discharging a yellowish serous fluid, ringed by a violet area Fontes said he could not interpret. On the chin he noted two patches, scars of a superficial lesion with bleeding, and the mental examination was normal. The same report notes that he had no fever, diarrhoea, haemorrhages or jaundice, no hair loss, and noticed no anaemia. The 1958 report makes no diagnosis; it was in a 1966 letter that Fontes wrote that the symptoms "suggest radiation-poisoning or exposure to radiation", but that the patient came too late for the blood tests and that, without that evidence, he decided not to talk about it.
