2a:T4c6,The statement the reverend signed for the RAAF is precise. The object was a dull grey, with four or five dark vertical bands at regular intervals and what looked like a short aerial array projecting outward and upward from its northern end; Mrs Browning thought it was more like a propeller rotating in a vertical plane. Browning, used to seeing Viscount aircraft in the area, judged it slightly longer than those and estimated about 100 feet, at about 400 feet up, over the Panbanger estate, some three and a half to four miles away. It moved north at about 60 to 70 mph, covered a mile and a half and stopped abruptly; within seconds, five or six small saucer-like objects came out of the low cloud at high speed and stationed themselves around it at half a mile, and after a few seconds the group reversed back toward the squall and disappeared into it. The cigar was in view about two minutes and the small objects about one; they heard no noise. Browning phoned the Western Junction airport control tower and on 9 October gave his account to The Examiner, which published it the next day with the length wrongly given as 300 feet; he later specified that the objects were below the skyline of the hills, not above it.2b:T610,Wing Commander G.
