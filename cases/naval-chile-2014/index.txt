2d:T66f,The Navy referred the material to CEFAA (Comité de Estudios de Fenómenos Aéreos Anómalos), under the DGAC. The Director General of Civil Aeronautics authorized a formal investigation that ran for more than two years and consulted the DGAC, the Chilean Air Force, the Navy, ENAER, the Chilean Meteorological Directorate, astronomers, physicists and French analysts from GEIPAN. The Air Force analysts concluded it was a real three-dimensional form with volume that "has control in its movements" and was not affected by the winds. The French analysts confirmed the helicopter flew at a constant 120 knots, that the object was on average about 55 km away, almost exactly the Navy's estimate, and that it kept pace with the helicopter; but they proposed it was a medium-haul aircraft landing at Santiago, with the two hot spots spaced like jet engines and the 'plumes' explained by dumping cabin waste water carried by the west wind.
