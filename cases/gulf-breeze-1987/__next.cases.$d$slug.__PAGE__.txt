1a:T696,The decisive blow came in 1990. After Walters and his family moved out, the new owners of the house found, hidden in the attic, a model UFO made of polystyrene (foam). The model (about 23 centimetres across and 15 high) was built from four foam plates, a blue gel sheet, an orange paper ring, a plastic tube and electrical tape, and reproduced with remarkable fidelity the object in Walters's photographs. Reporter Craig Myers, of the Pensacola News Journal, managed to duplicate Walters's images almost exactly using that model, and that same June of 1990 Tommy Smith, a young friend of Walters's son, and his parents told the Gulf Breeze police chief and the press that Walters had told him from the start that the story was made up, that he had seen him make double exposures and that Walters had asked him to take faked photos to the Gulf Breeze Sentinel as if they were his own; Smith handed those photos to the police and repeated the accusation in 1993. According to the 1990 press, the new resident found the model, made of four foam plates and drafting paper, while moving insulation aside in the attic to trace a pipe. Walters signed an affidavit denying any knowledge of the model and maintained it had been 'planted' in the attic by someone seeking to discredit him, but the match between the model and the photos, combined with the technical analysis of double exposures, decisively tipped the consensus toward a hoax.
