Middle East, CENTCOM area of responsibility (exact location undisclosed).Middle East · a sensor tracks two patches and keeps losing them

In 2026, a set of visible-light and infrared cameras aboard a U.S. military platform recorded 54 seconds of video in the Middle East, and U.S. Central Command (CENTCOM) sent it to the Pentagon office that studies UFOs (AARO). According to the official description, the sensor first tracks several patches that stand out from the background and then, at higher magnification, two of them, which it keeps losing. AARO released it on November 20, 2026, as an "unresolved" report, with no location, exact date or crew report.

Why no ordinary cause is identified
No source proposes what the sensor tracked: the official PR168 description only describes the video, warns that it is not an analytical judgment and releases it as unresolved, and no crew report has been released. The lack of an analysis is not evidence against an ordinary cause: according to AARO's 2024 annual report, of 57 reports from U.S. forces in the Middle East, 13 were resolved as balloons, drones or satellites, 2 have a preliminary assessment as satellite flares, and the remaining 42 were placed in its active archive "due to insufficient information to facilitate analysis." Record of the event

What happened

In 2026, on a date that has not been made public, an electro-optical and infrared system aboard a U.S. military platform recorded 54 seconds of video in the Middle East. Such a system pairs a visible-light camera, the electro-optical part, with a thermal camera, which builds its image from the heat that objects give off. U.S. Central Command (CENTCOM), which runs U.S. military operations in the region, submitted the recording as a report to the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO), the Pentagon office that studies unidentified anomalous phenomena (UAP).

AARO released the video on November 20, 2026, in the eighth batch of PURSUE, its program for publishing files on these phenomena, which has released them in batches since May 2026, through DVIDS, the U.S. armed forces' image distribution service. Its title is "DOW-UAP-PR168, Unresolved UAP Report, Middle East, 2026," the video's code followed by the region and the year. The location field on the page reads "(UNDISCLOSED LOCATION)." The official description does not name the type of platform or the mission, and the video has no sound.

AARO sums up the recording in three stretches. From second 0 to 15, the sensor pans to track several "areas of contrast," which move generally from right to left across the frame. An area of contrast is a part of the image brighter or darker than its surroundings; the phrase says nothing about its size or whether it is a solid object. From second 16 to 28, the sensor pans and changes magnification several times. From 29 to 54, it zooms in to track two areas of contrast, panning up and to the left of the frame, and, according to AARO, "intermittently loses contact" with them: at times it fails to hold them in its sights. The description does not say why.

Viewed at one frame per second, the video shows an almost uniform black-and-white image, with the reticle (the cross that marks the center of the image) and a north marker. The data the sensor overlays on the screen are covered with black rectangles. In the first seconds, several bright points a few pixels across are scattered around the frame, none with a recognizable shape. The image cuts out for an instant, to a flat black or light field, at the start and around seconds 16, 22, 25, 28 and 37, in the stretches where AARO describes the magnification changes and the zoom.

In the last stretch, with the image magnified, the frames do not show which are the two areas that AARO says the sensor tracks.

The official description gives only the year. The DVIDS page lists January 1, 2026, as the date taken, but that date does not mark the day of the recording: another CENTCOM video released the same day, PR166, which its description places in 2020, is listed with January 1, 2020.

Why it matters

The recording has a known chain of delivery: according to the official description, it was CENTCOM that submitted the report to AARO. What it lacks is anyone to explain it. No report from the crew or the sensor operator has been released, and AARO's description ends with a warning: it is provided "for informational purposes only" and should not be read as an analytical judgment, an investigative conclusion or a factual determination about the event's validity, nature or significance. The "unresolved" label means AARO has not closed the case, not that it found something anomalous. Another CENTCOM video, the one from the 2025 drone crews in the Middle East ↗ Middle East, came with mission reports from two of those crews describing what they saw; here the image is all that has been released.

Without that context, the video cannot give size, distance or speed. A sensor that pans and changes magnification moves the whole frame, so a patch's movement on screen may come partly from the sensor itself. AARO made that point about another Middle East video, the 2020 one in which an object seems to grow ↗ Middle East, where it said the object's apparent growth was probably due, at least in part, to the platform closing in. In its 2024 annual report, it added that birds are often mistaken for UAP because video compression and pixelation turn them into an amorphous blob or orb, and that glare in electro-optical and infrared sensors can distort an object's true shape. That is a general warning in the report, not an analysis of this video.

The same report gives a benchmark for the region. Between May 2023 and June 2024, U.S. forces deployed to the Middle East sent 57 reports. AARO resolved 13 as balloons, drones or satellites, made a preliminary assessment that two more might be satellite flares, and placed the remaining 42 in its active archive for lack of information to analyze them. What has been released of this video has no location, date, platform or crew report, though AARO may have received more data from CENTCOM than has been released. In the region, lack of information was the most common reason a report went unresolved, and missing data does not rule out an ordinary cause.

The video came out alongside two other CENTCOM videos, released the same day with the same tag: PR166, an infrared patch from 2020 ↗ Middle East, and PR169, another area of contrast, from 2023 ↗ Middle East. All three use the same wording and the same warning. It remains to be seen whether AARO will release the location or the mission report for this recording, which are the data that would allow what the sensor tracked to be checked against air traffic, balloons or drones in the area.
