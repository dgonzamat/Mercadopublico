United States.USS Jackson · a Navy chief says he saw a light rise from the sea

An active-duty Navy senior chief, Alexandro Wiggins, told Congress that on February 15, 2023, aboard USS Jackson off San Diego, he saw on radar, on the ship's camera display (Star SAFIRE) and with the naked eye a “Tic Tac”-shaped light that, by his account, rose from the sea, joined three other objects and left with them at the same moment. A video was recorded off the display and the officer on watch was told, but, by his testimony, there was no follow-up investigation; the witness himself acknowledged he cannot prove the object came out of the water.

What happened

On February 15, 2023, at about 19:15 Pacific time, Senior Chief Alexandro Wiggins, an operations specialist with about 23 years of Navy service, was on watch aboard USS Jackson (LCS-6), a littoral combat ship transiting Warning Area W-291, a military exercise area off the Southern California coast. According to his written statement, he was moving between the combat center (called ICC-1 on this class of ship, and located on the bridge) and the bridge wing, the open platform on one side, to compare what the sensors showed with what could be seen with the naked eye. The coordinates shown in the imagery (32.889 N, 117.934 W) place the ship about 60 km (about 33 nautical miles) off the San Diego coast; Wiggins describes it as the same general area as the ↗ incident.

Before Congress he said the contact first appeared on his radar and that he went out to the port bridge wing to check it. At about seven or eight nautical miles he saw a light that, in his reading, came out of the water and rose slowly to about 3,000 or 4,000 feet before accelerating. On the display of the Star SAFIRE, the ship's surveillance camera, which sees in visible light and infrared, the object looked self-luminous and "Tic Tac"-shaped, with no visible control surfaces and no exhaust plume. By his account, it joined three similar objects, and the four held their relative positions as if in formation; he did not see those other three with his own eyes, but when he went back to the radar and the display.

All four departed at once with an acceleration he described as near-instantaneous (a "subjective" impression, his written statement says, and an estimate of "1, 2 Mach" he offered as a non-expert), with no sonic boom and no signs of conventional propulsion on infrared. A few seconds later they no longer showed on his radar. On that detection, the summary of his written statement says the objects "were detected on multiple sensors, including radar", and the sensors section specifies that they "were described as detected on multiple reconnaissance platforms including radar". Wiggins estimated the encounter, from radar detection to the end of the video, lasted five to seven minutes.

Combat-center personnel recorded the SAFIRE display with a handheld camera, capturing at least two of the objects. The released imagery is stabilized from that recording and shows time, coordinates and sensor readouts; on the audio, an operator mentions an altitude of "22 thousand feet". Wiggins testified that he reported it at the time to the tactical action officer on watch, who was in the room, and then asked the SAFIRE operator to point the camera at that position: the tactical action officer, himself, the RCO and two other watchstanders were all watching the display together. Asked by members of Congress, he said that to his knowledge there was no investigation, no after-action report and no interview by any agency of him or his shipmates, and that no one told them not to document or discuss it.

The case became public on April 8, 2025, when journalists Jeremy Corbell and George Knapp posted the imagery and audio excerpts on their WEAPONIZED site. Wiggins did not bring out the video: he saw it once it was already circulating, recognized his voice at the end of the recording and contacted Admiral Tim Gallaudet. On April 15 he recounted it on the two journalists' podcast. On WEAPONIZED he had said: "I know the difference between a light on the horizon coming up and a light coming out of the water." On September 9, 2025 he appeared, in his personal capacity, before the House Oversight Committee's Task Force on the Declassification of Federal Secrets, with a written statement that the Defense Department's Office of Prepublication and Security Review cleared for publication on September 4. Representative Eric Burlison hailed him as the first currently serving witness to appear at these hearings; Wiggins said the Navy supported him in testifying and that he faced no pressure.

At that hearing he qualified the feature that gives the case its name. Asked whether the object had come out of the ocean, he said yes, but added that he could not be sure of that part: it was night, the object was far away and "it is very hard to tell the difference between something on the horizon and something surfacing from the water." His personal conviction is that it came from the water, but he acknowledged he has no visual evidence showing it. He also said he believed in UFOs before the encounter and had watched George Knapp his whole life, answered "It cannot, no" when asked whether any known technology could explain what he saw, and stated that the full, unedited video had been provided to Congress.

On the Metabunk forum (a secondary source), several analysts used the time, position and angles shown on the display to search for aircraft in the camera's direction and proposed tentative candidates, among them a helicopter about 30 miles away and a much more distant airliner, without reaching a firm identification because of uncertainty over the camera bearing and the cropping of the released imagery. The same thread noted that 22,000 feet was the altitude of Southwest flight 1822, one of the nearby aircraft, at that moment, so the operator's callout may refer to that flight rather than to the object.

Why it matters

An active-duty senior chief with radar training testified before Congress, with the Navy's support, that he saw an object rise from the sea and leave with three others. Five people were watching the display and the contact was reported at the time to the officer on watch, yet, per the witness, no one investigated it, no after-action report was written and no agency interviewed the crew; as far as he knows, the incident was not documented.

Everything rests on his testimony. The emergence from the water is, in his own words, a conviction he cannot demonstrate. The radar detection is part of his account, but no radar data have been released. The public video is a handheld recording of a display showing lights at an unknown distance, and the 22,000-foot figure and the nighttime hour leave open the possibility of air traffic, which analysts explored with specific candidates without being able to close it. The Department of Defense has not confirmed the material, and none of the other four people in the room has spoken publicly.

The testimony can be checked: if the original recording, which Wiggins says Congress already has, or the radar data are released, it will be known what the ship's sensors showed that night.
