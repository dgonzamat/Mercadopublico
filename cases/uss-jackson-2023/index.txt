29:T49e,On February 15, 2023, at about 19:15 Pacific time, Senior Chief Operations Specialist Alexandro Wiggins —an operations specialist with about 23 years of Navy service— was on watch aboard USS Jackson (LCS-6), a littoral combat ship transiting Warning Area W-291 off the Southern California coast. According to his written statement, he was moving between the combat center (called ICC-1 on this class of ship, and located on the bridge) and the bridge wing to correlate the sensor picture with visual observation. Before Congress he specified that the contact first appeared on his radar and that he went out to the port bridge wing to verify it: at about seven or eight nautical miles he saw a light that, in his reading, came out of the water and rose slowly to about 3,000 or 4,000 feet before accelerating. On the display of the Star SAFIRE electro-optical/infrared system the object looked self-luminous and 'Tic Tac'-shaped, with no control surfaces and no exhaust plume. The coordinates published with the imagery (32.889 N, 117.934 W) place the ship about 60 km (~33 nmi) off the San Diego coast; Wiggins describes it as the same general area as the 2004 Nimitz incident.2a:T508,Still according to his testimony, the first object joined three similar ones and the four held a formation geometry; Wiggins clarified that he did not see the other three with his own eyes, but when he went back to the radar and the SAFIRE. All four departed at once with an acceleration he described as near-instantaneous —a 'subjective' impression, his written statement says, and an estimate of '1, 2 Mach' he offered as a non-expert— with no perceived sonic boom and no conventional propulsion signatures on infrared; a few seconds later the tracks were gone from his radar. On radar detection, the executive summary of his written statement says the objects 'were detected on multiple sensors, including radar'; the sensors section adds that they 'were described as detected on multiple reconnaissance platforms including radar' and that he saw the picture stop displaying them. Combat-center personnel recorded the SAFIRE display with a handheld camera, capturing at least two of the objects; the released imagery is stabilized from that recording and shows time, coordinates and sensor readouts, and on the audio an operator mentions an altitude of '22 thousand feet'. Wiggins estimated the encounter, from radar detection to the end of the video, lasted five to seven minutes.2b:T574,There was a report at the time, but no follow-up. Wiggins testified that he reported to the tactical action officer on watch, who was in the room, and then asked the SAFIRE operator to slew to that position; the tactical action officer, himself, the RCO and two other watchstanders were all watching the display together. Asked by members of Congress, he said that to his knowledge there was no investigation, no after-action report and no debrief by any agency of him or his shipmates, and that no one told them not to document or discuss it. Nor did he bring out the video: he saw it once it was already circulating, recognized his voice at the end of the recording and contacted Admiral Tim Gallaudet.
