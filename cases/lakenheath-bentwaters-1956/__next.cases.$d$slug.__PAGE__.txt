17:T451,The RAF sent up a de Havilland Venom night fighter; the telex does not name its base, which later research places at Waterbeach. According to the message in the file, the pilot saw a bright white light and lost it; vectored to another target, he reported it on his radar and that he was 'locking on', then that he had lost it. The control center then reported that the target had begun a tail chase of the fighter; the pilot acknowledged, tried to circle to get behind it, said he could not shake it, and described the echo as the 'clearest target I have ever seen on radar'. He returned low on fuel; a second Venom made no contact and returned with a malfunction. The 1956 message lists the fighter's radar among the manners of observation ('Air-electronic was A-1 airborne radar equipment in… jet aircraft. Type of aircraft, Venom'); only the Blue Book record card leaves that box unticked. What has not survived is any recording from that radar: the lock-on is known from what the pilot and the controllers reported.
