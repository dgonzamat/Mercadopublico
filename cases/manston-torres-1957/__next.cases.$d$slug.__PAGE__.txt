12:T473,Vectored by GCI, Torres —then a 25-year-old lieutenant— climbed in afterburner to about 32,000 feet and flew inside cloud with no visual reference: 'I'm in the soup and it's impossible to see anything', he answered when asked for observations. He was ordered to fire a full salvo of his 24 Mighty Mouse rockets, asked for authentication and received it. He found the echo where he was told, 30 degrees to port at 15 miles (24 km), and described it as a return that was 'burning a hole in the radar', similar to a B-52's, with 'the proportions of a flying aircraft carrier'; he locked it within seconds, at the system's maximum range. About ten seconds before firing, the overtake indicator swung from about 800 knots closing to 200 knots opening, the largest negative value the equipment displayed; the echo pulled away, broke lock and left his 30-mile scale, and GCI told him it had lost it too, within two sweeps. Torres wrote that the real speed could not be measured, but that his impression was that the object was traveling at '2 digit mach numbers'. He returned to Manston without firing and without having seen anything by eye.13:T605,The next day, according to Torres, a civilian who looked like 'a well dressed IBM salesman' questioned him in the squadron operations area, told him the matter was highly classified and threatened him with a national security breach if he spoke of it, even to his commander. Torres stayed silent for about thirty years. In May 1988 the two pilots met at a veterans' reunion at Manston; Torres then wrote his account, and a British solicitor looking into the case sent it to the Ministry of Defence on 20 July 1988, asking whether it had been a bogus blip caused by the weather or an electronic-warfare experiment. The MoD acknowledged it and checked with the Air Historical Branch; neither the MoD nor the USAF found any record of the incident. Torres's wingman, Dave Roberson, sent his own account with significant differences: he recalled an unarmed training flight, a landing at RAF Bentwaters to load rockets and several unknowns extending as far as Scotland; he did not recall any warning to keep silent, though he said that did not surprise him and that he had no reason to doubt Torres's account. On the essentials, however, he confirmed it: 'we did chase UFO's that evening'. The controller told him the unknowns 'were changing speed and altitude quite frequently', and the low bogey he himself was vectored onto 'either accelerated or descended and the controller lost him'.
