29:T575,The dispatcher then phoned the National Weather Service office at Muskegon County Airport, where a single operator was on duty (according to WWMT, Jack Bushong). At first the operator says he sees nothing out of the ordinary (the automatic transcript gives 'ground fire', probably ground clutter). Then, with the radar on manual, he tracks a return at about 6,000 feet, then 12,000, and begins to see several: three, in a triangle and about 50 kilometres apart, over South Haven, over the lake north-west of Benton Harbor and near Decatur, plus another in Berrien County. 'These are huge returns. I've never seen anything like this, not even when I'm doing storms; these aren't storms, they're just popping up all over the place,' he says. In the final part he says that, sweep after sweep, the returns are in the same position and then suddenly pop up somewhere else, 'so it's instantaneous', and that they are moving south toward Chicago. The Chicago Tribune reproduced a year later his summary to the police: three and sometimes four returns that were not aircraft, because an aircraft shows up as a pinpoint on the scope and these were the size of half a thumbnail, between 5,000 and 12,000 feet, three of them toward Chicago. On the recording the operator also says he does not know if it is 'some kind of atmospheric phenomenon' and that he has been told the northern lights are very bright.2a:T641,In the following days there were reports in forty-two Michigan counties, as late as 10:30 p.m. on March 11, and UFO researchers spoke of more than three hundred witnesses, according to the Tribune of March 13, 1995. That article says the researchers ruled out a weather balloon, a small plane, marsh gas, an advertising blimp, a satellite, a shooting star, a helicopter, space debris and a military aircraft, and that MUFON interviewed dozens of witnesses and obtained the radar computer data from that night, which it was still analyzing. Several witnesses shot videotape, but, in the words of Shirley Tafoya, of MUFON's Michigan leadership, 'in the video, all you see are these lights darting about.' The same article carries opposing opinions. Michael Swords, of Western Michigan University, said 'the radar returns were definitely anomalous, completely unexplained.'
