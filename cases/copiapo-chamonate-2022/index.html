Chile.Copiapó · three airline crews see two slow lights to the south

On the night of November 7–8, 2022, the crews of three airline flights heading south to Santiago from Iquique, Calama and Antofagasta told Iquique control they had in sight, to the south, two lights moving slowly, some 40 miles away; one crew described them as "very large". Radar recorded nothing. SEFAA, Chile's official office for these phenomena, considered they could be the rocket and the Cygnus NG-18 cargo craft, launched on the 7th, but without images it closed the case as a "Report". By this entry's own calculation, both were in Earth's shadow and could only have been seen with their engines firing.

What happened

At 03:27 UTC on Tuesday, November 8, 2022, the crew of a JetSMART Airbus flying from Iquique to Santiago asked the Iquique Area Control Center whether there was traffic to the south of its position. When told there was none, it reported having in sight to the south, some 40 miles away by its estimate, two lights that were moving slowly and that it had been watching for a while. At 03:57 UTC, the crew of a Sky Airline Airbus coming from Calama, at 39,000 feet, reported the same lights, but this time said they were very large and that, in its judgment, they were level with Santiago, more than 700 kilometers away. At almost the same moment, the crew of a second JetSMART Airbus, coming from Antofagasta and about 60 miles northeast of Chamonate aerodrome, also at 39,000 feet, said it had them in sight. The report by SEFAA, the anomalous aerial phenomena section of Chile's Directorate General of Civil Aviation, describes what was seen as two white lights, with no size given, visible to the south from overhead Chamonate, the Copiapó aerodrome, by aircraft flying between 35,000 and 39,000 feet; its summary speaks of one to three lights.

The office had the synchronized radar video from 03:30 to 04:09 UTC and no images from the witnesses. It identified the aircraft: a JetSMART Airbus 321 from Iquique to Santiago, a JetSMART Airbus 320 from Antofagasta to Santiago and a Sky Airline Airbus 320 from Calama to Santiago, flight SKY253. It placed SKY253, when it spoke to Iquique, at 39,000 feet at 26° 38′ S, 70° 21′ W, and the JetSMART flight from Antofagasta, one minute later, on heading 184°, at 26° 45′ S, 70° 22′ W. It checked the traffic to the south: the only aircraft was another JetSMART Airbus 321, from Lima to Santiago, which at 03:56 UTC was at 30° 53′ S, at 33,000 feet, too far away and too low for its navigation lights to match what was described. Its summary was that the pilots may have seen one or several luminous objects, very far away and above their flight levels, and that the radars did not record them.

It then looked for space launches in the register of the United Nations Office for Outer Space Affairs. On November 7 there was only one: a Northrop Grumman Antares rocket launched from Wallops Island, in the United States, the cargo craft S.S. Sally Ride, of the Cygnus NG-18 mission, international designator 2022-149A, bound for the International Space Station, which it reached on the 9th. The report notes that the craft can restart its engines and quotes Northrop Grumman on the rocket's difficulties during orbital insertion: "During a stage separation event of the rocket, debris from an Antares acoustic blanket lodged in one of the Cygnus solar array mechanisms, which prevented it from opening". Its conclusion, with a resolution date of August 20, 2024, says that "the conditions existed" for what was seen to be "the launcher and spacecraft of launch 2022-149", which, being beyond the reach of the radars, could not be recorded from the ground, but that, lacking images from the witnesses, the investigation had to be closed as a "Report" (Relato).

Why it matters

Three crews in three different aircraft, on flights identified by the report itself, spoke by radio with Iquique control about the same lights to the south over half an hour, and radar recorded where each aircraft was. They were not wholly independent, because they shared a frequency and the second and third calls came after the first; and they do not agree on the essentials: one crew saw two slow lights some 40 miles away and another thought them very large and level with Santiago. What is missing is what would allow them to be identified: no image, no radar record of the lights and no precise direction or elevation in the sky.

The explanation SEFAA proposes has a difficulty the report does not address, and which follows from its own timeline. According to Jonathan McDowell's satellite catalog, the Cygnus craft separated at 10:41 UTC on November 7, about 17 hours before the first call, into an orbit of 227 to 332 kilometers, and the Antares second stage was left in one of 162 to 272 kilometers, from which it re-entered on November 9. By this entry's own calculation, at 03:27 UTC the Sun was about 44° below the horizon in the Copiapó area, toward the south-southwest, and at that depth no object at the height of the Cygnus or the stage, below some 330 kilometers, that was visible above the horizon could be lit by the Sun: Earth's shadow covered it. Only something considerably higher, above some 500 kilometers, could be. For the craft or the stage to be seen, they would have had to be firing their engines at that moment and in that part of the sky; the report recalls that Cygnus can restart them, but it documents no burn at that hour and does not check that the craft passed over southern Chile. The full Moon was high in the north, on the opposite side from the lights.

The report also has internal inconsistencies. It calls the first flight JAT011 and JAT111, the one from Antofagasta JAT029 and JAT209, and the one from Calama SKU, SKY253 or by its level, FL390. It dates the event at 01:27 local time, but in November 2022 Chile was on summer time, three hours behind UTC, so 03:27 UTC was 00:27 local, by this entry's own conversion. And it places SKY253 between Copiapó and Vallenar at coordinates, 26° 38′ S, that lie north of Copiapó, between that city and Chañaral. Of the JetSMART flight from Antofagasta it says it was "approximately 60 miles northeast" of Chamonate, but the coordinates it gives put it about 33 miles to the north.

The event took place in the night corridor of northern Chile along which flights from Iquique, Calama and Antofagasta head south to Santiago, which appears in other SEFAA reports (↗, ↗).
