🇨🇱1974·Pampa Soledad, between Oficina Victoria and Quillagua (Antofagasta)

Chile.Pampa Soledad · five performers say lights followed them

In the early hours of August 16, 1974, five Chilean popular artists, among them folk singer Tito Fernández "El Temucano," were driving from the Oficina Victoria toward Antofagasta when, at Pampa Soledad, lights followed them, among them an intense white one, and they ended up merging into a single light; Fernández said he saw a figure more than two meters tall beside the light. They fled at over 130 km/h; a truck driver also said he saw the lights. It stopped at a Carabineros post in Quillagua, where they spent the night. Fernández later wrote "El mensaje de Sirio," about an alleged telepathic communication with the entity.

What happened

In the early hours of August 16, 1974, five Chilean popular artists (folk singer Héctor "Tito" Fernández, known as "El Temucano"; Marcelo Hernández, creator of the television show "Cachureos"; comedian Jorge Cruz; and singers Gloria Benavides and Patricia Chávez) were driving back to Antofagasta in a Volvo after a performance at the Oficina Victoria, a nitrate works in northern Chile. Chile had been under curfew since the 1973 coup, and the artists carried a safe-conduct pass so they would not be stopped (according to a 2020 article in the newspaper La Voz del Norte). The stretch, known as Pampa Soledad, is an almost featureless desert between the works and the town of Quillagua, crossed that moonless night between 1:30 and 3:00 a.m.

According to the witnesses, Hernández, who was driving at that moment, noticed a fixed light beside the road, which Fernández compared to a light bulb; they thought it might be a military patrol. As they advanced, the light stayed aligned with the car: "it's always in the same place, as if it were moving with us," Hernández said. It then vanished and, minutes later, a very intense white light appeared behind the car, lighting up the road and the inside of the vehicle; Cruz put it at about 300 meters and compared it to the flashes of arc welding. Hernández stopped to let it pass and the light stopped too. Chávez then saw a second light almost at sand level, as bright as the first, and when the car stopped again, the lights stopped once more.

At some point Jorge Cruz pointed out that someone was inside or beside the light, and the group panicked. According to Fernández, quoted by La Voz del Norte, he found himself face to face with a figure more than two meters tall, with a body "of pure light," no face or details, that made a gesture of placing what he took to be an arm over his head without touching him. According to La Voz del Norte, the pursuit had lasted more than twenty minutes, and several of the travelers were crying with fear, when a truck approached head-on; the artists told the driver that lights were following them and he answered: "twenty years I've been doing this and I'd never seen anything like it."

According to the same paper, the truck driver's assistant flashed the truck's headlights three times and the lights following them merged into one. The group fled toward the Quillagua customs post at over 130 km/h, according to the researcher Scott Corrales (who told the case in English in 2011 on Inexplicata, the blog of the Instituto de Ufología Hispana), with the lights always behind them. On reaching the Carabineros post at the customs checkpoint, the phenomenon stopped; the carabinero on duty asked what was happening and the five stayed there until dawn. A taxi arrived a while later with the musicians who accompanied them on the tour, who, according to La Voz del Norte, had also seen the lights.

The sources do not agree on the order of events. La Voz del Norte says the truck driver fled with the artists; Corrales says they left him behind although he begged not to be left alone, and that he reached the Carabineros post minutes later saying the lights were following him too. The Cine y Literatura article (2018), which follows Fernández's diary, and Corrales both say the group got out of the car to look at the light; for Cine y Literatura it was about 100 meters away, the second light was yellow, and it was Cruz who suggested using the truck's headlights. Corrales describes an orange light approaching, then two, which merged in response to the flashes of the truck's high beams.

Years later, Tito Fernández, by then an established figure in Chilean folk music, recounted beginning to receive communications he interpreted as telepathic, attributed to the entity seen that night and to a declared origin in the Sirius B system. He published these experiences in the book "El mensaje de Sirio" and later founded a center for metaphysical studies. The case was revisited in 1999-2000 by "Ovni," a 25-episode documentary series by Televisión Nacional de Chile (TVN), which interviewed several of the original witnesses.

Why it matters

The five artists agree on the essentials (the lights following them, the flight and the refuge at Quillagua), although the published versions differ on the order of events and on details such as the distance or the color of the lights. Others said they saw them too: the truck driver and, according to La Voz del Norte, the musicians who arrived later by taxi. The truck driver does not count as an independent witness, because he saw them after the artists had already told him what was happening; of the musicians, all that is known is what that paper says. There is no physical evidence, medical examination, or contemporaneous instrumental record: all that exists is nighttime visual perception on a featureless desert stretch.

The linked sources on the episode are retrospective: the 1999-2000 TVN series and articles published between 2011 and 2023, one of them based on Fernández's diary. None proposes a cause. Fernández's later account of telepathic messages cannot be separated from what the group said they saw that night.
