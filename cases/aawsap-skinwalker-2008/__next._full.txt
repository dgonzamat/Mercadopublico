31:T52f,In September 2008 the Department of Defense awards Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies (BAASS) contract HHM402-08-C-0072 through the Defense Intelligence Agency, with a total award amount of USD 21,948,810 split between a base year and an option year. The formal program is called the Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program (AAWSAP); its DIA owner is Dr. James Lacatski, and its stated mandate is to study advanced aerospace threats with non-conventional propulsion and signatures. The main field site is Skinwalker Ranch: BAASS installs scientific instrumentation and a permanent team that documents incidents and gathers testimony, while the contract produces 38 Defense Intelligence Reference Documents (DIRDs), commissioned from outside scientists, on topics such as warp drive, metric engineering, negative energy, field effects on human tissue, positron propulsion and metamaterials. What came next is disputed. According to its participants, the work continued under the name AATIP, managed inside the Pentagon by Luis Elizondo until 2017; according to AARO's historical report, the names AAWSAP and AATIP were used interchangeably, AATIP "was never an official DoD program", and after AAWSAP was cancelled the name was used by an informal community of interest with no dedicated personnel or budget.32:T857,The program becomes public on December 16, 2017 when the New York Times publishes Helene Cooper, Ralph Blumenthal and Leslie Kean's investigation revealing AATIP and the military videos Tic Tac, GIMBAL and GOFAST. Between 2018 and 2020, and in a later FOIA response of March 2022, 37 of the 38 DIRDs are partially declassified, via The Black Vault and other requests. The remaining one never came out: per the documentation of those responses it is a classified version of one of the other documents, not a separate topic. In 2021 Lacatski, Kelleher and Knapp publish 'Skinwalkers at the Pentagon' telling the program from inside. In 2022 the Pentagon creates AARO (All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office), inheriting archive and mandate. In March 2024 AARO publishes the Historical Record Report (Volume 1), which explicitly reviews AAWSAP/AATIP and concludes (disputed by several participants) that it found no evidence of non-human technology or reverse-engineering programs. On the program, the report adds four things: that investigating UFOs was not in the statement of work and DIA "did not seek, nor specifically authorize" that work or the paranormal work at the ranch, though one of its employees set up and managed the contract; that on 24 June 2009 Reid asked for it to be made a special access program and Deputy Secretary Lynn declined on Clapper's recommendation; that the papers produced "were never thoroughly peer reviewed" and AARO found no other substantive case work beyond reviewing Blue Book and private case files and interviewing witnesses; and that, shortly before cancellation, the company proposed a campaign of "intellectual debates" starting from the assumption that "E.T. visitations are true". AARO dates the program's end to 2012, "upon the completion of its deliverables", whereas the published contract closes its period of performance on 21 December 2010.
