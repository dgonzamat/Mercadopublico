🇷🇸1954·Belgrade, Yugoslavia (now Serbia)

Yugoslavia (Serbia).Belgrade · glowing spheres cross the sky in the early morning

In the early hours of Monday, October 25, 1954, according to the press of the time, large glowing spheres trailing green were seen over Zagreb, Belgrade and other cities in Yugoslavia, and around six in the morning Belgrade residents saw strange objects at great height. No known account names a witness. The office of the U.S. air attaché in Belgrade sent the translated articles in a report dated November 24, and Project Blue Book filed it as insufficient data for evaluation, noting that the rash followed publicity in the French press.

What happened

Early on Monday, October 25, 1954, glowing objects that the press called flying saucers were seen in the sky over several cities in Yugoslavia. A dispatch datelined Belgrade that day, which the French daily Le Berry Républicain printed on the 26th, summarized a report in the Belgrade newspaper Večernje novosti: early that morning, large flying spheres trailing a fluorescent green wake had been seen over Zagreb, Belgrade and other places. The dispatch added that around six in the morning Belgrade residents had seen strange objects at a very great height. Neither that report nor any other known account gives a witness's name.

On the 27th, the Paris daily La Croix reported that the Yugoslav press and radio had told of many objects flying high and fast, often in formation. The same day brought similar accounts from neighboring countries. According to the dispatches the two French papers carried, thousands of people saw objects over Vienna, Graz and Burgenland in Austria; the Vienna weather station said the one over the capital was not a balloon but an object with a plume of flames that crossed from west to east without a sound. In Trieste, according to the Associated Press in the daily Le Provençal, about twenty people saw a disk that morning at great height, heading northeast and leaving a glowing trail.

The most detailed version of the Yugoslav event came much later. In 2021, the journalist Dragan Vujičić published a serial on UFOs in Yugoslavia in Večernje novosti that draws on what Jovan Knežević, then the paper's correspondent in Split, wrote down; the serial does not say when he wrote it. According to that account, almost every daily in the country ran the story on October 26. Several hundred people reportedly saw the objects over Ljubljana, Celje, Zagreb, Sarajevo, Šabac, Belgrade and other towns, Seven objects were reportedly counted over Šabac and Osijek, the total for the wave, and four over Belgrade. According to the serial, they flew level, at about 3,000 meters and about 5,000 km/h. They gave off a strong orange light, made no sound, sometimes left a trail like glowing gas, and crossed each city in five or six seconds.

The serial lists the air traffic controllers at Zemun airfield in Belgrade among the witnesses, without naming them, and reconstructs two groups of objects that went on toward Bulgaria, one arriving from the Adriatic and the other from Hungary or Austria. It warns that the time of each sighting cannot be fixed, because clocks did not agree and each pass was very short. According to the same text, the Yugoslav Air Force command told reporters that it knew of the phenomenon and was studying it, but no official statement was ever issued. The date of the first press report does not match the French dispatch, which was datelined Belgrade on the 25th and already cited Večernje novosti: either the story was printed that same day, or the serial has the date wrong.

A 2011 article by the Croatian journalist Kristian Benić, on the news site tportal.hr, adds an account from Split in the same October, without giving the day. The head of a section at the post office saw a glowing object one morning as he passed the Hajduk stadium; it moved in odd directions and at varying speed, and other residents confirmed seeing a strange light. Benić writes that similar news came that day from Zagreb and Belgrade, where people spoke of fireballs, and that the popular explanation even then was weather balloons. He does not cite his source.

A month later, the office of the U.S. air attaché in Belgrade sent report IR-793-54, dated November 24, 1954, which reached the Air Technical Intelligence Center (ATIC). According to the abstract kept in Project Blue Book, the Air Force program that studied sightings, the report contained translated newspaper articles on a rash of sightings over Yugoslavia in late October, and the increase had been noted right after the French press gave the subject a great deal of publicity. France had been living through its own wave since September, including the landing a worker reported at Quarouble ↗ Quarouble.

The case's record card, on a 1963 form, gives Belgrade as the place, newspapers as the source and, as the conclusion, that the data were not enough for an evaluation. The digitized copy holds only those two sheets, without the translated articles.

Why it matters

The case shows how a wave in a communist country that had broken with Moscow in 1948 reached the U.S. Air Force: not through pilots' reports or radar records, but, according to the report's abstract, through the local press, translated by the air attaché's office. What survives in the archive is a record card and a one-page abstract, with no named witness, no exact time and no number of objects, and Blue Book's conclusion says only that nothing could be evaluated from it.

The Blue Book note about the French press says only that the rash came right after; if that explains anything, it is the number of reports, not what was seen. The idea it suggests is that a widely covered wave in one country leads people in others to watch the sky and report what they would once have ignored. It does not explain why the accounts converge on a single day, October 25, in Yugoslavia, Austria and Trieste, and on the same morning in Belgrade and Trieste. For Italian accounts from that same day, of red and green objects with trails, the French researcher Patrick Gross, who transcribed and translated that press coverage, notes that they are obvious descriptions of meteors. No such analysis of the Yugoslav accounts has been published, nor any astronomical study of that morning.

The accounts do not agree with each other either. The 1954 dispatch speaks of spheres with a green trail, and the 2021 serial of orange light; La Croix had already mentioned formations in 1954. The 2011 article describes an object changing direction over Split. The precise figures, such as the 5,000 km/h and the 3,000 meters, come from the serial, which does not identify the 1954 articles they are drawn from, and they cannot be checked against the clippings the attaché translated, because the published copy does not include them. What is documented is the press coverage, not the observation, in the same week that a soccer match was halted in Florence ↗ Florence.
