Netherlands.Soesterberg · twelve servicemen see a lit triangle over the base

On February 3, 1979, at about 5:45 in the morning, twelve servicemen of the military guard service at Soesterberg air base (Netherlands), where U.S. personnel were stationed during the Cold War, saw a large triangular object over the runway, with three bright lights at the front and a red one at the rear. The Dutch Air Force stated that, apart from a mirage, there had been nothing to see (a temperature inversion would have mirrored car headlights). The sources mention no radar record, and almost all the accounts were gathered in a 2023 documentary.

What happened

On February 3, 1979, at about 5:45 a.m., twelve servicemen of the military guard service at Soesterberg air base (Vliegbasis Soesterberg), a Dutch base on the defense line against the Soviets where U.S. personnel were stationed, saw a large triangular object appear over the runway, with three bright lights at the front and a red one at the rear. They were on night duty (Defensiekrant). The object passed over the base twice and moved off; RTV Utrecht describes it as an enormous black triangle that, after crossing the base, shot away like a bullet from a pistol.

RTV Utrecht speaks of "at least twelve" servicemen and quotes a sentence from a 1979 newspaper, without naming it: "the sentries posted around the field did not know what they saw when they saw something". The detailed witness accounts the sources cite were gathered decades later, for the documentary "De ufo's van Soesterberg" by filmmaker Bram Roza, which premiered in November 2023; Roza took five years to finish it and funded it with 20,000 euros raised by crowdfunding. According to him, the servicemen were ridiculed by their colleagues and at home, and one of those he tracked down told him he would call the police if Roza approached him again. Roza says it is claimed that reports on the sightings were made, but that he has been unable to obtain them; he also says everything about Soesterberg during the Cold War is secret (Defensiekrant, 2024).

Wilmar Taal, who reviewed the documentary in the magazine Skepter, asks how much a witness can correctly remember after forty years, and reports that one of the twelve, present at a screening in December 2023, told him he had seen two lights, not a triangle. Roza says he first thought of a U.S. spy plane and ruled it out: the first test flight of the F-117 came years later and that aircraft was much smaller, and the B-2, which is that size, appeared in the late 1980s. He adds that, if the Americans were far ahead of what is believed, there is still no aircraft today that can fly so low without making a sound. Roza also mentions the hypothesis that UFOs are drawn to nuclear weapons, and Defensiekrant's own piece notes that there is no proof nuclear weapons were at Soesterberg.

Roza adds other observations from 1979 near the base: he says a woman saw the same triangle pass over her swimming pool in Amersfoort, and in the documentary a witness says she saw a UFO over the Leusderheide while riding a horse. Taal writes that the latter's description seems taken from the film "Close Encounters of the Third Kind", then showing in Dutch cinemas.

According to Skepter, the Dutch Air Force's official explanation was that lights may have been visible because of a temperature inversion, in which the glow of car headlights in the hilly landscape would have been mirrored on colder air layers. According to RTV Utrecht, the Air Force stated that, apart from a mirage, there had been nothing to see, and in 1979 the servicemen's story was not believed or was ridiculed. In October 1980, a column in the daily De Waarheid reported that the civilian bureau NOVOBO said it had traced back to an aircraft "that sensational report over Soesterberg airfield a year or so ago", though it did not know what kind or type, so it still counted as "an original UFO". The column gives neither the date of the event nor the number of witnesses, and does not say whether it refers to the sighting of February 3, 1979.

Why it matters

The event brings together twelve servicemen on guard at an active air base who describe a large object with orderly lights over the runway, and an Air Force that answered with an official explanation of a mirage. The detailed accounts were gathered decades later, and one of the twelve recalls two lights rather than a triangle.

The sources mention no radar record and no recovered material, and Roza has not been able to obtain the reports that are said to have been made.
