🇮🇱2021·Haifa, Israel

Israel.Haifa · a point of light filmed crossing the sky before dawn

At around 5:11 a.m. on May 9, 2021 (02:11 UTC), someone in Haifa filmed a point of light crossing the sky before dawn and posted the video on X as the core stage of China's Long March 5B rocket, which was falling uncontrolled ten days after launching the Tianhe module. Videos from Al Qurayyat, in Saudi Arabia, and from Oman also circulated in that half hour. The U.S. military placed the reentry over the Arabian Peninsula at around 02:14 UTC, and China gave 02:24 UTC as the time of its reentry, with a fall zone in the Indian Ocean near the Maldives.

What happened

On April 29, 2021, a Chinese Long March 5B rocket lifted off from Wenchang, on Hainan Island, carrying Tianhe, the core module of China's space station. According to astrophysicist Jonathan McDowell, of the Harvard-Smithsonian Center for Astrophysics, who publishes the newsletter Jonathan's Space Report and the GCAT catalog of space objects, the module separated at 03:31 UTC and left the rocket's core stage in orbit, empty and weighing about 21 tons, between 170 and 375 kilometers up. The stage had no way to steer its fall. For ten days, drag from the air slowed it down, and space agencies and satellite trackers published predictions of when and where it would come down.

Before dawn on Sunday, May 9, on its last orbit, the stage crossed the Middle East from northwest to southeast. At 02:11:32 UTC, 5:11 a.m. in Israel and Jordan, the X account @nadavision wrote in capital letters that it had just seen it over Israel: "HARD TO SEE ON VIDEO BUT IT WAS HUGE AND VERY VERY LOW VISIBLE FIRE." That account is now private, and its text is known from the Jerusalem Post, which quoted it that morning with a warning that the details were unconfirmed. At 02:17 UTC, the account of the online newspaper Al Bawaba (@albwaabh) posted a 12-second clip that, according to its text, was circulating on social media and showed the debris of the Chinese rocket passing over Al Qurayyat, a Saudi town near the border with Jordan. It shows a point of light above some palm trees, in a blue dawn sky.

At 02:40 UTC, another anonymous account, @CYA90930064, posted a 30-second video addressed to McDowell: "#CZ5B Confirmed sighting from Haifa Israel opprox 2:11 UTC." It added that the object had come "a little bit early and northern than predicted" and that the bright object on the left was Jupiter. The word "confirmed" is the poster's own, and the post does not say who confirmed it; the name of the person who filmed it does not appear in the post. The video shows a dark blue sky, with trees and lit buildings below, and a camera moving to follow a faint point of light.

At 02:42 UTC, a third account, @0_F_0, posted a clip of just over four seconds with the text "Chinese Rocket while crossing the skies of #Oman." It was filmed in daylight, above some rooftops, and shows a short white streak in the sky. McDowell quoted it shortly afterward: "Claimed observation from Oman which if genuine would imply the reentry was underway with impact over the Indian Ocean." Two hours later, the International Astronomical Center (IAC), based in Abu Dhabi, shared the same clip as a video from Oman showing "the beginning of its entry into the atmosphere." According to Sky News Arabia, the center also posted a video of about 42 seconds filmed in Amman, the Jordanian capital, by a member of its satellite reentry tracking program.

At 04:04 UTC, Space-Track, the site through which the U.S. Space Force publishes its tracking data, announced that the 18th Space Control Squadron confirmed the stage's reentry at 02:14 UTC. The coordinates it gave are inside Saudi Arabia, even though the message wrote them in the same sentence as the fall into the Indian Ocean north of the Maldives. Read alongside McDowell's newsletter, which puts the reentry over Saudi Arabia and the fall in the ocean, the coordinates are those of the reentry. U.S. Space Command stated that the stage had reentered over the Arabian Peninsula at about 02:15 UTC and that it was unknown whether the debris had hit land or water.

The China Manned Space Engineering Office gave a different time and place: the remains reentered at 02:24 UTC, 10:24 a.m. in Beijing, and fell into the sea around a point near the Maldives, after almost entirely burning up. That day, Lassina Zerbo, executive secretary of the Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty Organization, reported that one of the organization's infrasound stations, in Djibouti, had picked up a signal consistent with the reentry over the Arabian Peninsula. The next day, McDowell wrote in his newsletter that the stage reentered over Saudi Arabia at around 02:14 UTC, just after passing over Riyadh, and that the debris fell near the Maldives about ten minutes later.

Why it matters

The times of the videos match the path. By a calculation made for this case, the stage passed about 125 kilometers from Haifa at around 02:11 UTC, and about 90 from Amman and about 100 from Al Qurayyat in that same minute. The calculation assumes a circular orbit with the inclination given in GCAT, 41.5 degrees, passing over Riyadh at 02:14, the time at which McDowell places the reentry, just after that pass. That path passes about 35 kilometers from the fall point China gave and about 20 from the 18th Squadron's position. It reaches that point a minute later than the squadron's time, though; if the calculation is adjusted to that time, the stage passes near Haifa at 02:10 rather than 02:11. Both times from Haifa, the one written by the person who posted the video and that of the @nadavision message at 02:11:32, fit within that one-minute difference.

What the Haifa video shows also fits. At 5:11 a.m. in Haifa the Sun was about seven and a half degrees below the horizon and the sky was starting to brighten. By the same calculation, at that time the Earth's shadow reached only about 60 to 70 kilometers up beneath the path, so the stage, still in orbit and higher than that, was lit by the Sun, like the satellites seen at dawn. Seen from Haifa, it crossed the sky from west to southeast and sank below Jupiter, which stood about 33 degrees above the southeastern horizon: this fits the bright planet on the left that the poster mentioned. Dutch satellite tracker Marco Langbroek, who published the final path on his blog, wrote that the imagery from Jordan suggested the object was still intact when it passed over there.

The Oman clip fits less well. By the same calculation, the stage crossed southern Oman, over the Dhofar region, at around 02:16 UTC; it was 6:16 a.m. there and the Sun was already about ten degrees above the horizon in Muscat. The path passed about 200 kilometers from Salalah and about 650 from Muscat, and the clip does not say where in Oman it was filmed. If the video was genuine, Langbroek wrote, it would suggest the stage was starting to break up over southern Arabia; he and McDowell treated it as unverified. The IAC wrote that, according to U.S. sources, the stage entered the atmosphere at 02:14 while passing over Oman, but the position Space-Track gave for that time is in Saudi Arabia, and by the calculation the stage reached Oman about two minutes later.

The two official times differ by ten minutes. The American one, 02:14 to 02:15, places the entry into the atmosphere over Arabia; the Chinese one, 02:24, comes with a fall zone at sea, more than 3,000 kilometers away. McDowell's newsletter fits them into a single sequence: the stage reentered over Saudi Arabia and its debris fell near the Maldives about ten minutes later; Zerbo described the same from the Djibouti infrasound station's signal. The GCAT catalog combines the two sources, taking the U.S. time and the Chinese location, and records the fall at 02:14 with its destination at 72.5° E, 2.7° N. The videos from Haifa, Al Qurayyat and Oman were posted in the half hour after the pass of a known rocket stage whose fall agencies in several countries were tracking.
