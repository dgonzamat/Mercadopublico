15:T5aa,What sets the case apart is that it was set down in an operational document. That same day the tower transmitted an Accident and/or Incident Report (RAI) over the aeronautical network, which CORPAC's AIS-ARO team —the Corporación Peruana de Aeropuertos y Aviación Comercial, the state company that provides air traffic control— stamped as received on 27 February. Its description field says that from the tower 'apparently two UFOs' are observed 'west of the station approx 8000FT', that one of them 'appears intermittently on the radar screen at 5NM RDL274' (five nautical miles on the 274 radial, almost due west) and that the area control center supervisor was notified; the additional-information field notes that at 07:12 UTC the objects were no longer in view of the tower. That is about forty minutes, too long for a meteor or a fleeting flash, in Peru's most closely watched airspace. The report leaked onto social media within days, and ufologist Anthony Choy presented it on radio as the first official report of its kind in the country, although he placed the objects 'above runway 33', which the document does not say: it puts them five miles out, over the sea. It should be read on its own terms: it is the standard incident form, written by the controller on duty, and the word 'UFO' there means what it means in aviation, an object the tower did not identify; the operator, registration and route fields read TBN, to be notified.16:T462,) received the reports through the Center for Aerospace Interests (CINAE). The case came to light in June 2019, when CNN en Español reported that the FAP had confirmed it for the first time, in a verification it attributed to the Directorate of Aerospace Information and Interests (DINIA): it quoted Major General Robert Baxerias Vucanovich, who said the occurrence did happen and that air operations were never at risk, and Marco A. Barraza, presented as the office's lead investigator, who spoke of two objects over the sea, near San Lorenzo Island, and of a confirmation on the radar screen. Barraza was not an FAP officer: at the 2013 relaunch he was listed on DIFAA's civilian advisory board as a researcher and communicator. The next day the FAP issued a statement specifying that it had not confirmed any UFO sighting, only the existence of reports submitted for analysis, and that as an institution it could only speak to duly verified facts.
