Peru.Lima · tower and two flight crews see two lights over the sea

On February 27, 2019, around 1:30 a.m., the tower at Lima's Jorge Chávez airport saw to the west, over the sea, “apparently two UFOs” at about 8,000 feet, and the crews of two flights waiting to take off (LANPERÚ 2437 and LATAM ECUADOR 1442) confirmed they had them in sight. One showed up intermittently on the radar screen at 5 nautical miles, and by 07:12 UTC they were no longer visible. The Peruvian Air Force office that studies these phenomena (DIFAA) received the reports; a civilian adviser told CNN of a radar confirmation, but the Air Force stated that it had not confirmed any UFOs.

What happened

In the early hours of February 27, 2019, at 06:30 UTC (1:30 a.m. in Lima), the control tower of Jorge Chávez International Airport, in Callao, observed to the west of the station, out over the sea, two objects it could not identify, at about 8,000 feet (some 2,400 meters). The tower alerted two aircraft waiting to take off, LANPERÚ 2437 (an A321 on a flight plan to Córdoba, Argentina, holding short of runway 15) and LATAM ECUADOR 1442 (an A319 bound for Quito, holding short of runway 33), and both crews reported having them in sight.

The sighting was set down that same day in an operational document: an Accident and/or Incident Report (RAI) that the tower transmitted over the aeronautical network and that the aeronautical information team (AIS-ARO) of CORPAC (the Corporación Peruana de Aeropuertos y Aviación Comercial, the state company that provides air traffic control) stamped as received on February 27. It says that from the tower "apparently two UFOs" are observed "west of the station approx 8000FT", that one of them "appears intermittently on the radar screen at 5NM RDL274" (five nautical miles in the 274° direction, almost due west) and that the area control center supervisor was notified; it adds that at 07:12 UTC the objects were no longer in view of the tower. That is about forty minutes, too long for a meteor or a fleeting flash, in Peru's most closely watched airspace.

In that incident form, written by the controller on duty, the word "UFO" means what it means in aviation: an object the tower did not identify. The report does not describe the objects' shape, color, brightness or motion: those details ("very luminous", "at San Lorenzo Island") appear later, in the press and in the DIFAA adviser's account. The document leaked onto social media within days, and ufologist Anthony Choy presented it on radio as the first official report of its kind in the country, although he placed the objects "above runway 33", which the document does not say: it puts them five miles out, over the sea. According to Panamericana, citing Radio Capital, the lights were also seen from the terminal itself and photographs circulated, and two videos attributed to the sighting were posted on YouTube. Nobody has analyzed them.

The Peruvian Air Force's (FAP) Department for the Investigation of Anomalous Aerial Phenomena (DIFAA) (see ↗) received the reports through the Center for Aerospace Interests (CINAE). The case came to light in June 2019, when CNN en Español reported that the FAP had confirmed it for the first time, in a verification it attributed to the Directorate of Aerospace Information and Interests (DINIA). It quoted Major General Robert Baxerias Vucanovich, who said the occurrence did happen and that air operations were never at risk, and Marco A. Barraza, presented as the office's lead investigator, who spoke of two objects over the sea, near San Lorenzo Island, and of a confirmation on the radar screen. Barraza was not an FAP officer: at the 2013 relaunch he was listed on DIFAA's civilian advisory board as a researcher and communicator.

The next day the FAP issued a statement specifying that it had not confirmed any UFO sighting, only the existence of reports submitted for analysis, and that as an institution it could only speak to duly verified facts. Baxerias told Perú21 that his institution never spoke of a UFO and that what was seen could have been a kite, one of Google's Project Loon balloons or debris in the air.

Why it matters

Two unidentified objects were seen at the same time by the control tower and by the crews of two airliners, and one of them showed up intermittently on the tower's radar. The controller put it in writing that same night, and the Air Force office that studies these phenomena received the reports. What the case lacks is the institutional confirmation the press credited it with: the FAP denied it by statement the next day, and its own major general, Baxerias, offered conventional explanations (a kite, a Loon balloon, debris in the air), though with no published analysis behind them.

None of this proves what they were. Two objects seen at night over the sea, with no shape or motion described, admit several conventional explanations that, as far as has been published, nobody ruled out: drones (increasingly common in 2019), balloons, vessels, reflections or low bright stars. The intermittent radar appearance does not decide it either: it may indicate a small object or one that reflects little of the radar's energy, but also a weak or false return.

By a position calculation made for this entry, there were no planets to the west at that hour (Jupiter and the Moon were in the east), but there were two of the brightest stars in the sky: Sirius, to the west-southwest, sinking from about 17° to about 7° in altitude between 06:30 and 07:12 UTC, and Procyon, almost due west (azimuth 282° to 279°), sinking from 26° to 16°. The direction matches, but the sky was clear (the airport's 06:00 and 07:00 UTC weather reports give CAVOK, good visibility and no low cloud) and at 07:12 Procyon was still 16° and Sirius 7° above the horizon, so the disappearance at that time does not fit setting stars well; nor do they explain the radar return at five miles.
