25:T505,The corpus framing is explicitly skeptical about the phenomenon. The National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine itself concluded a review and officially discredited the report as unprofessional and lacking scientific rigor: the processing and interpretation had been done at an inadequate level and with significant errors in determining the distances to the objects. The most cited skeptical analysis is by Harvard physicist Avi Loeb, who showed the problem is geometric: the authors likely overestimated distances by up to a factor of ten, and because speed and size are derived from that distance, the error inflates them spuriously. If the 'Phantoms' were ten times closer to the camera than assumed, their size and speed suddenly match those of an artillery shell —objects entirely expected over Kyiv in 2022. Other analysts (Skeptical Inquirer, Live Science, Snopes) agreed that what was observed is consistent with birds, insects, ordnance or nearby artifacts mistaken for distant, fast objects. What anchors the case is not the phenomenon —almost certainly mundane— but the complete institutional episode: a national academy observatory publishing a study dedicated to UAP, and that same academy issuing a public correction.
