4e:T6b3,What sets the case apart from a mere tale is its documentary trace for the period: González was treated at the Central Emergency Post for slight bruises, and duty officers Manuel Moreno and E. Domínguez, of the General Traffic Inspectorate, recorded the time the men arrived and that they showed no signs of intoxication or mental disturbance. The press of the time qualifies that picture in both directions: El Universal wrote that González suffered "a nervous crisis" and was "quite nervous", and specified that their breath did not smell of alcohol; a wire report from early December added that the psychiatrists who examined him found him in perfect health, although it already gave another version (a "zeppelin"-shaped craft, four dwarfs one metre ten tall and a helper named José Carrizales, aged 19). The episode is part of the great worldwide wave of close encounters of 1954 and remained one of the most cited humanoid files in Latin America. According to the summary in the URECAT catalogue, which draws on the Caracas press and on González Ganteaume but states that it lacks the El Universal article of 29 November, several neighbours stated that day that at the time of the encounter they had seen a very bright light pass over their houses, 'like a very intense star', and heard an explosion and a strange noise; Police and National Security patrols searched the area for the source of the explosion, and El Universal reported that numerous neighbours said they had seen an unidentified flying object.
