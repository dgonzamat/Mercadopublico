2a:T623,On the night of 11 November 1979, TAE (Trans Europa) flight JK-297, a Supercaravelle registered EC-CUM according to the military telex, was flying from Palma de Mallorca to Tenerife with passengers aboard, under Captain Francisco Javier Lerdo de Tejada (his name is blacked out in the file, but he made it public himself). From 20:27 Z the Madrid SAR centre had been receiving reports of a signal on 121.5, the emergency frequency, placed about 40 miles north-east of Valencia. At 22:02 Z Barcelona control asked JK-297 to listen on that frequency; the crew heard the signal but could not identify it. At 22:05 Z, 15 miles south of Ibiza and climbing from flight level 230 to 330, it reported «two signals, red lights» about three miles away «at ten o'clock» and at its own altitude. Barcelona replied that there was no traffic. According to the transcript of tape 34, the crew insisted: they were «two steady red lights, without flashing»; the «traffic» was climbing «much faster than us»; it was «less than half a mile» away; it dropped about 3,000 feet and came back to their level. At 22:09 Z the captain turned towards Valencia («I don't like continuing with that traffic, it is following me»); the controller noted that he had raised his voice considerably. He later stated that he never made out an object, only the two lights, whose spacing and precise movement made him think of a large aircraft. At 22:24 Z, descending to 4,000 feet, they lost sight of the lights and at the same moment the 121.5 signal stopped. They landed at Manises at 22:45 Z.2b:T7d8,When the aircraft arrived, airport staff saw three lights that stood out for their brightness; the main one, to the east-south-east (radial 120) about 20° up, flashed green, red and white and, in the file's own words, «followed the movement of the sky quite faithfully». At that hour Sirius was rising in the east-south-east (azimuth 116°–135°, altitude 5°–21°, computed with ephem), and colour scintillation is typical of a bright star near the horizon; the captain stated that those lights did not look like his. A Mirage F-1 took off from Los Llanos air base (Albacete), contacted the Pegaso operations centre at 23:42 Z and withdrew at 00:57 Z. The file qualifies the radar picture. The Combat Air Command (MACOM) report says Pegaso had the Caravelle's track «but no echo of the object», and that «the EVA 5 manages to make out with its height discriminator up to five echoes» between 9,000 and 11,000 feet, «losing contact on reaching the coastline because of clutter»; among its deductions it notes that, «except for the EVA 5, which makes out 5 echoes», «there was no radar signal», and that «radar lock-ons occurred on the interceptor on three occasions».
