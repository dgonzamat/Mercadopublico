496

🇧🇲1962·West end of Bermuda

Bermuda.Bermuda · radio station men watch a still white object

On August 18, 1962, in daylight and under a clear sky, the chief announcer and the owner of a Bermuda radio station, with another man from the company, saw above a radio tower under construction a very bright white egg-shaped object about 70 degrees up. It stayed still for 10 to 15 minutes and then went off to the east in 15 to 20 seconds. That other man, who did not see it leave, saw three dimmer objects heading west. Kindley Air Base found no balloons or other trajectories that could explain it, and Blue Book, the Air Force's UFO program, left it as unidentified.

What happened

On Saturday, August 18, 1962, in broad daylight, at the west end of Bermuda, the chief announcer and the owner of a radio station were looking at the top of a 150-foot radio tower under construction. It was about 2100 UTC, 5 p.m. on the islands by a calculation made for this case. They were standing about 80 feet from the base of the tower. The base's message records a clear sky throughout the observation and visibility of 9 miles, though it notes scattered clouds at several heights; the August 29 reply specifies that there were no clouds in the area where the object was.

Above the tower, a little to the right of their line of sight, they saw a white object with a very intense glow, not quite round, close to the shape of an egg, about 70 degrees above the horizon. The first description said it was the size of a football. It stayed still for 10 to 15 minutes, with no change of position relative to the top of the tower. While it was still its outlines were not sharp; when it moved, they were, and it looked white. When it began to move it went straight east and disappeared in about 15 or 20 seconds, at a speed they described as tremendous. It made no maneuvers. It was lost from sight without going behind clouds or down to the horizon.

Another man from the company, who had looked away, looked back when he was told the object was moving. He then saw three egg-shaped objects, dull white and not brilliant, going west, not in formation, considerably lower, which disappeared over the horizon in 10 to 15 seconds. They seemed to waver as they moved: they did not look like birds in flight, but their movement was not stable, though they held a westerly heading until they were lost from sight. According to the message, one of the witnesses did not see those three objects and another did not see the single object move off; because the names are blacked out, the message does not make it certain who is who or what each of the three men saw.

The plans and intelligence officer of the 1604th Air Base Wing at Kindley, a major, sent on August 20 the report that Air Force regulations require on unidentified flying objects. He addressed it to Air Defense Command, to Air Force Systems Command at Wright-Patterson and to Air Force headquarters. He noted that the information was reliable, that it had been witnessed by more than one person and that the investigation indicated "there were no balloons or other trajectories in the area that could explain these sightings."

The Foreign Technology Division at Wright-Patterson asked for clarifications on August 24, and Kindley replied on August 29. The witnesses were looking west; the object was at 70 degrees of altitude, "very very high," and was lost from sight without going behind clouds or down to the horizon. Compared with the sun or moon it was "much smaller, looked like aspirin," and the reply explains that one of the witnesses "did not understand question during previous interview, size is now firm, aspirin."

Of the three objects, the reply says they were somewhat larger than the single object and much closer, dull white with no reflection, and compares them to an F-86 fighter at an altitude where aircraft commonly leave vapor trails, that is, the apparent size of a fighter flying very high. The Project Blue Book record card closed the case as "Unknown" and gives a total duration of 20 minutes, somewhat more than the sum of the figures in the message.

Why it matters

What gives the case weight is the fixed reference: the object stayed still for 10 to 15 minutes right above the top of a tower, in broad daylight and with 9 miles of visibility, so the witnesses could check that it did not change position. That makes it more than a light glimpsed in passing, though it does not say what it was.

The size went from a football to an aspirin on re-questioning, and the correction shows how little a size estimate says without the distance: by a calculation made for this case, an aspirin at arm's length and a football about 50 feet away fill the same angle. The questions from Wright-Patterson pointed at the usual explanations (the sun or moon as a comparison, hiding behind clouds, vapor trails, a glare that hurts the eyes), and the answers add nothing that ties what was seen to a specific object.

What is missing is usual in these cases: no photograph, no radar, no instrument, and the witnesses' names blacked out in the copy. No one proposed a cause, but that does not rule out an ordinary explanation: similar cases that were resolved almost always ended up as balloons, birds, aircraft or satellites.
