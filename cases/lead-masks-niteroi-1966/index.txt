29:T4cd,The scene was as orderly as it was inexplicable. The two men lay side by side on the grass, dressed in formal suits and covered with raincoats, despite the heat. They had handmade lead masks —over their eyes, according to the Evening Standard's report; beside their heads, according to Bowen's summary—, and remnants of the lead the masks were made from were later found in Miguel's workshop, together with a book on scientific spiritualism with passages marked on masks, intense luminosity and accompanying spirits. The bodies showed no trauma or external injuries. Miguel had 157,000 cruzeiros in a plastic bag and Manoel only 4,000, so most of the money they set out with was missing, and there was no trace of the equipment they said they were going to buy; the police's first theory was robbery. Nearby a notebook appeared with a handwritten note in Portuguese: '16:30 be at the agreed place. 18:30 ingest capsules, after the effect protect metals, wait for the signal mask', which the English translations of the time render as «protect half the face with lead masks»; other notes carried simple electrical formulae. According to O Cruzeiro of 9 September, the handwriting of the note was not that of either dead man.2a:T6e3,The investigation hit serious obstacles. The bodies were already decomposing. According to the 1966 press, the autopsy found no signs of violence, no burns and no poisons in the internal organs, and did not establish the cause of the cardiac failures, so it was never determined whether they died poisoned. The Jornal do Brasil of 25 August published the account of Gracinda Barbosa Coutinho de Souza, who on the evening of 17 August, driving with three of her children, saw over the hilltop an orange oval object, with a ring of fire around the edge and rays in all directions, rising and falling for three or four minutes; after her statement, other people called the police to confirm the sighting. It also emerged that both men had a deep interest in spiritualism and paranormal phenomena, and belonged to a circle that expected to 'contact' superior entities. According to the 1966 press gathered by Brian Dunning, a friend of both, Elcio Gomes, told police the three had been interested in contacting Mars and that a device they had built in Manoel's garden had exploded about two months earlier; Gomes was arrested for contradictions in his statements, but it was established that he and other friends were in Campos when the two climbed the hill. Charles Bowen, of Flying Saucer Review, also reported that another television technician, named Hermes, had been found dead in 1962 on Morro do Cruzeiro with a lead mask beside him, a detail Dunning could not corroborate. The most accepted hypothesis combines that background with a ritual of ingesting substances —capsules— that would have gone wrong, with the masks as protection against an expected 'manifestation'.
