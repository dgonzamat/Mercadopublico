15:T508,Alongside the four videos the release carries a transcript of what the officer was saying as he filmed: document LLE-UAP-D001. It is one page, and in it he gives the date, the time, the mile marker and the interstate number — all redacted —, his direction of travel, westbound, which survives, and describes what he sees: 'I am watching another orb that is flying approximately... 300 feet up off the ground. I did check radar, there is no aircraft flying in the area.' Fifteen seconds later he adds: 'this has been flying stationary for about 15 minutes.' A minute in, as a large truck goes past: 'Okay, the craft is flying closer to me now. I can see it.' There the published transcript ends. The redactions carry the (b)(7)(A) and (b)(7)(C) exemptions of the Freedom of Information Act, the ones protecting ongoing law-enforcement proceedings and personal privacy; the preliminary note says it was edited 'to protect the identity of the source, as well as law enforcement activities.' On the four video records AARO adds the officer's own description of the phenomenon: 'silent' and featuring 'red, blue, and green flashing lights.' The office also altered the footage, blurring the bottom half of the frame to obscure information that could affect privacy or commercial interests.16:T55f,The detail AARO itself underlines is the one that weighs most on the reading. The same comment appears at the foot of all four records: cellular devices of this type 'employ autofocus and other automated image-processing functions that may distort the apparent morphological and kinematic characteristics of distant subjects, particularly at high magnification.' And the shot breakdown confirms the magnification was used: in the first clip the camera 'increases and decreases its level of magnification several times'; in the third it zooms in and stays there. On the fourth, the record notes twice that an approaching vehicle 'influences the camera's automatic image-processing filters, including autofocus.' The picture described — a light source that does not move, makes no sound and shifts between red, blue and green, filmed on digital zoom from a roadside — is the classic signature of a bright star or planet under atmospheric turbulence: scintillation splits the light into colours, and a phone's autofocus can distort the apparent shape and motion of a point. The transcript does not say which radar the officer checked; either way, a radar check can rule out an aircraft, not a celestial object.
