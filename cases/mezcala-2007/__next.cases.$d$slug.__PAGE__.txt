12:T515,Mexican investigator Ana Luisa Cid took up the case, gathered videos and interviews with witnesses, and was among the first to spread it; the episode later appeared in productions such as History Channel and in popular ufology magazines (Año Cero, Espacio Misterio). Here is the case's limit: all of its documentation comes from popular-ufology channels, with no independent technical analysis —photogrammetry, triangulation, examination of the original videos— or skeptical evaluation to establish what it really was. And it is worth noting an internal tension in the account itself. A luminous object that stays fixed in the same position for days is, in casework, the typical pattern of a misidentified bright celestial body: a planet or low star on the horizon that reappears at the same point night after night and is perceived as 'settled over the hill'; the bluish tones and the flickering are, moreover, characteristic of the atmospheric twinkle of a bright star (for example, Sirius) near the horizon. That reading would economically explain the multi-day immobility. By contrast, the claims of approaching within a few meters, not instrumentally corroborated, are incompatible with a celestial object and could not be independently verified.
