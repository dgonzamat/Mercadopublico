Northeastern United States (exact location undisclosed).Northeastern U.S. · residents film spherical lights at night

Between November 2021 and July 2025, in a sparsely populated rural area of the northeastern United States, a local resident filmed three sightings of spherical lights on his phone and a couple filmed a fourth, all within a 25-mile radius. The FBI rated the first witness “highly credible” and the couple “credible,” and the government authenticated one of the videos. They were released on June 12, 2026, in the U.S. government's third release of files, along with FBI documents. Journalist Micah Hanks saw in the 2025 video what look “suspiciously like candle balloons,” the paper sky lanterns lifted by a flame.

Record of the event

What happened

Between November 2021 and July 2025, four sightings of spherical lights were recorded, all within a 25-mile (about 40 km) radius, in a sparsely populated rural area of the northeastern United States. One local resident filmed the first three with his phone; a couple filmed the fourth. In November 2021, around 5:00 a.m., the first witness saw a bright light near the horizon. At first it looked like a single, intensely bright object, then resolved into several lights rotating erratically around one another while the whole group drifted from left to right. He filmed it handheld, so the image shook unintentionally.

In March 2022, at 7:20 p.m., the same witness saw two bright red lights hanging near the horizon, nearly stationary. The lower one appeared to rotate slowly relative to the upper one, from the 6 o'clock position of a clock face to a little past 9, in the report's own analogy. He heard no sound.

In October 2024, at 6:51 p.m., he saw a light source hanging over a pond, below the horizon. It looked like a "plasma-like sphere" that changed shape and brightness intermittently. At times the main light seemed to split into smaller points, and one of them stayed just above the water without behaving like a simple reflection. The object stayed nearly still for about 45 minutes and vanished without a sound. It is the only one of the four videos that, according to the record, was "analyzed and authenticated by the U.S. government." It was cropped to protect the witness's privacy and received no other editing, enhancement or alteration.

That same month, the FBI interviewed the resident. Its documents on him, from October to December 2024, add details missing from the version published by the Department of War, the government agency that released the videos. The witness had installed trail cameras and other sensors and, in the FBI's words, was working to "de-conflict possible prosaic explanations." He cross-checked the times of his sightings against public air traffic records (ADS-B) to see which could be aircraft. He said he had seen spikes in gamma radiation that matched the sightings and odd effects on electronics and GPS data, and that it was "common to have corrupted video" when trying to record them.

In November 2024, two FBI agents visited his home at dusk and saw for themselves, from the patio, white lights pulsing at the tree line and a white light above a red one moving fast at treetop height. In a daytime survey in early December they found nothing unusual on the ground or in the treetops. They noted that several houses to the east had a line of sight to the spot, and judged it "unrealistic" for anyone to fly a drone there at night without hitting tree limbs.

The fourth sighting was in July 2025, at 9:00 p.m., and a couple saw it. One of them saw an intense light in his backyard as he parked his car after work, hanging about 8 m (25 feet) off the ground. His spouse came out to see what was happening and described it as a "brilliant red sphere" about one meter across, with a core that looked like a white plasma "sun" the size of a basketball. The sphere rose slowly and moved to the left; then they saw a second, identical one above the first.

While one of them filmed with a phone, the two spheres moved off together to the west above the tree line, silently and smoothly, "as if flying in formation or tethered together." As they moved away, both witnesses saw them appear to merge, at about 70 m (75 yards).

The FBI's two February 2026 interviews with the couple do not fully agree. The one held in person describes a yellow ball of light the size of a basketball and a second, larger one the size of a beach ball, not two red spheres. An agent's note puts the objects about 27 m (30 yards) away and 6 to 9 m (20 to 30 feet) off the ground. In that interview, one of the two (the redactions do not show which) also said that in 1987 they had seen something strange near a power station and have "always been interested in the topic of UAP" (unidentified anomalous phenomena) since.

The FBI rated the resident "highly credible" and the two members of the couple "credible." The official description of the fourth video places it in a location "well known" to the witnesses and sparsely populated. The Department of War published the four videos and the FBI documents on June 12, 2026, in the third release of PURSUE, its system for releasing files on these phenomena.

Why it matters

The case does not rest on a single account: the same neighbor had repeated sightings in the same area over almost three years, a couple reported a fourth four years after the first, and two FBI agents saw lights there. But the FBI's rating concerns the witnesses' credibility, not the nature of what they saw. Nor does the official note on the 2024 video say what the authentication checked, or claim that the phenomenon was identified. And the couple's two accounts differ on the color and size of the spheres.

Except for the 2025 video, no one has proposed a cause. There is, however, a general argument that no source makes for this case. Night lights that are stationary or slow-moving and silent, filmed on a phone with no instrument to measure their distance or size, such as a rangefinder, allow conventional explanations that cannot be ruled out without more data. They include high-altitude balloons with LED lights, civilian drones with position lights, Venus or Jupiter near the horizon, and paper lanterns, whose slow ascent and warm color match several of the descriptions.

Journalist Micah Hanks of The Debrief proposes that last reading for the 2025 video: what "look suspiciously like candle balloons" drifting along with the breeze. He adds that the footage resembles a video that an aviation-safety advocacy group, which he does not name, released in 2025 ("in fact, it may be the same video"), and which was also quickly assessed as sky lanterns.

Some details fit that explanation less well. The slow rotation of one light relative to another, the splitting of one light source into smaller points and the "merging" of two identical spheres are not typical of a single balloon or drone. Explaining them with those causes would take several coordinated units or an unusual atmospheric phenomenon.
