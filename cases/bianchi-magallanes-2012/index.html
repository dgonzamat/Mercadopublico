🇨🇱2012·Route 9, near Río Rubens, between Puerto Natales and Punta Arenas

Chile.Karim Bianchi · a senator says a light shut off his car

In May 2024, Chilean senator Karim Bianchi (independent, Magallanes) said on television ("Not News," Vía X) that in 2012, driving alone at night between Puerto Natales and Punta Arenas, a spinning light shut off his car and phone, and that he "appeared" about 200 km further on with no memory of the drive. He described aftereffects (owls "everywhere," shocks when shaking hands) and a visit from a man who gave him supposed secret NASA documents. He told it in a light tone before an incredulous panel, on a show where he had previously read tarot cards, and later defended it. No witness, record or document corroborates it.

Why no ordinary cause is identified
No prosaic candidate in the sources: BioBioChile, La Prensa Austral, Infobae and 24 Horas report the account without proposing a cause; Fayerwayer reviews the generic psychology of abductions (sleep paralysis, dissociation, fantasy, quoting Michael Raduga) but does not apply it to the senator's case. The absence of a published analysis is not evidence against an ordinary cause: in its 2024 report AARO stated that "all 174 cases have been finalized as resolved to prosaic objects including balloons, birds, UAS, satellites, and aircraft." The same report adds that "many other cases remain unresolved," so the figure is the rate among resolved cases, not among all cases. (source ↗)

What happened

By his account, given in May 2024, one night in 2012 Karim Bianchi Retamales, then a regional councilor in Magallanes who worked at Radio Soberanía, was driving between Puerto Natales and Punta Arenas, alone and talking on the phone, when "a spinning light approached, large, a saucer"; to Chilevisión he described it as "a very large light, circular in shape, of different colors." The vehicle shut off entirely (engine, lights, radio and the phone he was talking on), and the next thing he recalls is "appearing" about 200 kilometers further on, with no memory of the stretch in between. In a later Instagram video he added: "it was hard to start the car and I got back on the road." He resumed the call with the same person to tell them "I've arrived." He has not publicly identified that person nor produced any record of the call.

In that Instagram video he said he appeared "many kilometers further on," and that what happened "has to do with those located between Natales and Punta Arenas, at the height of the Rubens." The press quotes disagree on his direction of travel: BioBioChile quotes him as saying "I was on my way to Puerto Natales from Punta Arenas," while La Prensa Austral quotes him as saying "I was coming from Natales to Punta Arenas," which is also how Infobae tells it. The sources do not specify where on the route it happened or how far from each town, so the figure of 200 kilometers cannot be checked against the route.

By his account, the episode had aftereffects. When shaking hands, he said, he "gave something like electric shocks for many months, almost a year I'd say." He also began noticing owls everywhere (an owl he was given as a gift, a friend's owl T-shirt, a restaurant called "Las lechuzas," a bedspread with owl eyes), something he narrowed in another interview to "for a month"; an "expert" told him it was linked to extraterrestrials "because of the turn of the neck and the eyes."

The most specific element of the account came about three days after the roadside episode: an "old, bald, short" man, whom he said he had already seen a couple of times in Punta Arenas and who had insisted with the radio station's secretary, showed up at his office, told him he was "a person from another planet" and handed him papers he described as "NASA secrets," with the charge that he "had to spread the message." Bianchi says he keeps those documents at home, but has not made them public or submitted them to any examination. He also said it was not the only time he had seen something like this, but it was "the only time I've had an effect."

He told it in public for the first time on the talk show "Not News" on the Chilean channel Vía X, hosted by Nicolás Larraín, by then an independent senator for the 15th Circumscription (Magallanes Region), a seat he has held since 2022. La Prensa Austral, the Magallanes daily, described the panelists' "incredulous look." When the host asked whether the man with the papers was former senator Andrés Zaldívar, Bianchi answered "he looked like him," and closed with "maybe I could be a candidate on another planet." The same paper recalled that Bianchi is a regular guest on the show and that in an earlier appearance he had read Chile's future in tarot cards.

The interview spread through the Chilean press and reached international outlets such as Infobae. Bianchi explained that he had not wanted to talk much about it because of the criticism, and after the broadcast he answered a wave of mockery on Instagram: "I very much regret the violence that people unleash on social media." He repeated the account without retracting it, though he downplayed it: "it never mattered to me, nor changed my life, nor anything." There is no record of a statement on the case from the Chilean Senate or from the Committee for the Study of Anomalous Aerial Phenomena (CEFAA), the Chilean state body that investigates such phenomena.

Why it matters

A sitting senator said on television, under his own name and in the first person, that he had gone through an episode of lost time, and he called it an abduction himself: the man with the papers, he said, brought them to him "since I had been abducted." He is not passing on what others told him: he speaks of something he says he lived through, and he offers no document, no witness and no institutional backing. Only his word remains.

The account gathers nearly the entire classic repertoire of the contactee tradition: lost time and an unexplained displacement, physical aftereffects with no documented medical diagnosis, a run of coincidences involving an animal (the owls) with no proposed mechanism, and a messenger who delivers secret documents and asks him to "spread the message," which follows almost point for point the contactee encounters told since the 1950s and 1960s. That proves or rules out nothing about what happened, but so recognizable a structure leaves open the possibility that the memory was organized, consciously or not, around cultural scripts that already existed.

No source proposes a concrete cause. Fayerwayer paired the news with a general review of what science says about abductions: quoting researcher Michael Raduga, the feelings of paralysis, fear and helplessness in vivid dreams "can be so powerful that they blur the line between dreams and reality," along with explanations pointing to "paranoia, dissociation, or even intense fantasy"; it did not apply them point by point to the senator's account.

The setting fits that repertoire: at night, alone, on a long, monotonous Patagonian road. The light tone in which he told it makes it impossible to know how much of it was a joke; that he later defended it despite the mockery makes it impossible to reduce it to one. What remains is a single witness, with nothing that can be checked, speaking twelve years later. It remains to be seen whether the documents Bianchi says he keeps will ever be made public.
