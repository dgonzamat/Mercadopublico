4c:T566,The most cited observation is of an object of noticeable size that the crew noticed roughly a day out from the Moon, during the cruise phase. They looked at it through the window and Aldrin found it in the sextant, which, off focus, made them think it was a cylinder; Armstrong said it was 'right at the limit of the resolution of the eye', with no measurable shape or size; Aldrin said it 'certainly seemed to be within our vicinity and of a very sizeable dimension', and through the monocular it 'seemed to have a bit of an L shape', which Armstrong likened to 'an open suitcase'. The crew asked the ground about the S-IVB third stage and 'were told the S-IVB was 6000 miles away'. Collins summed up: 'we really don't have a conclusion as to what it might have been, how big it was, or how far away it was', adding that it 'wasn't part of the urine dump, we're pretty sure of that'. Aldrin recalled: 'We thought it could have been a panel, but it didn't appear to have that shape at all'; Collins thought of 'a part of the high gain antenna', which was giving trouble at the time, and said he had 'some reason to suspect that its origin was from the spacecraft'. Skipping ahead to the lunar module jettison, Collins added that pieces came off then and 'It could have been some Mylar or something that had somehow come loose from the LM.'
