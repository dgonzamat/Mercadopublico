United States / cislunar space.Apollo 11 · the astronauts describe an object and light flashes

In their technical debriefing after the flight, on July 31, 1969, Apollo 11 astronauts Armstrong, Collins and Aldrin described a sizable object about a day out from the Moon, "little flashes" inside the cabin and a "fairly bright light source" in lunar orbit, which they first put down to a "possible laser" and then, if no one owned up to aiming a laser at the Moon, to "more probably a reflection off a lake". They reached no conclusion about the object, and Collins suspected the spacecraft itself. The document was released in May 2026.

What happened

Apollo 11's Technical Crew Debriefing (the post-flight technical session with the crew, conducted on July 31, 1969, a week after the July 24 splashdown) records visual observations by astronauts Neil Armstrong, Michael Collins and Edwin "Buzz" Aldrin. It describes not a single incident but several phenomena seen during the trip to the Moon and the return.

Aldrin said the first unusual thing they saw was a day out from the Moon, or something close to it: an object of sizeable dimension. They looked at it through the window and the monocular, through which it "seemed to have a bit of an L shape", which Armstrong likened to "an open suitcase"; Aldrin said it "certainly seemed to be within our vicinity and of a very sizeable dimension". Armstrong said it was "right at the limit of the resolution of the eye" and that there was no way to tell the size without knowing the range. Aldrin then looked for it in the sextant, with which, off focus, it appeared to be a cylinder; Collins said that when the focus was changed the shape was replaced by an open-book shape and that it was "really weird".

The crew called the ground about the rocket's S-IVB third stage and were told it was 6000 miles away. Collins said that during the time when they thought it was a cylinder they had "almost convinced ourselves that's what it had to be", but that they had no further conclusions: "we really don't have a conclusion as to what it might have been, how big it was, or how far away it was", and that it "wasn't part of the urine dump, we're pretty sure of that". Aldrin recalled: "We thought it could have been a panel, but it didn't appear to have that shape at all"; Collins thought of "a part of the high gain antenna", which was giving trouble at the time, and said he had "some reason to suspect that its origin was from the spacecraft". Skipping ahead to the lunar module jettison, he added that pieces came off then and "It could have been some Mylar or something that had somehow come loose from the LM." The debriefing mentions no radar or photograph of the object.

Aldrin also described "little flashes" inside the cabin, spaced a couple of minutes apart, which he saw while trying to sleep with the lights out, the second night for sure. On the return he saw double flashes, maybe a foot apart, and at times a line with no direction of motion; he thought of static electricity, but noted a definite difference from the sparks he could generate with his hand on the sleep restraint. He estimated, as a guess, about one a minute, "certainly more than enough to convince you that it wasn't an optical illusion". Armstrong said he had seen some light and attributed it to sunlight leaking around the window covers; only on the last night, after an hour watching the inside of the spacecraft, did he make about 50 significant observations. He said it could be what Aldrin suggested: "Mainly a neutron or some kind of an atomic particle that would be in the visible spectrum."

In lunar orbit, after the lunar module ascent, approaching the CDH maneuver as the Earth came up above the lunar horizon, Aldrin saw what appeared to be "a fairly bright light source", which the crew "tentatively ascribed to a possible laser". That "seemed to be the best possible explanation" until, coming back in the command module approaching the Earth, they saw something with about the same appearance; through the monocular it looked like the reflection of the Sun from a relatively smooth body of water such as a lake. Aldrin said: "I think we've revised our initial conclusion [...] If no one owns up to having beamed the laser toward the Moon at that time, it was more probably a reflection off a lake." He still thought it "an unusual phenomenon, at that distance, to see so bright a source of light". On film, he said, it did not look as though it would show up at all, because the Earth was too bright.

Why it matters

The document is a primary record of how the first lunar landing crew went over, with NASA technicians, what they had seen on the flight. On May 9, 2026 Fortune ran an Associated Press story headlined "UFO files show Buzz Aldrin saw a ‘sizeable’ object close to the moon and a ‘fairly bright light source’ that the Apollo 11 crew felt could be a laser". The debriefing places the object a day's travel from the Moon, not close to it, and the story does not mention that the crew themselves later moved from the laser to a reflection off a lake; about the object, the astronauts drew no conclusion.

The flashes inside the cabin were later described by the Apollo 12 and 13 crews too, when questioned in their debriefings, and NASA studied them specifically: it scheduled dedicated observing sessions in the dark on the following flights and, on Apollo 16 and 17, the ALFMED experiment, a helmet with nuclear-emulsion plates that recorded the cosmic-ray particles passing through the astronaut's head so they could be matched with the flashes he reported. According to NASA's medical report on the program ("Biomedical Results of Apollo", 1975), these particles are believed to produce the sensation of light by interacting with the retina.

For the object a day from the Moon there is no instrument record: the testimony is the crew's memory a week after the flight.
