28:T4a2,The problem is the chain of evidence. The case reached the public mainly through the Brazilian physician and ufologist Olavo Fontes, a correspondent of the U.S. group APRO, who reported it in the late 1950s; most of the details —the soldiers' names, the military reports, the alleged joint Brazil-USAF visit— rest on that chain and not on an accessible, verified primary official document. Later researchers, such as Kevin Randle, reviewed the file and found the sources referring to one another without a firm documentary anchor. Moreover, an officer —Colonel Erasmo (Erasmus) Dias, who identified himself as commander of the fortress— stated years later that he never knew of such an incident, called it 'absurd' and a probable 'legend', and although he admitted there had been sightings of objects in the area, he denied the episode of the burned sentries and the electrical failures. The case is thus split into two equally possible readings: a real military event with physical effects that the institution never publicly documented, or a story that grew and solidified in ufological literature from a second-hand report.
