14:T834,Editorial honesty requires presenting the serious objections in detail, because they are central to calibrating the case. First, the context: two nights earlier, on 14 September just before 21:00, a fireball with a long tail of sparks crossed Zimbabwe's sky and was seen in several countries. Astronomers first spoke of a meteor shower, and it later emerged that the debris came from a Soviet launch in late August; the GCAT catalogue records the re-entry that 14 September of the second stage of the Zenit-2 that had launched Kosmos-2290. Hind collected dozens of reports in the following days, and she also documents that some children saw an object 'like a cigarette in the sky' on the 15th and that a neighbour saw an orange glow in the early hours of the 16th. The region was thus primed to read the sky in anomalous terms. Second, Hind's accounts contain doubts that the popular version leaves out: Fiona, 9, said the dark figure 'could have been a branch or something like that'; several children first thought it was the gardener or a labourer; two small boys cried because they feared the man in black was coming to eat them, an echo of the tokoloshe of local folklore.
