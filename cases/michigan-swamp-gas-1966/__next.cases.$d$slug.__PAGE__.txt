15:T44e,In March 1966, southeastern Michigan was accumulating reports of lights at night, and two consecutive nights drew national attention. On Sunday, March 20, at about 8:35 p.m., the Washtenaw County sheriff's office received a call from a farm near Dexter, about 8 miles northwest of Ann Arbor: there was a strange object in the swamp, which had risen about 500 feet and returned to the ground. The main witness, farmer Frank Mannor, later told the Air Force's scientific consultant, the astronomer J. Allen Hynek, that he saw a light he took for a falling star that stopped over the swamp north of his barn, and that he and his son walked toward it. According to the sheriff's report, from about 500 yards they described a brown object with a quilted-looking surface, flat on the bottom and cone-shaped on top, with two lights at its ends that changed from blue-green to brilliant red and a yellowish light between them; when the deputies' flashlights appeared, the light intensified, went out, a whistle 'similar to the sound of a rifle bullet ricochet' was heard and the object passed over the Mannors.16:T688,Deputies Stanley McFadden and David Fitzpatrick searched the woods and the swamp without result. According to the Selfridge base telex of March 23, McFadden saw the lights and heard the sound, though not as close nor for as long as Mannor, and agreed with him; he later told the Blue Book team that he first took the light for a radio tower beacon and that it was about three quarters of a mile away. He told Hynek he had not seen the object, although he drew it. Fitzpatrick told Hynek he saw only a glow behind a rise, which brightened and dimmed slowly 'as though stage lights were being brightened and dimmed', and that it disappeared as he approached with his flashlight. Back at their car, onlookers told them one of the objects had hovered over their flashlights and departed west at high speed, and in the patrol cars waiting there they were told that four lights had hovered above them while they searched.
