26:T41f,In the early hours of a day in early November 1968, a physician from south-eastern France — whose identity investigators protected under the pseudonym 'Dr. X' — was woken by the crying of his fourteen-month-old son. He got up with difficulty: days earlier he had injured his leg chopping wood with an axe, and since a war wound received in Algeria on 13 May 1958 he had also suffered from a hemiparesis of his right side. The child was pointing toward flashes coming through the shutters. There was a storm outside, with wind, rain and what looked like frequent lightning, although the doctor later recalled that the flashes came at odd intervals and did not match any thunder. Looking out, the physician said he saw two horizontal, disk-shaped objects, silvery white above and bright red below, with antennae and beams of light. The two disks approached, merged into a single one and, after a period of observation, the object emitted a beam of light toward the house before vanishing with a bang, leaving a kind of filament or cloud that dissipated.27:T7ac,What makes the case memorable is not the sighting itself but the sequence of physical effects the physician and his family reported afterward, which the investigation documented. In the following hours, the pain and swelling of the axe wound disappeared; the sequelae of the old war injury also subsided, something that — according to the follow-up — held throughout the observed period. But the most disconcerting element came days later: a triangle-shaped rash erupted on the physician's abdomen, and shortly after an identical triangular rash appeared on his son's belly, although the child had not witnessed the encounter in the same way. According to Michel's second report (1971), the father's triangle appeared on 19 November 1968 and the child's the next day, and for over two years it recurred about every three weeks, visible for two or three days, usually half a day earlier on the father than on the son. Michel watched it begin to appear on the doctor's abdomen on the evening of 1 November 1969, during a dinner at his house; the next morning the grandmother, who had the child 60 kilometres away and knew nothing of the incident, phoned in distress because she had found "a sort of wound surrounding the navel, shaped like a triangle". Associated effects were also reported — disrupted clocks and electrical circuits, and an episode of alleged levitation — of a far more anecdotal nature.
