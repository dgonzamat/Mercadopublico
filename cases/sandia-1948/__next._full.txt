40:T5a1,It brings together the summary by the OSI 17th District office (Kirtland) of April 19, 1949, the summary of observations of May 25, 1950, La Paz's reports to the OSI, and the minutes of the Los Alamos conference. In his report of December 20, 1948, La Paz lists ten differences from typical meteors: horizontal paths, very low height, lower speed, no noise despite that height, full brightness from the first instant, a vivid green he matches to copper salts, a duration of two to three seconds, and no train of sparks or dust. He notes that the groups of green fireballs coincided with the Quadrantid, Perseid, and Geminid meteor showers, which he read as an attempt to make them less conspicuous. In his report of May 23, 1950, he corrects the first feature, because many fireballs had fallen vertically (while warning that a strictly vertical infall is also very rarely observed in genuine meteor falls), notes two possible exceptions to their silence, and adds an eleventh difference: the hourly peak of the fireballs, around 20:30, coincides neither with that of ordinary meteors (around 03:00) nor with that of meteorite falls (around 16:00). The minutes of February 16, 1949 (room P-162) record La Paz, Teller, Bradbury, Reines, the Fourth Army, the FBI, and the Atomic Energy Commission/Sandia; AARO declassified them under code DOEUAPD004.
