🇨🇱1984·Aysén Region, southern Chile (claimed location, unverified)

Chile.Friendship Island · radio hams say they talk to a hidden group

From the mid-1980s, several Chilean radio amateurs said they were receiving shortwave transmissions from a community that called itself “Friendship.” Its main promoter, Santiago businessman Ernesto de la Fuente, claimed its members had cured him of a serious illness. As the story went, it was a community with advanced technology, hidden somewhere in southern Chile, and it became linked to a photo of a long white vessel in Patagonia. The case was all over Chilean television and newspapers in the 1990s, but no verifiable evidence of the community or the vessel ever emerged.

What happened

The case known in Chile as "Friendship Island" (sometimes "Isla de la Amistad") is less a sighting than a contact story told over more than a decade. According to Cristóbal Valenzuela, director of the documentary "Isla Alien" (2023), it became known in 1984 through radio communications: several Chilean radio amateurs said they were receiving shortwave messages from a community that called itself "Friendship." According to Interferencia, these "chosen ones" exchanged messages about life and the apocalypse for years with people who said they lived on an island south of Chiloé; other versions speak of the channels and fjords of Aysén or Patagonia. In the versions in circulation, the voices held conversations and answered questions without revealing a precise location. No version fixed a verifiable island.

The main promoter was Ernesto de la Fuente, a Santiago businessman. He said that, seriously ill, he had been put in touch with Friendship and healed by them, and that he kept in communication with the group for years. His testimony and that of other radio amateurs built the story of a discreet, technologically advanced community that chose certain interlocutors and offered them help, including healing.

According to the versions in circulation, a photograph came to be tied to the story: taken in Chilean Patagonia, it shows a large, long white vessel or object, which promoters presented as Friendship's "ship" or base. Its provenance, date and authenticity were never independently established.

The story went mainstream in 1998, with an episode of the program "OVNI" on Televisión Nacional de Chile (TVN), hosted by Patricio Bañados, with De la Fuente and a radio amateur named Octavio as its protagonists. According to the director of the documentary "Isla Alien," the program had very high ratings, and the credibility of Bañados, who had been the face of the "No" campaign in the 1988 plebiscite (the option against a new presidential term for Pinochet), helped establish the story. The Chilean press also covered it.

In 2023, "Isla Alien," by Cristóbal Valenzuela, investigated De la Fuente. According to the director (interview in Interferencia, November 12, 2023), De la Fuente claimed to be an engineer and was not. He claimed to have fought in Vietnam and was never there: U.S. transparency law showed he was not a veteran, and he took the story of a work colleague. And the boat Mitelus 2, which the director calls "important in this Friendship story," did not exist; the interview does not say what part it played in the story. Mitelus 1 did exist, a boat of the Servicio Agrícola y Ganadero (SAG) that ran between Quemchi and Ancud, where De la Fuente spent his holidays as a child; for the director, Mitelus 2 was a way of recalling it.

The director compares the case to other fabulists who invented a cancer they never had, and says De la Fuente takes it "to a surreal level." This discredits the promoter, but the sources do not explain each radio contact of the other amateurs.

The sources propose no cause for the transmissions. That amateurs sustained a fiction, or that legitimate communications were misread, are conventional possibilities that no source raises. There is no confirmed location for the community, no reproducible technical record of the transmissions and no authentication of the photo: the case rests on testimony.

Why it matters

Friendship went from a story among radio amateurs to a popular phenomenon in Chile. According to the director of "Isla Alien," there were ten years of radio communications before the 1998 TVN program made it a mass story. According to Interferencia, since the 1990s it has been one of the country's most widespread urban legends. The sources consulted mention no radar, telemetry or independent second channel: the story rests on testimony and radio messages.

The documentary also tied the story to the dictatorship. Valenzuela found that De la Fuente took part in the disappearance of two peasants, Luis Lobos and Carlos Maldonado: he drove them in his car to a police checkpoint, where they disappeared. The director could not confirm that he was an agent of the CNI, the Central Nacional de Informaciones, the dictatorship's repressive agency from 1977 to 1990. According to the director, in his memoirs De la Fuente wrote that he took people to Friendship island and that people "disappeared" there.
