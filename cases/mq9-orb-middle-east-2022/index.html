Record of the event

What happened

On July 12, 2022, a U.S. MQ-9 Reaper drone recorded 24 seconds of video in the Middle East. The Pentagon did not disclose the location. The footage comes from a mission report numbered 7750816; there is no record that the report has been published. On April 19, 2023, Sean Kirkpatrick, director of the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO), showed the clip at a public hearing of the Senate Armed Services Subcommittee on Emerging Threats and Capabilities. AARO is the Pentagon office that studies unidentified anomalous phenomena (UAP). The same day, the Department of Defense posted the clip on DVIDS, its media portal, under the title "Middle East Object."

The clip, which has no sound, opens for about three seconds with a green card: "Unclassified," "Footage from mission report: 7750816" and "12 July 22 2156Z." Then the camera looks almost straight down at arid ground: a house beside a road, with a few small figures that look like people, furrowed fields and isolated buildings. Gray patches cover parts of the on-screen display and the sides of the picture are blurred, so what viewers see is a version edited for release.

According to a frame-by-frame examination of the video made for this case, the object first appears around second 7 of the clip: a round, dark dot in the upper part of the frame, next to the house, that moves down toward the road and is lost at the lower left around second 8.3, after a little over a second. The slide Kirkpatrick showed the Senate points a magnifier at a dot in a scene with the road. Between seconds 10 and 11 there are one or two small dark dots on the ground, and it cannot be said whether they are the same object. Between seconds 16.5 and 22.4 it appears again, this time clearly: a small dark sphere with a white glint that enters at the right edge and stays in the frame for about six seconds while the ground moves past beneath it.

From second 20.6 the picture darkens, turns blue and, around second 22.6, goes almost white. In that stretch the dot looks white and is no longer visible after about second 22.4. Around second 23 normal colors return and, from about second 23.6 to the last frame, a dark dot with a white glint is visible again over ground with walls. The clip ends at 24 seconds. No source says what causes the blue and white stretch in the video.

At the hearing, Kirkpatrick described the clip as an MQ-9 in the Middle East observing an apparently spherical object with electro-optical sensors (visible-light cameras), which "are not IR" (infrared). He said the object comes through the top of the screen and pops in and out of the field of view, and that the camera will "slew," meaning turn, to follow it. He added that this was "essentially all of the data" on the event, "from some years ago," and that it would be "virtually impossible" to fully identify it from that video alone. Later he said the first clip is "an unresolved case we are still studying." AARO keeps it in the group of 52 percent of reports that are round or spherical, to compare traits across them.

The slide that accompanied the clip carries the stamp "Unresolved, in active-archive." It says the object's characteristics and behavior are consistent with other "metallic orb" observations in the region, that it shows no "enigmatic technical capabilities" and no apparent threat to airborne assets, and that the case stays in the active archive pending additional data. The official DVIDS description adds that AARO assesses the object is not exhibiting anomalous behavior but remains unidentified, and that the clip is a representative example of many cases with limited data.

There are two points the sources consulted do not reconcile. Kirkpatrick spoke of data "from some years ago," and the clip dates from nine months before the hearing. And by a calculation made for this case, 21:56 UTC (universal time) on the card is 12:56 a.m. on July 13 in Iraq or Syria (UTC+3), at night, while the video shows daylight and strong shadows. No source explains these differences.

On April 20, 2023, Fox News reported that the clip reminded documentary filmmaker Jeremy Corbell of the "Mosul Orb," a still image he and journalist George Knapp released in January 2023 and attributed to a 2016 recording. They are separate events: ↗ Mosul Orb has only a leaked still, with no sensor data, and neither the slide nor the hearing transcript mentions Mosul.

In October 2023, Nathan Patin and Logan Williams of Bellingcat, an investigative journalism group, proposed that the object could be a balloon. By their comparison of buildings with Google Earth imagery from July 13, 2022, the video was recorded northeast of Deir ez-Zor, in Syria. Using a wall of about 11 meters as a scale, they measured the object in the last frame: no more than 0.43 meters, and less if it floats closer to the camera than the ground. That measurement contradicts the "one to four meters" that AARO gives as typical on its slide and at the hearing (page 16).

They added that the apparent motion may be parallax alone, the apparent shift of a nearby object seen from a moving drone, and that it cannot even be said to move. Their model assumes a drone altitude and speed that have not been published, and the location is also redacted in the video. It is an independent analysis, and there is no record that AARO adopted that reading.

Why it matters

AARO presented this clip as an example of what it receives most: Kirkpatrick said 52 percent of reports are round objects or spheres, and the official description calls it a "representative example" of many cases with limited data. An unresolved case, here, means 24 seconds of video with no distance, altitude or speed, from which AARO cannot say what the object was.

What separates a resolved case from an open one is the amount of data. The South Asia clip ↗ South Asia, shown at the same hearing, was explained as an airplane because there was a second video at higher zoom and commercial flight data for the region. The western U.S. case ↗ Western U.S. was closed by matching the video against commercial flights and radar tracks. This one has a single camera and a mission report with no record of publication, and Kirkpatrick left it in AARO's active archive.

What is known is little. There are no witnesses and no statement from whoever operated the sensor, and the chain of custody is institutional: the video came from AARO and the Department of Defense. The object's size, distance and speed appear in no published source. The balloon explanation is a hypothesis from outside analysts, and there is no record that AARO adopted it: it maintains that the object remains unidentified. The points left open, the time elapsed, the time on the card and the cause of the blue and white stretch, do not change what the clip shows, but they prevent using it as a record of a precise instant.

Corbell, quoted by Fox News, linked it to the Mosul case. The link rests only on the similar shape and on the slide's line about other metallic orb observations in the region: no official source offers a fact connecting the two events.
