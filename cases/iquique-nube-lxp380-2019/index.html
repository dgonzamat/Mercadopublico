🇨🇱2019·Over the sea, about 37 km west of Iquique airport, Chile

Chile.Iquique · an airline crew photographs an “aircraft” at dawn

At 06:34 on August 9, 2019, the crew of a LATAM flight heading north to Arica at 32,000 feet off Iquique told approach control that they had “apparently” an aircraft at 3 o'clock, some 20 or 30 miles away and at their own altitude. Radar showed nothing and the nearest traffic was far away. The pilot took two photos and a 12-second video against the glow of dawn. After consulting a meteorologist, an astronomy adviser and an image analyst, SEFAA, Chile's official office for these phenomena, concluded in 2026 that what was photographed had the characteristics of a cloud that kept changing shape.

Record of the event

What happened

At 06:34 local time on Friday, August 9, 2019, 10:34 Coordinated Universal Time (UTC), the crew of LATAM flight LXP380, an Airbus A321 on a Santiago–Arica flight plan, called Iquique approach control by radio. The aircraft was heading north on airway UQ802, near the DADGI waypoint, at 32,000 feet (about 9,750 meters) and about 20 nautical miles (about 37 kilometers) west of Diego Aracena airport.

The transcript published by SEFAA, the section of Chile's Directorate General of Civil Aviation that studies anomalous aerial phenomena, records the query: "At 3 o'clock from our position we have, uh… an aircraft apparently. We estimate about 20 miles. Do you have it on your display?" Control asked for confirmation and the pilot repeated: "It seems to be an aircraft. It would be… apparently some 20 or 30 miles from our position. We estimate the same altitude." The controller answered that the nearest traffic was at 5 o'clock, ninety miles away. "I'm taking a photo of it, to send to you later." The tower logbook recorded the query and that the Air Force Air Operations Centre was informed. The pilot gave no path, behavior or speed.

SEFAA studied a 12.6-second video, 848 by 480 pixels, and three photographs, two of them copies of the same image. All of it arrived over WhatsApp, which removed much of the pixel data and, in the photos, the camera parameters; even so, the office pressed on because it was an aviation case. According to the metadata, the video was created at 11:15 UTC. The report says this is one hour and fourteen minutes after the logbook time, but the difference from 10:34 is 41 minutes. Since the files went through WhatsApp, which the report itself says altered them, that creation time is probably when the file was sent rather than when it was shot.

In the photo it reproduces, above the dark silhouette of the hills and below bands of reddish and orange cloud, a small dark elongated shape stands against the brightest strip of the dawn. The report says that "the image analyst considered what was studied to be a cloud"; in her detailed opinion she described it as a rectangular-looking object with one rounded end, and added that she "leaned toward thinking" it could be a cloud whose shape changed with the light and the breeze.

The office reviewed the traffic recorded by transponders, the onboard devices that identify each aircraft to radar: at the time of filming the only aircraft to the east was LAN864, some 214 nautical miles (about 396 kilometers) away in Bolivian airspace, beyond what the images could show. There were no institutional air operations in the sector, a search of primary radar, which detects any object even without a transponder, found nothing, and a commercial flight approaching from the south, asked by control, saw nothing.

The astronomy adviser ruled out an astronomical origin. The Iquique weather report gave visibility of more than 10 kilometers, overcast at around 3,500 feet and a light southwesterly wind. A meteorologist from the Chilean Meteorological Directorate reported that, having seen the background and the videos, a field of high cloud associated with a jet stream could be observed, consistent with the satellite imagery of the moment, and that, in view of this, he ruled out "the presence of any kind of meteorological phenomenon or radiosonde balloon." He had said the same in a first consultation, based on satellite images, for "the sector." The report does not specify which phenomenon was ruled out or how that fits with the high cloud he himself describes.

To illustrate how a lone cloud changes within minutes, the report adds its own photographs of isolated clouds over Santiago and Lagunillas taken in February and March 2026. Its conclusion, of May 11, 2026, almost seven years later, is that what the crew saw had the characteristics of a cloud whose outline and size evolved, conspicuous because of the lighting and because it lay between the aircraft and the mountains and clouds lit by the dawn glow, and that the aircraft that later followed the route did not see it, very possibly because it had broken up. It classified the case as "Resolved."

Why it matters

The case has a recorded radio call, a video and two photos, and that makes it possible to test what the pilot thought he saw. He believed there was an aircraft at his own altitude; transponder traffic, the primary-radar search and another aircraft that passed through the area found none. What the photo and video show is a small, elongated dark patch silhouetted against the brightest part of the sky. A check of our own, not the report's, fits the explanation: at 10:34 UTC, off Iquique, the Sun was some 8 or 9° below the horizon, to the east-northeast, roughly at 3 o'clock for an aircraft heading north. The object lay in the same direction as the dawn, and against the light a dark, compact cloud looks very much like the silhouette of a distant aircraft.

The report also has weak points. The image quality is poor, and the identification as a cloud rests on the analyst's judgment, which the report summarizes as having "considered what was studied to be a cloud" and which she herself phrased as "leaning toward thinking" it was a cloud, together with the traffic and radar analysis and the meteorologist's opinion; there is no measurement of the object. The file times cannot date the shot, and the report itself gets the gap from the radio call wrong. The nearest traffic is given as 90 miles on the radio and 214 in the later analysis. The clouds used for comparison are over Santiago and nearly seven years later. And the report holds no account from the pilot beyond the radio call.
