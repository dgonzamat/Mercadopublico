13:T490,Around 14:45, a formation of four F-51 Mustang fighters of the Kentucky Air National Guard, in transit from Marietta (Georgia) to Standiford Field (Louisville), was diverted to investigate; one landed and three climbed toward the object. The flight leader, Captain Thomas F. Mantell —a World War II veteran— began a climb in pursuit of the bright object. He was flying F-51D serial 44-63869. According to the tower statements, at 15,000 feet he reported the object "directly ahead of and above" him, moving first at half his speed and then as fast as he was, and that it appeared to be "a metallic object of tremendous size"; he said he would climb to 20,000 feet and, if no closer, abandon the chase. At 22,000 feet pilot Hammond reported he had no oxygen equipment and returned with Clements; Mantell kept climbing. Captain Carter's note in the file concludes that he "apparently blacked out at 20,000 ft" and crashed "thru lack of oxygen". The fighter came down about 8 km southwest of Franklin, Kentucky, some 145 km from Godman; witnesses told police the aircraft exploded in the air before it hit the ground, and the wreckage was scattered over about a mile.14:T41e,Project Sign investigated the accident. The first explanation, offered by a consulted astronomer, was "either Venus or a comet"; the file itself notes beside Carter's statement that this "does not seem to tally" with what was described, and in October 1948 Air Materiel Command requested Venus's position to check it. The same file holds the datum that points to a balloon: between 15:30 and 16:45, astronomer Seyfert of Vanderbilt University tracked by telescope, south of Nashville, an object he identified as "a pear shaped balloon with cables and a basket attached", at about 25,000 feet, moving at about 10 miles per hour. The Blue Book record card for the case bears the handwritten word "BALLOON". The specific identification as a Navy Skyhook balloon, a then-classified program of high-altitude plastic balloons, is not in the file: Ruppelt popularized it in 1956 and later reconstructions reinforced it, placing a launch of that type at Clinton County (Ohio).
