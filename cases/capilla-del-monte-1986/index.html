Argentina.Capilla del Monte · residents see a craft land by a hill

On January 9, 1986, several residents of Capilla del Monte (Córdoba, Argentina), including an 11-year-old boy, Gabriel Gómez, said they saw a large round craft with windows land beside Cerro Pajarillo. The next day a large scorched elliptical mark more than a hundred meters long appeared on the slope. Journalist José de Zer covered it on television to huge effect, but analyses found no soil anomalies or radiation. In 2016 a grandson of the hill's owner said the mark had been left by lightning in the previous night's storm and that his grandmother had invented the flying saucer to attract tourists.

What happened

On January 9, 1986, in Capilla del Monte, a small town in the Córdoba hills (Argentina) at the foot of Cerro Uritorco, several residents, including an eleven-year-old boy named Gabriel Gómez, claimed to have seen a large round craft, with windows and bright lights, land beside Cerro Pajarillo. Gómez described a large, metallic object emitting a strange glow.

The report in Tiempo Argentino of January 19, 1986, quoting the municipal government secretary Jorge Suárez, placed the landing at 11 p.m. on the previous Thursday, which would give January 16, a week after the date the other sources give, near Lomas del Pajarito, some twelve kilometers from the town. According to that report, a few residents followed its movements until it landed about two kilometers from them and, after it took off, saw the low vegetation burning; a nine-year-old boy drew it round, with many windows and bright colored lights, and others said a nearby willow had changed color. The mayor, Suárez and a councilor went to the site and verified the burnt grass, in a circle about a hundred meters across.

The day after the sighting a huge elliptical mark appeared on the slope, dubbed "la Huella del Pajarillo" (the Pajarillo Imprint), about 115 by 57 meters according to the reconstruction by the Factor blog (other sources give 120 by 70), with the vegetation inside apparently scorched and flattened. The mark could be seen and photographed, and it gave the case the look of physical evidence. Investigations found in it no conclusive traces beyond the surface burns: neither anomalous soil composition nor radiation levels above natural variation.

The coverage was led by journalist José de Zer, of the television program Nuevediario, whose sensationalist on-site reports, with suspense music, night shots and a cameraman nicknamed "Chango", hypnotized the Argentine audience and made Capilla del Monte famous worldwide. It became the country's most famous UFO case. The town turned into Argentina's "UFO capital", with an annual festival and a tourist industry, and the Uritorco, which according to Página/12 and Chequeado was little known outside the area until that coverage, became an esoteric center. None of this brought independent scientific corroboration of the original event.

In July 2002, five years after De Zer's death, his cameraman, Carlos "Chango" Torres, told journalist Mariano Blejman, in Página/12's Radar supplement, how those reports had been put together. According to Torres, De Zer saw in the local paper a photo of burnt grassland that looked like the imprint of a flying saucer; they went to the site, found the mark and put together "a little script". Asked whether they had invented everything, he answered: "The mark was real. But everything else was pure fiction." He said they threw dead, dried beetles on the road so that De Zer could announce "desiccated bugs", and that in some caves a local guide took them to they painted drawings on the ceiling and buried pebbles they had painted as little figures with nail polish; on camera, De Zer presented the drawings as "strange hieroglyphs" and the pebbles, which he dug up, as extraterrestrial life.

When the news director ordered them to camp on the summit, they sent the guide with three extras to pitch a tent up there, while De Zer, never leaving the foot of the hill, narrated as if from the summit and greeted as a "support plane" a light aircraft Torres had rented in La Falda. The pre-dawn lights, filmed from a tent pitched at the back of the hotel, were those of the road crossing a slope in the Los Terrones countryside. "We never got to the top of the hill," he said. He also said the residents, who at first resented them, later "made fortunes" from the coverage.

His confession does not touch the residents' sighting or the origin of the mark, which by his account had already appeared in the newspaper before they arrived. The Página/12 article places the crew's arrival "at the beginning of 1985", and Chequeado in "the summer of 1985"; the press of the time and the other testimonies date the mark to January 1986. In the same interview Torres said he never saw an extraterrestrial but believes in them, because "sometimes we saw inexplicable things", and gave as an example, without placing it at the Uritorco, some "completely desiccated" trees beside which a tethered dog "had shrunk and NASA came and took it away". Back then, when asked, he would insist: "The lights were the real thing."

The most concrete explanation came thirty years later. On March 17, 2016, in a radio interview, Gonzalo Vaquer, grandson of Lilly Arijón, the hill's owner, said that "it was all an invention of my grandmother", "a white lie": since a storm had broken the night before, on January 8, lightning had made the mark, and she asked the locals: "say a flying saucer came, that way more tourism comes". Historian Fernando Soto Roland and journalist Alejandro Agostinelli, who gathered that testimony on the Factor blog, also note that the scrub did not burn "from the top down", as if something had landed or radiated heat, but had scorched centers and leaves blackened on their undersides; that aerial photos show a V-shaped mark consistent with lightning; that the Gómez family, whose lights were cited as the sighting, actually pointed to a different hill; and that Arijón herself regarded the boy Gabriel Gómez's testimony as "induced".

Why it matters

In Capilla del Monte three things became intertwined: a sighting with local witnesses, a physical mark on the ground and television coverage with its own stake in the mystery, which may have contaminated the other two. The mark was real, but analyses found nothing anomalous in the soil and no radiation, whereas a landing that released energy would have left a trace, and the explanation the sources give is lightning from the January 8 storm, with the flying saucer added afterward to attract tourism.

The objections to the sighting come from the community itself: the Gómez family pointed to a different hill and the landowner regarded the boy's testimony as induced. The context, at night and under a strong UFO cultural frame, favors misidentification. What television showed as evidence, the desiccated bugs, the cave hieroglyphs and the pre-dawn lights, came from the staging the cameraman described, not from the residents' account.

Decades later, the Argentine Air Force commission that studies these reports, CEFAe (↗), examined in its annual reports for 2015, 2016 and 2018 seven photos and videos taken in Capilla del Monte and on the Uritorco between 2008 and 2018, and attributed an ordinary cause to all of them: birds, insects, drops and dirt particles on the lens, and objects held close to the camera. The 2018 report, for instance, attributed a video of April 11, 2018 in Capilla del Monte to the trail of an insect crossing in front of the camera with a long exposure time, and in a photo by Paola Carco from June 2016, with an object "at the limit of what can be analyzed", found a surface compatible with the wing of a white monjita (Xolmis irupero) about to turn. In all seven cases, whoever took the image had seen nothing at the time and found the object afterward, on reviewing it. None of those reports examines the 1986 mark or sighting.
