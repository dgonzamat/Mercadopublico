18:T4ee,What followed is one of the strangest encounters in the Project Blue Book archive. Shrum heard loud crashing in the underbrush coming toward him and took refuge in the lower branches of a tall pine. From there he saw two human-like individuals approach, about five feet tall according to the letter of astronomer Victor Killick, in a silvery suit, with no apparent neck and strongly protruding eyes. He believed they had seen him and were trying to dislodge him from the tree; then a third, different individual appeared, which he called a 'robot' for lack of a better description, and in Killick's version there were two robot-like objects. He shot arrows at the robot without driving it off; according to Killick he shot three, one hit with a metallic sound and he recovered it later with a dented point. He set parts of his clothing alight with matches and threw them, and the beings reacted violently while the flying objects rose. A vapor emitted by the robots made him gag and black out; his bow wedged in a crotch of the limb and kept him from falling, and later, harassed, he climbed higher and strapped himself to the trunk with his belt. The beings communicated with a 'cooing' noise. When he came to at dawn, the objects and their occupants were gone.19:T5e1,Shrum rejoined his companions, who according to the teletype told him it probably had something to do with the meteorite the government was looking for; later accounts credit one of them, Vincent Alvarez, with also seeing the lights, which the file consulted does not record. On his family's advice they called Killick, in charge of the Sacramento City College observatory, who wrote to Mather AFB on September 9: he called himself skeptical, but said the family seemed healthy and rational, that Shrum came home 'as pale as a sheet' and that he found no ulterior motive; he added that this part of the story checked because there was no moon that night. Shrum had a complete medical examination on his own, fearing radioactivity or lung damage. Captain McCloud and Senior Master Sergeant R. Barnes, of the 552nd AEW&C Wing intelligence at McClellan AFB, interviewed and taped him on September 21 and noted that he appeared 'stable and consistent' and believed what he described. The Foreign Technology Division asked whether the occurrence could be attributed 'to an owl or other such birds or animals, coupled with an overactive imagination on the part of the witness'; in its reply of October 8, McClellan answered 'Yes', added that the area could not be searched because of the fire danger and forwarded the tape and the arrow.
