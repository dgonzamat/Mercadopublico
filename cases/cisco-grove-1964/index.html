🇺🇸1964·Cisco Grove, Tahoe National Forest, California

United States.Cisco Grove · a hunter says two humanoids besieged him

On the night of September 4, 1964, bow hunter Donald Shrum, separated from his two companions near Cisco Grove, California, climbed a pine tree and later told the Air Force that two humanoid beings in silvery suits and a “robot” that exhaled a vapor which knocked him unconscious besieged him until dawn, after he saw lights circling above him. He defended himself with arrows and burning clothing. Intelligence officers from McClellan Air Force Base interviewed him and described him as stable and consistent; Project Blue Book closed the case as “Other (psychological).”

What happened

On Friday, September 4, 1964, three bow hunters from the Sacramento area went up into the Sierra on US 40, between Auburn and Truckee, near Cisco Grove, in the Loch Leven lakes area. One of them, Donald Shrum (his name is blacked out in the Blue Book file and appears in later accounts), about 27 according to the Air Force teletype, married and employed at a missile production plant, became separated from the group late in the afternoon and, rather than look for camp in the dark over rough terrain, decided to spend the night where he was. The teletype dates the event September 4 in its summary and September 5 in two other places; the Blue Book card dates it the 4th. Seeing lights over the ridge, he thought it was a helicopter looking for him and lit three signal fires on the ridge overlooking the Granite Creek valley.

According to the teletype, the light turned out to be three silvery objects, round and flat, with a rotating light, that approached, dropped two unknown objects as they descended and circled his position at about 50 to 100 feet (15 to 30 m), with a whirring sound; in the letter of astronomer Victor Killick, the first light made no sound. Shrum heard loud crashing in the underbrush coming toward him and took refuge in the lower branches of a tall pine. From there he saw two human-like individuals approach, in a silvery suit, with no apparent neck and strongly protruding eyes (about five feet, 1.5 m, tall according to Killick). He believed they had seen him and were trying to dislodge him from the tree. Then a third, different individual appeared, which he called a "robot" for lack of a better description; in Killick's version there were two robot-like objects.

He shot arrows at the robot without driving it off; according to Killick he shot three, one hit with a metallic sound and he recovered it later with a dented point. He set parts of his clothing alight with matches and threw them, and the beings reacted violently while the flying objects rose. A vapor emitted by the robots made him gag and black out; his bow wedged in a crotch of the limb and kept him from falling. Later, harassed, he climbed higher and strapped himself to the trunk with his belt. The beings communicated with a "cooing" noise. When he came to at dawn, the objects and their occupants were gone. The teletype gives a duration of three to six hours.

Shrum rejoined his companions, who according to the teletype told him it probably had something to do with the meteorite the government was looking for; later accounts credit one of them, Vincent Alvarez, with also seeing the lights, which the file consulted does not record. On his family's advice they called Killick, in charge of the Sacramento City College observatory, who wrote to Mather AFB on September 9. He called himself skeptical, but said the family seemed healthy and rational, that they told him Shrum came home "as pale as a sheet" and that he found no ulterior motive; he added that this part of the story checked because there was no moon that night. Shrum had a complete medical examination on his own, fearing radioactivity or lung damage. Captain McCloud and Senior Master Sergeant R. Barnes, of the 552nd AEW&C Wing intelligence at McClellan AFB, interviewed and taped him on September 21; they noted that he appeared "stable and consistent" and believed what he described.

On October 2, the Foreign Technology Division (FTD) asked that the interviewers be asked whether they felt the occurrence could be attributed "to an owl or other such birds or animals, coupled with an overactive imagination on the part of the witness." On October 8, McClellan answered "Yes" to that question, added that the area could not be searched because of the fire danger and forwarded the tape, the arrow and a copy of Killick's letter. The Blue Book card closed the case as "Other (Psychological)," describing it as an encounter with "beings" attributed to psychological causes, with the arrowhead and tape in the specimen file.

Why it matters

Everything extraordinary in the account, the humanoids, the "robot" that exhaled a vapor and the siege, rests on a single witness, interviewed 17 days later. The file consulted records no identified second witness, and the area was not searched. Two readings sit in the file at once: the interviewers and Killick saw no sign of deceit, and McClellan answered "Yes" to the question about an owl or other animals coupled with an overactive imagination. Blue Book closed the case as "Other (Psychological)."

The two period sources record what Shrum told them: the teletype summarizes his voluntary statement and his taped interview; Killick, what he told him by phone and in person.
