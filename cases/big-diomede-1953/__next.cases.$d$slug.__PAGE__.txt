50:T4e8,On 24 November 1953, the 5004th Air Intelligence Service Squadron, postal address APO 942, care of the Postmaster, Seattle, dated an Air Intelligence Information Report on AF Form 112, number IR 5004-40-53, with the subject "Sighting of Light on Big Diomede, Island, USSR". The country box reads "Alaska", but the area reported on is "Big Diomede, USSR", the Soviet island now called Ratmanov. The date of information is 8 October 1953, the source is Raleigh Iyapanna, the evaluation is "C-3", it was prepared by Major Francis M. Brown and signed, as commander, by Major Richard E. Loomis. It carries the control number AF 605523 and the handwritten note "Index" of the Air Force intelligence library. It was classified Confidential, had a single enclosure, a sketch of the observation, and was distributed to the intelligence staffs of the Alaskan Air Command, the Alaskan Command, US Army Alaska, the Alaskan Sea Frontier, the Far East Air Forces, the Northeast Air Command, the Air Defense Command, the 10th and 11th Air Divisions, the Pacific Fleet and the Office of Special Investigations of the Alaskan Air Command. The list does not include the technical intelligence centre (ATIC) that ran Blue Book, although the report was registered and indexed.51:T4a7,According to part II of the report, at about 1025Z (the cover sheet says 1015Z) the boy saw a light on the southern tip of Big Diomede, at an estimated height of 95 feet above sea level. He had been standing for about fifteen minutes in the centre of Little Diomede village, looking towards the Soviet island, when the light appeared; he saw it appear and watched it until it disappeared, several seconds later. It was the size of a nickel held at arm's length and about the colour of a burning match or a dim flashlight. In the boy's opinion, it came from a large flashlight or a spotlight. As soon as it went out he ran to his house, about 25 yards away, checked the time, which was 1030Z, and went back to his spot to keep watching, but the light did not reappear. The night was dark with no overcast, the stars were clearly visible and Big Diomede was plainly outlined against the sky; the wind was from the north at about 5 miles per hour.
