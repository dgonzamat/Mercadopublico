🇬🇹1953·Road from Guatemala City to Amatitlán, Guatemala

Guatemala.Volcán de Fuego · a golden object seen from a bus

On Saturday, January 17, 1953, at about 3:55 p.m., an American zoologist living in Guatemala was riding a bus down toward Amatitlán when he saw a greenish gold object outlined against the Volcán de Fuego. He said it flew northwest, nearly stopped, rose straight up, hung still for two seconds and flew on higher; he timed it at 22 seconds. A woman on the bus told him she saw it too. A U.S. Air Force agent interviewed him in New York on February 5, and Project Blue Book listed the case as unidentified.

Why no ordinary cause is identified
The only cause the file mentions, an aircraft, is ruled out by the Blue Book card itself: "Possibility of a/c is not feasible due to the hovering position of the object." The card concludes "Unknown (UNIDENTIFIED)," and the lists compiled by Don Berliner (1974) and Brad Sparks include the case without analysis. No source proposes another cause. The absence of an analysis is not evidence against an ordinary cause: in its fiscal year 2024 report, the Pentagon's AARO resolved "all 174 cases" to "prosaic objects," though "many other cases remain unresolved." What happened

On Saturday, January 17, 1953, at about 3:55 p.m. (9:55 p.m. Greenwich time), an American citizen living in Guatemala was riding the bus from Guatemala City to Amatitlán. He was a zoologist with an interest in natural history, had lived in the country on and off since 1934, and also worked as a sales representative for U.S. companies. The bus had just entered the stretch of Route 3 that drops steeply and starts to wind, southwest of the capital. According to the report, the bus was "…-10" kilometers southwest of the capital; the first digit is illegible. Through the open window, the passenger saw an object outlined against the slope of the Volcán de Fuego, which at that moment was off to his side.

He judged the object to be about 18 km away (the figure could also read 13), flying at about 6,500 ft (about 2,000 m), some 2,000 ft above the bus, which was at about 4,500 ft (about 1,370 m). It was heading northwest in a straight line at about 400 mph, twice the speed of a DC-3, the airliner he had seen at other times flying there in the same direction and used as a yardstick.

After 10 to 15 seconds, the object came almost to an abrupt stop, rose straight up about 1,500 ft (about 450 m), hung still for about two seconds, and flew on in the same direction at the same speed, higher up. The first digit of that new altitude is illegible; adding the initial 6,500 ft and the 1,500 ft climb would give 8,000. The witness lost it behind a ridge as the bus kept descending.

He described it as flattened, its vertical axis half the length of its horizontal one, bluntly rounded, with no flat surfaces, and perfectly symmetrical; it reminded him of a Goodyear blimp without its rear elevators or rudders. At that distance he could not tell whether it had depth: it looked flat. It was a brilliant greenish gold, mostly gold, perhaps because of the bright sun, he thought. He saw no trail, exhaust or means of propulsion, and heard nothing, perhaps because the bus was so noisy.

He gauged its size by looking through the opening of his loosely closed fist, a field method he said he knew well. According to the body of the report, the object looked about the size of a DC-3. He timed the sighting with his wristwatch, a stopwatch that read to a fifth of a second: 22 seconds, about 15 at the first altitude, 2 hovering and 5 at the second. The sky was clear with no haze, and it was cold. The plume of steam the volcano gave off every day did not change, he said.

The bus was full: the report says "24 with a driver and conductor," without making clear whether those two are part of the count. According to the report, the witness believed they were Indigenous people who would not have noticed the object or would not remember it. One woman passenger, whose name is blacked out, told him she had seen the "gold thing" for about four or five seconds; what she saw appears in the report only through him. The witness also suggested that a colony of Americans in Antigua Guatemala might have seen it. The witness also said there was no air traffic at the time other than the object, and that he had seen Aviateca DC-3s, the state airline's planes, in the area on other occasions.

On February 4, 1953, by then in New York, where he had gone to look after his sick mother, the witness telephoned District 2 of the Air Force Office of Special Investigations (OSI) to report that he had seen a "flying saucer." The next day Special Agent Henry M. Langlois interviewed him at the Savoy Plaza Hotel.

The witness showed his passport and handed over a sketch he had drawn about 30 minutes after the sighting, on letterhead of the Academy of Natural Sciences of Philadelphia, along with the notes he took at the time. He also marked his position and the volcano's on a road map of Guatemala. He said he had told no one about it until then. Langlois's report went to the Air Technical Intelligence Center (ATIC) in Ohio with a cover letter signed on behalf of Col. Frank P. Dunnington, the district commander.

The Blue Book record card, from the Air Force project that collected UFO reports, is on a form from the September 1963 edition, so it was filled in at least ten years after the event. It gives the time as 17/2155Z, one civilian source, one object, 22 seconds and a northwest course, and describes an "oblong" object twice the size of a DC-3. Its conclusion is "Unknown (UNIDENTIFIED)." It adds two comments: that riding a bus limited how long the witness could watch, and that an aircraft was not a workable explanation because the object hovered.

Why it matters

The case rests on a single named witness, whom the Air Force identified by his passport and whose name is redacted in nearly all of the released file. His account reached an agent 19 days after the event, although it was backed by notes and a sketch made half an hour afterward. The woman who also saw the "gold thing" appears in the report only through the witness, and what remains is what the two of them saw with the naked eye. What the nine-page file does preserve is detail: a position on a map, a heading, altitudes, a stopwatch timing and the state of the sky.

The file's own figures do not all agree. The synopsis of the OSI report and the Blue Book card say the object was twice the size of a DC-3; the body of the report says it was about the same size, and that what doubled was the speed. The report calls it "oblate," flattened, and the card "oblong." In the margin of the cover letter, someone wrote "More than one" by hand without saying of what; the card records one object and one civilian source.

The version repeated in case lists comes from Don Berliner, who read Blue Book's unidentified files at Maxwell Air Force Base in January 1974. It places the event "near Guatemala City," calls the witness a geologist and salesman, where the report says zoologist and sales representative, and says the object stopped and then "went straight up," as if it ended by climbing; according to the report, it climbed, hovered for about two seconds and flew on horizontally. Brad Sparks's catalog of Blue Book unknowns, on the NICAP website, copies that version. Neither mentions the woman passenger or the volcano, and an online forum spoke in 2026 of a dozen passengers; the report says 24.

According to a calculation done for this case, at 3:55 p.m. the Sun stood about 25° above the horizon in the west-southwest, 15° to 30° to the left of the line toward the volcano: the witness was looking toward the afternoon sun. Venus, the planet most easily seen in daylight, was about 66° up and the Moon about 55°, far from an object that, given the witness's distance and altitude, sat about 2° above the horizon. There were no artificial satellites in 1953.

The card ruled out an aircraft on a single feature, the pause in midair. That pause lasted about two seconds and was seen by a passenger on a bus going down a winding road, and the card's own first comment concedes that the bus limited the sighting. The file's nine pages record no inquiries in Guatemala: none with the woman passenger, with the Americans in Antigua, or with Aviateca's flight schedules.
