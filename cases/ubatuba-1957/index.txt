26:T53b,The provenance, however, could never be established: there were no identifiable witnesses to the supposed event and no chain of custody, only an anonymous letter. And later analyses —including those carried out or reviewed within the scientific panel convened by physicist Peter Sturrock in the 1990s, and other teams with modern instrumentation— wholly reframed the anomalous claim. The modern review qualified two things. First, the purity, though real, is not by itself anomalous: high-purity magnesium is producible by terrestrial means and in fact already was at the time. Second, the isotopic analysis, which would be the strongest evidence of a non-terrestrial origin, found no significant departures: the small differences detected are consistent with fractionation from heat treatment, not with material from elsewhere in the solar system. Some studies did note striking impurities —strontium, for example— that surprised the analysts, but that does not amount to a conclusive anomalous signature. The modern balance is that the sample is terrestrial magnesium, not proof of exotic technology.
