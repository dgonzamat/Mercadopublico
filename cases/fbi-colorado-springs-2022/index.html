🇺🇸2022·Cheyenne Mountain / Fort Carson, Colorado Springs (Colorado)

United States.Colorado Springs · five soldiers see an object hanging in the sky

At 9:35 a.m. on February 15, 2022, five U.S. Army soldiers at Fort Carson saw a motionless object about 6 miles to the west, over Cheyenne Mountain in Colorado Springs, home of NORAD's underground command complex. They described it as the size of a large jet, like "an angular, asymmetrical potato made of uneven panels," and watched it for 30 to 180 seconds. Years later, an FBI forensic artist drew the object from one soldier's account. An intelligence-community analysis, released in June 2026, concluded it was "possibly backscattering of sunlight," with "low confidence," and the case remains unresolved.

What happened

At 9:35 a.m. Mountain Time on February 15, 2022, five U.S. Army service members stationed at Fort Carson, a large base next to Colorado Springs, Colorado, saw an object in the air over Cheyenne Mountain, about 6 miles to the west. Beneath that mountain lies NORAD's hardened underground complex, now Cheyenne Mountain Space Force Station, one of the most sensitive command and early-warning centers in the country.

According to the summary of their accounts in an intelligence-community analysis, the object was over and slightly behind the mountain's silhouette, "roughly the size of a large jet and resembling an angular, non-symmetrical potato made of uneven panels." It stayed "completely stationary about 300-500 feet above" the mountain "while slowly changing shape," with a "slightly translucent, shimmering white appearance, with defined edges." They watched it for 30 to 180 seconds and, "despite keeping their eyes on the object, it suddenly disappeared." The witnesses said the sky was clear.

In June 2024, one of the five, a former U.S. Army intelligence officer, described the object to an FBI agent and an FBI forensic artist in New York so they could reconstruct its appearance. He placed the sighting on a day in February, at approximately 11:25 a.m., as the five left their office building, at about 50 degrees Fahrenheit.

He recalled a cloudless blue sky and a potato-shaped object with distinct edges, a creamy, opalescent white, made of irregular, non-overlapping panels like fish scales. The object itself was perfectly still, but each panel "shifted in slow waves." After about two minutes it vanished: it "cloaked," he said, in the time it took to turn a head, and left no shadow. The FBI also holds a digital rendering of the object reconstructed from the testimonies.

In March 2025, FBI agents interviewed one of the witnesses by video call. The record redacts his name and does not say whether he is the same former intelligence officer interviewed in 2024. This witness gave a different date and time: a workday in early February 2022, at about 1 or 2 p.m., under clear blue skies, as the five walked west toward the entrance of a building. The object hovered over a low point in the mountain, its "saddle": matte white, non-metallic in appearance, oval and horizontal, "bean-shaped," with a curved indentation underneath, and silent. They watched it for about 3 to 5 minutes and it did not change position relative to the saddle, so he did not believe it was drifting in the wind.

It was covered in intersecting lines that formed "an abstract polygon pattern." None of them had a phone; they "all simultaneously looked away momentarily" to decide who would fetch one from the vehicle, and when they looked back the object was gone. Afterward they went through what else it could have been, "such as a blimp or a weather balloon," and each drew what he remembered on his own; the drawings were consistent. He said AARO, the Pentagon office that investigates these phenomena, contacted him in early 2024 and that he responded, but that he "was never interviewed by AARO or anyone else."

The four-page intelligence analysis concluded that the object "was possibly backscattering of sunlight." The analysis computed the sun's position for 9:45 a.m., ten minutes after its own time for the sighting, without saying why: the sun stood some 27 degrees above the horizon, to the southeast; the snow covering Cheyenne Mountain, 6 to 12 inches deep, could have reflected light onto nearby low clouds and lit them up, and a slight shift of the clouds or the sun would have been enough to make the reflection vanish, which would explain the sudden disappearance.

Although the witnesses described a clear sky, several weather reports from that morning indicated partly to mostly cloudy skies. The analysis adds that no anomalous data were recorded, that the event did not represent an unknown adversary capability, and that no aircraft or balloons were active in the area. It holds its conclusion with "low confidence," because of uncertainty about each witness's field of view, the amount of snow cover, and the exact elevation and amount of cloud.

The accounts do not fully agree. The analysis dates the sighting to 9:35 a.m. on February 15; the former intelligence officer, to about 11:25 a.m. on a day in February; the witness interviewed in 2025, to early February, around 1 or 2 p.m. In the analysis, the object vanished while they were watching it; for that witness, when all five looked away; for the former intelligence officer, in the time it took to turn a head. No radar, telemetry or photograph of the event has been published.

Why it matters

Five soldiers saw, in broad daylight, a motionless object over the mountain that houses NORAD's command center, and the event left an unusual official record: two FBI documents with the witnesses' testimony, a reconstruction of the object, and a formal intelligence-community analysis.

The ordinary cause that analysis proposes depends on exactly what was not recorded: where each witness was looking, how much snow there was and how high the clouds were. That is why the analysis cannot go beyond a hypothesis.

The testimony itself sets the limit of what can be known. The witnesses remember a clear sky where the weather reports record clouds, and they disagree with one another on the time, on how long they watched (from 30 seconds to 5 minutes) and on how the object disappeared. Without an instrument record, neither the analysis's explanation nor the witnesses' account can be checked any other way.
