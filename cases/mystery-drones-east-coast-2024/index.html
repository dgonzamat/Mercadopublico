United States.Mystery Drones · East Coast (New Jersey + NY + bases)

Between November and December 2024 a wave of sightings of large, coordinated drones was reported over New Jersey and the U.S. East Coast, with reported overflights near Picatinny Arsenal, Naval Weapons Station Earle, the Salem and Hope Creek reactors and Governor Phil Murphy's residence; the White House said there were no confirmed sightings in restricted airspace.

Read the rest of the summary

The Biden administration said there was nothing anomalous and no foreign threat, and the Trump administration, on January 28, 2025, that the drones had been authorized by the FAA 'for research and various other reasons'. Several lawmakers questioned the explanation.

The night in question

The first public reports of large coordinated drones over New Jersey began in late November 2024. By mid-December, thousands of sightings had been registered through civilian apps and reports to the FAA, with videos of lights moving at low altitude, mostly at night. The FBI told Congress on December 10 that it had received over 3,000 tips from the public and that some of the craft were described as 'slightly larger than a commercially available drone, fixed wing as well as rotary'.

The reported overflights covered a wide zone: Picatinny Arsenal (US Army weapons R&D center), Naval Weapons Station Earle (naval ammunition transfer terminal for the Atlantic fleet), the Salem and Hope Creek nuclear reactors operated by PSEG Nuclear, Governor Phil Murphy's residential property in Middletown Township, and urban areas of northern New Jersey and Long Island. The FAA went so far as to impose temporary flight restrictions over Picatinny Arsenal and over the Trump National Golf Club in Bedminster. Governor Murphy convened press conferences demanding federal explanations. Senator Andy Kim (who personally joined a nighttime police patrol to observe the phenomenon) and Senator Cory Booker (both NJ) publicly pressed DHS and FAA, including by letter to Secretaries Buttigieg and Mayorkas and Director Wray. DHS and the FBI investigated with the FAA, the Department of Defense and local police, and deployed additional electronic detection equipment.

The official position was built in several acts. DHS and the FBI held that most sightings were cases of 'mistaken identity': the public confusing lawfully operating manned aircraft (planes on approach to busy airports like JFK, helicopters, and even stars and planets) with drones. On December 12, John Kirby, the White House national security communications adviser, said there was 'no evidence at this time' that the sightings posed a threat or had 'a foreign nexus'; that using 'very sophisticated electronic detection technologies' neither federal nor state authorities had been able to 'corroborate any of the reported visual sightings'; that many were actually manned aircraft operating lawfully; that the Coast Guard found no foreign involvement from coastal vessels, and that there were 'no reported or confirmed drone sightings in any restricted airspace'. The joint DHS, FBI, FAA and Department of Defense statement of December 16 summed it up as 'We have not identified anything anomalous'. At the same time, in a congressional hearing, a senior FBI official (Robert Wheeler, assistant director of its Critical Incident Response Group) acknowledged the limit of that knowledge: the FBI did not yet attribute the flights to any individual or group, and asked about the risk, he answered that nothing known led him to assert a threat, 'but we just don't know, and that's the concerning part'. Several lawmakers and Governor Murphy himself publicly rejected the explanation as insufficient. The Trump administration closed the matter its own way: on January 28, 2025 press secretary Karoline Leavitt read a statement from the president that, 'after research and study', the drones flying over New Jersey in large numbers 'were authorized to be flown by the FAA for research and various other reasons', many were hobbyists, it 'got worse, due to curiosity' and 'this was not the enemy'. The study behind that statement was not published, however, nor any forensic analysis of the drones, nor any identification of operators for the reported overflights near sensitive installations.

Why it matters

It combines four features: (a) media scale (thousands of sightings, sustained national coverage over weeks), (b) reports over national-security targets (Picatinny, NWS Earle, Salem/Hope Creek reactors), (c) explicit federal coordination DHS+FBI+FAA+Department of Defense, and (d) official explanation publicly disputed by sitting lawmakers of the affected state, including the Governor and senators. Three readings remain open: adversary technology (drones of unaccredited origin over critical infrastructure), a classified own-program reading (unacknowledged U.S. drones in testing) and the prosaic reading both administrations held (manned aircraft, lawful or FAA-authorized drones, hobbyists and stars). There was a joint federal investigation, sustained coverage and disputed public data, but no conclusive official identification nor physical recovery of a specific drone.
