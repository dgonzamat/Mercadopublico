Record of the event

What happened

At some point in 2023, U.S. Indo-Pacific Command reported to the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO), the Pentagon office that investigates these phenomena, something unidentified over the Yellow Sea. It came with footage captured by an infrared sensor (which records heat rather than visible light) aboard a U.S. military platform. The Department of War made it public on September 18, 2026, in the sixth release of the PURSUE program, split across five clips.

They run from 19 seconds to 1 minute 38 seconds, four minutes and ten seconds in all. All five carry the same location (Yellow Sea) and the same institutional provenance. Each official record presents its clip as a separate report, and none says whether the five belong to a single event, a single flight or different days; they are grouped here because they share command, year and location.

The official descriptions of the five videos are sparse. None asserts that there is an object: they speak of an "area of contrast," a region of the thermal image that differs from its surroundings, without committing to anything physical producing it. In every clip the sensor tracks that area, panning to keep it roughly centered or increasing magnification. In one, for the last few seconds, the platform's own structure gets in the way, blurring the image and blocking the right side of the screen. No description gives a shape, speed, altitude or operator testimony, and all five close with the same disclaimer: the text is informational only and is not an analytical judgment about the event.

One of the descriptions, for the 57-second clip, mentions the rectangle that appears near the center of the image, which it calls a "focus box": "This display element is a graphical component of the sensor system and does indicate a physical change in the environment." The two halves contradict each other: the sentence presents the box as an interface element and then states that it does indicate a physical change. The same sentence, identical character for character, appears in the description of another video, on the Middle East and unrelated to this event. No source explains it, but the repetition points to boilerplate text rather than one analyst's slip. The boilerplate most likely meant to say that the overlay does not indicate any physical change, because it is the focus marker the system itself draws.

The same command reported two other events captured by infrared sensors: one over the Yellow Sea on January 1, 2025, in which the sensor tracked a star-shaped pattern (↗ Yellow Sea), and one over the East China Sea in 2023, a thermal patch released the same day as these five clips with a description cut from the same cloth (↗ East China Sea).

Why it matters

The case proves little. It shows that a U.S. military command judged what its sensor recorded worth a report to AARO, and that the sensor was kept pointed at the same area, manually or automatically. It does not prove there was a physical object or say what it was, and the office itself is careful not to claim so.

No source puts it forward, but the context suggests one specific hypothesis: the Yellow Sea, bounded by China and the two Koreas, is a contested maritime space with dense military traffic, and 2023 is the year the Chinese high-altitude balloon program became a public matter in the United States; a surveillance platform belonging to another state fits that context. But the material itself imposes a limit: an "area of contrast" with no shape, no speed and no described behavior is also compatible with a bird, a balloon, a distant aircraft or an artifact of the sensor itself, and the official descriptions offer nothing that would separate those options.

The focus-box sentence also carries a lesson in how to read this release. Its descriptions come from templates, and an error in a template turns a caution about the interface into a claim about the physical world in every video it is copied into. So before taking an official sentence as data about the object, one has to check whether it recurs in unrelated records.
