Record of the event

What happened

At some point in 2023, U.S. Indo-Pacific Command reported to the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO), the Pentagon office that investigates these phenomena, something unidentified over the Yellow Sea. It came with footage captured by an infrared sensor (which records heat rather than visible light) aboard a U.S. military platform. The Department of War made it public on September 18, 2026, in the sixth release of the PURSUE program, split across five clips cataloged as DOW-UAP-PR143, PR144, PR150, PR151 and PR152. They run 19 seconds, 27, 57, 49 and 1 minute 38 seconds: four minutes and ten seconds in all. All five carry the same location (Yellow Sea) and the same institutional provenance. Each official record presents its clip as a separate report, and none says whether the five belong to a single event, a single flight or different days; they are grouped here because they share command, year and location.

The official descriptions of the five videos are sparse. None asserts that there is an object: they speak of an "area of contrast," a region of the thermal image that differs from its surroundings, without committing to anything physical producing it. In PR143 the sensor pans for all 19 seconds to track that area, keeping it generally centered, and PR152 says the same for 1 minute 38 seconds. In PR151, at 16 seconds the sensor increases magnification and continues tracking. In PR144 and PR150 the area is in the frame or enters it and the sensor tracks it; in PR150, between seconds 52 and 57, the platform itself blocks the view, blurring the image and blocking the right side of the screen. All five close with the same disclaimer: the description is offered for informational purposes only and should not be interpreted as an analytical judgment, investigative conclusion or factual determination about the event's validity, nature or significance.

PR150's description includes a sentence about the rectangular overlay that appears near the center of the frame, which the description itself calls a "focus box": "This display element is a graphical component of the sensor system and does indicate a physical change in the environment." The sentence presents the overlay as a graphical component, an interface element, and then states that it does indicate a physical change: the two halves contradict each other. The same sentence, identical character for character, appears in the description of DOW-UAP-PR133, on the Middle East and unrelated to this event. By this entry's own reasoning, not any source's, the repetition points to boilerplate text rather than one analyst's slip, and the boilerplate presumably meant to say that the overlay does not indicate any physical change, because it is the focus marker the system itself draws.

Another Yellow Sea event is documented, on January 1, 2025 (↗), likewise reported by Indo-Pacific Command and captured by an infrared sensor. On the same day as these five clips, and from the same command, DOW-UAP-PR148 was published: fifty-nine seconds of infrared sensor over the East China Sea in 2023, with a description cut from the same cloth; it is a different body of water and a different report (↗).

Why it matters

A U.S. military command escalated the report to the Pentagon office created to resolve these cases, and what was published, five clips and more than four minutes of infrared sensor, comes with a description that does not even assert that there is an object. "Area of contrast" describes what the sensor recorded and stops short of any inference. That the sensor followed that area across all five takes shows that it was kept pointed at it, manually or automatically; that says nothing about what it was.

By this entry's own reasoning, not any source's, the context suggests one specific hypothesis: the Yellow Sea, bounded by China and the two Koreas, is a contested maritime space with dense military traffic, and 2023 is the year the Chinese high-altitude balloon program became a public matter in the United States; a surveillance platform belonging to another state fits that context. But the material itself imposes a limit: an "area of contrast" with no shape, no speed and no described behavior is also compatible with a bird, a balloon, a distant aircraft or an artifact of the sensor itself, and the official descriptions offer nothing that would separate those options.

The inverted focus-box sentence changes what a reader understands of the image: it turns a caution about an interface element into an assertion about the physical environment. It appears identically in two unrelated descriptions, so the error may recur in other descriptions of the release.
