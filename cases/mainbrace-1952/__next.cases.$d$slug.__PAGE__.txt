13:T542,On September 13, on the eve of the exercise, the crew of the Danish destroyer Willemoes, near the island of Bornholm, reported a bluish triangular object moving at high speed toward the southeast. On September 19, at RAF Topcliffe (Yorkshire), several officers and aircrew on the ground observed a silver disc descending with a swinging, falling-leaf motion and rotating on its axis while following a Meteor jet on landing approach, before accelerating away to the west; the sighting was formally reported up the chain of command and is one of the episodes that kept the British Air Ministry's interest open barely a year after the Flying Saucer Working Party had recommended closing the subject. On September 20, aboard USS Franklin D. Roosevelt, photojournalist Wallace Litwin — embarked to cover the exercise — took a series of color photographs of a silver spherical object crossing the sky behind the fleet; Navy intelligence officers investigated on board, the hypothesis of a weather balloon launched from another ship failed to close the identification, and the file reached Project Blue Book.
