17:T48c,On 13 September, according to the press and later compilations — the original report has not been located — the crew of the Danish torpedo boat Willemoes, north of Bornholm, reported a bluish triangular object moving very fast toward the southeast. The best-documented episode is that of 19 September at RAF Topcliffe (Yorkshire). In his report to the station commander, preserved at the UK National Archives (AIR 16/1199), Flight Lieutenant John Kilburn of 269 Squadron said he was on the airfield with four other Shackleton aircrew watching a Meteor fighter descend, at about 5,000 feet, when one of them saw a silver, circular object between 10,000 and 20,000 feet and some five miles astern of the aircraft. They first took it for a parachute or an engine cowling; the object descended swinging 'similar to a falling sycamore leaf', appeared to follow the Meteor as it turned towards Dishforth, stopped, rotated on its own axis and accelerated towards the west before turning onto a south-easterly heading and disappearing, all within 15 to 20 seconds. The press carried it within days, distorted: an INS wire spoke of ten officers and men aboard a bomber.18:T532,On 20 September, aboard USS Franklin D. Roosevelt, photographer Wallace Litwin, sent by Metropolitan Sunday Newspapers to cover the exercise, saw a round object some 500 to 1,000 feet above the stern shortly after four in the afternoon and took three colour transparencies as it rose and moved away. His letter, reproduced in the Blue Book file, holds that it was not the ship's weather balloon, which according to the aerologist had been launched at 15:30 and lost in the clouds in about 50 seconds. Major Dewey Fournet of USAF Intelligence received the transparencies on 2 October, had enlarged copies made for Blue Book and returned the originals. The Navy Second Fleet's reply of 6 November states that no official investigation had been made until the USAF asked, that a 1,500-gram aerological balloon with a radar reflector was launched from the Roosevelt shortly before the pictures were taken, and that this balloon, about 8 feet in diameter at sea level and expanding with altitude, 'is believed to be the object sighted'; no other ship in the formation released balloons that afternoon. The Blue Book card carries the conclusion 'Balloon'.
