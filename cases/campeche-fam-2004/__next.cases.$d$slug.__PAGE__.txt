12:T4a2,The strongest prosaic explanation came soon and is robust. Several investigators —among them analyst and captain Alejandro Franz and skeptic Robert Sheaffer— concluded the 'objects' were flares from the oil platforms of the Cantarell complex, Mexico's largest field, located about 80 kilometers offshore in the same Bay of Campeche. Those towers burn excess gas with flames that can exceed 30 meters in height, whose thermal signature is visible to an infrared sensor several kilometers away. The apparent 'chase' or 'encirclement' of the aircraft would be a parallax effect: as the aircraft moves, fixed sources on the horizon seem to shift relative to one another in the sensor's two-dimensional image, with no depth. That the radar did not detect them is consistent with surface heat sources rather than solid objects in flight. The flares hypothesis is not universally accepted —part of the crew described motions it considers incompatible with fixed sources— but it economically explains the thermal signature, the radar absence and the apparent geometry.
