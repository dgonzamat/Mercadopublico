Turkey.Çandarlı · two Turkish Air Force pilots see an object circle them

On August 6, 2001, during a training flight in a T-37 jet trainer off Çandarlı, on Turkey's Aegean coast, two Turkish Air Force pilots reported a bright object, shaped somewhere between a cone and a disc, that approached at high speed and circled the plane for a time that sources put at anywhere from 2 to 30 minutes. They reported it to their controller and to the Turkish military's regional alert center. The Air Force later said it was a flock of birds or a weather balloon.

What happened

On August 6, 2001, two young Turkish Air Force pilots (named by the Turkish press, according to the MUFON-CES press release, as First Lieutenant Ilker Dinçer and Lieutenant Arda Günyer) took off around 12:30 Turkish time in a T-37, a U.S.-made twin-engine trainer, for a routine instruction mission off the coastal town of Çandarlı, in the Aegean Sea, in restricted airspace that MUFON-CES places over the Izmir-Çiğli air base. According to the press, a bright object appeared and approached the aircraft at high speed, then matched its altitude and speed and flew along with the plane and around it. The duration is unclear: sources speak of 2, 15 or 30 minutes.

According to the tower dialogue that leaked to the press from unidentified Air Force sources, and that MUFON-CES later reproduced, Dinçer described a shape like a cross between a cone and a disc, "with support legs, extremely luminous and high speed". He asked for radar confirmation, and control answered that it detected the maneuvering of his aircraft "but no detection of a second object around". Dinçer then moved his aircraft closer to the object, which also moved closer. The newspaper Hürriyet quoted the pilots' call: "Object approaching the wings. Now it's behind the plane ... now it's in front of us." Dinçer remarked that it seemed to be going for a dogfight. The encounter ended when the object disappeared at an incredibly high speed. The pilots reported the sighting in real time to their controller and to the regional war alert center of the Turkish army. ABC News published the story on August 8, 2001, adding that Turkish Air Force sources said they were investigating, that the Greeks were too, and that Turkey planned to report the encounter to international bodies, especially NASA. The MUFON-CES press release of September 6, 2001 covered it as well.

According to that release, the Turkish Air Force spokesman, Major Orhan Tamer, stated that the episode had been misinterpreted by the media and that the object was in fact a swarm of birds or a weather balloon, and added that disciplinary measures would be taken against the pilots to stop them from making further statements. The Turkish Meteorology Society told the newspaper Sabah it had no weather balloon in that area, that such balloons are launched only far from the military base and would be very easy to identify on military radar. Confronted with this, Tamer replied that General Erol Özgil had been told it was a balloon, but Özgil was not available for comment; that was the last official statement.

Why it matters

The two witnesses were military pilots on a training flight, and they called it in by radio as it happened. According to the MUFON-CES press release, its director, Illobrand von Ludwiger, holds that pilots are among the best witnesses because their job is to watch the sky and identify objects. He adds, however, that an unidentified object need not be an alien spaceship, and that the jet was in military airspace, since Dinçer could talk to ground control by VHF radio. There is no image or sensor record in the public material; what is known of the tower conversation is what unidentified Air Force sources leaked to the press, and the duration runs from 2 to 30 minutes depending on the source.

The Sirius UFO Space Sciences Research Center in Istanbul stated that a swarm of birds or a balloon could not dogfight with a jet at that speed, nor could the two pilots have been hallucinating at the same time, and called the balloon story a cover. According to the same center, a similar object had reportedly been seen less than 100 km from the Çiğli region before the pilots' sighting, and there was a wave of sightings in Turkey, many videotaped by civilians, police and military officers.
