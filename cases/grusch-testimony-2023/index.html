Part 01

The file in question

On July 26, 2023, David Charles Grusch — a former USAF intelligence officer previously assigned to the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency and to the UAPTF (Unidentified Aerial Phenomena Task Force) from 2019 to 2021 — testified under oath before the House Oversight Committee's Subcommittee on National Security, the Border, and Foreign Affairs. The hearing, titled 'Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena: Implications on National Security, Public Safety, and Government Transparency', was convened by Representatives Glenn Grothman and Jared Moskowitz and drew bipartisan backing — an unusual institutional framing for a subject historically marginalized on Capitol Hill.

Grusch stated under oath that during his service he received information from multiple primary witnesses indicating the existence of a 'recovery and reverse-engineering program' of vehicles of non-human origin, operated by Department of Defense contractors for decades, unauthorized to Congress, and that he had referred specific evidence to the Intelligence Community Inspector General (ICIG). ICIG Thomas Monheim had assessed the complaints as 'credible and urgent' in July 2022 — an administrative designation that, under the Intelligence Community Whistleblower Protection Act, triggers specific legal protection and congressional-referral processes. Grusch had filed his complaint in May 2022 and obtained protected-whistleblower status (Protected Disclosure of Urgent Concern), the legal framework that let him speak publicly without breaching his confidentiality agreement.

Grusch further stated that some witnesses reported 'non-human biologics' recovered alongside the artifacts. He repeatedly specified that his testimony was multi-source hearsay (not direct personal observation): he had not seen recovered material with his own eyes but was synthesizing what dozens of access-holding sources had described to him. He maintained that the primary witnesses were willing to testify in a SCIF (classified facility) and that he had given the ICIG and the congressional intelligence committee the names and locations of the programs in a classified setting. When lawmakers pressed for specifics, Grusch repeatedly invoked classification restrictions, which left most of his accusation without public documentary corroboration. Naval aviators David Fravor (Nimitz 2004) and Ryan Graves (Atlantic 2014-2015) testified alongside Grusch, their accounts of sensor-confirmed encounters contrasting with the hearsay nature of Grusch's testimony.

Part 02

Why this case moved the needle

The first sworn congressional testimony by a named former intelligence officer asserting the existence of undisclosed NHI (Non-Human Intelligence) recovery programs. The ICIG's 'credible and urgent' rating is a formal administrative act that conditions subsequent legislation (Schumer/Rounds UAP Disclosure Act 2023-2024) and establishes a legal floor for active congressional investigations continuing into 2026. Retired Colonel Karl Nell, a former adviser to the UAP Task Force, publicly corroborated Grusch's testimony in 2023 with no institutional contradiction — the first officer of that rank to do so.

The case's weight is twofold and must be read with epistemic honesty. On one hand, the procedure is solid: a named official with high-level clearances testified under penalty of perjury and obtained formal whistleblower protection; the case's probative value lies in that legal and institutional scaffolding, not in material evidence. On the other hand, the substance remains independently unverified: the testimony is entirely hearsay, the primary witnesses have not appeared publicly, and no artifact, declassified document, or biological remains have been produced to corroborate the claims. The Pentagon office dedicated to the issue, the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO), publicly stated that it 'has not discovered any verifiable information to substantiate' the existence of programs to possess or reverse-engineer extraterrestrial material, and its Historical Record Report (Volume I, March 2024) concluded that beliefs in recovery programs are best explained by misinformation, misunderstanding, and the internal circulation of rumor. Grusch and his supporters dispute the impartiality and scope of AARO's review. In turn, AARO's then-director, Sean Kirkpatrick, publicly countered — on LinkedIn and later in his 2024 Scientific American opinion piece, 'Here's What I Learned as the U.S. Government's UFO Hunter' — that Grusch 'refused to speak with AARO', so the specific details he said he had given to Congress never reached the office tasked with evaluating them; Kirkpatrick further holds that the coverup allegations derive from inadvertent or unauthorized disclosures of legitimate programs misinterpreted as non-human. The tension is unresolved: the case incontrovertibly documents that a credentialed whistleblower made these claims under oath and was taken seriously by the legal apparatus; it does not document that the claims are true.

On March 27, 2025, Representative Eric Burlison appointed Grusch as special advisor to the Task Force on the Declassification of Federal Secrets — a four-month term, with his security clearance reinstated after its loss had hampered earlier congressional investigations — carrying his advocacy role into the legislative process. In June 2026, Grusch returned to Capitol Hill in a bipartisan press event alongside Representatives Anna Paulina Luna, Tim Burchett, Eric Burlison, and Jared Moskowitz, pressing for UAP record declassification and reiterating allegations that intelligence agencies hide billions in secret spending through 'slush funds'. The continuity demonstrates that the 2023 testimony was not an isolated event but the start of a sustained institutional effort that continues without producing independently verifiable material evidence through 2026.
