17:T436,At the institutional scale the episode had a larger consequence: in October 1977 the Scientific-Technical Council of the Military-Industrial Commission, meeting in the Kremlin, recommended adding it to the defence research plan, and in 1978 two topics began, "Setka MO" (Ministry of Defence) and "Setka AN" (Academy of Sciences), to study anomalous atmospheric and space phenomena and their effect on military equipment and personnel. The program ran for 13 years, until 1990; physicist Yuly Platov was deputy chairman of the Academy's expert group. According to his assessment, published in 2000 with Sokolov, about 3,000 reports were received over 13 years, nearly all analysed and identified, of which just over 300 events were classed as unusual or anomalous, and practically all mass night-time sightings were identified as effects of rocket launches or aerospace tests; units serving at the test ranges sent almost no reports, because those effects were familiar there.
