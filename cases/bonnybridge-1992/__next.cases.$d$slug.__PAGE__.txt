12:T46e,What is distinctive about Bonnybridge is not any single, well-documented incident — there is no Nimitz or Lakenheath here — but the sustained volume and, above all, the local institutional response. Falkirk district councillor Billy (William) Buchanan took up the issue as a banner: he wrote letters to the Prime Minister and the Ministry of Defence asking for an official investigation, and in early 1993 organised a public meeting on the phenomenon at a Bonnybridge hotel. According to the Scottish press of January 1993, residents had already reported at least twenty-two close encounters, and Buchanan said he had been swamped by calls from frightened people who had seen UFOs in the previous two weeks; by late July 1993 he spoke of more than two hundred sightings, and his insistence brought a Japanese television crew to the town. Buchanan has kept the campaign going for decades, demanding that the Scottish and British governments investigate formally.
