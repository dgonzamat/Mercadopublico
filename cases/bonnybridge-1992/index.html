🇬🇧1992–1997·Bonnybridge, Falkirk (Scotland)

United Kingdom.Bonnybridge · hundreds of light sightings in one town

From 1992 on, the Scottish town of Bonnybridge, in the so-called "Falkirk Triangle," piled up hundreds of reports of lights and objects in the sky, nearly all at night, with no instruments and at most a home video. Councillor Billy Buchanan took the matter to government: he wrote to the Prime Minister, and in early 1993 organized a public meeting on the sightings. The conventional causes proposed include aircraft on the Edinburgh and Glasgow routes, secret military training and, according to ufologist Ron Halliday in 1993, a ScottishPower utility helicopter.

What happened

From 1992, the small town of Bonnybridge, in the Falkirk district (central Scotland), became one of the places with the most UFO sightings: the British TV channel History (UK), on its website, speaks of one of the busiest calendars for sightings in the world. The area was dubbed the "Falkirk Triangle." The best-known report is the 1992 one from local businessman James Walker, who according to History (UK) had to stop his car on a country road because a brightly shining star-shaped object hovered ahead of him, and who saw it take off at great speed. A 2014 report by Chris McCall for the digital outlet VICE counts more than 600 reports between 1992 and 1994. The local press of January 1993 also mentioned a home video by a Bonnybridge farmer, William Denholm, who said he had filmed lights over his house.

There was no single, well-documented incident: what grew was the volume of reports and the response of the Falkirk district councillor, Billy (William) Buchanan. He wrote to the Prime Minister seeking answers and, in early 1993, organized a public meeting on the phenomenon at the Norwood House Hotel in Bonnybridge. According to the Scottish press of January 1993, at least twenty-two close encounters had already been reported, and Buchanan said he had been swamped by calls from frightened people who had seen UFOs in the previous two weeks.

On March 29, 1993, the editor of SPI Enigmas, the magazine of the group Strange Phenomena Investigations, wrote to the British Ministry of Defence asking about the Bonnybridge sightings, which he said had been occurring since November 1992. On April 13, the ministry office that coordinated UFO reports replied that it knew from other researchers of the wave but had received no reports from that area, and that it could not suggest any military activity to explain it, other than the military jets the area sees from time to time.

By late July 1993, Buchanan spoke of more than two hundred sightings. The Bristol newspaper The Journal reported on July 30 that the councillor's enthusiasm was largely responsible for a visit by a Japanese television crew from the Nippon Television Network, led by Junichi Yaoi. In the same piece, the Scottish ufologist Ron Halliday, a driving force behind the Scottish UFO Report Line, said he was convinced most of the Bonnybridge sightings were down to a ScottishPower helicopter working in the area, and that Buchanan and others had pushed the affair so hard that it had become a bit of a circus.

In 2014, Buchanan told VICE that he and the investigator Malcolm Robinson had written to every prime minister since 1992 without getting any closer to an answer.

The conventional explanations offered cover the wave as a whole, not each report. According to VICE (2014), skeptics pointed out that Bonnybridge lies under the flight paths serving Edinburgh and Glasgow international airports, and that a commercial airfield operates only a couple of miles away, in Cumbernauld. History (UK) adds that secret military training has caused other UFO sightings and that the Scottish Lowlands make a good flight-testing area. It adds that marsh gases explain some of the smaller lights, though not the larger, louder sightings.

Why it matters

Bonnybridge brought together hundreds of reports with no instrumental record, and those reports reached an elected official, who wrote to the Prime Minister. The Ministry of Defence, in its archived reply of April 1993 to a group of investigators, says it had received no reports from the area and could see no military activity to explain it, other than the jets the area sees from time to time.

The wave lasted years and drew Japanese television, while those following it closely disagreed about its cause: Buchanan asked for a formal investigation and Halliday, who promoted a sightings hotline, pointed to a utility company helicopter.

It asks the ministry for information on a spate of sightings in central Scotland, particularly over Bonnybridge, which it says have been occurring since November 1992 and continue; it says Edinburgh, Glasgow and Cumbernauld airports could not clarify them, and asks whether they could be military exercises, the Aurora aircraft or drone tests over populated areas. It is the letter the ministry answers on April 13; it is a private letter, not a Crown work.
