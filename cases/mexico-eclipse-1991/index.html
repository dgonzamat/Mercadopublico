🇲🇽1991·Mexico City and central Mexico

Mexico.Mexico eclipse · amateurs film a bright object in the dark sky

On July 11, 1991, during totality of the last total solar eclipse of the 20th century over Mexico City, amateur videographer Guillermo Arreola filmed a bright, metallic-looking object hanging motionless in the darkened afternoon sky. Journalist Jaime Maussan aired it on television, thousands of viewers said they had seen the same thing, and footage from at least fifteen people was gathered. In the following years Mexico saw a wave of sightings. Amateur astronomer Tim Printy reconstructed the sky with planetarium software and identified the object as the planet Venus.

What happened

On July 11, 1991, millions of Mexicans went out to watch the sky for the last total solar eclipse of the 20th century over the center of the country: an exceptionally long totality (almost seven minutes) that darkened Mexico City in mid-afternoon. Among those filming the event, amateur videographer Guillermo Arreola captured a bright, seemingly metallic object, shaped like a dark-based disc, hovering motionless in the already-dimmed sky. He handed the tape to journalist Jaime Maussan, who aired it, according to the Unsolved Mysteries case page, a week later on his television news-magazine show.

The broadcast had a snowball effect. Thousands of viewers called in to say they too had seen (and in many cases recorded) an identical or similar object that same day. Over time, footage was gathered from at least fifteen different people, taken from points more than a hundred kilometers apart in and around the metropolitan area. The object was described consistently: metallic, with a rounded top and a flat or dark base, sometimes with lights, hanging below the cloud layer. The coincidence of so many independent recordings was presented as proof that something physical and real had been there, and the episode came to be called "the best-documented mass sighting in history."

Skeptic and amateur astronomer Tim Printy reconstructed the positions of the celestial bodies during the eclipse with planetarium software and concluded that the object matched the planet Venus, highly visible in the darkened sky during totality. Venus lay east of the Sun, a few degrees from the star Regulus. Its disc, 35 arcseconds across, was roughly 40% illuminated, which according to Printy "would give the appearance of a domed disc or half disc," although this phase "may or may not have been apparent" in the videos because the planet's brightness overwhelms it. Printy attributes the dark "shadow" on one side of the "disc" to a common artifact when recording Venus on video, producing the illusion of a three-dimensional metallic body. He does not explain its cause, but shows a video capture of his own of Venus with the same shadow on the planet's right side.

Several videos also show Jupiter, Regulus and Mars in their correct positions relative to Venus; in Sky & Telescope, Dennis di Cicco wrote that there were "dozens of reports of people finding brilliant Venus a half hour and more before totality." To Printy, the fact that the object was seen in the same area of the sky from widely separated places indicates it was very high ("45 million miles is pretty high!", about 72 million km): a nearby object would shift position between separated observers (parallax). He also notes that it did not move and was visible only about 30 minutes before and after totality, which in his view fits Venus. Britt Elders, who promoted the videos with Lee Elders, said on television that they had checked three computer programs and that there were no stars at that position. Printy replies that Venus is a planet.

Not every tape fits: of one brief clip with a low object Printy says "This is not Venus" and that it could have been the landing lights of a distant plane, and of another, with the horizon in view, that it "may be the bright star Sirius or Canopus."

Why it matters

The case matters for what came after. According to the case page of the U.S. program Unsolved Mysteries, which aired it in 1994, Mexico had thousands of sightings from July 1991 on, most of them around the capital. Two months after the eclipse, Vicente Sánchez filmed a bright dot during a military air show; a year later a disc was seen there descending fast and vanishing, and at the 1993 show a metallic one appeared beside a squadron of helicopters.

The identification with Venus rests on Printy's reconstruction of the sky and his review of the videos, not on an instrumental record of the object. Printy is sure of it for almost all the tapes he reviewed, not for every one. He adds that astronomers in Mexico City did not report the object in any scientific journal and that there are no wide-angle photographs of totality that show it.

Documents and sources

Documented evidence

  1. 01Guillermo Arreola's original recording during the totality of the July 11, 1991 eclipse, broadcast on television by Jaime Maussan.
  2. 02At least fifteen independent videos of the same object, taken from points more than 100 km apart in and around the metropolitan area.
  3. 03Consistent description of the object: metallic, rounded top and flat/dark base, motionless below the cloud layer.
  4. 04Astronomical reconstruction (Tim Printy, using planetarium software): the object's position matches the planet Venus, visible during totality.
  5. 05Venus was roughly 40% illuminated (35″), which would give it the look of a domed disc or half disc, though its brightness may have hidden the phase in the videos; according to Printy, the "shadow" on one side of the disc is a common artifact when recording Venus on video, and he shows it with a capture of his own.
  6. 06Absence of parallax between widely separated observers: the signature of a body at astronomical distance, not a craft over the city; the object was visible from about 30 minutes before to about 30 minutes after totality, like Venus; two clips do not fit Venus (a possible aircraft and a possible low star).
