17:T54f,On January 28, 1994, Air France flight 3532 (from Nice toward London) was flying over the Paris region in an Airbus A320. Around 13:13, cruising at about 11,700 m with excellent visibility and a layer of altocumulus over the Paris region, three people in the cockpit (Captain Jean-Charles Duboc, co-pilot Valérie Chauffour and a steward) saw an object in the sky. According to Duboc's own account, the steward saw it first and exclaimed "A sounding balloon!"; the co-pilot also took it at first for a sounding balloon, and Duboc for an aircraft banking at 45 degrees. Duboc placed it at about 10 o'clock, some 25 nautical miles (46 km) away and at about 10,500 m, almost directly above Paris: a brownish-red disc with slightly fuzzy edges, no wings or engines, which he estimated at nearly 1,000 m across and 100 m thick, and which they could see from several angles for almost a minute. As they left it behind on the left, he said, it "became transparent and disappeared without moving". The co-pilot saw it blur and turn into a "chevron" shape, and unlike Duboc, who thought it almost stationary, she felt it moved at the aircraft's speed, always on the left.
