2c:T680,The chase stretched east for about 138 kilometers (86 miles according to the Pennsylvania press; 'about 80' according to Spaur himself), crossing from Ohio into Pennsylvania. Along the way more badged witnesses joined. Patrolman H. Wayne Huston of East Palestine (Ohio), who was following the radio, stated in writing to investigator William Weitzel that he watched the object pass overhead at about 800 or 900 feet, shaped 'like an ice cream cone' with the dome on top, and fell in behind Spaur. In Pennsylvania, officer Frank Panzanella of Conway, who was not on the same frequency, signed that he had been watching a bright object shaped like 'half of a football' for about ten minutes when the Ohio cars arrived. The four watched it hover, let a plane taking off from Pittsburgh airport pass beneath it and climb vertically until it was the size of 'a ballpoint pen'. Separately, Mantua police chief Gerald Buchert said he watched it for 15 to 20 minutes from his home, about 15 km north of Ravenna, as 'two saucers' joined at the rim, and photographed it. On the negative side, the file itself records that the FAA radar at Pittsburgh and the Oakdale SAGE radar squadron saw nothing between 5:30 and 6:15, that no interceptors were scrambled, and that Blue Book's analysis found the light does not appear on Buchert's negative: the two images would be processing defects in fogged film. According to a press clipping in the same file, Buchert said an enlargement indistinctly showed the Moon and, to its right, a dark disc-shaped object surrounded by a ring of light; that morning, according to Hynek, Venus stood a few degrees to the upper right of the Moon.2d:T740,The official investigation was dismissive. Major Hector Quintanilla, head of Project Blue Book, handled the case with two phone calls to Spaur that Spaur timed at roughly two and a half and one and a half minutes. According to a signed statement by Spaur quoted by Hynek, Quintanilla wanted him to say he had seen the object for only a few minutes; when Spaur answered that they had kept it in sight almost continuously from one state to the next, Quintanilla ended the call. The file's written conclusion says Spaur first saw a satellite and then 'focused his eyes on the planet Venus', rising in the southeast at magnitude -3.9, and the record card adds a processing defect for the photo. The same file partly contradicts it: the Smithsonian told Blue Book it did not think an Echo satellite would have been visible over Ohio at that hour, and Spaur's phone report of April 18 already noted a waning Moon to the east and 'one other object like a star' to its right, that is, Venus seen as a separate point. Computed from ephemerides, at 5:06 Eastern time Venus stood about 11 degrees high at azimuth 110, to the right of and above an 11 percent Moon; in the taped interview Quintanilla claimed Venus had risen at 2:49 at azimuth 150 and elevation 25 degrees, figures that do not fit that morning. In May, former congressman Robert E. Cook wrote to Representative William Stanton that the Venus conclusion was 'ridiculous' and that no Air Force investigator had come to talk to the witnesses; only then did Quintanilla travel to Ravenna. Weitzel taped the interview at Spaur's request, and J. Allen Hynek, Blue Book's scientific advisor, reproduced excerpts in 'The UFO Experience'; Huston and Panzanella were never interviewed.
