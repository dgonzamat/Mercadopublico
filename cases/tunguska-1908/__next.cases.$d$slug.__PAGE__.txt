16:T48d,The remoteness and the political chaos of early-20th-century Russia delayed scientific expeditions. The first major one, led by Leonid Kulik, did not reach the area until 1927. In his report to the Academy of Sciences, Kulik described the flattened forest lying "in generally parallel rows", tops pointing away from the centre, standing trunks stripped of branches and bark, and traces of a "uniform and continuous burning" over an area several tens of kilometres across. He took the central basin for "a huge crater" and noted dozens of fresh "craterlets", from several metres to tens of metres across, which he proposed to excavate; he also collected the testimony of residents of the Vanovara trading post, some hundred kilometres away: S. B. Semenov said a sudden heat almost burned the shirt off him and the explosion threw him off his porch, and P. P. Kossolapov that something scorched his ears and the blast broke a window pane and tore off the stove door. But he found no meteorite, and no macroscopic fragment of the object has ever been recovered, nor an impact crater confirmed.
