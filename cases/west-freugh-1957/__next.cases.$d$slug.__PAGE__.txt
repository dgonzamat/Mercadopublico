4c:T57e,On April 4, 1957, while a test bombing exercise was being monitored over the Irish Sea, radar units of the Ministry of Supply Bombing Trials Unit, headquartered at RAF West Freugh (Wigtownshire, south-west Scotland), detected an unidentified echo. One of the stations (at Balscalloch, near Corsewall Point) did not receive the order to stand down when the exercise ended and kept tracking: it recorded a large, solid, almost stationary echo over the Irish Sea. Per the case reconstruction, the object held essentially still for about ten minutes at around 50,000 feet, 20-25 miles north of Stranraer; it then climbed to about 70,000 feet, turned south-east and accelerated to around 240 mph toward the Isle of Man. Altogether it was tracked by radar for about 36 minutes by three stations: Balscalloch; Ardwell, 14 miles south, which confirmed the echoes at the same range and height; and a third that at one point tracked four objects at 14,000 feet, in line astern 4,000 yards apart.
