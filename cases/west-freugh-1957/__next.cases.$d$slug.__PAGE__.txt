13:T582,On April 4, 1957, while a test bombing exercise was being monitored over the Irish Sea, radar units of the Ministry of Supply Bombing Trials Unit, headquartered at RAF West Freugh (Wigtownshire, south-west Scotland), detected an unidentified echo. One of the stations —at Balscalloch, near Corsewall Point— did not receive the order to stand down when the exercise ended and kept tracking: it recorded a large, solid, almost stationary echo over the Irish Sea. Per the case reconstruction, the object held essentially still for about ten minutes at around 50,000 feet, 20-25 miles north of Stranraer; it then climbed to about 70,000 feet, turned south-east and accelerated to around 240 mph toward the Isle of Man. Altogether it was tracked by radar for about 36 minutes by three stations: Balscalloch; Ardwell, 14 miles south, which confirmed the echoes at the same range and height; and a third that at one point tracked four objects at 14,000 feet, in line astern 4,000 yards apart. The Air Ministry technical intelligence report (DDI Tech) of 30 April deduced that altogether five objects were detected by the three radars; that at least one rose to 70,000 feet while remaining appreciably stationary in azimuth and range; that all appeared capable of about 240 mph; and that they were very effective radar reflectors, "either of considerable size or else constructed to be especially good reflectors".14:T426,Since the case was in the headlines, several Members of Parliament —among them Major Sir Patrick Wall, Conservative MP for Beverley— questioned the Air Ministry. The notes prepared for Minister George Ward to answer on 17 April said the object was tracked for 36 minutes, that no service or commercial aircraft was in the vicinity, that it could not have been a balloon "since it was moving against the wind", and suggested replying that the cause had not been established and that "it may well have been a private aircraft". An 11 April memo regretted that the Wigtownshire incident "fell into the hands of the press" and recommended not referring to two other, unpublicised cases as radar sightings. The file is part of the UK National Archives' records on aerial phenomena.
