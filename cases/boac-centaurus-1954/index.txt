2a:T8b2,On the ground, during the Goose Bay stop, U.S. Air Force intelligence officers interviewed Howard and his crew. By the time they reached London the story had already hit the British press, and on arrival Howard gave interviews —the BBC preserves footage in which he reproduces his sketch of the object. There was also a disputed instrumental component: several sources hold that the objects were detected on radar at the USAF air station in Cartwright, Labrador, which would raise the case from 'crew visual sighting' to 'visual plus radar'. That point was never documented with an accessible primary radar record, so it is cited with caution. The Project Blue Book file (1954-06-8714325) holds the military telex of the time: the pilot of aircraft G-ALSC saw 'one large object with five or six smaller ones around it' for about 18 minutes, from 0105 to 0123 GMT, at 51°53'N 63°10'W and 19,000 feet, some 10 miles off his port side; 'when we and fighter got contact with the a/c objects faded', and ten other crew members verified it. The same file carries two 1954 evaluations: a first report rated the sighting 'unknown natural phenomena, possibly a mirage', since a temperature inversion in the area made it possible, and the USAF's Air Technical Intelligence Center (ATIC) closed it as 'inversion reflection of the planet Mars'. The latter fits poorly with the telex geometry: by this file's own calculation for the place and time, Mars was very low in the southeast (azimuth ~145°, ~3° altitude), on the starboard side of an aircraft flying northeast, whereas the objects were seen to port, toward the northwest, where the Sun had just set. Another message in the file, describing a yellowish object at a bearing of 144° identified at the time as a planet, adds that 'no mirage effect had been announced during the day'. The prosaic explanations offered over the years —a mirage, a lenticular cloud deforming in the dusk light, an atmospheric optical illusion— account for the large object's shape-shifting but fit less well with the small objects holding formation and with the phenomenon's persistence over 18 minutes. Unlike a case with a calibrated sensor, here the record is a hand drawing and the concordant testimony of professionals: strong, but not decisive. The most serious prosaic hypothesis —a lenticular cloud or an atmospheric mirage of the dusk light— elegantly explains the large 'shape-shifting' object, and should not be dismissed; its weak point is the retinue of six or seven small objects holding formation, which a cloud does not produce.
