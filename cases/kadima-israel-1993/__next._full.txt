2e:T5c5,In the spring of 1993, the agricultural moshav of Kadima, near Netanya, was the setting for a series of reports of encounters with a non-human entity, the start of a wave that the area, today regarded as an Israeli ufological 'hotspot', would extend through 1998. Between April and July, according to journalist Barry Chamish, four professional women in their late thirties (three in the Kadima area and one in Rishon Letzion) reported encounters with bald, round-faced giants over seven feet tall, that is, more than 2.1 meters, in silvery suits. The first was Tsiporet Carmel: in the early morning of April 20, according to Chamish, she saw by her house something she took for a silo and, some ten yards away, a seven-foot being in metallic overalls with its head covered by something like a beekeeper's hat; where she had seen the craft a 4.5-meter circle appeared. Another, Shosh Yahud, the village treasurer, said that one night in May she woke to see beside her bed a being about seven feet tall, round-faced and in silvery overalls, who assured her he meant no harm and left through the wall; the next morning she saw a 4.5-meter circle from her window. Chamish later summed up that, since 1993, that small area had seen five highly documented encounters with tall beings, at least fifteen landing circles imbued with a red cadmium-based liquid and hundreds of craft sightings. That convergence between descriptions is the trait that drew local investigators' attention.2f:T482,The police and military response usually linked to the case belongs, in Chamish's own account, to another episode: in January 1995, in the village of Yatzitz, the local security chief said he saw a three-meter giant and called the police, who found deep tracks by the house, and army trackers, thinking of an infiltration, followed 8.5 kilometers of tracks. The sources consulted record no police deployment in Kadima in 1993. The case's problem is one of evidential provenance. Although the finding of high-purity silicon by a geological institute sounds impressive, pure silicon is not exotic (it is the base material of the semiconductor industry and appears in many industrial contexts) and no chain of custody was ever published that verifiably tied the analyzed sample to the field circle. Worse, the case's documentation and diffusion were handled by Israel's most enthusiastic ufology, in particular Barry Chamish, an author of highly questioned credibility who went so far as to frame the encounters within a biblical, prophetic narrative.
