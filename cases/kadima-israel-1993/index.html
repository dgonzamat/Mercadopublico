🇮🇱1993·Moshav Kadima, near Netanya (Israel)

Israel.Kadima · four women report silver-suited giants

Between April and July 1993, according to journalist Barry Chamish, four women (three in the moshav of Kadima, near Netanya, and one in Rishon Letzion) said they met bald, round-faced giants over 2.1 m tall in silvery suits. Chamish reported landing circles with oils and metal shards, one analyzed by the Geological Institute of Israel as elemental silicon. The footprints that army trackers followed for 8.5 km and the arrival of police belong, in his own account, to another episode, Yatzitz in January 1995. Almost everything known comes from Chamish.

What happened

Between April and July 1993, according to journalist Barry Chamish, four women in their late thirties described encounters with bald, round-faced giants over seven feet (2.1 meters) tall in silvery suits. Three lived in the area of the agricultural moshav of Kadima, near Netanya, and one in Rishon Letzion.

The first was Tsiporet Carmel: in the early morning of April 20, according to Chamish, she saw by her house something she took for a silo and, some ten yards away, a seven-foot being in metallic overalls with its head covered by something like a beekeeper's hat; where she had seen the craft a 4.5-meter circle appeared. Another, Shosh Yahud, the village treasurer, said that one night in May she woke to see beside her bed a being about seven feet tall, round-faced and in silvery overalls, who assured her he meant no harm and left through the wall; the next morning she saw a 4.5-meter circle from her window.

According to Chamish, in each case a landing circle with odd metals and oils appeared nearby. In his winter 1994 article in the British magazine Flying Saucer Review, Chamish says an object landed near Kadima on March 30, 1993, three weeks before the first of the four encounters, and that on returning the next day it left metal shards which Tel Aviv University, he says, found to be of unknown origin. The surviving summary of that article does not say who saw the landing or link it to any of the four women.

According to that same summary, Chamish quotes a Dr. Fuhner, whom he presents as director of the biochemical department of the State of Israel Institute for Geology; no source gives his full name: on May 27, 1993 he received a 2.7-gram sample, and two tests showed it to be elemental silicon, "a substance not found on this planet."

The red liquid in the circles, Chamish writes, was tested by the National Biological Laboratory in Ness Tziona, which found it composed mostly of cadmium. Chamish adds that other samples tested 99.35% pure silicon at the University of Manchester. The large 18-meter circle with that fluid and shiny shards that is often cited is not from 1993: Chamish visited it after another landing in Kadima in January 1995.

Chamish acknowledges that Israeli ufologists were divided over the silicon because there was a prankster loose in Kadima, and that many feared he had planted it in the circles. He rejects the suspicion with new analyses he attributes to Dr. W. C. Levengood, of the Pinelandia Biophysical Lab, and John A. Burke, of the Am-Tech Laboratory: Chamish had collected the material and sent it to Levengood in 1995 for testing on behalf of the TV show Sightings. According to Chamish, they confirmed the earlier results and proved that this silicon would be far too expensive for a hoaxer.

Their report, as he quotes it, says the crystals are pure silicon without the "growth lines" that would normally be expected if they had been thermochemically grown as a pure single crystal, that "silicon does not normally occur in this pure form," and that it is highly unusual to find the material in a field, since it is very expensive to produce. Apart from Chamish's quotation, no chain of custody is on record tying the sample analyzed by the Geological Institute to a specific circle.

The police and military response usually linked to the case belongs, in Chamish's own account, to another episode: in January 1995, in the village of Yatzitz, the local security chief said he saw a three-meter giant and called the police, who found deep tracks by the house; army trackers, thinking of an infiltration, followed 8.5 kilometers of tracks. The sources consulted record no police deployment in Kadima in 1993.

Chamish later summed up that, since 1993, that small area had seen five highly documented encounters with tall beings, a count that covers the following years and that he does not break down, along with at least fifteen landing circles imbued with a red cadmium-based liquid and hundreds of craft sightings.

Why it matters

Kadima combines an account of tall beings with landing circles and sample analyses, but almost everything comes from a single author, Chamish. The analyses by Israeli institutions, those of the Geological Institute, Tel Aviv University and the National Biological Laboratory in Ness Tziona, are known only through his account. The later tests at Manchester and by Levengood and Burke are also reported by him.

No source consulted proposes what happened in the encounters, and there is no skeptical, police or academic analysis of the case.
