19:T642,It opens Israel —and with it the Middle East beyond Iran and Iraq— in a corpus where the region was the largest geographic gap. Its value is double and deliberately modest: regional coverage and a contrast case. Kadima gathers ingredients that in other files would be powerful —convergent independent descriptions, sampled physical traces, documented police response— but builds them on the worst provenance base: a maximally exotic morphology (giant grays), no independent lab analysis of the residues, and diffusion dominated by a sensational author. Hence Tier B and low probability: it is exactly the kind of case the corpus's discipline requires including without inflating —high strangeness, fragile evidence— as a reminder that the vividness of an account is not its evidential weight. The Border Police response is the only firm institutional element. And it is worth puncturing the 'pure silicon' mirage: though invoked as a hard technical datum, high-purity silicon is an ordinary industrial material —the substrate of all microelectronics— and, without a chain of custody tying it to the field circle, it certifies nothing exotic. Kadima thus works as a teaching case on evidential weight: on paper it gathers more ingredients than many celebrated files —convergent testimonies, traces, analysis, police— and yet it collapses once the provenance of each piece is examined.
