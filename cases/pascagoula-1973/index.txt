2b:T4ed,Around 7:00 p.m. on October 11, 1973, Charles Hickson, 42, and Calvin Parker, 19, were fishing from an abandoned pier on the west bank of the Pascagoula River, on the Mississippi coast. According to the UPI dispatch of the next day, quoting the chief deputy, they saw a strange craft about two miles away emitting a bluish haze, which came closer and hovered three or four feet above the water. In his statement that night, Hickson described it as a blue light that circled a bit, with a buzzing sound, and as an oblong object about eight feet tall with an opening at one end. From it, gliding, came three robotic-looking figures (wrinkled, grayish skin, claw-like hands, what looked like conical protrusions on the face) that immobilized them and took them inside the craft, where they described being examined by a kind of optical instrument before being returned to the bank. Hickson estimated they were inside for 15 to 20 minutes. Parker, the younger man, told officers he passed out when the creatures emerged and did not regain consciousness until he was back on the pier; Hickson recounted that his own body floated toward the craft. Both said they had not been drinking beforehand, but admitted having "a drink or two" afterward to settle their nerves.2c:T7aa,Later checks reinforced the impression of good faith without supplying material proof. The next morning they were taken to Keesler Air Force Base, where doctors ran a radiation check and swabbed their hands and shoes, and the base intelligence chief heard their story; the base promised to send its report to the sheriff, but according to detective Tom Huntley it never arrived, and the base information office said the case was closed. The next day, according to the UPI dispatch, base officials said the examination by Air Force physicians had found no evidence of radiation. The UPI dispatch notes that neither had apparent injuries, and that the sheriff received other calls that night about a strange blue light in the sky. Ralph Blum spoke days later with some of those witnesses: city councilman E. P. Sigalas, riding in a car with probation officer R. H. Broadus, said that around 7:40 p.m. they saw something they first took for a large helicopter and that looked "perfectly cylindrical"; service station owner Larry Booth saw, shortly after 9:00 p.m., a low, slow, silent object with a row of rotating red lights; and detective Huntley told him a shipyard crane operator had also seen something.
