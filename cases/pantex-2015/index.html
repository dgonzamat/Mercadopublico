🇺🇸2015·Pantex Plant, Amarillo (Carson County), Texas

United States.Pantex · an unidentified object flies over the nuclear plant

On September 1, 2015, the ground surveillance radar at the Pantex Plant in Amarillo, Texas, the main U.S. nuclear-weapons assembly site, detected an unidentified object to the west flying north at 10–15 mph. Security officers activated the counter-drone plan, locked the gates and chased it by vehicle. Through binoculars they saw no means of propulsion and heard no sound; the object, a "diamond" about 4 feet tall, sped up and changed course as they followed. An armored vehicle's camera tracked it and it was filmed from the radar tower; the video went to Sandia laboratory and the evidence to the FBI. The report was declassified in 2026.

Record of the event

What happened

On September 1, 2015, at around 07:08–07:10, the ground surveillance radar at the Pantex Plant detected an unidentified object flying in a "non-threatening" manner west of the facilities, heading north at about 10–15 mph (16–24 km/h). Pantex, in Amarillo, Texas, is the primary U.S. nuclear-weapons assembly and disassembly facility. The object appeared roughly 1.75 miles (2.8 km) southwest of the Zone 12 South building, near Highway 60. The plant's security force activated the drone response plan approved by the production office of the National Nuclear Security Administration (NNSA): all pedestrian and vehicle gates in the security areas were immediately secured and patrols repositioned to protect assets.

A lieutenant and a security police officer pursued the object by vehicle along Pantex Drive, trying to get directly beneath it to observe it. They could not catch up; when they stopped and got out, they noted that the object made no sound and that, even with binoculars, they could not identify any propulsion system. They watched it for 1–2 minutes before it continued north off the site; they followed it several miles until they lost it for lack of roads, near County Road G and FM 1342. They noted the object "seemed to increase speed and change direction while being followed," its final heading east-northeast. The Carson County Sheriff's office was notified and a deputy responded, but never saw the object.

The descriptions were partly inconsistent: a "diamond" shape, more rounded at the top, about 4 feet tall by 2 feet wide at the base (1.2 by 0.6 meters); the color varied between black, silver, red and blue depending on the witness. Another team followed the object for 3–5 minutes with the camera on the remotely operated weapon station of a Bearcat armored vehicle, at 75–100 meters, and estimated its altitude at 100–200 feet (30–60 meters). Video was obtained from the nearest radar tower and sent to Sandia National Laboratories for enhancement and study, though the distance left little detail. All evidence, statements and video, was turned over to an FBI agent.

The report, marked Unclassified Controlled Nuclear Information (UCNI), was declassified in 2026 within the UAP Records of AARO, the Pentagon office for these phenomena (code DOEUAPD005). In September 2026 the NNSA released, in response to Freedom of Information Act request 23-00131-R, another copy of the same report, dated September 2, 2015, with almost all of the text redacted: what can be read confirms that Protective Force personnel followed the object by vehicle "for several miles north of the plant until it was no longer visible," that a comprehensive review of the incident was immediately initiated, and that the object was described as a "diamond" type shape "more round at the top." The cover letter of September 9 places the incident on September 2, one day after the date given in the report AARO declassified.

Why it matters

The object flew over the plant that assembles and dismantles the U.S. nuclear arsenal, and the institution itself documented it with several means at once: a security radar detected it, several officers saw it at close range, one with binoculars, an armored vehicle's camera followed it, and it was filmed from a tower. The video went to Sandia National Laboratories, the evidence ended up with the FBI and the report was filed as Controlled Nuclear Information.

The plant treated it as a drone incursion: it activated its drone response plan, and at no point did the object threaten sensitive assets. In 2015, amid a wave of drone incursions over U.S. military and nuclear installations, one hypothesis is an unmanned system, perhaps adversary surveillance. Against that reading stand the details the observers gave: no sound and no visible propulsion, a "diamond" shape barely over a meter tall, colors that differed from witness to witness, and the impression that the object sped up and changed direction when pursued. None is conclusive on its own, because judging a distant object by eye is easily deceived, but together they keep it from being closed as just a drone. Neither the video sent to Sandia nor the evidence given to the FBI has a published result.
