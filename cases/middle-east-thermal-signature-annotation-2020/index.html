Middle East, CENTCOM area of responsibility (exact location undisclosed).Middle East · a sensor records an unidentified hot spot

In 2020, U.S. Central Command sent the Pentagon office that studies UFOs (AARO) one minute and three seconds of infrared video recorded over the Middle East, which was released as “unresolved.” The sensor tracks an area of contrast that brightens and stands out more and more against the background. Before sending it, the person who reported it paused the video at the 10-second mark and drew a white line around that area with the note “U/I SMALL THERMAL SIGNATURE” (unidentified small thermal signature). The record does not say whether that was the sensor operator or an analyst.

Why no ordinary cause is identified
No source proposes what the thermal signature was: the official PR40 record only describes the footage, warns it is not an analytical judgment and publishes it as unresolved, and the reporter himself labelled it "U/I" (unidentified). The aircraft, vehicle or wildlife mentioned here are readings made for this case, not a source's. The absence of a published analysis is not evidence against an ordinary cause: in its 2024 report AARO stated that "all 174 cases have been finalized as resolved to prosaic objects including balloons, birds, UAS, satellites, and aircraft." Record of the event

What happened

In 2020, on a date that has not been made public, an infrared sensor on a U.S. military platform recorded one minute and three seconds of video over the Middle East. An infrared sensor builds its image from the heat that objects give off, not from visible light. U.S. Central Command (CENTCOM) sent it through its own official channel to the Pentagon office that studies unidentified anomalous phenomena (UAP), the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO), which filed it and released it under the title "DOW-UAP-PR40, Unresolved UAP Report, Middle East, 2020" (the video's code, followed by "unresolved UAP report").

According to AARO's description, during the first nine seconds an area of contrast (a patch that stands out from the background) brightens and becomes more and more distinct. At second 10 the video stops and stays paused until second 14. AARO explains that "the original reporter digitally altered the imagery by pausing the video playback and adding a white line encircling an area of interest at timestamp 00:10, annotated with the phrase U/I SMALL THERMAL SIGNATURE" (an unidentified small thermal signature). AARO adds that it "did not edit the originally reported material" and that it presents the video as received, mark included.

From second 15 to 63 the video resumes: the sensor pans to follow the area of contrast while it cycles through several contrast and zoom settings. A frame-by-frame review made for this case shows the sensor looking down through broken cloud, with a north marker on screen.

The circled object is a point only a few pixels across, located up and to the left of the reticle (the cross that marks the center of the image) rather than under it. Around second 9, the point appears as a short streak. After the pause, there is a flat, washed-out frame around second 16, and the contrast changes that follow make the point almost impossible to follow by eye for the rest of the clip. According to AARO, however, the sensor keeps tracking it.

The public record gives no coordinates, type of platform or exact date, and the video does not reveal them: the data the sensor overlays on the screen are covered with black rectangles. AARO released the video on May 8, 2026, in the first batch of its PURSUE release program.

Why it matters

The annotation describes no shape or behavior. In military shorthand, the note says only that there was a small heat source that could not be identified; a "thermal signature" is an object's heat as the sensor sees it. It carries no adjective suggesting unusual behavior: whoever wrote it noted what the instrument showed, a point of heat that grew sharper.

No source proposes what it was. The most direct conventional reading, made for this case and not taken from any document, is a small heat source, such as a small aircraft, a vehicle or even an animal, whose heat was enough to stand out in the sensor image. Without flight data, coordinates or mission context, the case cannot be closed any more precisely: the object is a point a few pixels across, with no measurable shape or path.

The lack of a published explanation is not evidence against a conventional cause: in its 2024 report, AARO stated that all 174 cases had been finalized as resolved to conventional objects, including balloons, birds, drones, satellites and aircraft, and added that many other cases remain unresolved. That "all" refers to the resolved cases, not to the total.
