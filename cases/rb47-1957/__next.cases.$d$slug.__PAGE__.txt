McClure recalled years later that it happened while crossing the coast near Gulfport. At 10:10 Z, now heading west, the pilot saw 'a very intense white light with light blue tint' cross in front of the aircraft from 11 to 2:30 o'clock and vanish; the copilot also saw it. McClure retuned to S-band and found the signal at 10:30 Z; at 10:39 Chase saw a huge light about 5,000 feet below, and at 10:40 the monitor showed two signals while the pilots saw two red lights. Chase got permission to leave the flight plan, asked the Duncanville (Texas) air defense radar, call sign 'Utah', for help, and accelerated to Mach 0.83 while the light pulled ahead.17:T415,The wing report records the key phase near Dallas-Fort Worth. Utah confirmed an echo about 10 nautical miles northwest of Fort Worth; around 10:50 Z the light appeared to stop, the aircraft overshot it, and Utah and the No. 2 monitor lost it at the same time; as the aircraft turned, the signal, the ground echo and the light reappeared. At 10:52 the aircraft closed to about 5 nautical miles, the light appeared to drop to about 15,000 feet and was lost visually and from the ground radar. At 10:57 the monitor had a signal but Utah had no contact; at 10:58 the pilots saw the light again, and around 11:02 they headed home for lack of fuel, with the signal behind the aircraft until it faded, at 11:40, abeam Oklahoma City. The wing concluded it had 'no doubt' that the electronic bearings coincided with the visual observations 'numerous times'.
