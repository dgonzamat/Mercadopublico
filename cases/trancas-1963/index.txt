2a:T423,Trancas became one of the most famous cases in Argentine and Latin American ufology, cited for its testimonial richness and for the rare fact of having a physical residue analyzed at a university. It should, however, be read with caution. On one hand, the men of the family were not present during the episode, so the bulk of the account comes from a core of witnesses related to one another, under conditions of nighttime fear; on the other, part of the story grew in later versions with dramatically toned details absent from the initial reports. And the residue's composition —calcium and potassium carbonates— is not in itself exotic: it appears in common organic and mineral materials, so what was deemed striking was its purity, concentration and distribution in that context, not an impossible chemical signature. Decades later, serious investigators of the phenomenon (such as Alejandro Agostinelli) have revisited the case skeptically, noting that its fame exceeds what the file actually supports.
