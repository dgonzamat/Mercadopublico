1963·Trancas, Tucumán

Argentina.The Trancas Case

On the night of 21 October 1963, the Moreno family reported several luminous objects at their farm in Trancas (Tucumán): one settled near the house, by the railway tracks, for about 45 minutes, while others lined up in the distance. A beam of light was directed at the house, and a residue was collected from the site. It is one of the most famous Argentine cases for the number of witnesses and for having a laboratory analysis of the residue (mostly calcium carbonate). It retains dramatically toned reported elements and never had a formal resolution.

Part 01

The night in question

On the night of 21 October 1963, at a Moreno family farm in the town of Trancas, Tucumán province (Argentina), the residents —the elderly couple Antonio and Teresa Moreno, three adult daughters, the children of one of them and the domestic worker; the husbands of two of the daughters, army officers, were away— reported the appearance of luminous objects. The chain of events, according to the accounts, began when a teenage domestic worker insisted she saw lights over the embankment of the Belgrano railway, in front of the house, some 200 meters away by some accounts and about 300 yards by Creighton's compilation. The witnesses described several objects —the accounts speak of up to six— one of which descended and settled near the home, by the tracks, where it stayed for about 45 minutes; others lined up in the distance. The people described a beam of light projected toward the house, a penetrating smell, and a sensation of heat and confinement as they sheltered inside. The wire report the Corriere della Sera published on 25 October already summed up the accounts this way: six discs about eight metres across, hovering some 40 minutes a few tens of metres up, with a smell of sulphur, a rise in temperature and two beams, one white and one purplish-red; and it added that the witnesses were «seemingly victims of a collective hallucination». According to Creighton's compilation, the maid and one of the daughters, Julia Moreno de Colotti, saw by the track «something like a small train, intensely illuminated» with figures moving about it, and Antonio Moreno said he saw silhouettes at the portholes of one disc. Gordon Creighton's press compilation also records that some neighbours, José Acosta and the Huanca family, saw the illumination on the embankment from a distance, and that Francisco Tropiano, some two kilometres away, saw six discs cross the sky between 10:15 and 10:20 p.m., the time the siege of the house ended. A journalist who came to the house the next day wrote that the abnormal heat and sulphurous smell were still noticeable in the rooms.

The case stood out from other sightings for the number and consistency of the testimonies, the long duration of the phenomenon and, above all, because the next day a residue was collected at the site: small white pellets that crumbled to the touch, found within a radius of nearly 30 meters, and similar material on the railway tracks. At the police's request, Dr. Walter Gonzalo Tell, of the Institute of Chemical Engineering at the National University of Tucumán, analyzed the sample and reported a composition of about 96.48% calcium carbonate and 3.51% potassium carbonate; later analyses (National University of the South) confirmed calcium and potassium carbonates, and small spheres of calcium and magnesium were also reported on the tracks.

Trancas became one of the most famous cases in Argentine and Latin American ufology, cited for its testimonial richness and for the rare fact of having a physical residue analyzed at a university. It should, however, be read with caution. On one hand, the bulk of the account comes from a core of witnesses from one family, under conditions of nighttime fear, and the neighbours only saw lights from a distance; on the other, part of the story grew in later versions with dramatically toned details absent from the initial reports. And the residue's composition —calcium and potassium carbonates— is not in itself exotic: it appears in common organic and mineral materials, so what was deemed striking was its purity, concentration and distribution in that context, not an impossible chemical signature. Decades later, serious investigators of the phenomenon (such as Alejandro Agostinelli) have revisited the case skeptically, noting that its fame exceeds what the file actually supports. In 1988, Roberto Banchs proposed that the family mistook anti-aircraft searchlights and an armoured vehicle wrapped in camouflage smoke during military manoeuvres that were never acknowledged; Agostinelli's dossier notes that the husbands of two of the witnesses were army officers who had left that same night for manoeuvres in the north of the country. Banchs added that the calcium carbonate residue might come from Sperry searchlights. Oscar Galíndez rebutted that reading in 2009: the Salta manoeuvres took place between 24 October and 6 November 1963, after the 21 October event, and were a rural anti-guerrilla operation that used no armour or anti-aircraft searchlights, although the Belgrano railway had been moving troops toward Salta. The case never had a closed, formal explanation.

Part 02

Why this case moved the needle

Trancas matters for a combination uncommon in South American casework: many sustained witnesses, long duration, and a physical residue that reached a university laboratory and was documented in writing. That lifts it above the anecdotal sighting and explains why, sixty years on, it remains the most cited Argentine case inside and outside the country.

But it is, above all, a good exercise in evidentiary discipline, and that is what makes it valuable for a calibrated corpus. The lab datum —calcium and potassium carbonates— sounds impressive until one notes it is not an exotic signature but a mixture found in many ordinary materials; without the original sample preserved under chain of custody, contamination, confusion, or a mundane origin for the dust cannot be ruled out. Add to that the fact that the account accumulated embellishments in later versions and that the core of witnesses was related and frightened. Trancas serves, then, as a teaching case: a genuine classic, with a real testimonial kernel and an honest analytical attempt for its time, to be appreciated for what it has that is documented without letting fame fill the gaps. Strong testimonial anomaly, prudent material conclusion.
