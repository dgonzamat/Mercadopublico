🇦🇷1963·Trancas, Tucumán

Argentina.Trancas · a family sees six luminous objects by its farm

On the night of October 21, 1963, the Moreno family saw six luminous objects at their farm in Trancas (Tucumán, Argentina) which, according to La Gaceta's first report, hovered very low for some forty minutes, almost all the time over the railway embankment, while one came to about 30 meters from the house's gallery; Creighton's compilation gives 45 minutes. A beam of light was aimed at the house, and a residue collected at the site was found by the laboratory to be mostly calcium carbonate. The case never had a formal resolution.

What happened

On the night of October 21, 1963, the people at the Moreno family farm in Trancas, Tucumán province (Argentina), were the elderly couple Antonio and Teresa Moreno, three adult daughters, the children of one of them and the domestic worker. The husbands of two of the daughters, army officers, were away. According to the accounts, it all began when the maid, a teenager, insisted that she saw lights over the embankment of the Belgrano railway, in front of the house, some 200 meters away by some accounts and about 300 yards (about 275 meters) by Gordon Creighton's press compilation.

The first report, by La Gaceta de Tucumán's correspondent and published on October 23, says a "squadron" of six objects stayed very low near the farm for some forty minutes, almost all the time over the embankment: three in front of the farm and two a little further north, more than a kilometer away. Another came to about 30 meters from the main gallery, behind the garden and over the entrance gate. From the gallery, where the heat had driven them, the women saw in front, over the tracks, three illuminated objects. After forty minutes, the report says, they moved off at low altitude toward the Medina hills. Creighton's 1966 compilation gives 45 minutes instead and holds that one of the objects stayed over the embankment the whole time and was the first to rise before the rest followed.

The witnesses described a beam of light projected toward the house, a penetrating smell and a sensation of heat and confinement as they sheltered inside. According to Creighton, the maid and one of the daughters, whom he calls Julia Moreno de Colotti, saw by the track "something like a small train, intensely illuminated" with figures moving about it; La Gaceta names her Yolie Moreno de Colotti, Alejandro Agostinelli's dossier calls her Yolié, and the 1963 report likens those figures to "a gang of track workers".

La Gaceta also says that Don Antonio, aged 72, was ill and even so tried to approach the object in the garden, and that his daughter Argentina, Mrs. de Chávez, went after him to bring him back to the house, saw the object from very close and maintains she saw six circular windows of changing brightness. According to Creighton, Don Antonio said he saw silhouettes at the portholes.

On the neighbors, the two sources disagree. Creighton records that José Acosta and the Huanca family saw the illumination on the embankment from a distance, and that Francisco Tropiano, some two kilometers away, saw six discs cross the sky between 10:15 and 10:20 p.m., the time the siege of the house ended. La Gaceta's report, written the next day, says that very few neighbors saw anything and that Tropiano managed to see, after ten o'clock, "the east sector of the place strangely lit", opposite his house. A journalist who came to the house the next day wrote that the abnormal heat and the smell of sulfur were still noticeable in the rooms.

La Gaceta's correspondent wrote that a collective hallucination was "very improbable given the personality and sensitivity of those involved". The wire report the Corriere della Sera published on October 25 summed up the accounts this way: six discs about eight meters across, hovering some 40 minutes a few tens of meters up, with a smell of sulfur, a rise in temperature and two beams, one white and one purplish-red; and it added that the witnesses were "seemingly victims of a collective hallucination".

The next day a residue was collected at the site: small white pellets that crumbled to the touch, found within a radius of nearly 30 meters, and similar material on the railway tracks. At the police's request, Dr. Walter Gonzalo Tell, of the Institute of Chemical Engineering at the National University of Tucumán, analyzed the sample and reported a composition of about 96.48% calcium carbonate and 3.51% potassium carbonate. Later analyses by the National University of the South confirmed calcium and potassium carbonates, and small spheres of calcium and magnesium were also found on the tracks.

In 1988 Roberto Banchs proposed that the family mistook anti-aircraft searchlights and an armored vehicle wrapped in camouflage smoke during military maneuvers that were never acknowledged, and that the calcium carbonate residue might come from Sperry searchlights. Agostinelli's dossier notes that the absent husbands had left that same night for maneuvers in the north of the country. Oscar Galíndez rebutted that reading in 2009: the Salta maneuvers took place between October 24 and November 6, 1963, after the event, and were a rural anti-guerrilla operation that used no armor or anti-aircraft searchlights, although the Belgrano railway had been moving troops toward Salta. The case never had a formal explanation.

Why it matters

A whole family said six luminous objects hovered very low beside their house for some forty minutes, one of them about 30 meters from the gallery, and the next day a residue was collected at the site that a university chemical engineering institute analyzed at the police's request. Many witnesses, a long duration and a physical sample with a written analysis seldom come together in a single event.

The core of the account, however, comes from members of one family, frightened and at night, and the neighbors, according to the 1963 report, saw little and from a distance. Part of the story grew in later versions: where La Gaceta says Tropiano saw the east sector lit up, Creighton's compilation has him watching six discs fly past. Alejandro Agostinelli, who revisited the case skeptically, notes that its fame exceeds what it documents.

The residue's composition, calcium and potassium carbonates, is not exotic: it occurs in ordinary organic and mineral materials, so what was striking was its purity, concentration and distribution at that spot. Without the original sample preserved under chain of custody, contamination, confusion or an ordinary origin for the dust cannot be ruled out. Banchs proposed a military explanation for the lights and the residue, but Galíndez dated the Salta maneuvers to after the event.
