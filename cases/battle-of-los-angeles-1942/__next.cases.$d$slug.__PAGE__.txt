13:T405,The famous photograph published by the Los Angeles Times on February 26 —half a dozen searchlight beams converging on a luminous point in the sky— became the visual icon of the episode and is still cited today by UFO culture as proof of a solid object. Later analysis, however, established that the image was heavily retouched in the darkroom, a routine editorial practice of the era to 'clean up' and dramatize press photographs; what looks like an illuminated disc is largely a retouching artifact. In 1983, the Office of Air Force History formally reviewed the case and concluded that the most likely trigger was one or more weather balloons released around 01:00 which, combined with the expectation of attack, set off the first alarm; the other batteries opened fire in a chain reaction on seeing their neighbors' muzzle flashes, fueling an hour-long barrage against an essentially empty sky.
