Researcher Robert Powell (then MUFON director of research), with radar analyst Glen Schulze, requested the FAA's radar data for that night under the Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) and reviewed on the order of 2.5 million return points. The report identified primary radar returns without a transponder —objects the radar saw but that were not identified as cooperative traffic— temporally correlated with the sightings, some at very high speeds. Critically, by the analysis, published on 4 July 2008, one of those tracks, built from 187 returns, moved at about 49 miles per hour for over an hour toward the restricted airspace of George W. Bush's ranch in Crawford, some 50 miles southeast of Stephenville, and corresponded to an object at least 524 feet in size. The same dataset confirmed F-16 jets operating in the area, which the report treated as objects distinct from the unidentified track. Aviation consultant Jay Miller objected in the Dallas Morning News that flares do not burn silently and that a jet dropping them is in afterburner, and Allen said the operating area was not close enough and that what he saw was flying near the ground.
