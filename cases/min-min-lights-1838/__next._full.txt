2a:T441,The scientific explanation came from Professor Jack Pettigrew, a neuroscientist at the University of Queensland, in work published in 2003. Pettigrew proposed and demonstrated that the Min Min light is a Fata Morgana-type mirage: on clear desert nights, a layer of cold, dense air settles over the ground beneath warmer air, creating a density gradient that acts as an optical waveguide. That layer can carry the light of a real source —a campsite fire, a vehicle's headlights, even a bright star below the geometric horizon— over tens or hundreds of kilometers, keeping it concentrated and making it appear as a floating luminous point. The apparent 'following' is explained because the source is so far away that the parallax is nil: as the observer moves, the refracted light stays in the same relative direction, as the Moon does.
