5:T433,On the remote plains of the Channel Country, in western Queensland, travelers of the Australian outback have recounted since the 19th century a light —the 'Min Min'— that appears at night, seems to follow whoever observes it, keeps its distance, and fades when approached. The name comes from a now-vanished hotel on the route between Boulia and Winton; the tradition is even older, present in Aboriginal accounts. In 2003, neuroscientist Jack Pettigrew (University of Queensland) proposed, from a number of direct observations and a field demonstration, that the Min Min light is a Fata Morgana-type mirage: light from real and very distant natural or man-made sources, ducted over tens or hundreds of kilometers by a thermal inversion of cold air over the ground.
